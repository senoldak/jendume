Nokia, Nscale, Skeleton, Telia, and Verda have joined forces to form a consortium named "AI Gigafactory Northern," aiming to secure a contract for the European Union's planned AI gigafactories. This strategic partnership seeks to address the EU's growing need for high-performance computing infrastructure by creating a federated network of facilities across the Nordic and Baltic regions. The proposed project plans to distribute its operations across sites in Oulu, Finland; Iru, Estonia; Valmiera, Latvia; and a fourth location in Sweden.

By spreading capacity across borders, the consortium intends to leverage the region's cool climate and low-carbon energy sources, which significantly reduce the power required for cooling data centers. Each partner brings a specific technical strength to the table. Nokia is set to provide the network connecting the sites, while Nscale offers platforms for training and running models. Telia contributes connectivity and cloud services, and Skeleton Technologies provides power systems designed to ease the strain on the electricity grid.

This initiative aligns with the EU's "InvestAI" program, which aims to mobilize a total of 30 billion euros for artificial intelligence development. The funding structure includes 20 billion euros from private companies and 10 billion euros from public sources. This effort reflects Europe's broader strategy to establish a trusted, scalable, and sovereign infrastructure. The tender is managed by the European High-Performance Computing Joint Undertaking, which also oversees shared supercomputers like Finland's LUMI. Successful bidders will be responsible for building and operating these facilities, from which member states will purchase computing time. This development marks a significant step in Europe's ambition to compete globally by controlling its own AI backbone.
