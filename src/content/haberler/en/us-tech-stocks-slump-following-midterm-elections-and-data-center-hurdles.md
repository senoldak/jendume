Technology and artificial intelligence stocks experienced market volatility following the United States midterm elections, as the country's most valuable tech companies faced a massive sell-off on Thursday due to investor anxiety. The election results and potential political shifts are viewed by financial analysts as a prospective roadblock to massive plans for building artificial intelligence data centers across the country. As markets contemplated what a changing political landscape might mean for tech regulation, investors reacted strongly to the uncertainty surrounding the future pace of the AI boom.

Chipmakers and neoclouds heavily reliant on data center projects were hit particularly hard during the market downturn. Nvidia lost 9.3%, AMD shed 17.3%, Microsoft dropped 3.6%, and Tesla fell 10.8%. Neocloud companies involved in serving AI computing power also experienced steep declines, with Nebius dropping 15%, Iren falling 17%, and CoreWeave dropping 10%. By Friday morning, U.S. tech stocks showed signs of recovery, but the market blip served as a warning of potentially rougher roads ahead for the artificial intelligence expansion in America.

Even before the election, AI stocks on Wall Street had been looking shaky due to concerns over rising interest rates and overspending on infrastructure. According to research by the International Data Center Authority, between January 2024 and May 2026, at least forty-six AI data center projects across twenty U.S. states were blocked, withdrawn, or stalled following community opposition, representing more than $170 billion in announced investments. Analysts note that opposition to data centers is a long-standing bipartisan yet local issue that makes the overall buildout increasingly expensive rather than halting it entirely.

Financial institutions like Goldman Sachs estimate that AI-related capital spending could total trillions of dollars across computing, data centers, and power in the coming years. However, rising regulatory and political risks, combined with local ballot measures restricting data center constructions, have caused investors to reassess the speed of infrastructure growth. Industry experts suggest that the artificial intelligence race is shifting toward a war of attrition where scarce power and regulatory hurdles favor the largest balance sheets in Big Tech.
