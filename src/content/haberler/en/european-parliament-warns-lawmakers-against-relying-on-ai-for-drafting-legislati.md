European Parliament President Roberta Metsola has issued a warning to members of parliament regarding the reliance on artificial intelligence when drafting legislation. She emphasized that while AI can assist in administrative tasks, it cannot replace the essential human judgment and direct debate required in the lawmaking process. The responsibility for every legislative decision must remain firmly with elected representatives, rather than being delegated to automated systems.

This caution comes amid growing concerns over a rise in amendments drafted with AI, some of which have incorrectly cited non-existent laws. The administration highlighted a specific instance where an MEP received flawed legal advice from an AI tool, requiring the Parliament’s legal team to spend two days verifying and correcting the information. Such incidents underscore the risks of relying on generative models for complex legal work without rigorous human oversight.

In response, the Parliament is preparing new guidelines centered on three core principles: transparency in AI usage, strict data security to prevent sensitive files from being uploaded to public tools, and mandatory verification of all AI-generated content before it is finalized. While MEPs currently do not have a formal requirement to disclose when AI has assisted in their work, parliamentary staff have been subject to such labeling rules since 2024.

To address these challenges, the institution is developing its own internal platform, the EPGenAI Hub, designed to handle sensitive tasks within a secure environment. Commercial tools like ChatGPT or Claude will remain accessible for less sensitive operations. This initiative follows previous security measures, such as disabling built-in AI features on tablets earlier this year due to data protection concerns. These steps reflect a broader effort to balance the efficiency gains of AI with the need for accountability and accuracy in democratic governance.
