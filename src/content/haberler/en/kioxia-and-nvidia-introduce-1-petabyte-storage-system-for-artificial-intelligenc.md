Kioxia and Nvidia have developed a new storage concept designed to address the growing data demands of artificial intelligence workloads. Tested as a joint proof-of-concept initiative, the experimental infrastructure utilizes high-capacity flash drives to achieve a total raw storage capacity of approximately 1.1 petabytes. The setup focuses on expanding data accessibility for advanced hardware configurations.

The installation consists of 18 server nodes working in tandem. Each node houses multiple individual drives, providing roughly 60 terabytes of flash memory capacity per node. This dedicated storage space can be accessed directly by the underlying graphics processors thanks to specialized software integration, facilitating smoother data transfers across the system.

The hardware components leverage modern interface technologies and consist of various models offering flexible capacity ranges. The experimental system supports small data block access, potentially enabling the processing of trillions of vectors within the infrastructure. This capability targets the efficient management, storage, and retrieval of large-scale vector databases used in complex analytical tasks.

By increasing the total data capacity directly accessible to processors, the initiative aims to simplify the handling of massive datasets that exceed traditional memory limitations. Both companies continue to evaluate advanced storage architectures and next-generation drive solutions to further enhance performance for large-scale data centers and emerging artificial intelligence applications.
