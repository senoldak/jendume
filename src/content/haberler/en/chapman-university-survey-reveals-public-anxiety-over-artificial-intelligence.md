The Chapman University Survey of American Fears has revealed that public apprehension regarding artificial intelligence has reached unprecedented levels. According to the annual poll tracking what Americans fear most, anxieties regarding AI disrupting the political process and the economy ranked notably high on the list, coming in behind only corrupt government officials and the prospect of another world war.

Perhaps the most striking finding in the survey is that artificial intelligence concerns are now outpacing the fear of a loved one becoming seriously ill or dying. This grim indication highlights just how much psychological space worries about advanced technology occupy for the average person. Previous polling similarly found that roughly two thirds of Americans believe there is a moderate chance advanced AI could eventually destroy humanity.

Chapman University sociology professor Christopher Bader noted in a statement that citizens are concerned not only about employment impacts but also about the overarching effects on political systems and economic stability. As the technology becomes increasingly embedded in daily life, understanding these deep-seated fears is becoming critical for policymakers and researchers alike.

At the same time, researchers emphasize that survey rankings should be viewed within a broader context. While some fears have surged, others have declined compared to previous years. Nevertheless, with the technology industry making colossal infrastructure investments and political campaigns increasingly leveraging automated tools, public apprehension regarding the trajectory of artificial intelligence remains a prominent fixture of the national conversation.
