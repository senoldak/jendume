Authors are increasingly turning to new digital tools to defend themselves against growing accusations that their work is AI-generated. This crisis of trust in the publishing industry has already led to tangible consequences, including the cancellation of book publications following claims that a novel was largely produced by artificial intelligence. To prove their innocence and protect the authenticity of their creativity, writers are adopting "paper trail" software that documents their writing processes step-by-step.

These platforms function by encrypting the writing process or tracking keystrokes to create a "digital fingerprint." The goal is to provide authors with a way to prove that they authored a text themselves or to establish provenance when their work is scraped into training datasets without attribution. However, this necessity places an added burden on writers. Many argue that the requirement to constantly maintain receipts to prove one's creativity is insulting and fundamentally at odds with the nature of artistic expression.

AI detection tools remain at the center of this debate. While some platforms claim they can estimate the likelihood of AI generation with high precision—in one instance flagging 78 percent of a text—these systems are far from infallible. Despite claims of a 0.01 percent false-positive rate, there are numerous instances where human-authored works have been incorrectly flagged as AI-generated. This has fostered a climate of distrust within the literary community, leading to what some describe as a "witch hunt."

Process-tracking tools aim to create a chain of evidence by recording a writer's history in platforms like Google Docs. Yet, these systems are not foolproof; the complex nature of writing, which involves switching between files, using voice notes, and drafting, often escapes the capture of such tools. As the line between artificial intelligence and human creativity continues to blur, authors are left navigating a new landscape of suspicion, struggling to protect their work while maintaining their creative integrity in an era defined by algorithmic doubt.
