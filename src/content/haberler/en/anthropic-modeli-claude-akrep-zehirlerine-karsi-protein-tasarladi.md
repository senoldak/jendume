Anthropic's Claude model has autonomously designed protein binders targeting scorpion neurotoxins, addressing a critical health issue that claims over 3,250 lives annually. These small protein molecules are engineered to latch onto specific target molecules, opening new pathways for medical research and treatments against scorpion stings from various species.

Following an initial prompt, Claude worked independently for 24 hours to generate potential solutions. During this period, the model produced 318,484 designs targeting five toxins from four different scorpion species. The research team then provided additional instructions to steer the workflow, leading Claude to pick 180 candidates while the team added 31 more for laboratory evaluation.

Researchers examined a total of 211 laboratory creations, and 17 successfully reached the required standards for a binder. The two strongest candidates bound extremely tightly to Cn2, a specific toxin from the Mexican scorpion. Laboratory observations showed these molecules position themselves right at or very close to the location where a known neutralizing antibody acts in mice, though their actual efficacy as a functional antivenom remains to be tested.

External experts Susana Vázquez Torres and Timothy P. Jenkins praised the work as a promising initial step, and two independent laboratories confirmed the results. The research was conducted inside Claude Science, Anthropic's specialized AI environment for researchers, building upon earlier studies where Claude successfully designed mini-binders against multiple target proteins with high hit rates.
