Meta is accelerating the transition to AI-driven content moderation across its platforms, including Facebook and Instagram. While the company frames this shift as an improvement in enforcement speed and accuracy, internal employees warn that the reduction of human oversight is leading to significant errors and operational chaos. According to the company, the new systems make 13% fewer mistakes than humans and identify 10% more violations. However, this technological shift has been accompanied by mass layoffs, leaving some teams operating with "skeleton crews" that have been cut by half or two-thirds.

Algorithms replacing human reviewers often struggle with nuance, particularly in languages other than English. This limitation leads to the wrongful removal of content and raises concerns about potential political bias in automated censorship, especially during sensitive periods like elections. Employees argue that the system lacks the necessary judgment for complex policy decisions and that the speed of automated algorithms means mistakes occur at a much greater scale. Independent monitoring groups report that account takedown incidents rose from 210 in 2025 to 657 in the first half of 2026.

The company previously faced scrutiny over the mental health and low wages of outsourced moderators in Kenya. Now, the shift to AI has sparked debates about transparency and accountability. Critics argue that AI provides "plausible deniability" for controversial moderation decisions, as the systems are opaque even to internal staff. Meta's moderation infrastructure relies on a combination of large language models and older filtering algorithms that weigh thousands of different factors. Employees suggest that these systems allow the company to obscure controversial decisions, while Meta maintains that its rules apply equally to everyone, that its systems are more effective than human reviewers, and that users have the opportunity to appeal decisions they believe are incorrect.
