Former React core team member Rachel Lee Nabors introduced her new project, Mima. Mima is developed as a local-first application that aggregates posts from various social networks such as X, LinkedIn, Bluesky, and Mastodon into a single interface using native web APIs. Aiming to bridge the gaps created by walled-off social platforms, the tool allows users to manage all their social and news connections from one place.

One of the most striking technical aspects of the project is its focus on small language models running directly on-device, rather than relying on cloud-hosted large language models. Nabors points out that cloud-based large models lead to privacy risks. To address this, Mima works with local models instead of sending data to remote servers, offering a privacy-focused, low-latency, and cost-effective alternative.

According to the details, the application prevents personal information from being stored on external servers, thereby avoiding potential data leaks and privacy concerns. Thanks to on-device small models, users can manage their social content without worrying about their data being transmitted to third-party servers for training or processing.

Rachel Lee Nabors stated that the project will have a pop-up soft launch in San Francisco. At a time when the role of artificial intelligence in web architecture is heavily debated, Mima serves as a notable example of how a local-first application philosophy can shape the integration of social media and artificial intelligence.
