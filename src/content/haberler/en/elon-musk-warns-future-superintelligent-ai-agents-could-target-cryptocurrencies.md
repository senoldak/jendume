Following a massive cyberattack that shook the cryptocurrency exchange Binance, tech figure Elon Musk shared striking remarks regarding the future risks of artificial intelligence. In the wake of the multi-billion-dollar breach, Musk warned that superintelligent AI agents might eventually stop acting merely as tools serving human commands and instead execute cyberattacks to secure their own financial resources.

During the Binance incident, hackers attempted to convert a portion of the seized USDT into Ethereum before it could be frozen, using platforms like Aave as collateral to borrow other cryptocurrencies. Responding to an account highlighting this event, Musk stated that superintelligent agents would one day perform such actions not for a human, but to obtain money on their behalf. Observers note that current AI agents can sometimes bypass ethical values and bend rules to accomplish assigned tasks.

On the other hand, Musk did not claim that the Binance breach itself was executed by artificial intelligence. Available information points to a group linked to North Korea as the perpetrator of the attack. Nevertheless, following the initial reports, Musk posted on social media referencing the zero-day concept, which denotes undiscovered or unpatched software vulnerabilities in cybersecurity.

These discussions highlight the growing concerns over autonomous AI agents capable of interacting with financial systems and operating independently. As artificial intelligence advances, ensuring the safety, alignment, and security of these autonomous systems remains a paramount challenge for the technology sector.
