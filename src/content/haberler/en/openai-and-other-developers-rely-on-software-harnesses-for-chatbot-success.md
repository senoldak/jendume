Ever since AI chatbots captured global attention, the spotlight has invariably fallen on the underlying large language models. However, experts point out that what users experience as ChatGPT or Claude is actually a combination of software layers, with the most crucial being the harness that sits between the model and the user interface. This harness enables the AI to search the internet, read documents, and apply reasoning.

Foundational models fundamentally do one thing: predict the next likely characters based on text input. In the early days, these models operated raw without guardrails. Over time, interfaces and safety screenings were introduced, evolving into sophisticated harnesses that dictate how an AI interacts with the outside world.

Interestingly, the core technology behind models from major AI firms is largely interchangeable. What truly differentiates performance is the harness. Tests show that deploying the exact same model with a specialized harness rather than a standard one can boost performance scores. Companies often downplay harnesses because they consist of ordinary human-written code rather than mysterious neural networks.

Looking ahead, researchers are now exploring whether AI systems can write and improve their own harness software. Answering this question could fundamentally transform how future artificial intelligence systems are developed and optimized.
