As crucial elections approach in the United States, AI chatbots are delivering political misinformation to the public with a high degree of confidence. Recent investigations show that top AI tools are providing significantly flawed information to users seeking voting guides. For instance, when ChatGPT was asked to create a guide for a Missouri resident, it included a Senate seat that was not even up for re-election. Similarly, in Wyoming, municipal candidates were incorrectly listed as running for federal offices.

An analysis of 3,000 AI-generated voting guides found that these tools frequently share false information traced back to other AI-generated websites. This phenomenon, where AI models consume their own low-quality output and present it as authoritative data, poses a significant challenge for information integrity. The models particularly struggled with geographic accuracy regarding electoral boundaries.

The findings indicate that chatbots failed to identify the correct congressional district for a specific address 48 percent of the time. Among the instances where the AI did provide a district, 22 percent of those answers were incorrect. This highlights the inherent risks of relying on large language models for precise, localized administrative data during election cycles.

Despite these technical failures, the actual impact on the electorate may currently be limited. According to a Pew Research Center survey conducted in late October, only eight percent of US voters reported using AI chatbots like ChatGPT, Copilot, or Gemini to research ballot-related information. While this low adoption rate provides some buffer against widespread dezenformation, the persuasive nature of AI responses remains a concern for future electoral cycles.
