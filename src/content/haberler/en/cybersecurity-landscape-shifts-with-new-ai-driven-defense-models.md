The internet's digital infrastructure, particularly open-source software, is facing unprecedented threats. Critical vulnerabilities in widely used languages like PHP are shaking the foundations of the digital world. Recent incidents, such as the PHP2Shell exploit, have highlighted just how fragile the security of millions of websites truly is. In this complex environment, artificial intelligence companies are not only identifying these threats but are also entering the market by developing new defense shields to counter them.

Tech giants like OpenAI, Anthropic, and Google have unveiled new models focused on cybersecurity. OpenAI’s GPT-6 Cyber, Anthropic’s Claude Mythos Preview, and Google’s Gemini Carbon 4 are promising to protect organizational digital assets. However, this development has sparked a significant debate in the tech world: Are these companies attempting to solve the chaos that their own technology has helped accelerate, using their own paid solutions?

Cybersecurity experts note that artificial intelligence provides immense power to both attackers and defenders. In what is being described as a "vulnerability tsunami," the process of identifying and patching software bugs is moving beyond human capacity. While initiatives like OpenAI’s "Patch the Planet" aim to find defects in open-source software, there is a risk that these services could evolve into paid, dependency-creating models over time.

The modern internet is built on critical infrastructure maintained by volunteers, often lacking sufficient resources. The entry of AI companies into this space could raise security standards, but it may also lead to the commercialization of digital security and its centralization under the control of a few major corporations. Moving forward, cybersecurity is becoming more than just a software issue; it is evolving into a "defense economy" managed by artificial intelligence models.
