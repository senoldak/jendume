Hong Kong police have integrated artificial intelligence-powered facial recognition capabilities into the city's public CCTV network. Rolled out under the SmartView initiative, the new technology allows law enforcement to trace the historical movements of suspects and generate real-time alerts for missing individuals. Police Commissioner Joe Chow Yat-ming stated that the AI tools would enhance crime detection and aid in identifying individuals who attempt to disguise their identities using face masks.

The system operates via two main functions. First, police officers can enter a suspect's facial features into the database to retroactively search specific camera feeds or time windows to track their past movements across the city. The second function provides live monitoring. When a missing person's facial profile is logged into the system, an automatic alert is dispatched to police units as soon as that individual enters the field of view of any connected camera.

Since the SmartView project launched in 2024, approximately 5,000 CCTV cameras have been installed across Hong Kong. Officials credit these cameras with helping resolve nearly 2,200 criminal cases and contributing to more than 2,800 arrests. Official documentation submitted to the legislature confirms plans to expand the camera network to 60,000 units by 2028.

To prevent potential misuse, biometric facial recognition searches require authorization from an officer holding at least the rank of inspector. Searches must be justified by national security, crime prevention, or public safety objectives. Raw video recordings are retained for up to 31 days, whereas metadata generated by the facial recognition system showing match locations and timestamps can be stored for up to 180 days.

While local residents raise concerns over transparency and data handling, the technology reflects broader global trends. Mainland China uses AI surveillance on a massive scale, while Western jurisdictions face ongoing debates over false matches and civil liberties. Notably, the European Union passed legislation in 2024 banning real-time remote biometric identification in public spaces for law enforcement, subject to narrow exceptions.
