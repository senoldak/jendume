Australian data center operator NextDC has announced a major strategic shift following intense community backlash against an expansion project in inner Melbourne. The company stated that in the age of artificial intelligence, shifting public expectations mean it will no longer pursue new data center developments near residential properties. The controversy arose around the M3 site in West Footscray, where power provider Jemena paused the construction of 22-metre power poles following protests from local residents. The subsequent delays and public friction caused NextDC's share price to drop significantly, while rival Goodman Group acquired a nearly 10% stake in the business during the turmoil. Chief Executive Craig Scroggie acknowledged that methods which built trust over the past fifteen years are no longer sufficient in the AI era. In response to the backlash, NextDC is establishing a community consultative committee, a dedicated project website, and formal contact channels with defined response times for local inquiries and complaints. Chairman Doug Flynn noted that while the M3 location had served as an industrial site for a century, community sensitivities regarding data infrastructure have fundamentally changed. Consequently, the leadership confirmed that future expansion sites will be purposefully located away from residential neighborhoods. These developments highlight how physical infrastructure supporting technological advancements must carefully navigate community expectations and local engagement.
