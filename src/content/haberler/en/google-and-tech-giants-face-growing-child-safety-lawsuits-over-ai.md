Tech companies have spent years claiming they are working to make their products safer for children. However, ongoing jury trials, settlements, and related litigation against companies like Meta, Google, and Snap suggest current measures fall short. Snap recently settled a case alleging its technology caused severe psychological distress to teenage users, following similar settlements by Meta, TikTok, and YouTube. Meanwhile, Google was sued by parents alleging their child suffered a psychological crisis after interacting with the Gemini AI chatbot in Google Classroom. Experts note that as artificial intelligence companies pursue massive valuations and rush to push advanced models into classrooms, the potential risks to young users multiply. Developmental psychologists and legal experts emphasize that children's safety must be integrated during the design stage rather than addressed after problems emerge. Google has also faced scrutiny for linking K-12 school access of search tools with its AI integrations. While internet companies have historically relied on federal liability protections regarding user content, plaintiffs are now targeting the systemic design of these platforms and AI systems. Legal analysts point out that these collective lawsuits are educating the public on product design risks and could serve as a catalyst for meaningful regulatory and industry-wide change.
