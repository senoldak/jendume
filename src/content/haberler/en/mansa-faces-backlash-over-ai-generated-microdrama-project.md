Mansa founders David Oyelowo and Nate Parker have introduced an entirely AI-generated microdrama titled 'They Left Us to Die,' aimed at bringing diverse Black stories to mainstream audiences. While the trailer released on October 29 drew favorable reactions, the impending premiere on the following day sparked severe criticism on social media over the absence of human actors and creators in the production process.

Co-founder Nate Parker defended the strategy, explaining that Mansa uses AI not as a replacement for human-centered content, but as a strategic tool to test audience demand and gather engagement data before pitching major studios. In the shrinking Hollywood landscape, securing studio backing for niche or edgy concepts remains difficult, making microdramas a cost-effective alternative for market testing.

Industry analytics show that vertical short-form video consumption is surging among younger demographics, with the global microdrama market projected to reach fourteen billion dollars in 2027. However, critics and industry observers argue that deploying artificial intelligence in creative fields exacerbates existing anxieties, especially as studio consolidations in Los Angeles threaten thousands of film and television jobs.

Experts note that Black artists and industry workers already face underrepresentation in Hollywood, compounding fears that automation will further diminish their career opportunities. While Mansa has produced numerous live-action projects alongside its AI initiatives, the company has not yet released performance metrics for its automated series.
