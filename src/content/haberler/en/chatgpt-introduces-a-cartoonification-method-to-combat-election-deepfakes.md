As the US midterm elections approach, major artificial intelligence companies are implementing new safeguards to enhance election security. In this context, OpenAI has introduced a policy update for ChatGPT designed to handle requests that could potentially interfere with elections or discourage voting. Instead of generating photorealistic images that could be mistaken for documentary evidence, the system now applies an image stylization approach, turning such requests into cartoonified illustrations while preserving room for social commentary.

This new strategy effectively neutralizes attempts to create political deepfakes using generative tools. Tests have shown that when users request sensitive political imagery in a photorealistic style, the system automatically converts the output into a cartoon format and ignores subsequent follow-up requests to change it back. Alongside OpenAI, companies like Google and Anthropic are also rolling out their own strategies to prepare for elections and ensure their respective chatbots direct users toward trustworthy and official political data.

These measures follow the collaborative commitments established during previous election cycles to combat deceptive AI-generated content. Google is integrating interactive tools and official data across Search and Gemini, while Anthropic has updated Claude to link users directly to nonpartisan voter resources like TurboVote. Such proactive steps by major technology firms highlight a concerted industry effort to prevent the misuse of AI models in ways that could threaten public trust and democratic integrity.
