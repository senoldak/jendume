A recent report published by the International Energy Agency highlights the escalating environmental cost driven by the rapid expansion of the artificial intelligence sector. According to the institution's data, carbon emissions caused by data centers could increase by up to 70 percent by 2030, largely fueled by surging investments in artificial intelligence technologies. This massive surge is pushing major economies toward fossil fuels to meet the growing power demand.

Data from the report indicates that global electricity consumption by data centers will double by 2030 compared to current levels. Approximately three-quarters of this increase is expected to be driven by the widespread adoption of artificial intelligence applications. Experts estimate that the electricity demand of artificial intelligence-focused data centers alone will grow by 350 terawatt-hours annually over the next four years, an amount exceeding the total annual electricity consumption of the United Kingdom.

While technology companies continue investing in renewable energy to power their facilities, fossil fuels such as coal and natural gas will remain significant energy sources. Particularly in the United States and China, the expansion of artificial intelligence infrastructure will boost demand for thermal power plants. In China, roughly half of the electricity required by data centers by 2030 is projected to come from coal plants, while in the United States, about 60 percent of the demand is expected to be met by natural gas facilities. Conversely, the European Union is projected to source roughly 70 percent of its data center electricity from renewables.

Industry analysts emphasize that for countries and technology companies seeking to lead the artificial intelligence race, developing powerful processors will no longer suffice. Securing clean and reliable electricity to run these hardware systems has become just as critical as raw computing power. The insights provided by the energy agency demonstrate how the digital transformation is reshaping the energy sector while amplifying environmental challenges.
