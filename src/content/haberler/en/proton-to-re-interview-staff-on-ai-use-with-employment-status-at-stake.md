Swiss privacy company Proton, known for Proton Mail, has initiated a re-interview process for engineering and product staff to evaluate how they integrate artificial intelligence into their daily work. According to internal communications, these assessments will be recorded and could lead to changes in job roles and continued employment.

Chief executive Andy Yen directed engineers to halt hand-writing code and instead leverage AI tools to accelerate development. The review process began with over forty senior leaders in engineering and product, with evaluations running through November. Interviewers are selected from a separate pool of leaders demonstrating strong AI capabilities.

Chief technology officer Bart Butler noted that artificial intelligence wrote ninety percent of Proton's code in October, with employees heavily utilizing tools like Anthropic's Claude. Internal documents revealed that Proton faced challenges meeting its annual recurring revenue targets due to insufficient velocity, prompting management to push for greater automated coding efficiency.

In response to public inquiries, Proton stated that the evaluations are not a precursor to layoffs. Instead, the company emphasized that it faces a talent shortage, is actively hiring, and aims to identify employees who require additional support. Proton continues to promote its privacy-focused chatbot, Lumo, which does not log conversations or train on user data.
