While the supercomputing world was built on scientific simulation, artificial intelligence infrastructure filling modern data centers raises much harder questions for enterprises. As predictive systems generate commercial income, managing digital infrastructure and maintaining administrative oversight becomes a vital corporate concern. A newly published buyer guide sets out to evaluate administrative oversight provider by provider across the technology sector.

The research establishes that sovereign infrastructure is not a single checkbox but rather a complex stack of regulatory and operational layers that buyers must examine individually. The evaluation framework rests on five pillars: territorial, operational, technological, legal, and financial. While geographic placement is relatively straightforward for most providers, operational layers remain much murkier and more complex to navigate in daily business operations.

To construct the guide, analysts graded seven hyperscalers across forty distinct offerings, handing out one hundred seventeen grades across five geographic regions. The scoring framework utilized seventeen yes-or-no tests, assessing legal implications separately due to varying local jurisdictions. Google’s air-gapped offerings scored relatively well, but the most surprising finding applied universally across every technology provider evaluated in the study.

The research revealed that no hyperscaler evaluated in the study had a definitive answer regarding the U.S. CLOUD Act. For European and other global entities, a production order from a U.S. court system means digital oversight can be legally overridden. This exposes enterprises to significant legal risks when choosing their artificial intelligence infrastructure partners.
