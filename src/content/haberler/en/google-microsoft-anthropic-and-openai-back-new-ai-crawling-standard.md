Google LLC, Microsoft Corp., Anthropic PBC and OpenAI Group PBC have announced plans to support an upcoming website crawling standard designed to simplify how publishers manage automated data collection tools. Historically, website operators have used robots.txt files to control search engine access, but restricting artificial intelligence training systems previously required adding separate entries for every single provider and tool.

The new standard backed by these major technology firms will enable site administrators to prevent harvesting systems from gathering model instruction materials through a single robots.txt entry. This setting will not apply to models that perform exclusively non-generative tasks. A draft version of the proposal has already been submitted to the standards maintenance organization.

Microsoft and OpenAI plan to incorporate support for the standard in the coming months, while Google will follow later. Meta Platforms has expressed support for the concept but has not yet committed to implementation. Additionally, Microsoft announced it will provide a setting called AI Grounding for web page access related to answering user questions rather than training.

This cooperative effort marks a significant step toward standardizing the relationship between web publishers and developers, offering creators clearer control over their digital content in the era of artificial intelligence.
