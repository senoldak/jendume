The United States and China have taken a joint step to contain the risks of artificial intelligence while continuing their intense race for global dominance. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent announced in New York that the two governments have established a new dialogue mechanism on super intelligence aimed at guarding against shared risks and averting misunderstandings between their systems. Under this new mechanism, both sides will exchange best practices for testing advanced models before release and keep human judgment firmly in command of the technology, while also alerting each other to serious incidents involving these models.

While outlining the new cooperation framework, Bessent stopped short of making any commitment to limit the advancement of the technology. The government has largely held back from expanding caps on artificial intelligence progress out of fear of losing a competitive edge, though signals indicating openness to regulatory measures have surfaced. Meanwhile, China's foreign ministry has previously dismissed calls to slow down frontier models, stating that confrontation would only disrupt global governance.

These diplomatic engagements come ahead of the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation Leaders' Meeting. During prior diplomatic visits, Washington and Beijing reached a consensus to initiate an artificial intelligence dialogue featuring a dedicated communications channel designed to help de-escalate serious incidents, and leaders are anticipated to hold their third meeting with Bessent participating on the margins of the summit.

As the technology advances, both nations must demonstrate how they can reconcile competitive pressures with leadership responsibilities. Chinese Ambassador Xie Feng emphasized at a recent gala that joint efforts are indispensable for improving artificial intelligence governance, noting that while cooperation may not resolve every challenge, none can be resolved without it.
