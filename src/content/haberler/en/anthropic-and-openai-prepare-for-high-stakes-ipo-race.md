Anthropic and OpenAI are preparing to enter public markets through initial public offerings (IPOs), marking a significant shift in the AI landscape. Anthropic is poised to list its stock under the ticker "ANT" as early as November 19, while OpenAI is expected to follow in early 2026. This move represents a critical juncture for the financial future of the artificial intelligence sector.

The primary driver behind these IPOs is the immense cost of computing power required to train and run state-of-the-art AI models. Both companies are engaged in a long-term infrastructure race that demands constant capital infusion. As private funding becomes more difficult to secure, public markets offer a necessary avenue to cover these staggering operational expenses.

Anthropic presents a more straightforward narrative for public investors, leaning heavily toward enterprise solutions. Because their business model is tied to recurring usage in workflows like coding and internal support, it aligns well with what public market investors typically value. Conversely, OpenAI operates a broader and more multifaceted business, spanning consumer subscriptions, advertising, and licensing agreements with major partners. This complexity means OpenAI will face a more rigorous challenge in articulating a clear, compelling story to public shareholders.

The first company to go public will effectively set the valuation benchmark for the entire frontier AI sector. Anthropic’s market performance will likely establish the terms by which investors evaluate similar companies. This puts OpenAI in a potentially uncomfortable position; if they cannot match the valuation metrics set by Anthropic, they may face a discount on their own valuation. Ultimately, this race is not just about raising capital; it is a strategic battle to capture investor mindshare and define the standard for what a successful, large-scale AI company looks like in the public eye.
