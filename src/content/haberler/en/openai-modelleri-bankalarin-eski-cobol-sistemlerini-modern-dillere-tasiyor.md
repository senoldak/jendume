OpenAI has announced enhancements to its models regarding software building and migration in legacy programming languages with dwindling programmer workforces, including older languages used across financial institutions. As the company explains, 40 percent of the world's banks still rely on these systems, and migrating to newer languages would cost millions while taking considerable time. Since programmers familiar with these aging architectures are retiring from the workforce, institutions using these systems must migrate within a short timeframe.

Guided by developer and enterprise customer feedback, OpenAI created an artificial intelligence evaluation suite called MultiLangBench to test language capabilities by asking models to create SQLite-compatible database engines. GPT‑6 Astra achieved an average test pass rate of 87.4 percent across 20 different languages, showing significant improvement over earlier versions. COBOL remained the most difficult language to crack, with Astra achieving a 72 percent pass rate in this category.

OpenAI also evaluated models by migrating older programs to C++, Java, Kotlin, Python, or Rust while preserving program behaviors such as handling excess decimal digits and missing records. In this evaluation, GPT-6.1 Sol reached the highest accuracy of 97.9 percent with high reasoning effort, and 86.6 percent accuracy at low reasoning effort.

OpenAI acknowledges that migration is only successful when all tests pass, emphasizing that banking tasks require extreme precision. While these behavior-preserving capabilities are useful for teams maintaining older systems, applying them to production software still requires expert review and testing against business rules and dependencies.
