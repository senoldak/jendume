The speed at which AI models identify software vulnerabilities has triggered a new era in cybersecurity. Anthropic has announced a significant reduction in the time it grants software maintainers to fix security flaws. The company's "Frontier Red Team" stated that starting January 1, 2027, the disclosure window for high and critical-severity flaws will be reduced from 90 days to 30 days. Other vulnerabilities will be subject to a 45-day window.

The primary driver behind this change is the ability of AI models to find software bugs much faster than humans. Anthropic notes that the previous 90-day window was based on the assumption that a vulnerability could remain private for months, a premise they no longer believe holds true. With AI-powered tools, it is increasingly likely that a bug discovered by one model will soon be identified by another. This reality necessitates a faster response time from software maintainers to secure their systems.

Under the new policy, if a maintainer fails to respond to an initial report, the escalation to an outside coordinator will occur after 15 days, down from the previous 30-day limit. Furthermore, the waiting period for sharing full technical details after a patch is released has been shortened from 45 days to 30 days. The seven-day window for flaws under active attack remains unchanged.

This acceleration in cybersecurity is not limited to Anthropic. Google’s "Project Zero" team is adopting a similar strategy, announcing that it will shorten its disclosure window to 60 days starting February 2, 2027, and further reducing it to 30 days beginning January 3, 2028. These developments underscore how AI is fundamentally changing the pace of vulnerability discovery, forcing developers to adopt more proactive security measures in an increasingly automated threat landscape.
