More than 100 campaign groups across 15 countries in Europe have launched a two-day wave of protests targeting the datacenters that form the backbone of artificial intelligence technologies. Under the banner "Stop Dirty Data Centres," these organizations argue that the environmental and social costs of large-scale projects have become unsustainable. Demonstrations spanning from Dublin to Warsaw, outside a Microsoft facility in Denmark, near a planned site in Amsterdam, and in London’s Brick Lane area, aim to highlight the physical footprint of AI infrastructure.

These protests underscore that AI is not merely a digital phenomenon but a pressing societal issue, characterized by massive energy consumption and significant physical land use. Activists are raising concerns about the prioritization of these facilities over housing, the impact of chatbots on mental health, and the threat of AI eliminating jobs. Conversely, the tech industry maintains that datacenters create jobs during construction and operation, and drive economic growth by supporting the development and deployment of AI.

The scale of this expansion is significant: between 2021 and 2024, approximately 250 global projects exceeding 100 megawatts in energy demand were announced. In the UK alone, 315 datacenters are currently waiting to connect to the power grid, representing a demand of 73 gigawatts—significantly higher than the country’s 45-gigawatt peak energy demand. This movement highlights the growing tension between the rapid expansion of AI and the limits of environmental and infrastructural sustainability, as communities across the Atlantic demand that politicians impose moratoria on large-scale developments.
