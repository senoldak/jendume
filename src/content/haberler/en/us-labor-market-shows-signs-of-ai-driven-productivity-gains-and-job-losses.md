The impact of artificial intelligence on the labor market is beginning to manifest in concrete data, moving beyond long-standing theoretical debates. New reports from financial researcher Eric Basmajian and Goldman Sachs highlight a direct link between high AI adoption and a combination of rising productivity and falling employment. This shift is particularly evident in sectors such as information technology, professional services, and finance.

According to analysis by Eric Basmajian, the three sectors with the heaviest AI adoption—information, professional services, and finance—have seen their productivity trend jump from 2.2% to 5.0% since 2022. This surge represents a significant leap following the integration of AI tools into business processes. However, experts caution that these productivity gains do not necessarily reflect higher quality output or increased revenue, but rather a reduction in headcount to achieve the same results.

Internal reports from Goldman Sachs reinforce these findings, suggesting that the 'AI labor market impact' is spreading beyond technical fields like software engineering. The data shows a rising trend between an industry's exposure to AI and unemployment rates in non-tech professional sectors, including telecommunications, technical services, and administration. Currently, it is estimated that approximately 15% of jobs are being impacted by this technological shift.

Interestingly, this trend is currently most visible in the United States. The clear correlation between AI usage, productivity growth, and falling employment seen in the US is conspicuously absent in other global markets for the time being. Analysts suggest this may be due to broader post-pandemic economic factors or local labor regulations. Nevertheless, the data provides the first empirical evidence that the AI revolution is fundamentally restructuring the professional landscape.
