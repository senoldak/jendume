Dozens of WhatsApp web pages designed to answer user questions about Meta's messaging application inadvertently exposed the company's internal instructions for its AI chatbot. An analysis by privacy company Redact revealed that around 80 help and FAQ pages on the platform exposed text instructions intended specifically for a large language model. The hidden blocks included sections titled 'WHAT NOT TO SAY' and explicit directions instructing the chatbot not to provide users with legal advice.

The hidden text appeared to guide the language model away from unhelpful responses and provided a glimpse into how chatbot instructions are engineered. For instance, instructions on how to delete an account included a directive telling the bot not to recommend contacting any data protection authority, regulator, or supervisory authority. Dan Saltman, CEO and founder of Redact, noted that the key takeaway is the gap between what users are told versus what instructions are given to language models.

Following inquiries regarding the issue, Meta removed the text from all affected pages. A WhatsApp spokesperson stated that the text was displayed due to a bug that has now been fixed, emphasizing that these were instructions for the support chatbot rather than direct messages to users to ensure accurate responses. The spokesperson added that the company wants its product support chatbots to focus on providing practical advice rather than offering legal opinions.

The exposed instructions appeared under code blocks headed 'HelpContentGalaxyLLMInclude' and are no longer visible in live page code. Additional guidelines instructed the chatbot on how to respond if users mentioned legal action or data protection regulations, directing the model to avoid citing terms of service or privacy policies. Company representatives reaffirmed their commitment to providing transparent information regarding data rights while keeping support bots focused on core troubleshooting.
