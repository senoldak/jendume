Microsoft has officially reported $10 billion in revenue from its commercial partnership with OpenAI for the quarter ending in September 2026. This figure represents approximately 11% of the company's total revenue for that period. The disclosure marks the first time Microsoft has provided a quarterly figure for this arrangement, illustrating how rapidly artificial intelligence technologies are becoming a significant revenue stream at scale. The revenue generated from the OpenAI partnership has surpassed the earnings of Microsoft's Xbox gaming division and its search and advertising operations, positioning it as a primary growth driver.

According to the financial filings, Microsoft has paid $12 billion of its $13 billion investment commitment to OpenAI. Meanwhile, OpenAI's outstanding balance owed to Microsoft reached $7.2 billion by the end of September, up from $6 billion in June. These figures underscore the scale of capital flow and commercial interdependence between the two entities.

The partnership between the two companies is evolving. Microsoft has relinquished its right of first refusal to supply OpenAI's computing capacity, allowing OpenAI more freedom to utilize other cloud providers. Microsoft has also expanded its warnings regarding reliance on external AI model makers, citing limited visibility into how these models are designed and secured. Additionally, the company adjusted its capital spending forecast for 2026, lowering it from $175 billion to $170 billion, citing delays in certain GPU service contracts. This transparency allows investors to better track cash flow and operational risks within the artificial intelligence ecosystem.
