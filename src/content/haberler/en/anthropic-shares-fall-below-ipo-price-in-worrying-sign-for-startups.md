Anthropic, a leading force in the artificial intelligence sector, faced a significant setback as its stock price dipped below its initial public offering (IPO) price. By midday Tuesday, the company's shares were trading as low as $76, falling nearly 12% from Monday's close and sliding under the $83 IPO benchmark. This downward movement has sent ripples through the tech investment community.

Market analysts and venture capitalists have been closely monitoring Anthropic’s trading activity, treating it as a primary indicator of how public markets value the AI sector. Falling below the IPO price is traditionally viewed as a negative signal, as underwriters typically aim to price offerings at a discount to ensure a positive trading trajectory. Anthropic’s breach of this level suggests a shift in investor sentiment regarding the immediate returns of AI investments.

The slump is particularly dramatic given that Anthropic debuted on the Nasdaq just at $111.50 per share. If the stock continues to struggle in the coming weeks, it could exert downward pressure on the valuations of private AI startups. This trend might also weaken the IPO prospects for other major AI firms waiting in the wings, as investors demand more concrete evidence of long-term sustainability.

The sell-off was not isolated to Anthropic. Nvidia, the dominant provider of AI hardware, also saw its shares fall by more than 7% by midday Tuesday. This decline followed a massive 30%-plus gain on Monday, which was triggered by the reinstatement of Jensen Huang as CEO. The combined volatility of these major players highlights the current uncertainty and high stakes within the global AI economy.
