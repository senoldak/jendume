The rapid expansion of artificial intelligence has sent the prices of computer components soaring, creating major supply issues for everyday consumers. Reports and social media footage reveal that large-scale industrial facilities in China are buying up high-end Nvidia RTX 5090 graphics cards and modifying them for use in AI servers. Operations of this scale demonstrate the lengths to which industrial players will go to secure scarce hardware for expanding computational demands.

The modification process involves swapping out circuit boards and fitting turbine-style coolers to ensure the cards fit the high-density arrangement requirements of AI server chassis while occupying less space. Consequently, off-the-shelf availability has plummeted, with market prices for an RTX 5090 surging well past 10,000 dollars compared to its official retail price of 1,999 dollars. The involvement of middlemen in the supply chain also obscures the final destination of these heavily modified hardware components.

Nvidia has maintained that production of the card has not ceased, though company executives acknowledge that the supply chain remains heavily constrained. Even Nvidia's own online marketplace currently lists the hardware as unavailable, reflecting the fierce competition among companies eager to capitalize on the ongoing AI boom and secure every available unit on the market.

Ultimately, individual PC builders and gamers bear the brunt of these market shifts. Left to contend with extreme scarcity and inflated prices, everyday users are finding it increasingly difficult to acquire powerful components for personal use, often being forced to settle for outdated or underpowered hardware. The current electronics market reflects a broader industrial scramble where desperate entities fight over existing stock.
