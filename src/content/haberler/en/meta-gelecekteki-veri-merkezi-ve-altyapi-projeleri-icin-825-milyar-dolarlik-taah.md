Meta has signed up to about 825 billion dollars in future leases and contracts, with the majority dedicated to the data centres and computing power driving its artificial intelligence push. The figures were detailed in its quarterly filing with US regulators by summing up three key items from the regulatory document. The largest portion of these commitments consists of 454.5 billion dollars in non-cancellable contracts. These agreements primarily cover cloud capacity acquired from third-party providers, servers, network equipment, energy supply for data centres, and hardware for its virtual reality unit, Reality Labs. Out of these contracts, 45.9 billion dollars fall due in the remainder of the period, while 121.4 billion dollars are scheduled for the following year. In addition, Meta holds 355.7 billion dollars in uncommenced leases that do not yet appear on its balance sheet. These cover data centres, energy infrastructure, shared server space, and network equipment, with start dates running into future years for terms lasting as long as 30 years. A third item involves an agreement to purchase up to 14.7 billion dollars of cloud capacity over a five-year period. These forward-looking commitments arrive alongside heavy current spending. In its third-quarter financial results, Meta reported that capital expenditure reached 34.9 billion dollars in the three months leading up to the end of September, while free cash flow stood at negative 2.1 billion dollars. Consequently, Meta narrowed its capital expenditure expectations for the year to a range between 135 billion and 145 billion dollars.
