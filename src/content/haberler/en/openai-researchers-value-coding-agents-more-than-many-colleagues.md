OpenAI has published its second report on internal research acceleration, detailing how deeply AI agents are now embedded in the development of new models. According to the company, usage of internal coding tools has surged significantly, with theoretical token consumption rates climbing sharply over recent months. The computational runtime of these agents has scaled to unprecedented levels within the research teams.

The report shows that autonomous agents working without human intervention have improved their success rates on tasks ranging from a few hours long. Systems now handle training-run monitoring and technical queries with high reliability, though they still struggle with complex engineering work and lack a nuanced 'taste' for code quality without close human oversight.

In internal surveys, researchers indicated a high dependency on these tools, noting they would rather forfeit a substantial portion of their team staff or compute capacity than go without current coding agents for three months. Qualitative interviews revealed that while agents accelerate routine tasks, key conceptual breakthroughs still stem from human ingenuity.

OpenAI cautions that human-AI collaboration data may not extrapolate linearly, pointing out that abrupt advances in mathematics could potentially translate into deep-learning research itself. Documenting these indicators of recursive self-improvement remains a core goal as the company tracks how AI systems accelerate future generations of development.
