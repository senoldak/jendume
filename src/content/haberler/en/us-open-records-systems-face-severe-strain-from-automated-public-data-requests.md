Public agencies and local governments across the United States are struggling to cope with a surge in Freedom of Information Act (FOIA) requests driven by AI tools and data mining startups. These public record inquiries are increasingly utilized to feed machine learning models and construct AI-driven public sector sales tools.

This trend is significant because it turns public transparency laws into an unpaid data sourcing pipeline for private corporations, putting severe operational pressure on municipal staff. Small town clerks and county officials report spending hours processing broad, machine-assisted filings, prompting some state legislatures to enact measures making it easier to deny records requests altogether.

The capital and scale driving this phenomenon are substantial. Venture-backed startups collecting public records have raised upwards of $100 million to turn public documents into commercial AI data signals. Key players include Starbridge, which raised $52 million, and Pursuit, which secured $25.5 million. Individual data-scraping firms file anywhere from 500 to 10,000 requests per day, with major providers submitting nearly 180,000 requests annually. Federal agencies received a record 1.7 million public records requests last year.

While generative AI allows individuals to quickly draft broad public records queries, machine learning tools enable commercial vendors to process unstructured PDFs and spreadsheets back into structured sales databases. Consequently, public offices are struggling to distinguish legitimate citizen inquiries from automated corporate scraping operations.
