Google has introduced Tabletop, an experimental platform designed to help groups build custom planning tools using artificial intelligence. Developed by Google Labs, the tool allows friends, families, or teams to manage shared tasks such as trip itineraries, dinner polls, expense splitters, and tournament brackets within a single, collaborative digital space. Users can input links, files, and ideas into a shared environment, then prompt the AI to organize or transform this information into functional tools.

The core strength of the platform lies in its real-time collaborative nature, which Google compares to working together in a document editor. Once a creator shares an invite link in a group chat, all members can access and interact with the space simultaneously. A key feature is the ability to adapt tools as plans evolve; members can ask the AI to modify rules or add new elements without starting from scratch. For instance, a simple chore tracker can be transformed into a gamified system with points and shared rewards, demonstrating the platform's flexibility.

Currently, Tabletop is rolling out exclusively to adults aged 18 and over in the United States via a waitlist, with new users being granted access on a weekly basis. Google has not disclosed the specific AI model powering the experiment.

Google Labs has a history of incubating projects that eventually evolve into full-scale products, such as NotebookLM, which was rebranded as Gemini Notebook earlier this year. Whether Tabletop follows a similar trajectory depends on how effectively users integrate it into their daily routines. The company suggests a wide range of use cases, from organizing road trips and sports leagues to managing weekly meal plans and school pickups, positioning the tool as a versatile assistant for collective decision-making.
