Alphabet shares fell sharply by over 10% in premarket trading following its latest quarterly results. The company's total revenue reached $127.6 billion, falling just short of forecasts, as search advertising revenue faced growing pressure from AI chat agents eating into Google's search volumes. Financial analysts noted that the report marked the first double miss for both earnings per share and revenue since the fourth quarter of 2022.

In contrast, Microsoft posted stronger results driven by robust cloud growth. Azure cloud revenue grew by 48% to reach $33.2 billion, becoming the tech giant's single biggest business, while total revenue rose 19% to $92.3 billion to beat expectations. Microsoft forecasted up to $97 billion in second-quarter revenue while projecting full-year capital expenditure guidance at $170 billion. Microsoft executives noted that demand continues to exceed supply, yet market observers point out that investors are no longer giving tech giants a free pass to spend without limit on AI data centres.

Alphabet's spending on property and equipment reached a record $51.4 billion during the quarter, resulting in a negative free cash flow of $9.1 billion. Analysts note that the market is shifting from viewing all tech giants as a single trade to closely scrutinizing how fast each company can turn AI investments into tangible revenue. Software and cybersecurity stocks also came under downward pressure, reflecting broader shifts in how markets evaluate technology sector investments.

These financial shifts highlight the growing tension between massive infrastructure costs and immediate returns in the artificial intelligence sector.
