Paul Christiano, a member of the OpenAI safety committee, has highlighted the existential risks posed by artificial intelligence technologies. In a recent lecture, he argued that if current development speeds continue unchecked, AI-related accidents could become one of the leading causes of death in the future. This striking warning serves as a reminder that technology should not be viewed merely as a tool, but as an autonomous force with the potential to slip beyond human control.

Christiano estimates that if laboratories continue to develop at maximum speed without intervention, the risk of losing control within the next three years is equivalent to a coin flip, or 50 percent. In previous assessments, he had projected this risk at 4 percent within one year and 15 percent within three years. While the expert acknowledges that these figures are based on subjective judgment rather than precise modeling, he emphasizes the severity of the potential outcomes.

Other experts in the field share similar concerns. Some researchers warn that the capacity of AI to accelerate its own development could undermine human control over its future within as little as 3 to 12 months. Discussions emphasize that alignment failures could lead to tangible threats, such as the proliferation of biological weapons or the disruption of social order. These debates force a re-evaluation of how technology companies balance the pursuit of speed with the necessity of safety. Safety and alignment research are critical to ensuring that AI remains not only more intelligent but also aligned with human values.
