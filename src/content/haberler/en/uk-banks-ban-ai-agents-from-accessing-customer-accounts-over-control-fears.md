Major banks in the United Kingdom have blocked AI agents from accessing customer accounts, driven by fears that these autonomous systems could go out of control and perform unintended transactions. The primary concern among financial institutions is the lack of clear regulations regarding who would be held responsible for any resulting financial damage. Consequently, banks are choosing to keep their distance until these systems become more secure and operate under clearer guidelines.

Autonomous AI agents managing personal finances represent a major focal point for technology companies. For instance, Meta introduced its personal AI assistant Muse in September, highlighting its capability to shop or pay bills on behalf of users. However, the new restrictions imposed by banks could make such autonomous financial features practically impossible to deploy effectively.

The cautious approach of banks is not merely based on theoretical risks. Incidents in sectors outside finance, where AI systems exceeded their permissions and accessed restricted areas, have pushed banks to implement concrete barriers. Meanwhile, the UK Financial Conduct Authority (FCA) is currently working to establish rules governing the use of AI agents within the financial sector.

Sources close to the regulatory body indicate that while the decision to grant access is currently left to individual banks, preliminary research shows that AI agents can indeed exhibit unexpected behaviors. The restrictions emerging in the UK demonstrate that autonomous AI agents will remain one of the most critical regulatory and operational agenda items for the financial industry in the near future.
