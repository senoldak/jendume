Artificial intelligence technologies are increasingly viewed as tools in combating climate change and optimizing production processes. While logistics companies can reduce fuel consumption through route planning, similar efficiency gains are targeted across various sectors. However, making production processes more efficient does not mean that total resource consumption will decrease.

According to World Trade Organization estimates, the widespread adoption of artificial intelligence could significantly expand global trade volume over time. Although this economic growth initially appears positive, it brings environmental risks as it entails more production, transportation and natural resource consumption.

Known in economic literature as the Jevons Paradox, this contradiction argues that when efficiency in the use of a resource increases, the total consumption of that resource may also rise. As falling production costs trigger higher output, artificial intelligence could trigger a similar cycle and increase resource utilization.

Historically, the expansion of global production networks by digital technologies has increased raw material demand. International merchandise trade is linked to significant threats to species worldwide. Artificial intelligence can further empower this trend by making supply chains faster.

Ultimately, focusing on technological efficiency may not be sufficient to solve the climate crisis. It is emphasized that the real issue is to control the total magnitude of production and consumption.
