The widening gap between what artificial intelligence can achieve and the evidence supporting trust in these systems has become a central debate in the tech world. Impressive demonstrations do not guarantee that a model will remain within safe operational boundaries, raising the critical question of who determines when a system is safe to deploy.

Discussions with industry figures like Appian Corp. CEO Matt Calkins highlight the urgent need to bridge public policy with autonomous business agents. Much like established high-risk industries such as banking or energy, artificial intelligence requires proactive regulatory frameworks rather than retrospective penalties after damage has occurred.

While recent white-house accords and external assessments represent a step forward, they currently lack the mandatory compliance and enforceable penalties needed to ensure accountability. Analysts emphasize that global competition arguments should not bypass the fundamental requirement for proven safety standards before releasing advanced capabilities.
