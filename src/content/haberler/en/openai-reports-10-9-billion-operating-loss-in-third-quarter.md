OpenAI has disclosed in its third-quarter financial report to investors that its operating losses reached $10.9 billion. This figure, which stood at $10.1 billion in the previous quarter, continues to rise despite the company increasing its revenue by approximately 50 percent between the second and third quarters. The data underscores the immense costs associated with developing advanced artificial intelligence and the significant financial gap the company must bridge to achieve profitability.

To boost revenue, the company pivoted to an advertising model, an approach previously described by leadership as "unsettling" and a "last resort." Since introducing ads into its chatbot in February, the company has faced backlash from users. By August, the company reported that its advertising division was on track to generate $1 billion annually. However, this shift has drawn criticism, particularly as it coincides with the company moving further away from its original commitment to open-source development.

Discrepancies in financial projections have also raised concerns. In October, the company reported an annualized revenue estimate of $50 billion—a figure that fell $20 billion short of what it had communicated to investors just one month prior. This inconsistency has fueled speculation regarding the robustness of business models within the AI sector and has intensified discussions about a potential market bubble.

As OpenAI and its primary rival, Anthropic, prepare for highly anticipated initial public offerings, both companies are facing increased scrutiny. While Anthropic has secured a strong foothold in the corporate market with its coding tools, OpenAI is shifting its focus from a consumer-centric model to enterprise sales. Although the AI sector has rapidly become the star of the tech industry, the fundamental question remains whether frontier labs like OpenAI can transition from burning cash to building sustainable, profitable businesses.
