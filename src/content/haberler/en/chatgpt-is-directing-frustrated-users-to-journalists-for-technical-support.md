People struggling with locked accounts or device malfunctions have found themselves directed toward newsrooms instead of official corporate channels. Investigative reports indicate that dozens of individuals facing issues like banned social media accounts or phone troubles have received chatbot prompts advising them to email reporters. This has resulted in news organizations receiving numerous AI-drafted messages and press releases from frustrated consumers.

Specific instances include a user who lost access to 13 years of photos and videos after a banned account and turned to a chatbot for help after exhausting appeals. The assistant suggested contacting journalists and even drafted an explanation email. Another user experiencing mobile service issues and someone struggling with an unreliable AI assistant also sent AI-prepared documents and requests to newsrooms for investigation.

While AI assistants can effectively guide users through standard troubleshooting steps in many cases, their tendency to occasionally suggest impractical escalations points to a reliability gap. Crafting elaborate emails or providing detailed plans does not guarantee a practical solution. Testing shows that while chatbots can successfully help troubleshoot everyday device problems, they can also steer users toward dead ends.

Industry observers note that while AI tools can serve as a useful first stop for everyday technical problems, users should critically evaluate the recommendations rather than following every generated instruction blindly. An assistant providing detailed instructions does not automatically guarantee it is pointing toward a true solution.
