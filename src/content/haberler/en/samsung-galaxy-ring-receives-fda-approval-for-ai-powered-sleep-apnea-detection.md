Samsung has announced that its advanced sleep apnea detection feature for the Galaxy Ring and Galaxy Watch 9 has received FDA approval. This authorization, granted in July 2026, allows the devices to analyze respiratory irregularities during sleep with higher precision. The new feature aims to help users who feel exhausted despite a full night's sleep understand the underlying causes of their fatigue through data-driven insights.

The system operates using AI algorithms that process blood oxygen levels and movement data during sleep. The AI calculates the frequency of breathing disruptions for every hour the user sleeps, creating a detailed profile of their sleep quality. Through the Samsung Health Monitor app, the system can identify signs of mild, occasional, or frequent sleep apnea. To generate a report, the device requires at least five hours of sleep data for two nights within a ten-day period.

Samsung emphasizes that while this technology does not provide a formal medical diagnosis, the concrete figures obtained can lead to more productive conversations with healthcare professionals. The company had previously received authorization for detecting moderate to severe sleep apnea in 2024; however, this new AI-powered version offers a much more granular level of tracking. This move positions Samsung ahead of primary competitors like Oura and Superhuman, which currently lack FDA-approved sleep apnea detection features.

The feature is currently available on the Galaxy Ring and Galaxy Watch 9 models running Wear OS 7.0 or later. Samsung stated that the feature will also be rolled out to Galaxy Watch 7 and Galaxy Watch 8 models via future software updates. This advancement serves as a prime example of how AI is transforming everyday accessories into proactive personal health assistants, bridging the gap between consumer tech and medical monitoring.
