Google lost up to ten percent of its market value after third-quarter search revenue grew by about 12 percent, missing the 15 percent growth analysts had expected. Company executives confirmed that the strong growth in AI-mode queries impacted overall financials due to lower monetization rates compared to traditional search methods.

Despite the revenue shortfall, Google noted that the annual growth rate for search queries reached its highest level in five years and expressed satisfaction with its overall trajectory. To improve AI monetization, the company announced plans to expand its chatbot advertising program in the U.S. and internationally. Chief Executive Officer Sundar Pichai reported that the Gemini app has reached one billion users, establishing itself as the fastest-growing product in company history.

Google's cloud division grew 103 percent year over year to reach $30.7 billion, prompting massive corresponding investments. Capital expenditures hit $51.4 billion in the third quarter alone, with the full-year 2026 forecast remaining between $195 billion and $205 billion. Cloud chief Thomas Kurian expects autonomous AI agents to drive further growth by demanding increased computing power, storage, and database capacity for reasoning tasks.

Furthermore, Google plans to deploy smaller, distilled versions of its new Carbon model to billions of users through its cloud runtime, while reserving the full model for selected cybersecurity experts and the U.S. government. Management declined to disclose the profit margins associated with selling proprietary TPU-based AI infrastructure to external customers.
