The Dutch cabinet has announced a comprehensive multi-year strategy centered around artificial intelligence to improve public sector efficiency and reduce government costs. According to the official cabinet announcement, the government aims to eliminate 25,000 full-time jobs by the year 2035 as part of its wider cost-cutting measures. This major restructuring move highlights how automated systems and advanced digital tools can fundamentally transform working methods and workforce structures within government institutions.

Under the announced economic framework, the administration aims to achieve structural financial reductions, targeting a cost reduction of 900 million euros, or approximately 1 billion dollars, by 2027. Furthermore, official estimates indicate that this savings goal will rise significantly to 3.5 billion euros, which is approximately 3.9 billion dollars, by 2035. The policy details also explicitly noted that certain crucial sectors, specifically police and defense agencies, will be completely excluded from these workforce reductions while other public departments adapt to new technological requirements.

While specific deployment details regarding how automated technologies will be implemented are yet to be finalized, the Dutch cabinet plans to introduce concrete goals and savings targets for utilizing AI to work more productively in the first quarter of 2027. State Secretary Eric van der Burg wrote in an official communication to the Dutch parliament that the overarching effort is part of a broader strategy to make state administration simpler, smarter, and more compact following preliminary technological trials across various government sectors.

This transformation initiative underscores the massive economic and structural dimensions of technology integration within the public sector. The broader adoption of artificial intelligence tools across government operations sparks essential discussions on administrative efficiency while raising important questions about the future of public employment, administrative organization, and the long-term role of digital automation in governance.
