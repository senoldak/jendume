Neura, a robotics startup headquartered in Metzingen, Germany, is advancing the field of embodied artificial intelligence by training sophisticated robotic systems to navigate and interact with the real world. The core of its operations centers around a secretive facility located in Munich, where numerous humanoid robots from various global manufacturers constantly practice complex tasks ranging from household chores to intensive factory work. While large language models can easily draw on existing internet text and books to learn about abstract concepts, an equivalent corpus of structured data does not exist for the physical world, making physical training grounds an absolute necessity. Neura founder David Reger compared this unique learning process to learning how to swim, noting that watching instructional videos is never enough and one must physically get into the water. Having successfully secured up to $1.4 billion in a funding round backed by prominent technology giants like Nvidia and Amazon, the startup aims to operate five specialized gyms globally by the end of next year. The company already maintains a presence internationally, including a facility in Hangzhou, China. Meanwhile, international competition is intensifying, with Chinese competitors operating over ninety humanoid data collection centers, while European infrastructure remains comparatively smaller. Although traditional factory robots are typically built for highly specialized repetitive tasks, modern humanoids strive for broad versatility, prompting major industrial players like BMW and Japan Airlines to begin experimenting with them in real operational environments.
