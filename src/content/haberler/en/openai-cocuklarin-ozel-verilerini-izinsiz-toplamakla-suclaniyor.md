A new class action lawsuit filed in a San Francisco federal court has accused OpenAI of gathering sensitive data from minor users without valid parental consent. Filed on behalf of two unnamed minors aged twelve and sixteen, the complaint argues that the company used personal secrets and health information from children to train its large language models. While OpenAI's terms of service technically forbid users under thirteen, the lawsuit contends that age-gating mechanisms are ineffective and that minors should not be expected to navigate complex legal jargon.

The court filing details how a twelve-year-old child shared her age with the chatbot, yet the system continued to engage with her on deeply personal subjects, including emotional struggles and physical insecurities. Legal representatives emphasize that if OpenAI has to pay to use kids data, it will ultimately incentivize a safer product. The lawsuit requests financial compensation for minors whose data was collected, arguing that such measures would ultimately hold the company accountable for building enterprises using children's private thoughts and images.

Attorneys involved in the case point out that the goal is to ensure companies stop taking minor information without valid consent. Legal experts note that children cannot legally sign away rights without parental permission, making the reliance on standard terms of service problematic for the broader industry.

This legal challenge emerges amid ongoing debates regarding the effectiveness of safety features designed for teen users on artificial intelligence platforms. While developers argue their systems provide secure environments, independent reviews often highlight vulnerabilities. The outcome of this lawsuit could establish significant precedents for how artificial intelligence developers handle minor user data and parental consent moving forward.
