OpenAI has signed four major agreements with the Abu Dhabi government to expand the use of artificial intelligence across public services, investments, and energy. OpenAI Chief Executive Sam Altman announced the projects during a signing ceremony attended by Crown Prince Sheikh Khaled bin Mohamed bin Zayed. The Abu Dhabi government aims to become the world's first AI-native government through these initiatives.

The first project, named the Machine-Readable Government initiative, turns government policies and regulations into a format that AI systems can read. As part of an initial pilot led with the Abu Dhabi Agriculture and Food Safety Authority, routine compliance checks for food imports and permit applications are being assisted by AI. Human officials retain oversight and make all final decisions, with computing running on local data centers within the UAE.

A second deal involves Mubadala, the emirate's sovereign investor, partnering with OpenAI and G42 subsidiary Inception42 to utilize AI for investment analysis and risk assessment. A third five-year agreement between Inception42 and OpenAI will bring AI models to government entities, businesses, and schools throughout the UAE, building tailored products for large organizations.

The fourth agreement establishes state-owned oil company ADNOC as the first energy company to adopt OpenAI's platform. Speaking at an energy conference in Abu Dhabi, Altman emphasized that AI will transform the economics of the energy world. Abu Dhabi investors have previously co-led major funding rounds for both OpenAI and Anthropic.
