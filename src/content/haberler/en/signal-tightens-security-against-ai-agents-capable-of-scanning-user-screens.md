Signal has launched new security measures to protect users against AI agents integrated into mobile operating systems that can scan information displayed on screens. The company expressed concerns that agents with "onscreen awareness," such as Google’s Gemini or Apple’s Siri, could potentially capture and store private data outside of Signal's control. To mitigate this, the app is blocking screenshots on iOS and enabling screen security on Android whenever an AI assistant attempts to read the screen content.

This move is part of a trend among major organizations, including United Airlines, JPMorgan Chase, and Charles Schwab, which are restricting or blocking third-party AI agents due to security and customer protection concerns. United Airlines, for instance, has restricted unauthorized agents from booking or managing tickets to prevent the exposure of customer information or unintended purchases. Similarly, banks like Singapore’s OCBC Bank have stated they will not allow third-party agents to interact with their systems for autonomous money transfers.

Security experts warn that the level of access required by these agents, such as email, account details, and payment information, creates a risk of account hijacking. According to the 2026 State of AI report by data management firm AvePoint, 88.4% of surveyed organizations experienced at least one security breach related to AI agents in the previous year. The survey included 750 IT leaders globally.

Signal’s leadership is calling on major operating system developers, including Apple, Google, and Microsoft, to provide technical tools and documentation that allow developers to prevent AI agents from accessing sensitive application data. Experts emphasize that application-level protections will always have gaps, meaning data must be classified, permissioned, and recoverable before an agent can interact with it. While some companies, like Singapore Airlines, are partnering with AI agents to enhance services, the industry is increasingly recognizing that agents require tiered safeguards to match the sensitivity of the tasks they perform.
