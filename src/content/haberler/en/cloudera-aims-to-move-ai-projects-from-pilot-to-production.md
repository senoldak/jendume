Companies are shifting their focus from experimenting with artificial intelligence to generating measurable business results. Data platform provider Cloudera is updating its strategy to help enterprises move AI projects from the pilot phase into full-scale production environments. To support this, the company has reorganized its post-sales teams under a single leadership structure, aiming to ensure customers derive tangible value from their technology investments.

The core of this shift lies in overcoming the hurdles that businesses face when scaling AI. According to a study of over 1,300 IT specialists, approximately 80% identify data access as the primary obstacle to AI deployment. Because data is often scattered across various silos with differing governance and sovereignty rules, moving it to the cloud or integrating it into AI models remains complex. To address these challenges, Cloudera plans to release its "Anywhere Cloud" solution in November.

Furthermore, the company is partnering with Mistral AI to integrate open-weight models directly into its platform. This collaboration is designed to provide businesses with greater flexibility and security when running AI models on their own data. The company emphasizes that running a pilot on a subset of data is fundamentally different from full-scale deployment, where total cost of ownership and return on investment become critical metrics that are often unclear during the experimental phase.

This development signals a transition in the AI industry from the "experimentation" era to the "implementation" era. Businesses are no longer just asking what AI can do, but how it can be integrated into operations to drive value and manage costs effectively. Solving structural issues like data access and governance is essential for AI to become a reliable, long-term tool in the corporate world.
