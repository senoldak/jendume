New York Governor Kathy Hochul enacted legislation establishing a five-year prohibition on the sale of artificial intelligence-powered chatbot toys. This measure specifically targets dolls and robotic devices that utilize internet connectivity to engage in conversations with children. The decision follows concerns regarding the safety and privacy implications of these products, which are often marketed to minors.

The legislative action was prompted by findings from the US Public Interest Research Group Education Fund, which conducted evaluations of chatbot toys designed for children aged three to 12. The research revealed that several of these products exposed young users to sexually explicit content and provided dangerous advice that could create fire hazards within households. The study also found that these devices frequently lacked adequate parental controls, and in many instances, safety restrictions diminished as the duration of the interaction increased. Some devices were capable of recording a child's voice or scanning their facial features, raising privacy concerns.

Under the new statute, companies found in violation of this ban face civil penalties reaching 15,000 dollars per day for each infraction. New York Attorney General Letitia James said her office intends to enforce these regulations strictly to prevent children from being harmed by the expansion of large technology companies.

This regulation is part of a legislative effort in New York to address risks associated with emerging technologies. State legislators also enacted a separate measure titled "AI Chatbot Restrictions for Minors," which prohibits unsafe features for individuals under 18, carrying penalties of up to 25,000 dollars per infraction. While major retailers like Amazon maintain that they require third-party sellers to comply with all applicable laws, managing compliance across vast inventories, which include approximately 12 million different toys, presents a logistical challenge for the industry.
