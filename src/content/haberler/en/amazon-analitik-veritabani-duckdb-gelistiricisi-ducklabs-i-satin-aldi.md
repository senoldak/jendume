Amazon has announced the acquisition of DuckLabs, the Amsterdam-based company behind the analytical database DuckDB. Following the deal, DuckDB creators Hannes Mühleisen and Mark Raasveldt will continue leading the technical direction of the open-source project within AWS. All components of the Duck Stack will remain free and open-source under the MIT license, while the non-profit DuckDB Foundation continues its supervision.

According to AWS technology vice president Mai-Lan Tomsen Bukovec, DuckDB was designed to handle rapid data queries, making its optimizations particularly relevant for agentic AI workloads. AI agents frequently inspect data and run small exploratory queries before taking action, a pattern that aligns naturally with DuckDB's high-performance architecture. Before reaching this agreement, AWS and the DuckLabs team collaborated extensively over a multi-year period to explore integrations with various cloud services.

As part of the integration, Amazon Aurora PostgreSQL has become the first service to feature DuckDB capabilities. Previously, combining transactional data with historical records stored in Amazon S3 required complex extract, transform, load pipelines that increased infrastructure costs. The new integration allows a single query to process live operational data and historical records directly within Aurora, minimizing infrastructure configurations and avoiding additional network hops.

The feature is now available across all commercial regions and AWS GovCloud at no additional charge, with users paying only for incremental compute and storage requests generated by their queries. This move highlights the growing convergence of cloud data analytics and modern AI workloads, aiming to simplify engineering efforts for developers worldwide.
