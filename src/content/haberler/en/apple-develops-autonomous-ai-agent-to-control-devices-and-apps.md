Apple has initiated the development of an autonomous AI agent capable of operating computers and devices like a human user. Recent job listings from the company reveal that this technology is designed to manage applications, browse the web, write code, and execute complex, multi-step tasks. This project is part of the company's broader artificial intelligence strategy.

This development is intertwined with Apple's efforts to protect and expand its ecosystem. As the company builds this agent, it is prioritizing privacy and security, focusing on "sandboxing" (protected test environments) and permission models. This strategy extends beyond internal development.

Simultaneously, Apple has introduced a restrictive update in macOS 27.3 that impacts how third-party AI tools interact with the operating system. The process for granting control permissions, which previously required four steps, has been increased to 14. While Apple maintains that these changes are necessary to enhance user security, many in the AI industry and user community view the move as a strategic effort to slow down competitors.

Apple's move begins a phase in the race for "computer-use" agents. With competitors like OpenAI, Anthropic, and Google already deploying similar autonomous tools, Apple's control over its own operating system could alter the competitive environment. The company is aiming to launch this project by the end of 2027. This initiative changes how users interact with their devices and sets a standard for AI integration at the operating system level.
