OpenAI has published the pretraining loss data for 13 models developed since GPT-3.5, revealing a striking acceleration in the learning pace of its AI systems. Since early 2026, the pretraining loss has been falling approximately seven times faster than it did between 2022 and 2025. This trend suggests that AI development is advancing significantly faster than what standard scaling laws would typically predict. Under standard scaling laws, each tenfold increase in compute is expected to yield a smaller drop in loss than the previous one; however, the current data points to an acceleration that defies these traditional expectations.

Experts and researchers remain divided on how this data translates into real-world capabilities. Some argue that this drop in pretraining loss is a fundamental indicator of deeper model capability, which will inevitably lead to a leap in real-world performance. Others point out that benchmark scores have not shown a comparable acceleration, raising questions about the relationship between pretraining metrics and downstream utility. Some researchers are attempting to interpret this by converting the data into "effective compute," suggesting that newer models are achieving results with significantly higher efficiency.

The drivers behind this acceleration are also a subject of intense debate. While OpenAI attributes the progress largely to long-term research and compute investments, it acknowledges that AI is increasingly contributing to the design process itself. Independent assessments, such as those analyzing developers like Anthropic, estimate that a majority of the current acceleration (approximately 80%) is driven by AI-assisted development. This level of transparency is a critical step for the industry to understand the trajectory of AI R&D. By sharing this data, developers are helping the public and the research community grasp how AI development is compounding. Whether this acceleration will translate into broader real-world impacts in the coming period remains one of the most significant questions in the field.
