Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Co. reported a record quarterly profit that surpassed analyst estimates, powered by an unrelenting global appetite for artificial intelligence chips. Operating as the largest contract chipmaker globally, the enterprise posted a record net profit for a sixth consecutive period, marking a 68.7% surge compared to the same timeframe a year earlier. The enterprise achieved an operating margin of 58.7%, exceeding the upper bound of its guidance range and demonstrating strong pricing power amid the ongoing semiconductor boom.

Looking toward upcoming periods, the chipmaker forecasted revenue between $52.3 billion and $54.3 billion, representing a 58% jump from the previous year at the midpoint of its guidance. Leadership expects capital expenditures spanning from 2026 to 2028 to exceed previous spending levels significantly to support growing customer requirements. Advanced technologies, categorized as 7-nanometer and more advanced processes, accounted for 79% of total wafer revenue during the quarter, with 3-nanometer processors leading at 33%.

Platform analysis indicates high-performance computing represented the largest share of revenue at 68%, followed by smartphone applications. The enterprise also confirmed completing construction for its second fabrication facility in Arizona, with equipment installation underway and mass manufacturing anticipated to commence in the third period of 2027. Evaluations continue regarding further manufacturing expansion within the United States, including a potential investment in Texas alongside a planned $265 billion regional footprint.

Industry analysts observe that while the chipmaker carefully evaluates whether customers possess adequate financing to deploy systems, demand remains well above supply capacity. Free cash flow surged to NT$476.56 billion from NT$139.38 billion previously, reflecting robust financial health and confidence in sustained long-term growth across the global hardware supply chain.
