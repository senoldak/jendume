Nvidia has simulated a 104-qubit quantum computer capable of correcting its own errors by utilizing JUPITER, the supercomputer located at Germany’s Jülich Supercomputing Centre. The company stated that this represents the largest full simulation of such a machine to date. Qubits, the fundamental building blocks of quantum computers, are extremely sensitive to interference, meaning even minor noise from control equipment can introduce errors. Fault-tolerant quantum systems can detect and correct these errors during execution, which experts view as essential for practical utility.

The research team tested a technique known as magic state cultivation to aid quantum computers in executing specific calculations. Nvidia reported that this method uses 90 percent fewer qubits than previous approaches. The simulation also incorporated noise that typically impacts real quantum hardware, expanding upon previous work conducted by Jülich researchers who simulated 50 qubits under ideal conditions.

JUPITER, launched as Europe's first exascale supercomputer, is capable of performing over a billion billion calculations per second. For this recent test, the team deployed Nvidia GH200 chips across 8,192 GPUs. Nvidia noted that the model was initially developed on a smaller system at its own quantum lab, reducing processing time from weeks down to days.

The primary objective is to help researchers evaluate how a quantum computer design will perform prior to constructing physical hardware. The entire simulation operated on conventional computers rather than a quantum processor. Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang emphasized that useful quantum computing will demand extraordinarily complex, fault-tolerant systems.
