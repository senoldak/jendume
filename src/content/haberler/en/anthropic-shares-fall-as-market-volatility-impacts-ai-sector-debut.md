AI developer Anthropic experienced a sharp decline on its second day of public trading, with shares falling more than 10% in premarket activity. This pullback occurred immediately after the company closed 17% higher on its first day. Market analysts note that this decline coincides with a broader sell-off across global stock markets, reflecting a cautious environment for high-growth technology stocks.

Following the exercise of the underwriters' over-allotment option, known as a greenshoe, the total capital raised by Anthropic and its selling co-founders reached $119.5 billion. While company leadership maintains that this capital position provides a foundation for future operations, investors remain wary. Concerns persist regarding the broader AI industry, particularly following shifts in market leadership and ongoing debates about the sustainability of massive infrastructure spending. Financial reports from the company show the significant cost pressures involved in scaling AI models.

Anthropic reported an operating loss of $567 million for the third quarter, driven largely by $5 billion in computing costs tied to short-term commitments. Analysts at Goldman Sachs project that the company may not reach positive annual free cash flow until 2030, with revenue modeled at approximately $685 billion by that year. This long-term horizon reflects the uncertainty investors face when pricing the future value of AI enterprises.

Market sentiment is currently characterized by what some analysts call a "wall of worry." Factors such as potential difficulties in approving new data centers and competition from major tech firms developing their own autonomous AI agents are weighing on investor confidence. Despite the volatility, market observers view Anthropic as a real-time signal for the AI risk trade, as investors continue to navigate the balance between innovation potential and immediate financial performance.
