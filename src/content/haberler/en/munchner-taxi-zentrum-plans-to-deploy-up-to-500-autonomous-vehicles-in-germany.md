Munich's largest taxi operator, Münchner Taxi Zentrum (MTZ), and US-based autonomous vehicle company Tensor are joining forces to bring self-driving cars to German streets. The companies announced a signed letter of intent at the Madrid Autonomous Mobility Summit, outlining plans to run up to 500 Tensor Robocars in Germany. Under the agreement, MTZ will operate the service under its own brand while maintaining control over its fleet, customers, and data sovereignty.

The deployment depends on final agreements and obtaining Level 4 driving approval in Germany, where the vehicle operates independently without a human driver under set conditions. Tensor will supply the Robocars and charge a subscription fee for its driving software, fleet tools, and support, while riders will continue booking through MTZ's established dispatch system.

Gregor Beiner, CEO of Münchner Taxi Zentrum, noted that the partnership brings autonomous vehicles to Munich with trusted service quality, while emphasizing that professional drivers remain indispensable. Tensor founder and CEO Jay Xiao praised MTZ for its regulatory relationships and decades of operational experience. Furthermore, the partners aim to deploy an additional 10,000 Robocars with European operators within 24 months of the vehicle's European launch.

This initiative joins a broader wave of robotaxi developments across Europe, with companies like Bolt, Uber, Pony.ai, and Wayve advancing their own autonomous fleet plans. Founded in Silicon Valley in 2016, Tensor highlights that its Robocars feature self-cleaning sensors and allow passengers to interact verbally to adjust routes or add stops during trips.
