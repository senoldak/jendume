An investment summit organized by regional financial authorities brought together approximately 280 senior finance figures to discuss the current economic landscape. Bankers and fund managers at the event presented clashing perspectives regarding the rapid acceleration of artificial intelligence spending and high asset valuations. Emmanuel Roman, chief executive of bond investor Pimco, warned that AI financing could reach about $10 trillion over five years, cautioning that climbing borrowing expenses, geopolitical conflicts in the Middle East, or financial strains in Europe could eventually trigger a market downturn. John Connaughton, chairman of Bain Capital, noted that some AI assets are already valued very highly, leaving investors questioning whether future returns will match the costs. Franklin Templeton chief executive Jenny Johnson added that the biggest cloud companies are transitioning from businesses requiring little capital into ones requiring substantial investment. Conversely, optimism was voiced by other financial leaders; Morgan Stanley chief executive Ted Pick suggested that constructing necessary digital infrastructure might require between $10 trillion and $15 trillion over the upcoming decade, potentially expanding real economic output by 0.5 to 0.7 percentage points. David Solomon, chief executive of Goldman Sachs, added that demand for computing power continues to exceed supply, even as tech shares face increased scrutiny following recent corporate earnings reports from major firms that questioned whether heavy spending will pay off. Blackstone president Jon Gray noted that current market skepticism is healthy and that AI will drive further capital demand. The gathering follows a sharp fall in tech shares, highlighting ongoing debates over whether heavy infrastructure spending will yield expected returns.
