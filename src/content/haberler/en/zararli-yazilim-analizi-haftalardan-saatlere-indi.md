Reverse-engineering a sophisticated piece of malware has historically been a painstaking process taking top security researchers anywhere from four to six weeks. With the integration of advanced AI capabilities, that deep analysis can now be accomplished in roughly eight hours.

Gregor Stewart, Chief AI Officer at SentinelOne, notes that this acceleration gives security researchers the capacity to investigate a much higher volume of threats and connect findings across different samples. This broader visibility reveals relationships that isolated investigations might otherwise miss.

Company leadership emphasizes that ultimate accountability remains in human hands. While automation handles time-consuming reverse-engineering tasks, critical architectural decisions still require human oversight. Furthermore, this capability democratizes security expertise, allowing more team members to engage in threat investigation rather than relying solely on a small group of specialists.
