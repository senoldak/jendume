Cybersecurity-focused venture capital firm Ballistic Ventures is expanding its investment capacity to address growing industry demands. According to regulatory documents filed with the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission, the firm is seeking to raise a total of $490 million across two separate investment vehicles. This fundraising initiative comes as the widespread adoption of artificial intelligence raises concerns about sophisticated cyberattacks while creating entirely new opportunities for specialized security startups and the venture capital firms supporting them.

The San Francisco-based firm filed to secure $360 million for its third flagship fund, matching the exact size of its previous flagship vehicle raised previously. In addition to this primary fund, the company is targeting $130 million for a separate fund called Ballistic Ventures Fund B. Official records indicate that neither vehicle had recorded any sales at the time of the regulatory filings.

Founded in late 2021 by industry veterans including former Kleiner Perkins partner Ted Schlein, Barmak Meftah, and Roger Thornton, Ballistic Ventures has built a strong reputation in the digital defense sector. The firm has previously backed prominent companies such as Talon Cyber Security, which was acquired by Palo Alto Networks, and Pangea, bought by CrowdStrike, alongside various other emerging security startups. Its investment strategy closely aligns with other cybersecurity-focused venture capital investors in the market.

Broader industry data highlights the growing dominance of artificial intelligence in the digital defense sector. According to a recent report by JPMorgan Chase citing market data, U.S. cybersecurity startups successfully raised $11.5 billion in 2025. Furthermore, artificial intelligence-enabled enterprises accounted for approximately 72 percent of U.S. cybersecurity venture transactions through May of the year, demonstrating the massive shift toward automated and intelligent security solutions.
