As public data is exhausted, artificial intelligence labs are spending billions of dollars to acquire private company data, emails, call transcripts, and workflow documents. Data acquisition startups like Mercor, Micro1, and Handshake are aggressively purchasing digital archives from businesses and even bankrupt organizations to train advanced AI models. Industry practitioners note that this acquisition market is expanding rapidly, driven by the labs' insatiable hunger for data alongside compute power.

This rapid expansion in data purchasing brings significant concerns regarding privacy and the handling of personal information. Controversies surrounding the digital asset sale of bankrupt Spirit Airlines highlighted the risk of employees' private messages and internal files being exposed. Unions and privacy advocates argue that anonymization may not fully eliminate privacy risks, pointing out that unstructured data often contains sensitive personal details.

Industry executives report that labs and enterprises currently spend tens of billions of dollars annually on data acquisition. Micro1 founder Ali Ansari predicts this market could exceed a trillion dollars a year in the near future. Mercor CEO Brendan Foody emphasizes that the AI industry urgently needs these datasets to capture the true complexity of operating businesses rather than relying on simulated workflows.

However, this trend raises troubling questions about consent and the future of work, as employees inadvertently contribute to datasets whose ultimate goal is to automate human labor. Experts warn that the data acquisition industry is moving much faster than regulatory standards, leaving crucial privacy and labor issues unresolved.
