Volvo Group Trucks Operations and Danish engineering consultancy Resolvent, alongside academic and industrial partners, have developed a simulation app powered by deep neural networks to optimize battery pack lifespan in electric commercial trucks. The initiative, titled the LaserBATMAN project, was initiated in May 2022 and wrapped up in May 2025 to solve the premature battery degradation issues that hinder long-distance highway freight.

Heavy delivery trucks require resilient energy storage systems capable of enduring long highway routes under continuous high loads. A critical stage influencing battery robustness is laser welding, used to join battery cells via busbars. Improper welding depths or thermal stresses can trigger electrochemical degradation across the battery pack. To analyze this multiscale problem, the research team turned to machine learning rather than relying solely on slow, resource-heavy traditional simulations.

Resolvent engineers built an electrochemical cell model incorporating chemical reaction kinetics. Data from this physical model was then used to train a deep neural network (DNN), creating a fast reduced-order model. This trained neural network served as the core of a specialized simulation app, allowing Volvo's research and development teams to input parameters such as pack geometry, cell count, operating temperatures, and charge-discharge current profiles.

The application includes dedicated tools to evaluate busbar weld integrity, cooling airflow, and solid-electrolyte interface layer thickness over numerous operating cycles. By predicting thermal bottlenecks and mechanical trade-offs digitally, engineers can fine-tune battery architectures before physical prototyping. The project also received backing from the European Commission, Vinnova, Innovation Fund Denmark, DTU, and Aurobay.
