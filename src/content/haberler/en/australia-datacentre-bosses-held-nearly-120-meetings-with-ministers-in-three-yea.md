An analysis published by Transparency International Australia has shed light on the heavy political engagement behind the expansion of datacentres and artificial intelligence infrastructure. According to the report, executives from ten datacentre companies secured 119 meetings with ministers and premiers across five Australian states over a three-year period. Experts note that most of these discussions took place in 2026, warning that this intense lobbying represents just the tip of the iceberg regarding corporate influence on public policy.

The research indicates that these meetings coincided with a near-tripling of political donations from major datacentre operators and their contracted lobbying firms, rising from $685,000 to $1.8 million over the past three years. Transparency advocates argue that these financial contributions create an uneven playing field and grant privileged access to decision-makers. Meanwhile, industry representatives maintain that robust engagement is necessary given the unprecedented speed of AI investments and the critical stakes involved for the broader economy.

One of the most prominent examples highlighted in the report occurred in Queensland, where state leader David Crisafulli met with datacentre executives 13 times. Public records show these encounters preceded the rollout of a dedicated framework designed to fast-track datacentre approvals by bypassing local councils. Crisafulli also traveled to the United States in September to secure a major agreement with AI giant Anthropic for a facility projected to become Australia's largest datacentre.

The report also pointed to significant personnel movement between the public sector and the tech industry, noting that four senior public servants recently left federal government departments to take on leadership roles at companies such as OpenAI, AirTrunk, and CDC. Three of these transitions occurred within the current year. While industry associations defend these interactions as a natural component of modern policymaking, the findings underscore the deep intersections between government strategy and the booming AI infrastructure sector.
