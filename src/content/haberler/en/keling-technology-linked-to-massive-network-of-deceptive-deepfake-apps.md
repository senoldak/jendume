Security researchers have uncovered a vast network of applications on major app stores that disguise themselves as everyday services while secretly hosting explicit artificial intelligence deepfake tools. Investigations revealed that over 400 connected domains and apps, often marketed as language tutors or workout coaches, load hidden web interfaces that allow users to generate explicit sexual images and videos using uploaded photos. The network appears to be operated by Chinese developers and a Hong Kong-registered entity named KeLing Technology Limited.

The operation came to light after researchers investigated thousands of video advertisements on Meta platforms that linked to deceptive apps on Apple and Google app stores. Users were able to generate explicit content and make payments using standard mobile payment systems. The network has reportedly been active for about a year, occasionally publishing dozens of applications per week while evading initial platform reviews.

According to security analysts, the apps use sophisticated tactics to bypass detection. Upon download, they may display advertised benign features, but backend server calls determine whether to load the hidden deepfake layer based on user origin and ad interaction. For instance, an app named DiffAI, created in December, was downloaded over 100,000 times from the Google Play Store before being removed.

Following inquiries, both Apple and Google removed the violating applications from their respective stores. Representatives noted that developers utilized bait-and-switch tactics, altering the source websites of the apps after initial approval. Cybersecurity experts warn that such commercialized operations pose ongoing challenges for platform safety and AI content regulation.
