Liquid Energy has announced the development of solar-powered, off-grid NanoDC modules designed for AI inference. The company states that the timeframe from contract to the first token is under 8 weeks. Rather than traditional energy sales, the business model focuses on selling inference capacity and tokens directly.

According to the company details, the NanoDC units are fully manufactured in factories and deployed on-site by unfolding solar panels. This approach eliminates the lengthy construction, permitting, and grid connection processes typical of traditional data centers. The company claims these units can be deployed anywhere in the world in about 4 weeks.

The system operates on solar power during the day and batteries at night, with each unit carrying its own compute, power, cooling, and networking components. Resembling a standard shipping container, the units feature accordion-style folding solar panels. Liquid Energy claims that deployment costs are 60 percent lower compared to conventional data centers.

The first NanoDC units have already been manufactured and are scheduled to go live in November 2026. The company outlines a scaling model by placing units side-by-side to reach gigawatt-scale capacity. Currently, Liquid Energy operates approximately 20 units with a production capacity of around 2 megawatts per month.

Based in Waterloo, Ontario, Liquid Energy is backed by Y Combinator. The company aims to scale its manufacturing capacity to approximately 20 megawatts per month next year. This infrastructure move offers a fast solution to the rising energy and computational demands of the artificial intelligence sector.
