The rapid growth of investments in artificial intelligence technologies has pushed the debt burden of technology companies to record levels. Loans taken out for data centers, chips, and cloud infrastructures have sparked concerns in financial markets, with new debt undertaken by companies approaching 850 billion dollars and total debt exceeding 3 trillion dollars. Investors are questioning how these massive loans will be repaid if AI demand falls short of expectations.

CoreWeave has emerged as one of the most notable examples of debt risk in the sector. The company carries over 35 billion dollars in debt to finance its rapid growth and reported a loss of over 1 billion dollars in the first half of the year. Its bonds and credit default swaps reflect growing investor anxiety, and its shares have seen declines. Rising borrowing costs further increase interest expenses, making profitability harder to achieve.

While CoreWeave's clients include Microsoft, OpenAI, and Nvidia, and the company holds 103.7 billion dollars in remaining contract obligations, about 40 percent of this amount is expected to be paid in 2028. Experts warn about the long-term sustainability of this debt model if AI demand weakens, noting that non-cancellable client contracts shape the overall risk profile.

Debt pressures extend beyond CoreWeave. Oracle, SoftBank, SpaceX, and major cloud providers have also increased their borrowings to finance infrastructure investments. Alphabet, Amazon, Microsoft, and Meta have expanded their borrowings, while JPMorgan Chase estimates that capital expenditures to support AI technologies will reach approximately 5.5 trillion dollars by 2030. This outlook reveals that sector growth is increasingly fueled by debt.
