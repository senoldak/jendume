TikTok has announced the global rollout of its Likeness Detection tool, giving creators worldwide a way to identify AI-generated videos that use their likeness without consent. Following a trial period with US creators that began in July, the tool is now expanding to eligible creators everywhere, including public figures and political accounts. Creators verify their identity through a third-party partner to flag potential misuse and report matching content to safety teams.

In addition to creator tools, the company has launched a public checker hosted in its Transparency Centre, allowing anyone to upload an image or video to check if it was created using TikTok's AI tools. The system scans for both C2PA Content Credentials standards and VeraDura, TikTok's proprietary invisible watermark designed to be much harder to remove. Content violating rules on nonconsensual or misleading depictions is swiftly removed by safety teams.

According to the company, systems have labelled over 5 billion AI-generated videos so far, and more than 99% of rule-breaking content is removed before any user reports it. Viewers also receive new controls through reworked topic management settings, enabling them to tailor the amount of AI-generated content appearing in their feeds.

This security push comes as platforms face mounting pressure over AI fakes, ranging from celebrity scam ads to deepfake videos. With video giants like YouTube introducing similar likeness detection capabilities, the digital media industry is increasingly adopting standardized markers and watermark technologies to ensure authenticity and safeguard users.
