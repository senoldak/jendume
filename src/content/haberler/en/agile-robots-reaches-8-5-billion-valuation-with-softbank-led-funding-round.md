Munich-based robotics startup Agile Robots secured $500 million in fresh capital through a funding round spearheaded by SoftBank, pushing the company's valuation to $8.5 billion. The round saw participation from major technology players, including Nvidia, Hitachi, and T.Capital. The company plans to utilize this capital to expand its operations in the United States and other markets, as well as to significantly increase its manufacturing capacity.

Established in 2018 by a team of researchers originating from the German Aerospace Centre, the company focuses on developing humanoid robots, robotic arms, and the proprietary software required to operate them. The company recently opened a new humanoid robot factory near Munich and expects to produce approximately 2,000 units in 2026. Management anticipates that this output could double or triple by 2027. Having already installed more than 20,000 robotic systems worldwide, the startup is now focused on moving its technology beyond pilot projects and into large-scale industrial production.

CEO Zhaopeng Chen describes the race to develop physical AI as a capital-intensive "war." Chen noted that while the industry is nearing a breakthrough, significant hurdles remain regarding the longevity of batteries, the optimization of weight distribution, and the acquisition of high-quality data from real-world environments to train these machines. The company is currently testing its latest systems with major manufacturing customers, including Volkswagen’s Audi, to overcome these technical hurdles.

This investment solidifies Agile Robots' position as Europe's most valuable privately held robotics company. The startup previously became Germany's first robotics unicorn following a $220 million Series C round in 2021. SoftBank's continued support aligns with the Japanese group's broader strategy to place robotics at the core of its AI initiatives. With this new financial backing, the company stated it is in a stronger position to consider a future initial public offering (IPO). In a market where valuations often precede large-scale sales, the ability to deliver thousands of units will be the true measure of the company's progress.
