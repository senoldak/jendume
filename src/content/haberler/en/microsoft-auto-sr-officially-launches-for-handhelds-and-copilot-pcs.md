Microsoft has officially expanded the availability of its AI-based image upscaling tool, Automatic Super Resolution (Auto SR), positioning it as a direct competitor to NVIDIA's DLSS. The latest update brings the full version of this technology to ASUS ROG Ally X handheld consoles and specific Copilot+ PCs. This AI-driven system processes low-resolution game frames in real-time to transform them into higher-quality visuals, providing a performance boost without overtaxing the hardware.

The Auto SR technology specifically aims to resolve image quality issues faced by handheld users when connecting to larger screens. Microsoft stated that the system can now offer 1440p-like detail alongside smooth framerates. While originally designed for docked mode on external displays, the feature has been enabled for handheld mode due to popular demand. This allows demanding games to feel smoother even while playing on the go.

A significant technical improvement has been made to the resolution settings. In the preview version, users had to manually lower the in-game resolution for the tech to kick in. With the new release, Auto SR works automatically with games running at 1080p by default. However, Microsoft warns that performance might not be as optimal in 1080p handheld mode compared to other configurations, though visual quality improvements remain significant, especially for older titles.

Furthermore, Microsoft has pushed the boundaries of framerate support with this update. Unlike the preview version, which was limited to between 60 and 75 fps, the new Auto SR can reach up to 120 fps. This 'Performance Mode,' along with newly added HDR support, promises a much smoother visual experience for fast-paced and competitive shooters. These enhancements are set to roll out to all compatible devices through future Windows updates.
