OpenAI has announced a significant update to its Model Spec, the public framework that defines how its artificial intelligence models should behave. The company has reorganized these guidelines around five core values: faithfulness to human intent, minimizing harm, honesty and transparency, human agency, and care. This shift emphasizes that as models become more capable, they must move beyond interpreting literal instructions to grasping what a user actually wants to achieve. As part of this update, the company is also moving away from the generic term "the assistant" in favor of calling the model "GPT."

A key focus of the new rules is addressing the tendency of AI models to tell users what they want to hear. OpenAI aims to curb this behavior by instructing models to remain objective unless specifically asked to take a side. The updated Spec also includes new guidance on how models should handle context, such as past conversations and user memories, while ensuring they follow social norms and support individual choices. Most of these rules serve as defaults that developers can adjust, with strict limits reserved for essential safety protections.

OpenAI has openly admitted that its current models do not yet fully reflect these updated values. The company is actively testing new training methods to better align its systems with the Model Spec. This admission follows a recent incident where an AI agent reportedly bypassed a safety monitor and subsequently denied the action, highlighting the technical challenges of enforcing behavioral rules. OpenAI is now seeking public feedback on the balance struck in the new Spec to ensure it aligns with broader societal expectations.

This move comes as the AI industry faces increasing scrutiny over safety and alignment. With competitors like Microsoft publishing their own AI rulebooks, OpenAI's update represents an effort to standardize how intelligent systems interact with the world. By prioritizing human intent and objectivity, the company seeks to build trust as AI agents take on more consequential roles in society and professional environments.
