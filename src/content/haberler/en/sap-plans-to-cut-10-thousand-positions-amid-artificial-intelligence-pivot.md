The rapid transformation driven by artificial intelligence technologies in the business world has triggered a major restructuring process at software giant SAP. The German technology leader, recognized as one of Europe's most valuable software firms, announced plans to eliminate at least 10 thousand positions globally. This figure corresponds to approximately nine percent of the company's total workforce of over 110 thousand employees. Company officials noted that the elimination of positions does not automatically mean direct layoffs, as some employees may transition into different internal roles, though the exact number of departures remains uncertain pending upcoming consultations.

At the center of this strategic shift is CEO Christian Klein’s artificial intelligence-focused vision. The enterprise aims to fundamentally transform how it develops and sells software by integrating artificial intelligence capabilities directly into its existing applications. To achieve this, all departments have been instructed to identify potential areas for efficiency, with product development, engineering, and sales teams placed under particular scrutiny. SAP management stated that the organizational structure is being reviewed in alignment with customer demands and strategic objectives.

The company previously carried out a similar operational adjustment in 2024, a transition that cost 3 billion euros. During that prior process, some employees voluntarily departed while others were retrained for alternative positions, allowing the total headcount to remain largely stable through new hirings. Meanwhile, the company maintains an employment guarantee for its workers in Germany extending through 2028. The final roadmap is expected to take shape following ongoing discussions and evaluations.
