Microsoft is reshaping its workforce structure to align with an AI-driven model through a voluntary exit program. CEO Satya Nadella clarified that this initiative is not about replacing employees directly with artificial intelligence, but rather transitioning the company toward a structure where employees manage and utilize AI systems. The program aims to empower employees to make informed decisions about their career paths within this evolving landscape.

The Voluntary Exit Offer (VEO) program is available to approximately 60,000 employees, representing about 27 percent of the company's total global workforce. The program targets specific teams in the US, Canada, the UK, and India, specifically those at level 70 and below. Microsoft describes the program as a voluntary offer that provides financial support and career planning resources to help employees explore opportunities outside the company.

During a recent industry event, Nadella highlighted how this transformation is altering team organization, particularly in fields like software engineering. While the rapid advancement of AI in software development does not eliminate the need for engineers, it fundamentally changes their daily workflows and responsibilities. For instance, the team developing the AI agent Cowork within Microsoft 365 consists of only 11 developers who integrate AI directly into the product development process, effectively operating as a "software factory."

Not all teams within the company are adapting to the AI transformation at the same pace. Consequently, the company is offering options for employees who feel constrained in their current roles, including internal transfers or the voluntary exit program. Financial reports for the fiscal year ending June 30, 2026, confirm that AI is central to Microsoft's strategy. During this period, the company generated 331.8 billion dollars in revenue, with Microsoft Cloud revenues growing by 27 percent to reach 214.4 billion dollars. Nadella emphasizes that this shift is permanently redefining not just the company's products, but how it organizes its human capital.
