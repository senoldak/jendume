The U.S. Department of Homeland Security has made public an official notice ordering federal civilian agencies to remove products from Chinese artificial intelligence startup DeepSeek from their IT systems. The directive originating from late July mandates that agencies must not only clear direct DeepSeek applications but also any third-party products and services developed using DeepSeek technology. Civilian agencies are also barred from procuring new systems containing these components.

The department cited risks involving user data processing in China and potential access by Chinese authorities as primary security concerns, stating that the models present multiple threat vectors. However, the order does not constitute a general nationwide ban for individual users, focusing strictly on federal civilian agency technology infrastructure and procurement.

In response, Beijing criticized the move, with the Chinese Foreign Ministry stating that China will resolutely safeguard the legitimate rights and interests of its companies. Officials in Beijing opposed what they termed the overstretching of national security concepts and the politicization of technological issues. This regulatory action underscores the deepening friction in the global AI race between the world's leading superpowers.

For government agencies, this process requires careful inventory control that goes beyond simply searching for application names, as underlying models can differ from visible brands. Signed documentation also points to technical measures such as monitoring network traffic and enhancing record keeping during the transition period.
