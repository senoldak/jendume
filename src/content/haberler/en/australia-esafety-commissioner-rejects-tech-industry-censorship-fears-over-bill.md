Australia’s eSafety Commissioner Julie Inman Grant has rejected warnings from major tech companies that a proposed law would force them to censor too much content. Testifying before a Senate inquiry, Inman Grant stated that she had seen no indication of platforms over-censoring user content over the past two years. The legislation, known as the Digital Duty of Care bill, would require internet companies to proactively identify and prevent harm to their users.

Under the proposed rules, companies failing to meet these standards could face substantial financial penalties. The bill also mandates age verification where children are at risk of viewing pornography. Additionally, users would be given the option to opt out of content selected by algorithms and view posts exclusively from accounts they follow. Major technology firms including Google, Meta, TikTok, and Snap have argued that the law could lead to widespread censorship as platforms attempt to avoid liability.

During the inquiry, a senior Google executive warned that the age-check requirement could force the company to block users under eighteen from accessing email services. Supporters of the bill maintain, however, that comprehensive legislation is necessary to keep users safe. Inman Grant noted that companies naturally prioritize showing users as much content as possible regardless of potential harm, adding that most excessive content removals stem from automated moderation running without human oversight rather than regulatory pressure.

The bill has already passed the House of Representatives and requires backing from crossbench senators to clear the Senate. The government aims to secure its approval as part of a broader framework of strict digital regulations in the country, which recently includes a social media ban for individuals under sixteen implemented in late 2025.
