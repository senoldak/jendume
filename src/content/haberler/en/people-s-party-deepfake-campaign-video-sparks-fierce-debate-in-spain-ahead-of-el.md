Ahead of the upcoming general elections in Spain, a political advertisement released across the social media channels of the main opposition People’s party (PP) has sparked widespread outrage. The campaign video utilized artificial intelligence to manipulate the likenesses of individuals linked to the ruling Socialist party, portraying them as admitting to various wrongdoings. Although the video included a tiny disclaimer stating it was created using AI, critics and government officials argued that the content could easily be mistaken for reality.

The video quickly garnered millions of views across social platforms. Spain's digital transformation minister strongly condemned the distribution of such content by an official party account, while one of the individuals whose likeness was used filed a legal challenge against the PP for defamation and digital identity theft. Legal experts remained divided on whether the video crossed legal boundaries, with some classifying it as political satire while others questioned its ethical and political appropriateness.

This incident highlights the broader global challenges regarding disinformation and the increasing integration of generative AI into political campaigns. Academic researchers pointed out that utilizing deepfakes of real people to put unsaid words in their mouths represents a dangerous crossing of a red line in democratic discourse. Meanwhile, representatives from the PP defended the ad, claiming it was merely a digital tool used to highlight hypocrisy rather than deceive the public.

The growing presence of artificial intelligence in elections is not isolated to Spain. Recent reports from research groups in other democratic nations also indicate a sharp rise in AI-generated political advertising, often lacking clear disclosures. Experts emphasize that these developments demand closer regulatory scrutiny to protect voters from sophisticated manipulation.
