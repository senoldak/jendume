Anthropic has informed US lawmakers that it will rewrite parts of its draft AI safety bill following intense backlash from the AI safety community. The confidential draft was originally published publicly, sparking fierce debates among researchers, legal experts, and state legislators who argued the proposal was weaker than existing federal initiatives and would make dangerous models harder to block.

The leaked draft stipulated that the government could only block a model's release or use in narrow circumstances where risks were foreseeable and imminent. Such interventions would require damages exceeding 1 billion dollars, or the death and serious injury of more than 50 people, stemming specifically from bioweapons, runaway models, or cyberattacks. These thresholds closely mirrored established standards in California legislation.

The proposal drew immediate condemnation from multiple fronts. Critics argued that the lack of strict licensing for independent evaluators would lead to low-cost self-certification, while state lawmakers claimed the text undermined local safety laws and emergency shutdown powers. Conversely, some industry voices argued that the training thresholds were set too low, potentially stifling early-stage startups across Silicon Valley.

In response to the controversy, Anthropic's federal affairs leadership acknowledged that the preemption wording was insufficiently precise and promised updates. The company stated it plans to release refined proposals regarding independent evaluator standards and maintain a collaborative approach with state authorities on critical safety measures.
