London-based startup Sequent has secured $2.2 million in pre-seed funding to develop "Alan," an AI-powered tool designed to mathematically prove that software code adheres to safety rules. The company aims to address the limitations of traditional software testing by leveraging AI to perform the rigorous, repetitive tasks required for formal verification.

In the software industry, proving that code is free of errors is typically a manual, time-consuming process that can take experts months or even years. Sequent allows engineers to define safety rules in plain language, while the AI hunts for the mathematical proofs to verify them. This approach is critical for high-stakes environments like financial systems and infrastructure, where a single bug can lead to catastrophic losses.

To ensure reliability, the system incorporates a non-AI checker called HOL4. Once the AI generates a potential proof, this independent checker verifies every step, rejecting the proof if it fails to meet the criteria. This design prevents the AI from taking shortcuts. The platform is already being utilized by the crypto exchange Curve to protect depositor funds.

The company’s origin traces back to a significant 2021 incident where a compiler flaw led to the loss of over $60 million in assets. Following this, the founders, including former Google DeepMind safety researchers, developed Sequent to ensure code is proven correct down to the machine-instruction level.

Currently supporting Vyper smart contracts, the company plans to expand support to languages such as Solidity, Solana, Rust, C, TypeScript, and Python. With early access starting at $99 per month, Sequent is working to transform software security from a manual, error-prone task into an automated, mathematically verifiable standard.
