London-based startup Mindmax has secured $3.3 million in pre-seed funding to transform how people prepare for professional exams and acquire new skills. Founded by the team behind the language learning app Memrise, the company is building an AI-powered tutor that moves beyond passive video watching or note reading. Instead, it allows users to learn through real-time, interactive conversations with an AI that adapts to their specific knowledge gaps and goals.

As AI tools become more prevalent, educators and institutions have raised concerns about the "illusion of learning," where students consume information without truly grasping it. Mindmax aims to address this by fostering active engagement. By enabling users to interrupt the AI, ask questions, and steer the lesson toward difficult topics, the platform mimics the benefits of one-on-one tutoring—a method that typically leads to significantly higher engagement than traditional classroom settings. This is particularly relevant as an estimated billion people will need to upskill or change careers in the coming decade.

The investment round was led by Concept Ventures, with participation from Redrice Ventures, Daybreak Ventures, and Lerer Hippeau. The startup also attracted notable angel investors, including Torsten Reil, co-founder of the defense AI company Helsing, and Maria Raga, former CEO of Depop.

Co-founder Ed Cooke, who previously built Memrise to reach over 80 million users, brings over a decade of experience in learning tools to this project. His co-founder, Daniel Zohar, who served as CTO at Memrise, complements this expertise. Mindmax positions itself as a tool to help people maintain critical thinking skills at a time when many are increasingly delegating mental tasks to AI. By prioritizing dialogue over passive consumption, the company seeks to redefine the role of technology in education.
