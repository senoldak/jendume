Latvia-born 16-year-old Henrijs Dandzbergs has founded Polemos Industries to develop a new class of precision-guided missiles driven by artificial intelligence. Admitted to Y Combinator's fall cohort, Dandzbergs stands out as one of the youngest solo founders in the accelerator's history. The startup's primary mission is to create missiles that are easier and cheaper to manufacture, can be fired from portable launchers, and are resistant to jamming attempts. The guiding ambition of the company is to optimize the "cost per kill" in defense operations.

Dandzbergs attributes his focus on defense technology to the conflict in Ukraine, which he believes has permanently altered the theater of war. Having attended Astra Nova, a school focused on lateral thinking and problem-solving, he developed early technical skills in robotics and aerospace engineering. His background includes creating bionic arms and drone sensors, as well as winning a hackathon by building an AI system that designs hardware circuit boards from natural language prompts.

Polemos Industries enters a competitive market dominated by established defense companies like Anduril, CoAspire, and Leidos, all aiming to develop mass-producible missiles. Industry experts note that building cheap missiles involves significant engineering trade-offs, particularly regarding range, payload capacity, and reliability. Despite these challenges, Dandzbergs is focused on producing a working missile prototype by December.

The public announcement of the startup has sparked broader discussions regarding the ethics of autonomous weapons. Dandzbergs maintains that autonomous systems are an inevitable part of modern conflict and expresses a desire to be at the forefront of this technological shift. However, the potential for technical errors and the moral implications of his technology remain significant challenges that the young founder must navigate as he moves forward with his development plans.
