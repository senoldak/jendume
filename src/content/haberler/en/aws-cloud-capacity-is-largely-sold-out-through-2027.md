Amazon's cloud computing arm, AWS, has announced that the vast majority of its compute capacity through 2027 has already been reserved. Company management indicates that allocations for 2028 have also begun. This situation highlights the intense global demand for the massive processing power required to train and run artificial intelligence models. Companies are securing cloud resources years in advance to avoid losing their competitive edge in the AI race.

While AWS continues to reserve some headroom for "On-Demand" services and its own AI platform, Bedrock, most large-scale infrastructure blocks are no longer available. This development creates a critical bottleneck, particularly for technology companies developing large language models. Startups or smaller-scale projects may struggle to access the high-performance GPU resources they require.

The company's total capital expenditure planned for this year is approximately $220 billion. Additionally, rates for capacity blocks dedicated to machine learning were increased by about 15% in January. These figures demonstrate that even massive investments in cloud infrastructure are struggling to keep pace with the surging demand.

Industry analysts and software developers argue that the situation is more nuanced. The "sold out" announcement from AWS refers to large-scale, pre-reserved "hyperscale" blocks. While access to standard server services remains possible for daily use, developers report that they have experienced significant constraints on general cloud resources recently. Some technical operations managers state that they are forced to turn to reservation systems to secure their projects. This proves that cloud infrastructure is no longer just a service, but has become a strategic resource in the age of artificial intelligence.
