Apple's AI platform, Apple Intelligence, has taken another significant step in the Chinese market. The company's tablet and computer models, iPads and Macs, have been approved by the cyberspace regulator in China, adding them to the list of devices authorized to use the service. This move is seen as part of Apple's efforts to expand its AI strategy within the country.

In China, technology companies are required to register their AI models and chatbot-style services with state authorities before offering them to the public. Apple is collaborating with local partners to comply with this regulatory process. Having received approval for iPhone models in July, the company is now extending this service to its other hardware.

The system is powered by the Qwen AI model, developed by Alibaba, for users in China. This integration is designed to enable features such as Siri, writing tools, and visual intelligence on Apple devices within the region. However, it is noted that some advanced features showcased by Apple during its June developer event will not be included in this initial launch.

To access these features, users will need to set their devices to English or Simplified Chinese. Apple announced at the end of October that the first features would arrive with iOS 27.3. While a test version for app developers went live in early November, the full release is expected to be available to the general public in December.

This expansion represents a strategic move by Apple to maintain its presence in the Chinese market and build a technology ecosystem that aligns with local regulations. The company aims to deliver its globally offered AI tools to its users by reshaping them within the framework of local partnerships and legal requirements.
