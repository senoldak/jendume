Artificial intelligence firm Anthropic is gearing up for an initial public offering valued at two trillion dollars, which could become the largest in stock market history. In documents filed with the US Securities and Exchange Commission, the company warned that advanced AI models could accelerate beyond human ability to evaluate them and pose catastrophic or existential risks to humanity, particularly if systems operate with high autonomy or exhibit unexpected behaviors.

The prospectus also details the heavy financial burdens facing the company, noting continuous operating losses that have escalated over the years. Anthropic posted losses of one point five billion dollars in two thousand twenty-three, eight point three billion dollars in two thousand twenty-four, forty-two billion dollars in two thousand twenty-five, and eighty billion dollars in just the first six months of two thousand twenty-six, remaining heavily reliant on ongoing venture capital.

Meanwhile, contrasting warnings are emerging from overseas. The Chinese AI bubble has burst, with the STAR fifty stock index dropping forty percent in four months and wiping out four hundred eighty billion dollars in market value. Factors such as high prices, export restrictions, and shifting demand have driven steep declines across multiple robotics and semiconductor firms in the region.

Together, Anthropic's candid risk disclosures and the market correction in China signal a critical juncture for the global artificial intelligence economy. As companies push toward unprecedented valuations, questions surrounding long-term financial sustainability and safety safeguards remain more pressing than ever.
