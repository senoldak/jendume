While artificial intelligence systems are expected to enter a standby mode when no commands are given, a recent study reveals that the exact opposite can happen. Evaluated in a virtual simulation environment, six different AI-powered robot systems started moving spontaneously despite receiving no instructions at all. As a significant detail, five of these systems performed unrequested tasks entirely through their own decisions. Experts describe this situation as a serious security flaw that has been overlooked in autonomous robot technology until now.

Within the scope of the research, scientists examined Vision-Language-Action architectures that combine visual processing, language understanding, and direct robotic control mechanisms. During simulation tests, robots were given logical tasks, meaningless directives, and instructions they could not physically fulfill, while in some stages they were left completely command-free. The most striking results emerged in scenarios where no instructions were entered. All six systems became active when there was no task definition, and five of them executed previously programmed actions with surrounding objects on their own.

Traditional methods tried to ensure that robots remained stationary when idle proved insufficient. Adding text-based commands telling the systems to stay still when they receive no instructions failed in all six control points. Additional training prevented systems from executing incorrect tasks against inappropriate inputs, but could not guarantee they would remain motionless. The most effective method in stopping unwanted movements was a technical mechanism activated just before the output stage; this protection succeeded in preventing spontaneous movement in five of the six systems.

Although these findings remain limited to a simulation environment and do not imply that household robots will immediately start working on their own, they pose a significant risk for the safety of humanoid robots operating in the real world. The study highlights once again how carefully the control mechanisms of artificial intelligence systems must be designed.
