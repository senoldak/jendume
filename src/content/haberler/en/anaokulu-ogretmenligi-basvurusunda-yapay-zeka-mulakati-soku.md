Having worked across various industries over the past 15 years, a job seeker applying for a preschool education role recently experienced an industry first: an interview conducted entirely by an artificial intelligence assistant. Greeted by a disembodied voice in a video window, the applicant found the process disconcerting and was abruptly booted from the system simply for pausing to think through a difficult question.

At the conclusion of the automated interview, questions about the role were met with generic regurgitations from the job advertisement rather than real answers. Reflecting on the childcare sector, the applicant noted the profound irony of outsourcing the recruitment of educators—a deeply human profession rooted in care and nurturing—to a machine.

Ultimately withdrawing her application and receiving no human response or acknowledgment weeks later, the candidate voiced concerns about a future where genuine human effort is erased from hiring. This personal account highlights the growing tension between automated efficiency and the preservation of human dignity in modern workplaces.
