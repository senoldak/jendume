Forrester's State of AI Survey 2026 has revealed a growing disconnect between corporate spending on emerging software and the financial returns those tools generate. According to the research, artificial intelligence is projected to represent one-fifth of all business technology expenditures by 2027. Yet as enterprises shift away from simple experiments toward structured deployments, more than half of surveyed chief information officers anticipate missing their return on investment targets next year.

The benchmark for what constitutes a successful deployment remains tight. Around 45 percent of technology leaders expect to see a positive return within a single year to classify an initiative as successful. However, actual financial outcomes have lagged behind these expectations, with only 22 percent of decision-makers reporting a measurable positive impact on company earnings over the past twelve months.

Economic headwinds and shifting pricing structures are further compounding budget pressures. While most enterprise leaders plan to raise technology spending next year, only 53 percent expect their budget growth to outpace inflation, meaning many are simply struggling to cover baseline operational increases. Additionally, providers are moving away from fixed seat-based subscriptions toward consumption-based pricing models that charge per token, driving operational expenses higher.

To keep consumption costs manageable, businesses are increasingly adopting open-weight models such as Llama, Gemma, and Qwen. The report indicates that these alternatives are sufficiently capable for heavy enterprise workloads, including document processing, customer support dialogues, and internal knowledge searches. Furthermore, looming breakthroughs in quantum computing may soon place additional strain on resources, especially since 87 percent of familiar enterprises currently allocate zero budget to that field.
