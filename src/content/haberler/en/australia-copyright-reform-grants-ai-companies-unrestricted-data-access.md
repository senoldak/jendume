The Australian government's proposed copyright reforms aimed at attracting big tech investment are facing severe criticism for threatening the country's decades-old creative ecosystem. According to industry evaluations, the proposed changes would grant tech companies unrestricted access to Australian and international content under an opt-out model. This approach strips creators of their ability to negotiate fair prices and protect their intellectual property effectively. Critics warn that if the legislation proceeds, the country risks becoming a global hub for intellectual property laundering. The unrestricted scraping of TV shows, news, and books could deter international creative businesses and news publishers from licensing content in the country, ultimately reducing consumer choice and running counter to international treaty commitments. Industry experts emphasize that they are not anti-AI but advocate for responsible adoption where AI firms properly license the content their models rely on. Just as companies pay for electricity, talent, and water, content creators argue they should not be expected to provide their work for free. Stakeholders are urging the Australian government to slow down, re-evaluate the economic implications of the proposed reforms, and pursue a balanced policy framework rather than adopting models dictated by foreign tech giants. International creative businesses continue to monitor these developments with growing concern, noting that economic security serves as the foundation for broader national security and protection against potential digital threats.
