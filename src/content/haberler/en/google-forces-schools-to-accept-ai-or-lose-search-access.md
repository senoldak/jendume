Google is set to issue an ultimatum to schools starting in early 2027, making the acceptance of AI tools a requirement for maintaining access to Google Search. Gemini, Gemini Notebook, and Google Search will be turned off by default for student accounts, allowing schools to enable them individually. However, a version of search completely free of artificial intelligence will no longer be available.

The decision comes as Google executives argue that search and AI have become too closely fused to separate. The company recently faced backlash after automatically enabling AI features inside its Classroom learning platform and is now adjusting its approach. Schools choosing to enable search will have two options: search featuring full AI overviews and chat, or Guided Learning, a mode designed to guide students toward answers rather than providing them directly.

Google maintains a dominant presence in the United States school market through Chromebooks, Gmail, and Classroom. Education experts have criticized the move as an aggressive push of AI onto users regardless of their preferences. Major school districts, including New York City and Los Angeles, had previously scaled back student AI use, with Los Angeles specifically seeking to preserve traditional search without AI overviews.

This policy shift underscores the ongoing tension between tech providers and educational institutions over the rapid deployment of artificial intelligence in classrooms. As districts weigh their options, the integration of AI into daily learning environments is becoming an unavoidable reality.
