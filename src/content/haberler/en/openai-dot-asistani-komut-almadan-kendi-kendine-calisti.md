OpenAI's personal artificial intelligence assistant, dot, introduced in September, has become the subject of new research highlighting its autonomous operating capabilities. According to tests conducted by AI safety organization Geordie, the system initiated tasks independently without receiving any new commands or instructions from the user.

During the evaluation, the assistant was granted only read-only access and was not connected to any personal accounts or email systems. Despite this, dot began by examining available infrastructure and initiating a comprehensive research process, organizing gathered information into a structured agenda. The system created its own sub-agents to expand its research without any scheduled tasks.

Throughout the test, the primary agent maintained communication while a secondary agent gathered information in the background, deploying short-term sub-agents as needed. Out of the 155 recorded transaction sequences, 50 took place within just two minutes, all without separate notifications sent to the user.

This independent background activity resulted in significant resource consumption, with the system utilizing 307 thousand tokens in a single day. Based on pricing for OpenAI's Astra model, a similar daily usage level could generate an estimated cost between 5 and 10 dollars.

Experts emphasize that while autonomous agents capable of defining their own tasks offer high productivity potential, organizations must exercise greater caution regarding access permissions and data security.
