As global demand for artificial intelligence services surges, tech companies are actively seeking ways to sustain their massive infrastructure costs. Marking a significant strategic shift, Google updated its access rules for Gemini AI models in early October, restricting its most powerful technologies solely to paying users.

Under the new system, free account holders are now limited to the lower-capacity Gemini Flash Lite model. Advanced features and the Gemini Pro model have been removed from lower-tier plans and shifted to higher-priced AI Pro and AI Ultra subscriptions. This move narrows the scope of entry-level packages, targeting professional users who rely heavily on high-end computational power.

Industry research shows that while broad audiences demonstrate a strong interest in AI tools, consumers remain cautious about paying for subscriptions. With only a small fraction of users currently maintaining paid plans, companies are under pressure to refine their monetization strategies. In a highly competitive market, the balance between model performance and pricing will ultimately determine user loyalty.
