Coherence has raised $45 million in a seed funding round to advance its technology using brain implants for cancer detection. The startup notes a growing synergy between major AI laboratories and companies building devices to decode neural data. By leveraging advanced machine learning models, Coherence aims to process massive datasets generated by neural devices that are too complex for human analysis alone.

The San Francisco-based company has already conducted preliminary safety tests on patients undergoing brain tumor surgeries, with implants recording neural activity for brief periods. The next phase involves testing both recording and stimulation capabilities on additional patients. The device is ultimately designed to emit mild electrical stimulation to inhibit tumor growth and provide real-time treatment based on detected cancer signals.

Ian Hathaway, a partner at the OpenAI Startup Fund, praised Coherence's innovative approach to developing data-driven cancer therapies. Meanwhile, Coherence leadership clarified that OpenAI will not have access to any patient health or brain data generated during the research process.

This investment reflects a broader trend of major AI stakeholders investing in brain-computer interfaces. OpenAI previously invested in Merge Labs, a company focused on ultrasound technology for neural decoding, signaling a strategic interest in bridging human biology with machine intelligence.
