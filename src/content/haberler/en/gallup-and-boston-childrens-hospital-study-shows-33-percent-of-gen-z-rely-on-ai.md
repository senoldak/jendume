A comprehensive study conducted by researchers from Gallup and the Digital Wellness Lab at Boston Children’s Hospital has shed light on how young people form social bonds. The report found that more than one-third of Gen Z, specifically 33 percent, rely on AI for companionship. Among daily AI users, this figure spikes to 50 percent, while reaching 42 percent for those who maintain online-only friendships. Rather than solely measuring screen time, the study examined the nature and quality of youth relationships. While in-person friendships scored the highest for strength and support and were associated with positive emotional experiences, young people relying on AI or online-only friends may face higher odds of loneliness. The survey sampled 15,925 individuals aged 15 to 22 across 16 countries. David Bickham, research director at the Digital Wellness Lab, noted that adolescents are naturally seeking various contexts to socialize. As AI emerges as a powerful tool for finding information, young people increasingly engage with its social dimensions. Although AI helps fill social gaps, researchers emphasized that in-person and digitally mediated friendships currently coexist within the same social landscape. The findings point toward a broader pattern of digital social engagement among Gen Z. Researchers suggest that AI companionship could become even more common in the coming years as technology embeds itself deeper into everyday youth experiences, leaving the long-term psychological impacts open for future study.
