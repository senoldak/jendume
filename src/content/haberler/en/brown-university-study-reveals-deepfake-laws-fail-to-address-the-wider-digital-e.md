A new study conducted by computer scientists at Brown University examines the shortcomings of federal and state laws enacted to combat the spread of AI-generated nonconsensual intimate images. The research shows that over 92% of existing legal regulations target only individual creators and distributors, largely overlooking the broader technological ecosystem that enables the issue.

Scientists state that this approach leads to an endless game of whack-a-mole as new content constantly pops up across the web. For instance, the study highlights that last December, a tool on the X platform was generating over 6,000 unauthorized images every hour. To get to the root of the problem, the researchers mapped the ecosystem into 11 technology categories spanning five distinct roles, including image creation, distribution, discovery, infrastructure, and monetization.

The study reveals how a vast network ranging from the development of AI models to app stores, social media platforms, and payment processors facilitates these abuses. The research team argues that regulators and tech companies should focus on these technical infrastructure pressure points rather than just chasing individual users.

Study authors point out that current mitigations are mostly reactive steps that intervene only after images are already published. The researchers state that efforts should instead focus on technical interventions that prevent AI models from generating such harmful images in the first place.
