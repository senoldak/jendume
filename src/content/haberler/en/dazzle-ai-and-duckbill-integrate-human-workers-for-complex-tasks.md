Autonomous AI agents that have recently gained popularity are capable of scouting the internet and finding products for users. However, these agents, powered by foundation models from companies like Meta and OpenAI, often hit roadblocks when tasked with real-world actions such as making phone calls, scheduling appointments, or bypassing CAPTCHA tests. Utilizing humans when agents face logical roadblocks helps ensure accurate completion while saving computational resources and avoiding costly processing errors.

Marissa Mayer, founder of agent startup Dazzle AI, notes that while top-tier models provide great recommendations, they can easily confuse details when booking complex home renovations or repairs. To bridge this gap, startups are integrating human contractors to take over tasks where artificial intelligence typically fumbles. Human intervention allows complex scenarios to be sorted out within seconds compared to automated methods that might struggle indefinitely.

For instance, digital agent startup Duckbill recently launched an application programming interface that allows companies building personal agents to delegate tasks to human workers. Duckbill CEO Meghan Joyce explains that they break tasks down into bite-sized pieces in a model similar to ride-hailing services. Other startups like Wajo AI also incorporate human workers to handle phone calls and security verifications, emphasizing that collaborative intelligence is necessary to get the job done.

To address privacy concerns regarding human intervention, startups like Duckbill and Dazzle AI conduct rigorous background checks and screen contractors for discretion and good judgment. Industry leaders point out that while foundation models are constantly improving, the market remains in a transitional period. Utilizing humans in the loop serves as a vital transitionary benefit until autonomous systems can match human efficiency in complex real-world tasks.
