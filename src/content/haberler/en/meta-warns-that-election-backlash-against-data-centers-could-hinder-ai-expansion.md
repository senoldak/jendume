Meta leadership has voiced concerns regarding the future of data center projects following the recent midterm elections. The company warned that the anti-data-center platforms adopted by some politicians during the campaign could make it significantly harder to build the infrastructure necessary for AI expansion. Meta executives noted that data centers are increasingly viewed as the visible manifestation of AI, leading to community resistance and local opposition.

The company acknowledges that it needs to do a better job of explaining the benefits of these investments to local communities to shift negative sentiment. Meta's current approach to securing data center deals involves paying for its own electricity, directing tax surpluses toward public services like teachers and first responders, and prioritizing the local labor force. The company believes that demonstrating tangible value to the community is essential for project approval.

Currently, Meta operates data centers across 23 states, with plans for several projects to reach a capacity of one gigawatt or more. This massive compute power is crucial for scaling the company's consumer AI agent, Muse. However, following the election results, the company expects that future data center projects will face more rigorous reviews and will be weighed against the specific desires of local constituents.

Despite the political headwinds, Meta remains committed to its AI investments. The company argues that even if its consumer AI products do not scale as expected, the compute power built in these data centers remains a valuable asset. Meta reports receiving daily inquiries to lease its compute capacity, providing a clear path to monetization regardless of the direct success of its AI agents. The future of these infrastructure projects will depend on the company's ability to align its goals with the interests of the communities where they are built.
