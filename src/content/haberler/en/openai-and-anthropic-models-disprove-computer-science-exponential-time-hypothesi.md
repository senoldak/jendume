A foundational hypothesis in computer science that has guided hundreds of studies for decades has reportedly been disproved by artificial intelligence. OpenAI and Anthropic announced that their AI models successfully overturned the exponential time hypothesis. The hypothesis long held that certain problems are so complex that computers can only solve them by attempting nearly every conceivable answer. This claim has triggered widespread soul-searching and surprise among mathematicians and researchers worldwide.

The findings were initially proposed around twenty-five years ago by computer scientists Russell Impagliazzo and Ramamohan Paturi, who are now helping organize an independent review of the corporate claims alongside other leading researchers. While some scientists feel vindicated by the challenge to a theory they doubted, others express regret that the discovery was made by a machine rather than a human who could explain their reasoning process. The situation highlights growing concerns over proprietary AI models driving research agendas and the unequal access to powerful computational tools.

While there are no immediate consequences for ordinary people, this theoretical milestone could eventually pave the way for vastly improved data analysis tools across various branches of science. Researchers note that future extensions might eventually necessitate stronger encryption standards as computational efficiency leaps forward. The scientific community is now working to independently assess the claims and understand what research methodologies will look like in the near future.
