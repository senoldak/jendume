Enterprises are increasingly granting AI agents access to their most sensitive data, yet governance frameworks are struggling to keep pace with this shift. Hewlett Packard Enterprise and Daxa are collaborating to address this critical gap by enforcing governance directly at the data layer. As discussed during the SC26 event, for agents to deliver real value, they must interact with core enterprise assets, including intellectual property, customer relationship management data, and proprietary trading algorithms. This access creates an unprecedented level of risk, as agents are not merely reading data but are capable of manipulating it at scale.

Traditional security measures, such as firewalls, data loss prevention tools, and identity management systems, were built for human users and standard applications. They are ill-equipped to handle the operations of AI agents, which function at what is described as "layer 8," or the cognitive layer. This layer involves complex prompts, responses, retrievals, and tool calls that traditional tools cannot effectively monitor. To counter this, the focus has shifted toward reasoning-based access controls and systems that flag abnormal behavior in real time.

Hewlett Packard Enterprise aims to integrate governance into its Private Cloud AI stack from the very beginning, ensuring that security is a fundamental part of the workflow rather than an afterthought. By utilizing techniques such as semantic caching, specialized small language models, and a distributed data plane, the collaboration enables inline enforcement without hindering agent performance. A regional bank has already deployed these integrated solutions to connect its agents to sensitive source code and regulated customer data.

Industry leaders suggest that the winners in the agent era will not necessarily be those with the most graphics processing units, but rather the enterprises that can trust their agents with their data. Building governance into the workflow from day one is becoming a prerequisite for safely scaling artificial intelligence in business environments.
