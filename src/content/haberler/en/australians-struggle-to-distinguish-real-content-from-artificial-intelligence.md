The rapid integration of generative artificial intelligence tools into daily life is increasingly blurring the line between authentic and synthetic content. A comprehensive public survey conducted in Australia shows that a significant portion of the population has encountered material they initially believed was real, only to later discover it was generated by artificial intelligence.

The findings indicate that while individuals with higher education and AI literacy are better equipped to recognize when they have been misled, those with lower literacy levels often remain unaware. Despite widespread awareness and regular usage of generative AI platforms, with over half of respondents using chatbots at least weekly, public trust in major AI tools remains notably low.

Furthermore, the survey highlights deep public skepticism regarding how certain sectors utilize the technology. While citizens express distrust toward politicians and social media companies employing artificial intelligence, they view scientists and healthcare professionals as more reliable and benevolent users. However, despite these concerns, few people actively verify content origins, often relying merely on intuition rather than checking independent sources or seeking disclosure labels.

Experts stress the urgent need for a multi-pronged response from technology platforms and governments. Clearer and more consistent labeling of synthetic content, alongside nationwide media and AI literacy education programs, is considered essential to help citizens navigate the evolving information landscape safely.
