Google has introduced two significant updates for its AI assistant, Gemini, aimed at enhancing both educational engagement and ecosystem integration. The first update, "Immersive Explorations," is an interactive library designed to help users explore complex topics through layered, visual experiences. Users can navigate through subjects like historical trade routes or the internal mechanics of a jet engine, zooming in from a broad perspective down to the molecular or atomic level. This feature transforms the learning process from static text into a dynamic, guided simulation. Currently, the library supports English, Indonesian, Hindi, Brazilian Portuguese, French, and Simplified Chinese.

The second major development is the launch of the "Connected Apps Platform," which allows third-party services to integrate directly with Gemini and Google Search. Previously, such integrations required direct collaboration with Google, but the new platform enables developers to build and deploy their own integrations using cloud-based servers or Android-specific APIs. Developers can register, test, and submit their applications through a dedicated console, which also provides insights into traffic, latency, and error rates.

These updates signal a shift in how AI assistants function, moving beyond simple question-and-answer formats toward becoming interactive hubs that collaborate with external software. Services such as Adobe, Experian, Localiza, and Peloton are already operational within Gemini. According to Google, more than 50 applications have already been integrated through this new platform. This move is crucial as it allows AI assistants to take on more active roles in daily workflows, enabling users to access necessary tools and services without leaving the AI interface. As the platform expands, it is expected to encompass more markets and Google products, further embedding AI into the fabric of digital productivity.
