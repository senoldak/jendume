A proposed 40-megawatt AI datacentre near Bristol in the United Kingdom has triggered a significant debate between environmental protection bodies and the government. Natural England, the independent nature adviser, urged the local city council to reject the planning application due to potential threats to protected wildlife habitats and migratory birds located nearby. The agency pointed out that the site is within 600 metres of a special protection area for birds, risking disturbance and displacement of vulnerable species, while another protected site at Hallen Marsh was located only 270 metres away. Additionally, the Environment Agency raised concerns over flood risks, noting that the proposed site is situated on a high-risk flood plain without providing adequate information on water use and pollution risks.

Following these interventions, government officials signaled that independent agencies accused of blocking development could potentially be scrapped with responsibilities handed to ministers. Across the country, numerous AI datacentres are currently scheduled for construction, with more than 300 facilities lined up for connection to the national electricity grid, creating a total requirement of 73 gigawatts, amounting to almost twice the peak winter energy requirement of the entire UK. Rivington Energy, the company developing the project, stated that it acknowledges the feedback and is preparing detailed environmental assessments as part of the ongoing planning process to address ecological and technical concerns.
