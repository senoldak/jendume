The legal dispute between US technology firm Palantir and London Mayor Sadiq Khan over a 50 million pound police intelligence contract has officially come to an end. Both parties announced they have dropped their respective allegations, avoiding a high court trial that was scheduled for January. The conflict began when the mayor's office blocked the company from securing a deal for Scotland Yard to use artificial intelligence in criminal investigations.

In a joint statement, the parties clarified that the decision to block the contract was based on statutory procurement guidelines rather than the company's ethical values or public controversies. The mayor's office acknowledged that prior statements might have created a misunderstanding, while confirming that a new procurement process is now underway.

This resolution provides clarity for both public safety administration and technology suppliers operating in the region. As governments increasingly rely on complex data systems, the case underscores the delicate balance between efficient public spending and transparent regulatory oversight.
