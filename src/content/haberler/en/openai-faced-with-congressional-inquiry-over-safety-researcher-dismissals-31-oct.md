Over twenty US House Democrats have written a letter to OpenAI leadership questioning the dismissal of safety researchers who shared information with outside groups. Led by Representative Greg Casar of Texas, lawmakers asked the company to guarantee that whistleblowers will not be penalized and urged a pause on its most advanced models until independent oversight is established. Lawmakers submitted eleven sets of questions to the executive team regarding the incidents.

The letter named the researchers as Tomek Korbak, Mikita Balesni and Jasmine Wang. OpenAI maintained that the employees were fired for breaching internal rules regarding sensitive information rather than raising safety concerns, while the researchers stated they never shared confidential information outside the company. Meanwhile, OpenAI published details on how it monitors its frontier models and continues to evaluate its security protocols, stating it is working with external audit groups to address security vulnerabilities.

The process has sparked new debates regarding the rights of researchers working in the field of artificial intelligence safety and the transparency obligations of companies. These developments serve as a reminder of how critical auditing mechanisms and independent oversight are across the industry. The upcoming steps by the involved parties remain closely watched.
