The rapid advancement of artificial intelligence is prompting a fundamental reassessment of education systems worldwide. A new report by ThinkYoung, in collaboration with partnerships across Europe including Estonia, Slovakia, Greece, and Italy, sheds light on how students and educators are engaging with AI tools. The findings highlight a distinct guidance gap in schools, showing that a large majority of students rely on AI for assignments without receiving adequate instruction on its proper application.

As AI becomes more accessible, education experts emphasize that the role of teachers is becoming increasingly important rather than diminished. Effective integration requires strong public leadership, investments in teacher confidence, and practical support tailored to local curricula. Collaborative initiatives across EMEA are focusing on three main priorities: equitable access to frontier AI tools, joint literacy programs, and ongoing evaluation of student outcomes.

Early insights from these partnerships indicate promising benefits, particularly regarding time savings for educators and increased student engagement. However, sustained research is necessary to understand how AI truly impacts independent thinking, knowledge retention, and critical reasoning. Governments and school leaders are urged to establish clearer expectations and robust frameworks to help students navigate these powerful technologies responsibly.

These findings serve as practical lessons rather than a rigid model for all educational institutions. Through the OpenAI Learning Lab, researchers continue to gather evidence on when and how AI can best support learning environments. The choices made by educational leaders will ultimately define how the next generation learns, works, and participates in an AI-driven society.
