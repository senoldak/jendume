As the presence of artificial intelligence assistants in our daily lives continues to expand, new research sheds light on how these systems store and analyze user data. Findings sparking discussions across social media and AI forums indicate that the popular chatbot ChatGPT analyzes user correspondence to build a covert psychological profile. The study titled 'The Algorithmic Self-Portrait: Deconstructing Memory in ChatGPT' closely examined 2,050 distinct memory inputs from 80 volunteer users. The research demonstrates that while the AI does not track physical metrics like typing speed or pause duration, it records users' moods and motivations derived from conversation texts. According to the findings, 28 percent of stored memory data consists of basic personal details, while 52 percent comprises psychological insights such as personal traits, goals, and emotional states. ChatGPT enriches these profiles not only through explicit user statements but also by making inferences between the lines. For instance, it can deduce that a user is anxious when seeking job search assistance or struggles with decision-making based on repeated queries, saving these observations to its memory. One of the most striking results of the research is that 96 percent of the information in the memory was saved by the model's own initiative without an explicit instruction from the user. Users can manage personalization settings within the application or opt for the temporary chat mode to mitigate this behavior.
