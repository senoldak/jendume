At the Open Source Summit Europe 2026 held in Prague, the open-source operating system community openKylin outlined its comprehensive vision for integrating artificial intelligence into Linux. During the event, community leaders introduced a unified AI runtime alongside a system-level agent execution environment, addressing the ongoing disconnect between powerful AI models and standard desktop computing tasks.

To bridge this architectural gap, openKylin proposes shifting the operating system paradigm from traditional resource management to intent management. Instead of merely handling CPU, memory, and files, modern operating systems must understand user goals and break them down into coordinated system actions. The newly proposed three-layer architecture includes a runtime layer with KART, an agent execution environment, and a capability layer for desktop integration.

These architectural concepts come to life in openKylin 3.0, which introduces core desktop features such as an AI assistant, global intelligent search, and intelligent space organization. Furthermore, the community has optimized RISC-V vector extensions, delivering a two to five times speedup in FP32 inference performance using ONNX Runtime.

With over 27 million deployments and 2.9 million active users globally as of October 2026, openKylin continues to expand its open-source ecosystem. The community plans to contribute its hardware enablement and kernel optimizations upstream, fostering broader collaboration across global Linux and RISC-V ecosystems.
