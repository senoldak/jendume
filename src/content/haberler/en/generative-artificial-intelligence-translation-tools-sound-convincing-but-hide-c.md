Generative artificial intelligence tools are widespread, offering fast and real-time translation features to a broad audience. However, experts warn that these translations are far from perfect. While AI-generated translations often appear very fluent, fluency does not equal accuracy, and even proficient bilingual speakers can overlook errors hidden beneath the surface.

Some translation mistakes stem from a lack of learning data, insufficient prompts, or hallucinations where the model presents false information confidently. Beyond technical limitations, fundamental challenges arise from the inherent complexity of human language and cultural differences between languages. Even widely used language pairs such as English and Chinese encounter revealing examples of these translation hurdles.

Common issues include ambiguous words with multiple meanings, verb tense confusion, and sentences with multiple valid interpretations. While human translators resolve such ambiguities through context, AI models make decisions by predicting the next probable words. Consequently, an output can sound completely natural while subtly altering the original meaning.

Furthermore, language serves to establish relationships and cultural connections. AI models may reproduce older or less appropriate terminology when newer, more inclusive expressions are underrepresented in their training data. This can render a translation linguistically understandable yet socially insensitive. Experts emphasize that while AI is useful for grasping general ideas, its output quality cannot be guaranteed without human input.
