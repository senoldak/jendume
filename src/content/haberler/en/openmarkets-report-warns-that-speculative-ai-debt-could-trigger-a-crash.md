A new analysis published by the Washington, D.C.-based think tank OpenMarkets concludes that the odds of a deep and severe artificial intelligence crash are rapidly increasing due to mounting speculative debt. Titled "Brace For Impact," the report highlights that as leading laboratory facilities find their operational expenses outstripping incoming funds, warning signs indicate the interconnected financial structure could soon collapse. Multiple trillions of dollars are now resting on this success, much of it bound up in speculative debt.

The researchers point out that if the AI industry fails to pay back its mounting debts, a financial crash could follow and spread well beyond the technology sector. The current financial situation is described as unique in recent history regarding its size, scope, and risk, while sharing eerie similarities with the subprime mortgage crisis of 2008. The interconnected and highly concentrated nature of the speculative pursuit introduces severe vulnerabilities for the broader technological ecosystem and the economy at large.

According to the report, financiers desperate to keep AI labs afloat are repackaging speculative data center debt and selling it to pension funds, retirement accounts, university endowments, and other institutional investors. This practice dangerously exposes everyday working people's savings to outsized risk exposure reminiscent of past financial bubbles, echoing the mechanisms seen during earlier mortgage crises.

Furthermore, the analysis criticizes political leaders and federal financial regulators for stripping away regulatory guardrails to keep the bubble afloat in the short term. OpenMarkets warns that these actions only exacerbate the potential harms of a crisis, urging lawmakers to shine a light on murky industry finances, halt regulatory giveaways, protect retirement savings, and strengthen the overall resilience of the financial system.
