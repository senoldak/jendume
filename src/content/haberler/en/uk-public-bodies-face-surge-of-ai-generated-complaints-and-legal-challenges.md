Members of parliament, local authorities, schools, and legal institutions across the UK are grappling with a relentless surge of grievances, requests, and legal challenges generated by artificial intelligence. Caseworkers and public officials report that correspondence has become significantly more complex, lengthy, and time-consuming to address.

The rapid growth of generative AI tools such as ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot, and Gemini over the past few years has enabled everyday people to access free, highly formal legal and administrative drafting assistance. Researchers and commentators have termed this phenomenon 'AI clog' and 'agentic flooding' as automated requests swamp organizations with stretched resources.

Deborah Evans, chief executive of Lawyers in Local Government, notes that complaints which used to average about two pages are now often ten times longer, with some reaching up to 200 pages. These AI-crafted documents frequently include irrelevant details, out-of-jurisdiction case law, and occasional hallucinations, making them harder for human employees to review and answer.

The UK Information Commissioner’s Office highlights that despite inaccuracies or AI generation, public authorities are still legally required to respond to these requests. Meanwhile, businesses and legal advisors report rising legal fees and growing backlogs in employment tribunals as individuals utilize AI tools to challenge dismissals and decisions.

Experts point out that while AI tools empower individuals to voice grievances, the resulting documents often lack a clear focus on practical resolutions, instead presenting verbose arguments that strain public and private sector operations alike.
