China’s National Data Administration has launched an initiative to establish standards for measuring, billing, and ensuring the quality of "tokens," the fundamental units of AI processing. Tokens, which represent the small units of text, audio, and video processed by AI models, have become the primary metric for determining the commercial value of these technologies in China. According to the agency, these new regulations aim to prevent the proliferation of low-quality content across the industry and expand the utility of token-based services.

AI technology is evolving from a research-focused field into a concrete economic model. In the emerging structure referred to as the "token economy" in China, companies are combining rented computing power, AI models, and data to establish "token factories." These entities sell tokens directly to consumers through banks and telecommunications carriers. The introduction of standardized regulations is intended to increase market transparency and ensure that the quality of services received by users is measurable.

Data indicates that the volume of token usage in China is growing rapidly. As of March 2026, daily token usage stood at 140 trillion, a figure that surged past 500 trillion by June 2026. This represents an increase of more than 1,000-fold compared to levels seen in early 2024.

Chinese authorities aim to use these regulations to facilitate the safer and more efficient commercialization of AI services. While no specific date has been announced for the publication of the new standards or their technical details, this move is part of China's broader strategy to establish its AI infrastructure as a global benchmark. The growth in the sector indicates that AI is no longer just software, but an industry with its own distinct infrastructure and economy.
