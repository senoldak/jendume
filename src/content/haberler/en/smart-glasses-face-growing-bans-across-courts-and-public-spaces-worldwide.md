AI-powered smart glasses equipped with cameras and microphones are facing a growing wave of bans in courthouses, schools, military facilities, and other public venues.

Pakistan's Punjab province imposed a thirty-day ban on the use and sale of camera-equipped smart glasses featuring hidden recording capabilities, following complaints regarding illegal surveillance, harassment, and blackmail. Similarly, New York courts have prohibited smart glasses containing cameras, microphones, or computers across all state and local courts.

Public schools in Western Australia have also restricted students, staff, and visitors from wearing smart glasses, while Victoria has announced similar measures. SAT test sites prohibit them during exams, and military facilities enforce strict rules against them. Public pools, movie theaters, and cruise lines are implementing comparable restrictions to address privacy and piracy issues.

Meanwhile, Norway's government is considering temporary limitations that could potentially cover parks, beaches, shopping centers, and gyms. In Paris, prosecutors opened investigations following complaints regarding individuals being filmed without consent using smart glasses.

Although manufacturers argue that recording indicator lights help maintain transparency, ongoing privacy incidents continue to drive stricter regulations in public spaces worldwide. This expanding wave of restrictions reflects society's ongoing struggle to balance technological innovation with the fundamental right to personal privacy.
