Oracle has officially confirmed that it has secured all the necessary funding to fulfill its infrastructure contract with OpenAI. The company rejected recent speculation regarding potential financial constraints, stating that its $20 billion debt and equity sale earlier this month has covered the requirements for this investment cycle. This milestone establishes Oracle as the first major hyperscaler to be fully funded for this specific phase of AI infrastructure development.

This financial security is critical for ensuring the uninterrupted expansion of the data center capacity required to train next-generation AI models. The Abilene campus, where the Astra-6 model was trained, is nearing completion, and the next phase, the Shackelford campus, has received all necessary permits. The company is also on track to receive Nvidia’s latest Vera Rubin hardware racks in the coming weeks, ensuring that the physical infrastructure keeps pace with the software demands of its partner.

Oracle’s financial outlook remains ambitious. The company expects to achieve positive free cash flow by the second half of fiscal year 2028. Looking further ahead, it targets more than $30 billion in free cash flow for fiscal year 2029, with the expectation of self-funding future growth from that point onward.

The capital intensity of the AI infrastructure race is significant. With Microsoft beginning to wind down its deal to rent AI computing capacity from Oracle, the market is shifting. By securing its own financial independence for this project, Oracle aims to demonstrate that its partnership with OpenAI is independent of external market fluctuations. The race to build AI has evolved into an industrial competition for energy, hardware, and physical data center capacity.
