In 2006 there were 19.2 fixed telephone subscriptions per 100 people in the world. That is the highest value in the series the World Bank compiles. The number of subscriptions peaked in the same year, at 1.26 billion. We used values up to the year 2024: the rate fell to 10.2 and the number to 833 million. Since 2006, the rate has fallen by 46.9% and the number by 33.9%.

Over the same period mobile subscriptions rose from 41.7 to 110.4 per 100 people. The number went from 2.74 billion to 9.0 billion. So while fixed lines declined, the rise on the mobile side was far larger than the fall on the fixed side. This article asks when and where that change happened, and where Türkiye stands in the picture.

Here a fixed telephone subscription counts telephone service tied to a fixed point: wired lines, voice over the internet, fixed wireless connections and public payphones among them. A mobile subscription is a subscription to voice service over a cellular network. The data are series that the World Bank compiles from data of the International Telecommunication Union (ITU).

## The climb to the peak

*Fixed telephone subscriptions per 100 people, 1998–2024. Source: World Bank.*

Over 1990–2006, the world series rose. In 1990, there were 9.8 fixed telephone subscriptions per 100 people. The value was 12.0 in 1995, 15.9 in 2000 and 19.1 in 2005. It reached 19.2 in 2006, a total rise of 9.4 subscriptions since 1990.

Counts point the same way. Fixed telephone subscriptions in the world numbered 1.24 billion in 2005, and 1.26 billion in 2006. In 2007, they fell to 1.25 billion. The highest value, in both the rate and the count, is in 2006; the values in 2005 and in 2007 are lower.

Here the word break means a change of direction: the slope of the series turned from positive to negative. So the year 2006 is a break point: the series rose up to then and began to fall afterwards. In the World Bank series, every year over 2006–2024 has a lower value than the year before.

Fall and rise are comparable in size. From the peak to the year 2024, the number of subscriptions per 100 people fell by 9.0. Almost all of the 9.4 gained since 1990, up to the peak, was given back: in 2024, the value is only 0.4 above the value in 1990.

## The break: mobile overtakes fixed

The peak of the fixed line and the moment mobile overtook it are not the same event. In the world average, mobile subscriptions in 1999 stood at 8.1 per 100 people, a little over half of the fixed value of 15.0; the ratio of the two was 0.54. In 2005, mobile was 33.9 and fixed 19.1, a ratio of 1.77.

We cannot give the exact year of the crossing from the world series. The World Bank's world total has a mobile value in 1999, and the next one is in 2005; for the years 2000–2004 there is none. The crossing must have happened within that range, but the data do not say in which year.

Totals with data show the year. Among OECD members, in 2000, mobile was 44.0 and fixed 49.8. In 2001, mobile was 52.2 and fixed 49.6. The fixed line among OECD members peaked in 2000, at 49.8; so the crossing followed the peak immediately.

Mobile subscriptions stood at 1.6 per 100 people in the world in 1995, and 8.1 in 1999. We do not link the two years. That the peak and the crossing are close does not show that one caused the other; the two series moved side by side in the same period, and that is all.

## After the peak: a little less each year in the world

![Line chart: Mobile and fixed broadband subscriptions rose while fixed telephone subscriptions fell: Mobile: 41.7 in 2006, 68.0 in 2009, 88.5 in 2012, 96.1 in 2015, 103.1 in 2018, 106.7 in 2021, 110.4 in 2024; Fixed broadband: 4.3 in 2006, 6.9 in 2009, 9.2 in 2012, 11.3 in 2015, 14.0 in 2018, 16.7 in 2021, 19.3 in 2024; Fixed telephone: 19.2 in 2006, 18.4 in 2009, 16.7 in 2012, 14.1 in 2015, 12.4 in 2018, 11.2 in 2021, 10.2 in 2024.](/grafik/sabit-hattin-kirilma-noktasi/graf-6.svg)
*World, subscriptions per 100 people, 2006–2024. Source: World Bank.*

After the peak, the path is slow and steady. Fixed telephone subscriptions were 19.2 in 2006, 14.1 in 2015 and 10.2 in 2024. Mobile subscriptions in the same years were 41.7, 96.1 and 110.4. The fixed line lost while the mobile phone gained fast, but the speed of the gain was not constant: 46.8 subscriptions were added over 2006–2012, and 21.9 over 2012–2024.

Fixed broadband rose in the same period: 4.3 in 2006, 9.2 in 2012, 14.0 in 2018, and 19.3 in 2024. The rise in mobile slowed over 2015–2021: 7.0 subscriptions were added over 2015–2018, and 3.6 over 2018–2021 (96.1 in 2015, 103.1 in 2018, and 106.7 in 2021). The widening gap also shows in the ratios for three years.

![Key figure: The number of mobile subscriptions per fixed telephone subscription rose worldwide: Ratio: 10.82 in 2024; Earlier years: 1.77 in 2005, 6.82 in 2015.](/grafik/sabit-hattin-kirilma-noktasi/graf-7.svg)
*Ratio of mobile to fixed telephone subscriptions, world. Source: World Bank.*

For each fixed telephone subscription there were 1.77 mobile subscriptions in 2005. In 2015 the ratio was 6.82, and in 2024 it was 10.82.

Count and rate fall at different speeds. The number of fixed telephone subscriptions fell by 33.9% over 2006–2024, while the rate per 100 people fell by 46.9%. The difference between the two comes from the denominator: the denominator of the rate is population, and world population grew by 22.2% over 2006–2024. When the same number of subscriptions is divided among more people, the rate falls.

On the mobile side the opposite happens: the number of subscriptions rose by 228.1%. Because the rate reached 110.4, there are more than 100 mobile subscriptions per 100 people in the world average.

## In Türkiye the peak came earlier, and the crossing in the same year

Türkiye's fixed-line peak came five years before the world peak: 28.5 per 100 people in 2001. In the same year, mobile rose to 29.5 and overtook the fixed line. A year earlier, in 2000, mobile was 24.7 and fixed 28.1.

After the peak, Türkiye's series did not make a straight decline. Fixed telephone subscriptions were 26.9 in 2006, 18.4 in 2012 and 13.6 in 2016. They rose to 13.8 in 2018, and to 14.5 in 2020. They fell back to 10.3 in 2024. The total fall from the peak is 63.9%.

Across every even year shown in the chart, Türkiye's value stayed above the world value, but the gap is not constant: it narrowed to 0.2 in 2016, widened to 3.0 in 2020, and fell to 0.1 in 2024. In 2024, the value is very close to the world value: 10.3 in Türkiye, 10.2 in the world. In 2006 there was a large gap between the two: 19.2 for the world, 26.9 for Türkiye. Mobile subscriptions in Türkiye reached 107.8 in 2024.

In Türkiye the mobile value was 29.5 in 2001, and 107.8 in 2024; so on the mobile side too, Türkiye moved in the same direction as the world average, with a far larger rise than the fall of the fixed line. This comparison rests on no judgement. It says only this: in Türkiye the fixed line peaked before the world average. In the same 2006–2024 window the world series fell by 46.9%, while Türkiye's series fell by 61.7%.

## Regions did not peak in the same year

![Small multiples: The year of peak fixed telephone subscriptions differs from region to region: World: 15.9 in 2000, 18.7 in 2004, 18.5 in 2008, 16.7 in 2012, 13.4 in 2016, 11.5 in 2020, 10.2 in 2024; Europe and Central Asia: 37.5 in 2000, 38.5 in 2004, 37.4 in 2008, 35.7 in 2012, 31.9 in 2016, 28.1 in 2020, 24.3 in 2024; North America: 68.2 in 2000, 60.8 in 2004, 53.4 in 2008, 44.5 in 2012, 37.3 in 2016, 30.5 in 2020, 24.0 in 2024; Türkiye: 28.1 in 2000, 27.9 in 2004, 24.5 in 2008, 18.4 in 2012, 13.6 in 2016, 14.5 in 2020, 10.3 in 2024; South Asia: 2.6 in 2000, 3.6 in 2004, 3.0 in 2008, 2.5 in 2012, 1.8 in 2016, 1.5 in 2020, 2.5 in 2024; Sub-Saharan Africa: 1.4 in 2000, 1.5 in 2004, 1.5 in 2008, 1.3 in 2012, 1.1 in 2016, 0.7 in 2020, 0.4 in 2024.](/grafik/sabit-hattin-kirilma-noktasi/graf-8.svg)
*Fixed telephone subscriptions per 100 people, 2000–2024. Source: World Bank.*

One year holds the peak of the world series, but the peaks of the regions are not in the same year. In the World Bank's regional series the peak years are these: North America in 2000, at 68.2; South Asia in 2005, at 3.8; Europe and Central Asia in 2006, at 39.0; and Sub-Saharan Africa in 2009, at 1.6.

Measured from each peak up to the year 2024, the change differs by region. In North America the value fell by 64.8%, in Sub-Saharan Africa it fell by 75.0%, in Europe and Central Asia it fell by 37.7%, and in South Asia it fell by 34.2%. Among OECD members the value fell by 37.1% over 2000–2022. In South Asia, however, the series fell to 1.5 in 2020, then rose again and reached 2.5 in 2024; so there the path after the peak is not a straight fall.

South Asia and Sub-Saharan Africa have peak values, 3.8 and 1.6, that did not reach double digits. The peak levels are very different: 1.6 in Sub-Saharan Africa and 68.2 in North America. In a region with a low level, a percentage fall from the peak can look large. So the peak year and the level need to be read together. Because the panels are drawn on a common scale, they can be compared directly; the line of North America runs far above the others, and the line of Sub-Saharan Africa runs close to the floor.

## When did broadband overtake the fixed line?

It is easy to think that fixed broadband took the place of the fixed line, but the data do not say so. What they show is this: in the world average, fixed broadband subscriptions were 3.4 per 100 people in 2005 and 19.3 in 2024, a rise of 15.9 subscriptions. In 2016, the fixed line stood at 13.4 and fixed broadband at 12.2. In 2017, the fixed line was 13.0 and fixed broadband 13.5. Fixed broadband overtook fixed telephone in that year. In 2006, fixed broadband was 4.3 and fixed telephone 19.2; in 2024, they were 19.3 and 10.2. This swap is the second of the crossings we see in this article: the first was mobile overtaking the fixed line, the second was broadband doing so.

Fixed broadband subscriptions were 1.89 times fixed telephone subscriptions in 2024. We cannot learn from these series how many people have both services. A household may have both. The series count how each service spread separately, not a swap between them.

## The counter-reading: a subscription is not a person

For mobile phones, a value of 110.4 per 100 people can mean that everyone has a phone and that some have more than one subscription. A subscription is a record, not a person. According to the World Bank definition, prepaid accounts used in the last three months are also counted. So a ratio above 100 does not prove that everyone has access to a mobile phone.

On the fixed side, too, the definition is broad: the World Bank note includes analogue lines, voice over the internet, fixed wireless connections, ISDN voice channels and public payphones in fixed telephone. The decline in the series is the decline of the sum of these services, not only of the wired line.

Use is a third counter-reading. The number of subscriptions shows that a service is offered; it does not show use. The World Bank note confirms this: subscription data give a general idea of access, and the share of households with access is a more precise measure.

## Limits of measurement

The world mobile series has no value for the years 2000–2004. So we could not give the year of the crossing at world level; we saw it only among OECD members and in Türkiye. The World Bank's world series also have a value for the year 2025; this article builds its comparisons up to the year 2024.

Second, the series rest on reports from operators. According to the World Bank note, operators have traditionally been the main source of telecommunications data. So subscription counts are a general indicator of access, not an exact measure.

Third, regions are a matter of definition. The regional series depend on the World Bank classification; the World Bank decides which countries belong to the groups of Europe and Central Asia, South Asia, Sub-Saharan Africa and North America.

Fourth, the peak itself needs a definition. For the regions we looked only at the peak of the rate per 100 people; because population changes, the peak year of the number of subscriptions may differ in a region. For the world, both are in 2006.

Finally, this article draws no causal link. The rise of mobile and the fall of the fixed line are seen in the same period; the data do not show that one caused the other.

## How we calculated

- From the World Bank's World Development Indicators (WDI) database we took six series: fixed telephone subscriptions per 100 people (IT.MLT.MAIN.P2), mobile subscriptions (IT.CEL.SETS.P2), fixed broadband subscriptions (IT.NET.BBND.P2), and the numbers of fixed telephone and mobile subscriptions (IT.MLT.MAIN, IT.CEL.SETS) and world population (SP.POP.TOTL). The original source of the subscription series is the ITU.
- The peak year in each series is the year of the highest value in the World Bank's annual data. We checked the world, Türkiye and four regions.
- Percentage change: later value minus earlier value, divided by the earlier value, times one hundred. Ratio: the mobile value divided by the fixed telephone value for the same year. Difference: one value subtracted from the other.
- The values per 100 people are rounded from the raw values in the source, and calculations on them were done on the rounded values. The changes in the numbers of subscriptions and in population were calculated from the raw values; counts are written in billions and millions.
- For the charts we took annual values for selected years: even years for the world and Türkiye, every fourth year for the regions. Each point can be fetched again from the World Bank API with a one-country, one-year query.
