One of the most challenging parts of scientific research is balancing a proposed idea against existing literature and proving its novelty. Researchers from the Allen Institute for AI and the University of Pennsylvania have developed a new system called LitPivot to streamline this process. The system segments researchers' drafts into distinct facets such as problems, contributions, and evaluations, linking them directly to relevant academic papers to help scientists refine their work.

With LitPivot, researchers can select specific parts of their draft to receive critiques and suggested revisions backed by excerpts from selected studies. The system identifies gaps in the existing literature and provides concrete suggestions on how to improve the research proposal while keeping the researcher in full control of the final text. This connects the ideation phase directly with evidence from published papers.

In a study involving 17 researchers, domain experts evaluated proposals developed with LitPivot against those created using standard text editors and AI chat tools. Experts rated the ideas developed with LitPivot as significantly better grounded in relevant literature and more novel. Participants also reported a stronger understanding of the surrounding academic landscape. In a follow-up assessment, five researchers tested the tool on their own ongoing projects across multiple phases.

LitPivot aims to transform how scientists shape early-stage research ideas by connecting evidence directly to the writing process. The tool provides a new workflow for choosing literature, using evidence to revise ideas, and adapting as the proposal evolves. The system has been presented at the UIST conference in Detroit.
