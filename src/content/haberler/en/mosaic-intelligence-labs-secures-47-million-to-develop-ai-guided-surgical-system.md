Mosaic Intelligence Labs has emerged from stealth mode with $47 million in seed funding to develop AI-guided robotic systems designed for endovascular surgery. The San Francisco-based startup is focusing its initial efforts on procedures performed through the vascular system, where surgeons currently lack direct line of sight. Instead, medical professionals must rely on live X-ray images, pre-operative scans, and tactile feedback, making these delicate procedures difficult to master and execute.

Mosaic aims to solve this by integrating sensors into surgical instruments and equipping them with artificial intelligence. This platform is designed to provide doctors with a constantly updated, three-dimensional view of the patient's anatomy. Furthermore, the company is developing semi-autonomous catheters capable of navigating through blood vessels on their own, effectively "finding their way" as the surgeon guides them.

The company describes this technology as a significant step toward creating a surgical "world model." A world model enables a machine to predict how its actions will alter its surroundings, a concept increasingly applied in autonomous driving and humanoid robotics. The founders argue that traditional surgical instruments are structurally limited because they cannot learn from their actions. By establishing a continuous feedback loop between perception and action, Mosaic intends to scale the knowledge of top surgeons and turn repetitive surgical procedures into valuable learning experiences.

Founded by Ileana Pirozzi and Alison Mackanic, who met during their doctoral studies at Stanford University, the company is assembling a team with diverse engineering backgrounds. Members of the team have previously contributed to the development of rockets, artificial hearts, and autonomous vehicles. With this new capital, Mosaic plans to expand its team of engineers and physicians to advance its vision of data-driven, intelligent surgery.
