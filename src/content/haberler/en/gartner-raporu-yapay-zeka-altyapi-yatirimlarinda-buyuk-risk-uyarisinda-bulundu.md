According to a research note published by Gartner on October 14, 2026, more than 3.5 trillion dollars of artificial intelligence infrastructure spending faces potential impairment by the end of 2030 if providers keep spending at their current pace. Companies would be forced to write down these impaired assets because they can no longer earn back their costs. Gartner analyst Will Sommer noted that while companies want to provide full-stack services, not all of them will succeed.

The research firm also predicts that more than 80 percent of neoclouds, which rent out computing power built for AI, will stop operating independently by 2030. This expected shift is driven by cost pressures and a lack of differentiating business models. Gartner expects AI providers to spend nearly 1 trillion dollars on infrastructure in 2026, with an additional 6 trillion dollars forecasted between 2027 and 2030.

Through 2030, AI data center owners would need 8.5 trillion dollars in revenue to cover that spending and its financing costs, including a 12 percent return on invested capital. However, Gartner forecasts no more than 3.6 trillion dollars of business and consumer AI revenue tied to these data centers during the same period. Sommer stated that enterprise and consumer AI revenue will be insufficient to support profitable returns on all planned capital expenditures.

If spending stays on its current path, more than 4 trillion dollars in asset value could face write-down risk through 2032. While hyperscalers possess large and varied revenue streams to support their spending, specialist providers and neoclouds face heavy debt burdens and market pressures that will force many to exit or restructure in the near future.
