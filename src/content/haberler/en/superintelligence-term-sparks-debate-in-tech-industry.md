Leading figures in the technology industry are shifting the language they use to describe their products and visions. Some major tech executives have begun replacing the term AI with superintelligence. This shift gained momentum following a directive from the US administration instructing federal agencies to adopt the term superintelligence instead of artificial intelligence. However, the industry is not unified in this change; giants like Microsoft, Amazon, and Alphabet continue to stick with the traditional terminology.

This conceptual shift should not be viewed merely as a marketing strategy or an effort to align with political directives. Superintelligence generally describes systems that exceed human intellectual capabilities across a wide range of activities. Yet, this ambitious label remains an empty promise unless supported by scientific evidence or concrete metrics. The fact that a system excels in a specific test or can generate text quickly does not mean it has surpassed human intelligence in a meaningful way.

The divergence in terminology has the potential to directly impact user trust. Labeling a chatbot or digital assistant as superintelligence can artificially inflate a user's trust and expectations. This might lead individuals to scrutinize the system's responses less carefully or to delegate critical decisions to these tools more readily. In reality, current technologies are still tools that can make mistakes and require human oversight.

The desire of companies to pursue the goal of superintelligence may be a legitimate commercial ambition. However, having a goal is entirely different from claiming that the goal has been achieved. The tech industry needs to provide concrete evidence to support such grand claims and explain how these achievements can be verified. For users, the ability to distinguish between marketing language and actual capability is critical for the healthy development of technology.
