Etsy has announced plans to revise its updated terms of service following backlash from sellers regarding a clause that would have allowed the platform to use their content for training artificial intelligence models. The policy, initially scheduled to take effect on December 16, explicitly stated that the company and its third-party partners could use seller content to develop, test, and improve machine learning technologies. This triggered protests, with long-time sellers closing their shops, fearing that their creative work would be used to train systems that could eventually replace their own craftsmanship.

In response to the outcry, company leadership clarified that they would not use seller content to train AI models designed to copy original work or replace human creativity. The company stated that the clause was intended to facilitate internal functions such as fraud detection, automatic translation of listings, and personalized marketing campaigns. Despite these assurances, many independent creators remain skeptical, arguing that the language of the policy was too broad and lacked sufficient opt-out mechanisms for those who do not wish to contribute to AI development.

This situation reflects the tension between digital marketplaces and the creative community regarding data usage in the age of generative AI. Etsy, which previously faced a seller strike in 2022 over fee increases, is now navigating a broader cultural pushback against the use of user-generated content for AI training. The incident presents a challenge for platforms: balancing the integration of advanced technological tools with the protection of the intellectual property and livelihoods of the human creators who drive their ecosystems.
