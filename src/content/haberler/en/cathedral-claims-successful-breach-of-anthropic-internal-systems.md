Cathedral, an AI-driven cybersecurity startup founded by former DOGE members focusing on military applications, says it has successfully breached internal data and messaging systems at Anthropic. The startup claims it accomplished the feat in less than twelve hours, accessing internal company data.

According to Cathedral, the breach was achieved by exploiting vulnerabilities in business software used by Anthropic and many other enterprises. The accessed data allegedly included meeting transcripts, salary information, job offers, and internal messaging discussions regarding artificial intelligence models. Anthropic confirmed that Cathedral accessed a limited set of its systems through third-party tools as part of external security testing and stated that it has since shut down those access points.

The startup closed a $160 million funding round in July, valuing the company at $1.4 billion with backing from investors including Andreessen Horowitz and Sequoia Capital. Cathedral's founders include Gavin Kliger, Luke Farritor, Marko Elez, and Jack Stein, and the firm is reportedly developing software aimed at military cybersecurity applications.

Tensions have previously existed between the administration and Anthropic, with government figures criticizing the company and the Pentagon designating it as a supply-chain risk. These political dynamics combined with the recent security breach emphasize the critical need for robust enterprise software security across the industry.
