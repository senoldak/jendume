Visa has launched its "Agentic Commerce Benchmark" to measure the commercial capabilities of AI models, and the initial results indicate significant reliability issues. In the first round of testing, 22 AI models from 12 different labs failed 42.8% of their assigned shopping tasks. This study marks the first comprehensive benchmark of its kind, specifically designed to evaluate how consistently AI agents can make purchasing decisions in real-world scenarios.

The tasks involved purchasing items such as laptops, televisions, and personal care appliances. Crucially, the simulated customers did not provide all necessary details upfront. The AI models were required to ask follow-up questions to uncover missing information like budget constraints and specific technical requirements. In 54% of the failed runs, the AI proceeded to make a purchase anyway, selecting an item that failed to satisfy the user's requirements or finalizing the transaction before gathering all necessary data.

The results highlighted a wide performance gap between different providers. While the top-performing models reached a success rate of 82.2%, the lowest-performing model managed only 28.9%. Agents from Google and Meta shared the top spot with an 82% score, followed by Anthropic's Claude at 76% and an OpenAI agent at 64%. The data also showed that the five most expensive models achieved a 67.1% success rate, compared to 50.2% for the five cheapest ones. Models that were better at identifying hidden buying factors had significantly higher success rates.

Visa executives noted that autonomous digital assistants have the capacity to drive the coming phase of trade, but the industry is not there yet from a trust or technology perspective. A survey published in September found that while 72% of US consumers have used an AI assistant, only 23% trust them to handle payments. Visa plans to expand these benchmarks to include checkout and ordering processes, sharing the findings with model providers to improve the consistency and reliability of autonomous commercial agents.
