Anthropic has officially filed for its initial public offering (IPO), revealing plans for its seven co-founders to sell a portion of their shares as the company moves toward a potential valuation of $1.4 trillion. This milestone underscores the immense economic scale the artificial intelligence sector has achieved and highlights the significant investor appetite for companies shaping the future of the technology.

The seven co-founders—Dario Amodei, Daniela Amodei, Tom Brown, Jack Clark, Jared Kaplan, Sam McCandlish, and Christopher Olah—are each planning to sell a specific portion of their holdings. Based on the projected price range, each founder is expected to net approximately $250 million from the sale. While this transaction will adjust their individual equity stakes, it does not diminish their collective influence over the company’s direction.

According to the regulatory filings, the founders will retain a combined 51.1% of the voting power through a special class of supervoting stock. This governance structure is designed to ensure that the leadership maintains control over the company’s strategic decisions, particularly those related to its foundational commitment to AI safety. Even with other entities like Amazon and Google holding non-voting shares, the founders remain in control.

The company’s journey began in 2021 when the founders departed from OpenAI due to fundamental disagreements regarding how AI should be developed and deployed. By prioritizing safety and ethical considerations from the outset, Anthropic has positioned itself as a distinct leader in the field. The anticipation surrounding the IPO is further evidenced by the interest from high-profile figures, including board member Reed Hastings, who has signaled intent to purchase significant amounts of stock. This public offering represents more than just a financial event; it is a pivotal moment that reflects the evolving power dynamics and corporate governance models within the rapidly maturing artificial intelligence industry.
