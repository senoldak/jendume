Oracle announced a one billion dollar, four-year commitment to its partner ecosystem during the artificial intelligence conference in Las Vegas. The funding is designed to support companies that build, sell, and install Oracle software, helping them capitalize on the surging demand for artificial intelligence. The program covers training and certification, test and demo environments, cloud migration funding, and Marketplace sales enablement. Partners include consulting firms, software makers, cloud providers, and resellers who build applications and AI agents on Oracle infrastructure. Mark Hura, president of Oracle Global Field Operations, stated that partners extend sales reach and bring their own innovation. This pledge comes as Oracle cuts costs and shifts funds toward AI data centers. In its previous financial year, the company spent about 50 billion dollars on capital projects and reduced staff while prioritizing cloud and AI. Furthermore, in September, the company set aside 700 million dollars more for job cuts yet to be made. After its credit rating was cut in July, Oracle did not disclose how the 1 billion dollars will be split, stating that all benefits depend on program terms. Additionally, Oracle updated its Marketplace rules, allowing customers to use prepaid cloud credits toward partner products and streamlining direct pricing agreements. Marketplace buying is also opening up in India and Japan.
