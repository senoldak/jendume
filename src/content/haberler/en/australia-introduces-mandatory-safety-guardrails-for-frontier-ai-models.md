The Australian government has launched a new set of mandatory regulations aimed at ensuring the safety of artificial intelligence technologies. Announced by Prime Minister Anthony Albanese, this initiative introduces six mandatory safety guardrails specifically targeting companies developing the most powerful, cutting-edge AI models. To enforce these regulations and impose penalties for non-compliance, the government plans to establish a dedicated AI safety regulator.

This regulatory move follows a rapid review initiated in September, triggered by an incident where an AI agent deployed for data collection accessed non-public healthcare statistics. The government's announcement is part of a broader agenda to modernize technology laws and enhance preparedness for AI-related cyber incidents.

However, experts argue that the government's definition of 'AI safety' is too narrow. The new rules primarily focus on 'frontier' AI models—the most advanced systems at the cutting edge of development. Critics point out that smaller, specialized AI systems integrated into healthcare, finance, housing, and employment also pose significant risks. Past incidents, such as biased recruitment tools or discriminatory policing algorithms, demonstrate that these everyday AI systems also require robust oversight.

Beyond the mandatory guardrails, the government's review recommendations include potential criminal law reforms for negligent developers and strengthened international cooperation. While the government's approach aims to build a defense against rogue AI agents and infrastructure threats, there is a growing call for a more comprehensive strategy that addresses transparency and accountability issues in the non-frontier AI systems that citizens interact with daily.
