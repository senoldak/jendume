OpenAI has announced a significant partnership with the UK-based youth charity The King’s Trust. The company is donating £1 million to support a new program aimed at helping up to 25,000 young people acquire practical AI skills over the next 18 months. The initiative specifically prioritizes individuals who are not currently in education, employment, or training (NEET), providing them with the tools needed to navigate an increasingly AI-enabled economy.

In an era where artificial intelligence is rapidly transforming the workplace, ensuring that the next generation is prepared for these shifts is a critical challenge. The program is designed to bridge the gap between young people’s current experience and the demands of modern employers. By combining AI skill-building with nationally recognized qualifications and practical experience, the initiative seeks to boost participants' confidence and help them transition into meaningful employment or further education.

Data indicates that a significant majority of 16-24-year-olds who are not currently in education or employment are eager to upskill to improve their job prospects. This partnership leverages OpenAI’s technical expertise alongside The King’s Trust’s 50-year legacy of supporting youth development. It aligns with OpenAI’s broader commitment to making AI learning more accessible, building upon existing efforts like the OpenAI Academy, which provides free resources to students and job-seekers.

The program is being developed in consultation with employers, AI specialists, and young people themselves to ensure the curriculum remains relevant. By integrating AI literacy into existing employability training, the partnership aims to equip the next generation with the skills necessary to thrive in a world where AI is becoming a part of everyday life. This effort represents a strategic move to ensure that the benefits of technological progress are shared broadly across the workforce.
