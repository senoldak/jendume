As autonomous artificial intelligence agents become more common in the workplace, enterprises face a hidden financial burden: every time a new agent is deployed, it lacks any knowledge of the company and must learn operational workflows from scratch. To tackle this inefficiency, startup Uvi, officially known as Naboo Research, announced it has raised $7.5 million in seed funding.

The funding round was led by CT Innovations, with participation from Cardumen, Mana VC, and 91 Ventures. Uvi Co-founder and Chief Executive Gilad Salinger noted that while early enterprise AI adoption centered on accuracy and security, companies are now grappling with high costs, token consumption, and processing speed as agents struggle with repeated tasks.

To solve this, Uvi has developed a context reasoning layer that acts as a secure, unified institutional memory sitting beneath a company's deployed AI tools. By linking fragmented enterprise platforms such as Slack, Jira, Confluence, and Salesforce, the system builds an interconnected map of decisions and operational workflows. Instead of relearning data repeatedly, AI agents tap directly into this layer.

According to Uvi's benchmarks, routing agent responses through this context layer reduces operational costs dramatically and accelerates task completion. Already adopted by companies like Monday.com and Lemonade, the platform features built-in access controls to ensure data privacy. Salinger believes that future enterprise AI stacks will move away from isolated chat interfaces toward persistent context layers that every model can plug into.
