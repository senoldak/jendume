Researchers from Tel Aviv University and Stanford University have developed a method that could revolutionize AI training. Traditionally, artificial intelligence systems learn about the world by being fed billions of texts, images, and recordings from the internet. However, a newly published study proves that a system can develop general predictive skills using data generated entirely within itself, without any external information. This achievement has long been considered a major milestone in AI research.

The researchers utilized a "self-play" method where two models work together from scratch. The first model generates short computer programs that produce sequences of symbols, while the second model is trained to predict the next symbol in those sequences. As the process continues, the generating model receives feedback and begins to select challenges better suited to the learning model's developing capabilities. This closed-loop training allowed the AI to acquire the ability to predict real-world data it had never encountered before.

The system's success was confirmed through rigorous testing after the training phase. Despite being trained only on artificial sequences, the model demonstrated consistent predictive performance across text, music, code, DNA sequences, and images. Remarkably, the system independently discovered universal mathematical patterns, such as the Fibonacci sequence, during its internal training. This finding provides an initial answer to whether intelligence has a universal component independent of external data.

While currently a proof of concept involving small models, this method could pave the way for AI to learn much more efficiently with significantly less data in the future. The researchers emphasize that while the system cannot learn specific facts about reality without exposure to it, it can develop the underlying capacity to understand rules and structures through self-generated experiences. This approach could accelerate AI development in fields where data is scarce.
