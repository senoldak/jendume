In a collaborative effort, OpenAI, PerturbAI, and Scripps Research deployed thousands of AI agents to analyze complex experimental data from millions of mouse brain cells. Powered by GPT-Rosalind, these agents examined gene disruptions across a whole-brain CRISPR atlas, crossed-referenced cellular responses with scientific literature, and completed the analysis in just 32 minutes.

Modern biological experiments generate massive amounts of data that surpass traditional human review capacities. By dividing the workload among thousands of parallel agents, researchers mapped connections between gene disruptions and cellular effects that would normally take months or a year to review manually. The agents successfully reproduced existing findings while identifying unexpected biological connections.

Among their discoveries, the agents highlighted subtle differences in how disease-linked genes affect specific brain regions, as well as a potential link between cholesterol regulation and neurodegeneration. When tested against a single agent performing the same workload, the distributed agents cited more than 700 references compared to just 11, demonstrating the superiority of parallel reasoning in handling complex scientific literature.

These insights provide scientists with concrete leads for future laboratory experiments, particularly concerning neurodegenerative conditions like Perry syndrome and tuberous sclerosis. By combining artificial intelligence with experimental biology, researchers can transform large datasets into testable hypotheses much faster than before.
