Microsoft executive Satya Nadella predicted that the rise of AI agents will reshape the economics of the internet and business models reliant on advertising. Speaking at a summit, Nadella argued that the shift toward an agentic web will restructure human web traffic. He compared this transition to the historical shift from web portals like Yahoo to search engines like Google years ago. This ongoing transformation is expected to profoundly impact how digital platforms operate and handle transactions.

Nadella noted that current aggregators depending on human web traffic will face a reshuffling of their business models. He suggested that companies monetizing eyeballs through ads may need to shift toward cost-per-click or more transactional approaches. The discussion also addressed Amazon's decision to block AI agents from accessing its retail platform and browsing product catalogs on behalf of users. Companies overly attached to legacy models may struggle to adapt to these new dynamics.

Nadella suggested that companies attempting to protect existing business models might lose ground to competitors willing to embrace AI agents. For instance, if Amazon continues blocking agents, alternative shopping platforms like Shopify could permit them and gain a competitive advantage. As AI agents become more capable of navigating the web and executing transactions for users, digital platforms are forced to rethink their access policies. Nadella concluded that businesses failing to accept the agentic shift risk losing market share to those that integrate the technology.
