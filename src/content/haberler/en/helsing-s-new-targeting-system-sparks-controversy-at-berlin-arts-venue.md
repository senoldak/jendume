The German defense technology startup Helsing unveiled its new AI-powered targeting platform, "Clarity," at the Kraftwerk Berlin arts and events complex. The event sparked outrage due to the transformation of the venue into a mock "war room," complete with aerial footage projections, drones, and security guards in tactical gear. Berlin’s arts and nightlife community condemned the display, and the venue management later apologized for what they termed a "serious error of judgment."

The incident reflects the tension between Europe’s strategic push to develop sovereign defense technologies and its cultural values. Helsing describes Clarity as an agentic intelligence platform designed to utilize drone, satellite, and sensor data to identify and lock onto targets such as radar systems, launch pads, and ships. While the company maintains that human operators remain responsible for approving any strikes, the system's ability to analyze "patterns of behavior" has fueled ongoing ethical debates regarding the automation of warfare.

European governments are seeking alternatives to US-developed military software, positioning Helsing as a homegrown solution built under European control. However, in a city like Berlin, where nightlife has historically served as a space for anti-militaristic expression, the commercialization of such venues for military-tech events faces strong resistance. The venue management has vowed to revise its commercial rental policies in response to the backlash.

Founded in 2021, the Munich-based company counts Spotify co-founder Daniel Ek among its investors. The operating company behind the venue, Schaltraum Berlin, reported total assets of €4.3 million as of June. The controversy shows that integrating artificial intelligence into military operations presents technical challenges and societal issues, forcing a re-evaluation of ethical boundaries in public spaces.
