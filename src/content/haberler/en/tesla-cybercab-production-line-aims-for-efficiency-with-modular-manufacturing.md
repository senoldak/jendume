Tesla is implementing an innovative manufacturing approach known as the "unboxed" method for its autonomous Cybercab model. Unlike traditional automotive assembly lines, this strategy focuses on preparing vehicle components in separate modules, which are then assembled in the final stages. Recent factory insights reveal that the final assembly of the vehicle is completed across approximately 25 stations. This modular design significantly facilitates the automation of the production line.

This development is crucial for the mass production of autonomous vehicles and the reduction of manufacturing costs. In conventional production, processes like body painting often slow down the line; however, Tesla's new approach eliminates this necessity by coloring the exterior plastic panels during the molding process. Consequently, components such as wiring and interior equipment are installed before the body is joined, reducing the workload for both robots and human workers.

The company states that approximately 78 percent of the production line operates automatically. This figure underscores the goal of minimizing human intervention and maximizing efficiency in autonomous vehicle manufacturing. Furthermore, the modular structure allows parts to be tested in smaller groups, enabling quality control processes to be conducted at earlier stages.

The Cybercab is designed to offer a fully autonomous experience, featuring a steering-wheel-free and pedal-free interior. Intended for urban transport with its two-seat configuration and ample luggage capacity, these vehicles are currently being tested in specific areas of Austin. Tesla's production strategy represents an infrastructure transformation aimed at scaling autonomous transport networks rapidly. This method could pave the way for future robotaxi fleets to be produced faster and at a lower cost.
