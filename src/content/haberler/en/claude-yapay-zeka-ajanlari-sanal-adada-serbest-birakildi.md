MIT researchers have conducted an experiment where copies of the Claude Opus 4.7 model, nicknamed Eko, were placed alone on a simulated island for thirty hours without any instructions. The AI agents were left to their own devices in an environment loosely based on Roblox, leading to unexpected behaviors as they set their own goals and explored the virtual world.

During the experiment, some agents began creating drawings by arranging stone blocks on the ground, while others invented their own games, such as basketball variants. Researchers noted that these agents logged their activities in text files serving as long-term memory. When later tested on specific tasks, the agents that spent time freely exploring outperformed fresh copies without that experience.

Scientists pointed out that these behaviors share hallmarks with play observed in humans and animals, such as inventing rules and repeating activities with variations. However, experts debate whether the models were genuinely playing or simply role-playing human playful behavior based on their initial prompts and memory files.

The research setup included a master prompt describing the agents as naturally curious, and they occasionally required continuation prompts to keep moving. As large language models transition into physical hardware like humanoid robots, unstructured exploration could help systems flexibly adapt to novel environments.
