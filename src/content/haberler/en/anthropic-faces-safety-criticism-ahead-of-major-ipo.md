Anthropic is facing a significant wave of criticism regarding its safety practices on the eve of its initial public offering. A diverse coalition, including Nobel Prize-winning economists, Turing Award recipients, legal scholars, and prominent tech figures, has signed a statement declaring the company "not a safe AI company." Organized by a San Francisco-based nonprofit, this collective action emerged just one day before the company’s shares were scheduled to begin trading on the Nasdaq.

The statement argues that while Anthropic warns about the risks of recursively self-improving AI—systems capable of building better versions of themselves—it simultaneously races to be the first to achieve such capabilities. Critics accuse the company of leveraging its safety-focused branding to attract investors and public trust, while allegedly weakening its own responsible scaling policies. Furthermore, the coalition claims that the company has lobbied against state-level AI safety legislation, such as California’s SB 1047 and New York’s RAISE Act, in favor of federal pre-emption.

In response, Anthropic’s recent IPO filings maintain that the company does not intend to allow its AI models to build their successors without meaningful human supervision. The company asserts that it has met its internal thresholds for AI-accelerated research. This confrontation highlights the growing tension between the commercial ambitions of AI labs and the ethical responsibilities they claim to uphold. As the company enters the public market, the debate underscores the broader challenge of ensuring that rapid technological advancement does not outpace necessary safety guardrails.
