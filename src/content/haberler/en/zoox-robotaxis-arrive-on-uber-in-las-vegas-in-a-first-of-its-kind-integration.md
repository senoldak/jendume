Amazon-backed autonomous vehicle developer Zoox has launched its steering-free and pedal-free robotaxis on another company's platform for the first time. Starting Monday in Las Vegas, riders requesting standard rides through the Uber application may be matched with a Zoox vehicle at no extra cost. This marks the initial appearance of a purpose-built, steering wheel-free vehicle on the Uber network.

According to the joint announcement, the Zoox fleet operates around the Las Vegas Strip, Downtown, and the Arts District, serving various resorts and venues. Zoox will continue offering rides through its proprietary application while starting with a small fleet on Uber, with plans to expand operations over time. Additionally, Zoox and Uber signed a multiyear agreement with MGM Resorts International to streamline pickup and drop-off logistics across local properties.

Las Vegas serves as Zoox's primary commercial market, where the company initiated paid rides in August. While Uber already facilitates autonomous rides in the city through Hyundai-backed Motional—which utilizes safety drivers—Zoox operates fully driverless vehicles. Nevada regulators previously approved permits allowing up to 1,000 robotaxis to operate in Clark County.

Looking ahead, Zoox plans to introduce initial operations in Los Angeles by the end of the year, followed by a joint launch with Uber in 2027. The company is also testing vehicles in secondary markets such as Atlanta, as autonomous fleet deployments continue to scale across major urban centers.
