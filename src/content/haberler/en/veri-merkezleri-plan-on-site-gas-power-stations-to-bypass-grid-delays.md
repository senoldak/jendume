Developers of artificial intelligence datacentres are planning to build on-site fossil fuel power stations on a massive scale to meet surging energy demands, according to research. Datacentres planned across the United States, the United Kingdom, and mainland Europe have put forward proposals to build on-site gas-fired capacity totaling 181 gigawatts. This figure is nearly four times the total peak electricity demand of Great Britain.

Research indicates that this push for speed to power could emit about 345 million metric tonnes of carbon dioxide equivalent annually in the US alone, where 90% of these on-site gas power stations are planned. Experts noted that the extraordinary economics around artificial intelligence make speed to power a primary constraint for developers.

While developers claim these gas supplies will be temporary while waiting for national grid connections, analysts warn that once online, economic incentives could lock in long-term fossil fuel dependence. In the UK, planned datacentres made more than 100 inquiries to connect to gas networks due to long waiting times for electricity grid access, with some projects being fast-tracked through national infrastructure regimes.

Experts emphasize that ensuring new datacentres connect directly to clean electricity grids remains the more sustainable option to limit environmental impacts and prevent long-term carbon lock-in.
