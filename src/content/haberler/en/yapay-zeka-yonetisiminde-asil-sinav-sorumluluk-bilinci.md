While artificial intelligence integration accelerates in the corporate world, recent analyses reveal that companies focusing on technological investments neglect proper governance processes. Many businesses treat this technology as a standard information technology project, and a vast majority have yet to adapt their job descriptions to the new era. Experts state that current bottlenecks stem not from a lack of technical infrastructure, but from a failure to clarify who assumes ownership of risks and potential errors.

The fact that artificial intelligence systems can occasionally produce erroneous or misleading results brings along serious legal and financial risks. The presence of error margins in research tools makes it essential to establish robust auditing mechanisms to verify outputs before implementation. Leading corporate executives emphasize that this technology should not be viewed merely as an IT issue, but rather handled under direct business accountability.

Autonomous systems, which will become even more prevalent, require monitoring every step of the workflow rather than just final results. This reality transforms artificial intelligence management from a single department's duty into a collaborative structure involving legal, compliance, and operational units. It is crucial for companies to design new operational models centered on human intervention and oversight to build lasting trust.
