New York City mayor Zohran Mamdani has strongly criticized Google's decision to block search engine access for schools that restrict its artificial intelligence features. In a social media statement, Mamdani stated that the corporation is attempting to use its monopoly power to force artificial intelligence into public schools. He emphasized that no corporation should possess the authority to dictate how children are taught, adding that public schools exist to serve students rather than to expand the profit margins of large technology firms.

Google previously announced it would turn off artificial intelligence features in student software unless administrators specifically chose to activate them. However, the company attached a condition: if educational institutions decide to restrict all artificial intelligence tools, the company will revoke their connection to Google Search. Google vice president for education Chris Phillips suggested that search capabilities and artificial intelligence features are deeply integrated, framing the issue as a technical reality rather than a deliberate policy choice.

Educators and parents have expressed growing concern that automated summaries and chat interfaces reduce students' incentives to develop critical thinking and independent research skills. Many school districts remain wary of introducing technologies associated with effortless cheating into classrooms. Mamdani previously introduced a one-year moratorium on New York City schools using artificial intelligence tools in classrooms, describing it as the most comprehensive policy of its kind.

Given Google's dominant position as the primary gateway to the internet, critics view this ultimatum as an aggressive maneuver. A federal judge ruled in 2024 that the company maintained an illegal monopoly over the search engine market, leading to concerns that a similar approach is currently being applied to educational institutions.
