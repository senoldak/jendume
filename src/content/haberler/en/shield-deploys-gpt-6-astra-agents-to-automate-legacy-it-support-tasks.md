Shield, the developer of the Forge AI operating system for IT service providers, has announced the integration of OpenAI's GPT-6 Astra model. The platform leverages this new model to enable autonomous agents to log into and navigate legacy web portals that lack native API access, completing support tasks end to end.

Managed IT service providers supporting small and medium-sized businesses frequently handle critical issues ranging from locked computer accounts to misconfigured firewalls. Speed is paramount in these scenarios to prevent extended operational downtime. However, technicians spend much of their day managing legacy portals for ticketing, software licenses, and device settings. Because these older systems lack APIs, previous AI workflows often stalled, forcing human technicians to complete tasks manually.

With GPT-6 Astra, Forge's computer-use agents read screens, click buttons, and type text to operate software interfaces much like a human technician. The AI Engineer within Forge assigns tasks to these agents, while human technicians maintain complete control by deciding which actions can be performed independently and which require manual review or approval.

Shield reports that these automated browser workflows are currently live and processing thousands of executions each month across its network of IT providers. The adoption of GPT-6 Astra has yielded an average speed increase of 35% to 40% compared to earlier computer-use models, alongside higher customer satisfaction and fewer task timeouts.

Tasks involving phone calls, remote desktop intervention, or physical on-site work still require human technicians. Moving forward, Shield is actively testing remote-desktop workflows to allow agents to execute more complex actions directly on a computer's desktop environment.
