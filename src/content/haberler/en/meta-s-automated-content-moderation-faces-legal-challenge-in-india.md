India’s main opposition Congress party has filed a lawsuit against Meta and the federal government in the Delhi High Court. The legal action, initiated by the party's youth wing, centers on allegations that Meta’s automated content-removal system is unfairly blocking political posts and images of opposition leader Rahul Gandhi. The system in question was mandated by an amendment to India’s IT Rules, which came into effect in February, requiring large platforms to use automated tools to remove copies of previously blocked content.

However, the Congress party argues that the system is over-reaching, flagging benign content such as birthday greetings, tributes, and wedding invitations simply because they contain Gandhi’s name or photograph. This raises concerns about the ability of AI-based moderation tools to understand context and the potential for these systems to stifle political discourse. Meta executives have stated that their moderation practices in India comply with local laws and that the takedowns are related to AI-generated content.

The volume of content restriction has risen under the new mandate. According to Meta, the company restricted more than 5.4 million posts in India during the first half of 2026. This is a sharp increase compared to the approximately 70,000 posts restricted throughout the entirety of 2025, illustrating the massive expansion of AI-driven moderation. The lawsuit seeks to have the automated-takedown mandate struck down, demands the restoration of blocked posts, and calls for Meta to disclose its blocking criteria.

This dispute relates to global debates regarding the impact of AI algorithms on democratic processes. With similar moderation systems already under scrutiny by regulators in the UK, the case in India tests how tech companies balance legal compliance with the protection of free speech. The outcome of the hearings is expected to influence how AI is used in content governance and its effect on political expression.
