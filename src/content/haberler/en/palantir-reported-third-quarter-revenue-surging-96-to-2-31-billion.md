Palantir released its third-quarter earnings report, announcing that its revenue grew 96% year over year to reach $2.31 billion. This marks the first time the company has ever surpassed the $2 billion threshold, beating analysts' expectations of $2.18 billion. Adjusted earnings per share came in at $0.44, also topping the anticipated $0.41. The primary driver behind this surge is strong enterprise demand for the company's AI software, which allows organizations to run frontier models securely without sharing data with outside labs. For the first time ever, Palantir's U.S. commercial revenue surpassed U.S. government revenue, hitting $984 million compared to $953 million from government contracts. Despite these record numbers, leadership noted a shifting trend in enterprise sales. While corporations are moving faster than ever to test AI software, they are increasingly signing shorter-term trials. Consequently, total contract value from U.S. enterprises dropped to $1.46 billion in the quarter, down from a record $2.13 billion in the previous period. CEO Alex Karp noted that corporations are willing to quickly try AI software with less bureaucracy, even if it means shorter initial contracts that can later be expanded. Bolstered by strong demand, Palantir raised its full-year guidance, projecting U.S. commercial revenue growth of at least 143% to exceed $3.559 billion.
