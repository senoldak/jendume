The massive financial growth achieved by artificial intelligence companies is heralding a new era in American philanthropy. Anthropic, the maker of the Claude chatbot, announced it would commit 5% of its shares to a new public benefit fund. If the enterprise attains the high market capitalization investors anticipate ahead of a stock market debut, this equity allocation might reach an estimated value of 100 billion dollars. Combined with its co-founders' pledges to give away 80% of their equity, roughly a fifth of Anthropic's shares will be dedicated to public benefit purposes.

Experts describe this development as a major philanthropic milestone comparable to the expansion seen during historical periods of rapid economic growth. Historically, figures like Andrew Carnegie and John D. Rockefeller channeled their fortunes into education, medicine, and public projects through trusts and foundations. AI entrepreneurs are preparing to generate resources on a similar scale, with the OpenAI Foundation also holding a significant stake in its respective company.

Anthropic has chosen to structure its fund as a wholly owned limited liability company rather than a traditional nonprofit foundation. While this trade-off keeps decision-making within the enterprise, it also sparks academic debate regarding transparency. Experts emphasize that the fund's management decisions and supported projects must be monitored closely.

Reports suggest the initiative might distribute financial support directly to communities impacted by technological shifts in the labor market. Meanwhile, industry figures like Elon Musk have criticized this approach, arguing for shares to be distributed directly to the public instead. The societal implications of this new wealth wave and its reshaping of philanthropy are being closely watched.
