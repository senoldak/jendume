Tesla announced through its vehicle engineering executive Lars Moravy that it aims to deliver the Cybercab model to customers before the end of 2026. The company intends to start on a small scale to gain experience in delivery, ownership, and fleet management. This development brings the timeline for driverless cars reaching individual customers back into focus while highlighting the fundamental questions autonomous transport faces as it leaves the laboratory environment. The absence of a steering wheel and pedals eliminates the passenger's ability to intervene in driving, making safety standards and regulatory reviews a primary focal point.

Following the commercial deployment of vehicles in Austin, regulatory authorities initiated reviews regarding safety compliance statements. In the regulatory framework where manufacturers self-certify compliance with federal safety standards, agencies retain auditing authority. Deploying the vehicle does not indicate prior comprehensive approval by the agency, nor does initiating an inspection constitute a violation ruling. For prospective robotaxi owners, this creates a regulatory landscape distinct from traditional car purchases.

The conditions and regions where driverless cars can operate are defined by operational design domains. Road types, speed limits, and environmental conditions affect system performance. Other autonomous developers also address the risks of specific operational areas in their safety assessments. Published data indicates millions of miles driven without a human driver, alongside reported reductions in injury crashes. However, these figures do not apply universally to all regions or different manufacturers.

The lack of a driver inside autonomous vehicles does not mean human support is absent in the background. Advanced systems can request additional information in complex traffic situations or rely on roadside assistance teams when needed. This demonstrates that autonomous transportation systems evolve based not only on software capabilities but also on field teams and safety guidelines established by regulatory bodies.
