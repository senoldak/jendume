Political parties in Australia are moving toward a consensus on artificial intelligence adoption, even as community trust in the technology continues to decline. Experts note that only thirty percent of the population believes the risks of artificial intelligence outweigh its benefits. Public concern focuses heavily on how new technologies and data centres will shape the economy, environment, and society for generations to come. The formation of a joint select committee on artificial intelligence signals that current decisions regarding datacentres and AI adoption are critically important.

Workplace impacts represent a pressing issue for employees and families. Reports indicate that AI-enabled worker surveillance is rising rapidly across various sectors. Remote workers are subjected to intrusive monitoring software that tracks screen activity and records audio from conversations with family members. Rideshare drivers face penalties and account deactivations due to road conditions and customer complaints unrelated to their actual performance.

Existing labor laws fail to protect workers from unreasonable surveillance or provide employers with clear legal boundaries regarding lawful monitoring. Experts urge the government to update the Fair Work Act to ensure that any employee monitoring is fair and reasonable, addressing real-world harms that occur in modern workplaces rather than focusing solely on hypothetical existential risks.

The debate highlights the tension between embracing technological innovation and safeguarding worker rights. Clear legislative reforms are seen as essential to protect individuals from invasive monitoring practices. Australian workers expect the government to take action and implement straightforward legal reforms to protect them from workplace AI harms.
