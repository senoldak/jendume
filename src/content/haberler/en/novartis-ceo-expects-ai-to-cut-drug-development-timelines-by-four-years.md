Novartis CEO Vas Narasimhan has offered a more cautious perspective on the role of artificial intelligence in the healthcare sector. Challenging the optimistic forecasts of leaders like Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei and DeepMind founder Demis Hassabis, who have suggested that AI could cure major chronic diseases such as cancer, Alzheimer's, and heart disease within a decade, Narasimhan points to the inherent complexity of human biology.

The drug development process is inherently a long and costly journey. Narasimhan argues that the realistic impact of AI in this field is about optimizing existing workflows. Noting that our limited understanding of human biology necessitates the continuation of animal and human trials, he predicts that AI will provide significant efficiency gains primarily in the early phases before clinical trials begin.

According to Novartis's projections, AI-powered technologies could reduce the average drug development timeline from 12 years to eight years. This four-year reduction represents a time saving, particularly in the pre-clinical phases. Furthermore, success rates in clinical development are expected to rise from 8 percent to 12 percent. While these percentages may seem modest, they represent billions of dollars in savings for the pharmaceutical industry and faster access to treatments for patients.

This industry excitement is driving substantial investments. Anthropic has entered the space directly by acquiring biotech startup Coefficient Bio and building its own biology lab. Meanwhile, DeepMind's drug discovery spinoff, Isomorphic, recently raised $5 billion in funding and is collaborating with major pharmaceutical companies, including Novartis. Narasimhan believes that the true impact of AI on the pharmaceutical industry will settle "somewhere in the middle" between the ambitious forecasts of tech leaders and the skeptical stance of the traditional pharma industry. This balance will be a key factor in determining the pace of development for future health technologies.
