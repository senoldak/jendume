In a milestone test in the United Kingdom, an AI agent has successfully completed the country's first cardless purchase for a real customer. The order of nine mini margherita pizzas from Papa John's was placed without sharing any bank account numbers or passwords. The transaction was conducted as part of the Financial Conduct Authority's regulatory sandbox trials, utilizing Lloyds Bank and payment specialist TrueLayer.

London-based TrueLayer stated that this marked the first time an AI agent was used to pay a UK retailer directly from a bank account without cards. TrueLayer CEO Francesco Simoneschi explained that the transaction was completed under the highest security profile by simply communicating dietary requirements and occasions with the agent, which then suggested options and placed the order.

The Papa John's AI agent operated under a strict mandate established with Lloyds, enforcing maximum spending limits per transaction and month while keeping security information entirely private. Simoneschi noted that this test represents the first step toward mass public adoption of a technology that will reshape purchasing habits, potentially transforming how consumers shop and manage finances in the near future.

While praising the convenience of AI shopping assistants, Simoneschi emphasized that personal judgment, supervision, and rigorous security standards remain essential. Existing consumer protection rules will continue to safeguard buyers in case of any ordering errors. Meanwhile, the Financial Conduct Authority has recently warned financial firms developing AI agents that they must strictly comply with established regulatory frameworks.

In related developments, Meta recently launched its personal AI assistant, Muse, in the UK, which also enables online purchases, though utilizing traditional customer bank cards rather than cardless account-to-account transfers.
