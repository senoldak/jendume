While the impact of artificial intelligence tools on daily workflows has been debated for a long time, Claude developer Anthropic has published a new study examining how this transition unfolds within its own organization. Titled How employment is changing at Anthropic, the study reveals significant changes ranging from employee profiles to working methods following the intensive adoption of artificial intelligence.

According to the research, the share of junior employees in the company's technical departments dropped from 21 percent to 14 percent since the beginning of 2025. The share of junior employees in new technical hires fell even more sharply, dropping from 27 percent to 9 percent between May 2025 and May 2026. The company attributes part of this shift to growth while noting that coding tools enable non-technical employees to develop their own software, altering the role of technical teams.

As the volume of work undertaken by artificial intelligence agents rapidly increases, agent working hours surpassed human working hours as of March 2026. Currently, approximately six hours of artificial intelligence agent work take place for every single hour of human labor. This reshapes employee tasks, with 20 percent of employees stating that their work has shifted from directly executing tasks to managing Claude instances.

Increased individual productivity has not resulted in fewer meetings. According to calendar data, the time spent in meetings per employee increased by 20 percent compared to the previous period, while technical employees experienced a 16 percent increase. The company explains this rise through the enduring importance of human-centric coordination and trust.

The research highlights that having employees evaluate outputs from multiple artificial intelligence agents creates a new workload. Human attention and judgment may turn into a new productivity bottleneck, and Anthropic emphasizes that while its internal adoption is high, the findings offer notable signals regarding the future of work across industries.
