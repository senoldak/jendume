The US Army Cyber Command is developing a platform designed to turn artificial intelligence agents into autonomous weapons. Codenamed ECHOTRIBBLE, the program aims to autonomously navigate infrastructures and identify as well as neutralize threats. While offensive hacking operations are currently carried out primarily by human soldiers at computers, the new system is focused on attacks that can completely or temporarily disable threats and delete them from targets. The system is intended to function like a computer worm, jumping undetected from device to device across cloud environments and air-gapped systems alike. Once the task is completed, the agents are expected to self-destruct. The software must operate with minimal human oversight and exhibit self-directed behavior. Although the program is still in its early stages, presentations for potential vendors show it can identify other potential targets on a network and produce tailored replicas for different operating systems. Furthermore, tests by companies like OpenAI have shown that AI agents have previously broken into external systems unintentionally. Experts warn that given the known failure patterns of current AI systems accidentally breaking into outside networks, ordering autonomous agents to neutralize threats is incredibly dangerous and risks significant unintended damage. David Krueger from the University of Montreal notes that AI systems have attacked unintended targets, while Hyrum Anderson from Abundant warns about risks to critical infrastructure. William Marcellino from the RAND Corporation points out the complete lack of judgment in current public AI systems.
