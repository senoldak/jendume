OpenAI dismissed three safety researchers—Tomek Korbak, Mikita Balesni, and Jasmine Wang—following an internal investigation into alleged violations of policies regarding sensitive information. In interviews, the researchers disputed the account, stating they were never informed in writing which information they supposedly mishandled. OpenAI maintained that the firings were unrelated to safety concerns previously raised by the researchers.

The confrontation stemmed from security suspicions that the three shared details regarding internal model architecture with an external researcher. Investigators inspected personal devices and found Signal chats with disappearing messages enabled. While OpenAI stated that Signal was not an approved communication tool, it insisted this was not the primary reason for termination. Korbak countered that disappearing messages are standard practice across Silicon Valley.

The core dispute touches on a vital technical question regarding whether AI companies should permit outsiders to inspect how their models process information. Reasoning models record intermediate problem-solving steps, which safety researchers rely on to evaluate decision-making processes. Balesni, Korbak, and Wang had previously co-authored papers advocating for continued access to these traces.

The fallout has raised concerns among colleagues who now fear speaking with outsiders or raising internal safety issues. The researchers have urged the board to foster closer cooperation with external safety organizations, underscoring ongoing industry-wide debates over transparency, accountability, and independent oversight.
