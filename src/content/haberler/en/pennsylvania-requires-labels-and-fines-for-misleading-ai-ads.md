Advertisers in Pennsylvania utilizing artificial intelligence to create or modify content must now comply with strict labelling requirements. Governor Josh Shapiro signed the Artificial Intelligence Deceptive Advertising Act, establishing new rules designed to prevent consumers from being misled about goods and services. The legislation covers synthetic content, including images, videos, audio clips, and text generated or significantly modified by AI systems.

Under the new law, advertisers are prohibited from running such content without a clear and conspicuous disclosure if it creates a false impression regarding the nature, origin, or authenticity of products. The disclosure must explicitly state that the advertisement contains content modified or generated using artificial intelligence. Furthermore, the warning must appear during the consumers' first encounter with the material and remain permanent or difficult to remove where technically feasible.

The legislation details specific formatting standards for disclosures across different mediums. Audio warnings must be clear and paced appropriately, while on-screen text must remain visible long enough to be read. Exemptions are granted for evidently fictional content, parody, satire, commentary, and certain text-limited materials. State and district attorneys are empowered to seek civil actions and court injunctions against violators, carrying civil penalties of up to three thousand dollars alongside legal costs.

The bill was originally introduced by state senator Nick Pisciottano, subsequently passing through the state senate and house with substantial bipartisan support. This state-level action aligns with broader global efforts to regulate artificial intelligence transparency, mirroring watermarking and detection initiatives undertaken by major technology developers and international institutions.
