Geoffrey Hinton, one of the acknowledged godfathers of artificial intelligence and a Nobel laureate, has stated that humanity might have been better off never developing the technology. Speaking ahead of a documentary screening, Hinton warned that AI could trigger a large-scale incident, such as a temporary blackout of financial or power systems, as early as this year or next. While acknowledging AI's potential for a wonderful future, he emphasized the severe risks involved.

Hinton criticized the risk-taking behaviors of leaders at major AI companies, including OpenAI CEO Sam Altman, Meta's Mark Zuckerberg, and Elon Musk. He stated his belief that these leaders understand the risks but prioritize money and market supremacy. The computer scientist expressed deep concern that Altman leads one of the frontier US AI companies racing against China to develop the most powerful systems.

Comparing recent AI agent misbehaviors to the 1979 Three Mile Island nuclear accident, Hinton suggested a major incident could occur soon to wake people up. He clarified that this would not be a nuclear disaster but rather a massive brownout caused by an AI-enabled attack on electricity generation or an incident that halts the financial system for a week. To mitigate these risks, Hinton endorsed tougher plans for mandatory testing and independent evaluation by an authority akin to the US FDA.

Despite his stark warnings, Hinton remains hopeful that catastrophe can be averted if a massive effort is put into safety measures. Having spent decades researching neural networks and leaving Google in 2023 to speak freely about risks, he continues to advocate for intergovernmental common standards.
