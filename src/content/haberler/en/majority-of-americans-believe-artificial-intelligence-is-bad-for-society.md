A comprehensive new poll has revealed a growing tide of anti-AI sentiment among the American public. According to the survey results, 57 percent of Americans now believe that artificial intelligence is outright bad for society, while only 42 percent view the technology as positive. The findings come amid rising concerns over cybersecurity threats, critical infrastructure risks, and the future of employment in an increasingly automated world.

The poll highlights profound public anxiety regarding the security implications of advanced AI models. A striking 73 percent of respondents stated that there is a high or very high risk of AI being used to hack secure computer systems. Meanwhile, longstanding fears surrounding job displacement remain prominent, with 65 percent of Americans viewing AI as a high risk for eliminating numerous jobs. Conversely, only 34 percent believe there is a strong chance that AI will make everyday tasks easier, and a meager 13 percent think it will create a significant number of new jobs.

Demographic and political divides also shape public perception. Approximately 65 percent of Americans under the age of 30 view AI negatively, compared to 53 percent of those over 40. Politically, 63 percent of Democrats and 58 percent of independents hold pessimistic views regarding the societal impact of AI, whereas 51 percent of Republicans share the same sentiment.

In addition to software and job concerns, the physical footprint of artificial intelligence faces fierce local opposition. A sweeping 66 percent of Americans oppose the construction of new data centers in the United States, with opposition figures rising even higher when residents face the prospect of a facility being built directly within their own communities. These widespread reservations underscore the mounting challenges AI developers face in securing public trust and acceptance.
