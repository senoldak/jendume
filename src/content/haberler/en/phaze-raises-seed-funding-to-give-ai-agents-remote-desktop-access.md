Phaze, a startup established by the creators of remote desktop software Parsec, has secured an $8.75M seed round led by a16z. Additional financial backing for the round came from Indie, NextView, Makers Fund, and Lerer Hippeau. The Utah-based enterprise provides a platform enabling individuals and artificial intelligence agents to access powerful computing resources from any location, allowing designers and engineers to execute resource-heavy 3D software on remote workstations while autonomous agents manage operating system displays and applications. The primary objective is to eliminate redundant hardware expenditures by centralizing workstations into shared resource pools that can be dynamically assigned to personnel or agents as required.

Autonomous agents connect through a common integration standard designed for linking machine learning models to external tools, though the enterprise continues to classify this integration layer as experimental. In a demonstrated workflow, a communication platform assistant processes technical support inquiries, accesses the designated workstation environment, and escalates to a human operator when encountering operational blocks. To ensure system security, agents are strictly restricted to the permission levels assigned to their human operators. Chief executive Benjy Boxer emphasized that the physical hardware stationed on a desk should not dictate the computational capacity available to a user.

The broader technology landscape features multiple entities developing specialized execution environments for autonomous systems. Baseten previously acquired Blaxel, an organization building isolated execution sandboxes for digital agents, while Perplexity introduced configurations to convert desktop hardware into continuous agent systems. The rising adoption of autonomous workflows has generated measurable market demand. Meanwhile, Boxer's previous venture, Parsec, was acquired by Unity.
