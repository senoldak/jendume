Japan-based TDK has announced plans to invest $635 million to double its output of high-end magnetic sensors, known as tunnel magnetoresistance (TMR) sensors, by 2031. These precise and low-power sensors are increasingly in demand across automobiles, humanoid robots, and AI data centers.

Takao Tsutsui, head of TDK's sensor unit, stated that current demand is already stretching manufacturing capacity and that the company could invest further if growth accelerates. Tsutsui noted that the industry has passed a tipping point and significant demand lies ahead. According to company estimates, TDK currently supplies about 80% of the world's TMR sensors, often acting as the sole supplier for its customers.

The increased output will originate from a new facility in Niigata prefecture, which is scheduled to open in 2029. Customers requested production outside of TDK's existing Nagano plant as demand surged. Each humanoid robot utilizes dozens of TMR sensors, while data centers rely on them to monitor liquid-cooling systems and regulate power flows amid changing computing loads.

Historically famous for cassette tapes, TDK largely missed out on the initial AI boom. With this major manufacturing expansion, the company aims to strengthen its position in the magnetic sensor market where it faces competition from rivals such as Germany's Infineon and Bosch.
