NVIDIA introduced the DSX POD modular AI infrastructure design at the GTC event in Berlin. Based on the Vera Rubin platform, the solution integrates computing systems and components inside factory-assembled containers. According to the company, deployment times at sites with pre-prepared power and cooling infrastructure can drop from six months down to 14 days. This approach shifts a major portion of on-site work to the manufacturing facility, allowing parallel progress.

Partners place servers, establish connections between cabinets, prepare electrical wiring and liquid cooling pipes, and test systems before shipment. The modules delivered to customers need to connect to ready power, cooling, and network infrastructures. Focusing initially on large-scale, newly established, and liquid-cooled AI infrastructures, DSX POD is not offered as a one-size-fits-all box for every existing data center. The 14-day duration is not a delivery guarantee for all projects, but the shortest commissioning target declared by NVIDIA when site preparation is completed in advance.

The technical design addresses networking, storage, management systems, power distribution, and cooling together rather than just processor and server placement. A standard 800 V direct current power input is defined at the container boundary, while prior power conversion is arranged by partners. Working with partners including Dell Technologies, HPE, Lenovo, Schneider Electric, and Vertiv, NVIDIA plans to deliver DSX POD-based systems globally starting in early 2027.
