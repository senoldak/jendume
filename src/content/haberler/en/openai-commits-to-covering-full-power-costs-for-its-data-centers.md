OpenAI has pledged to cover the full cost of powering the data centers it owns and operates. This commitment follows a series of local elections in the United States where voters expressed significant opposition to the rapid expansion of data center facilities. Alongside the power cost commitment, the company announced it will solicit community input for new projects, publish construction plans, and avoid using non-disclosure agreements with public officials. This policy blueprint represents an effort to redefine how tech companies engage with local governments and residents.

Data centers are the backbone of modern AI, essential for training models and serving users. However, their massive energy demands have sparked concerns among residents about rising utility bills and environmental impacts. Recent election data revealed that in 41 local ballot measures across 10 states, voters largely rejected or sought to restrict new data center developments. This trend highlights the friction between the rapid scaling of AI infrastructure and the concerns of local communities.

The company has previously set a target of $600 billion in total compute spending by 2030, with major projects already announced in Georgia and Ohio. Company representatives noted that these commitments are part of a policy blueprint developed since August, aimed at fostering better cooperation with local authorities in the upcoming legislative session. This approach is viewed as a concrete step toward aligning the physical foundations of the AI era with public expectations and community needs.
