Apple has strengthened its artificial intelligence capabilities in music and audio production by acquiring the Zurich-based startup Samplab. According to official notifications shared by the European Commission, the agreement took place in June, leading Samplab to cease its operations on September 17, 2026, and redirect its website to Apple's official page. During this transition, existing subscribers received refunds as the startup officially wound down its independent services.

Founded in 2020, Samplab specialized in AI-driven plugins for digital audio workstations. The startup's technology enabled users to separate vocals and instruments from mixed recordings, convert audio files into MIDI data, and edit individual notes within polyphonic recordings without compromising audio quality. These advanced features provided significant convenience for musicians and audio engineers in digital music production.

Under European Union Digital Markets Act regulations, regulatory filings revealed that Apple acquired specific assets from Samplab and brought several company employees on board. These transfer procedures carried out within the framework of regulatory requirements highlighted the tech giant's ongoing strategy to enhance its audio technology capabilities.

While Apple's Logic Pro software already includes stem-splitting and chord identification features, Samplab's advanced polyphonic note-editing technology is expected to bring innovative capabilities to Apple's suite of audio software, including Logic Pro and GarageBand. This integration will provide millions of content creators and musicians with much more advanced audio editing tools directly through professional software suites.
