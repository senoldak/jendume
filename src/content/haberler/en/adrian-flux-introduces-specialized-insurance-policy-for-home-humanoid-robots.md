UK-based insurance provider Adrian Flux, in collaboration with Texas-based Tokio Marine HCC, has created a specialized insurance policy for domestic humanoid robots. Expected to enter British homes starting in twenty twenty-seven, these next-generation robots are already being secured against potential safety risks. The newly developed policy covers physical damages caused by devices, theft incidents, and liabilities toward third parties. Positive evaluations by major technology companies regarding the future of in-home robots are accelerating the insurance sector's preparations for this field.

Humanoid robots, which are starting to be used in many areas ranging from industrial facilities to showrooms, are also preparing to enter home life. Leading figures in the sector predict that these smart assistants will become an indispensable element of households. The first examples offered for sale in the US market at a cost of around twenty thousand dollars aim to support individuals' daily lives. However, the complex nature of the systems and potential software errors bring concerns that they may lead to unintended accidents in the physical world.

The comprehensive coverage package introduced by Adrian Flux aims to keep unforeseen situations that may arise during the use of robots under control. For instance, situations such as a robot damaging household goods or causing personal injuries during an accident are evaluated within the scope of the policy. Additionally, processes such as safely shutting down the device during a technical malfunction or intervention by expert teams are included in the service. Company officials state that this practice is designed with an approach similar to the insurance system previously offered for autonomous vehicles and will be updated according to technological advancements.

Not only residences but also production and commercial areas are growing under the influence of humanoid robots. Various automotive manufacturers evaluate robots in sales processes, while different projects aim to increase industrial efficiency. This situation demonstrates that robot technologies have established a permanent place in the commercial field and have laid the groundwork for the insurance sector to establish risk management infrastructure early.
