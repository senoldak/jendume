Google Meet is bringing its tile pairing feature, which keeps a sign language interpreter and a speaker in side-by-side video boxes, to mobile devices. Previously limited to the web version, this function helps users maintain visual connection during meetings. The Android rollout has already begun, while the iOS release is scheduled for November 2.

With this feature, users can pair their video tile with another participant, allowing everyone in the meeting to see the two feeds linked together. This arrangement keeps the interpreter or co-presenter visible regardless of who is currently speaking. For deaf or hard-of-hearing participants, ensuring that the interpreter does not get lost outside the active speaker view is crucial.

The system does not provide automatic translation services or add an interpreter on its own; rather, it makes it easier to follow an existing interpreter or shared presentation. Checking the display briefly before the meeting starts helps ensure that the interpreter appears large enough on smaller screens, especially when switching between landscape and portrait modes. Organization administrators can disable this feature at the unit level, and users must activate the pairing within the meeting itself.

The phased rollout for Android devices began on October 12, while the iOS rollout starts on November 2. The update covers personal Google accounts, Workspace Individual users, and all Workspace customers.
