Specialists at Mass General Brigham, an integrated academic healthcare system located in Massachusetts, have created a novel artificial intelligence system capable of predicting a cancer patient's survival prospects using a single facial photograph. According to findings shared in a scientific publication, the system estimates an individual's biological age and forecasts survival outcomes for patients diagnosed with cancer. Building upon this technology, researchers established two complementary tools named FAHR-FaceAge and FAHR-FaceSurvival. These instruments evaluate distinct facial zones to generate forecasts regarding age and mortality risk. The underlying model underwent training utilizing data compiled from more than 40 million facial images spanning various ethnic backgrounds and age brackets. Research outcomes demonstrated that among cancer patients receiving radiotherapy, individuals appearing five or more years older than their chronological age exhibited a 21 percent higher mortality risk during the average four-year follow-up period. Conversely, patients looking five or more years younger displayed a 17 percent lower risk. Specialists noted that the intelligence system also identified correlations between lifestyle choices such as smoking, heavy alcohol consumption, and substance use with accelerated biological aging. Researchers emphasized that the tool could assist clinicians in gauging physical reserve before recommending demanding interventions like radiation or surgery, while noting that additional research remains essential prior to widespread clinical adoption.
