Blackstone Digital Infrastructure Trust (BXDC) has acquired a 75% stake in two joint ventures with Amazon Web Services (AWS) for $5 billion, marking a significant move in the technology infrastructure market. This deal represents the first major acquisition for the Blackstone-managed trust, which went public in May. The 13 data centers involved are newly built facilities designed to support AWS's expanding cloud and artificial intelligence operations.

The massive computing power required to train and run AI models is driving an ever-increasing demand for physical data centers. Such large-scale infrastructure investments provide the capital that tech giants need to expand their AI capabilities, while allowing investment funds to capture a share of this rapidly growing market. Data centers are increasingly viewed as the physical "factories" that form the foundation of the modern digital economy and the AI revolution.

Under the terms of the agreement, AWS will retain the remaining 25% stake and continue to occupy the buildings under a long-term lease agreement. The lease is initially set for 12 years, with the potential to extend up to 32 years. Blackstone previously entered into a similar cloud infrastructure partnership with Google. This move highlights investor confidence in AI and cloud computing infrastructure and the speed of capital flow into this sector.

Company management notes that sale-leaseback channels with large-scale cloud providers effectively double their total addressable market. These facilities, referred to as "powered shells," are delivered with power connections ready, allowing the tenant to complete the internal fit-out. This model stands out as a method that optimizes risk-sharing between data center operators and technology companies. Through these strategic investments, Blackstone aims to strengthen its digital infrastructure portfolio and generate long-term, stable income streams.
