Medical institutions maintain extensive archives of patient histories, predominantly documented as unstructured text, including physician notes, operative reports, and pathology results. To comprehend a patient's complete medical narrative, clinicians typically face the daunting task of reviewing years of charts. Dyania Health has introduced Synapsis AI, a clinical reasoning engine engineered to transform this labor-intensive process. The system comprehensively evaluates all physician documentation, diagnostic reports, and laboratory findings contained within a patient's medical file, connecting disparate data points to address clinical inquiries with the precision of a specialist.

Cleveland Clinic employed this technology to facilitate research involving a cohort exceeding 7,800 individuals who underwent aortic valve replacement procedures between 2005 and 2021. The platform successfully generated a model capable of forecasting potential valve degradation and the necessity for subsequent medical interventions at three, five, and ten-year intervals. This automated analysis significantly reduced the time required for chart review, a task that would otherwise require years of manual effort.

Data security remains a cornerstone of the platform's architecture. The technology utilizes NVIDIA's accelerated computing infrastructure, operating securely within the internal network of a healthcare provider. Consequently, sensitive patient information is processed locally and never leaves the hospital's digital perimeter. It is important to note that the platform functions as an assistive tool rather than an autonomous decision-maker; it highlights specific data points derived from medical files, which require validation and final judgment by a healthcare professional.

This methodology is versatile and adaptable to various medical obstacles where the solution is embedded within existing patient documentation. Potential applications include pinpointing candidates for clinical research studies, addressing deficiencies in cardiology and oncology care, and maintaining clinical registries. By alleviating the administrative burden on clinical teams, such solutions are facilitating a shift toward more data-driven and personalized patient care, allowing medical staff to dedicate more time to direct patient interaction. This method effectively transforms raw medical data into actionable clinical insights, ultimately enhancing the quality of care provided to patients.
