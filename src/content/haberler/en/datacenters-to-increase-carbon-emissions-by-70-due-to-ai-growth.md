The International Energy Agency (IEA) has highlighted the profound impact of global AI expansion on energy infrastructure. According to its latest report, carbon emissions from datacenters are projected to rise by 70% by 2030. This surge is primarily driven by the rapid growth of the AI sector, with AI operations accounting for three-quarters of the increase in electricity demand within these facilities.

This development underscores the environmental cost of technological advancement. Major players in the AI race, particularly the United States and China, are increasingly turning to fossil fuels to meet this skyrocketing energy demand. The report forecasts that by 2030, half of the electricity powering China's AI datacenters will come from coal plants, while in the US, approximately 60% of the power for such facilities is expected to be derived from natural gas. In contrast, the European Union is projected to rely on renewable energy for about 70% of its datacenter electricity needs.

The energy appetite of these datacenters is quantified by significant growth metrics. Annual shipments of AI servers have increased eighteen-fold since 2020. In just the last 24 months, global datacenter capacity has grown tenfold, reaching 17 gigawatts. The IEA estimates that by 2030, AI datacenters will consume an additional 350 terawatt-hours of electricity annually—a figure exceeding the total annual electricity consumption of Great Britain.

This scenario demonstrates that the AI economy requires not only software and hardware innovation but also massive energy infrastructure development. As governments and tech companies navigate this expansion, reshaping energy policies to ensure sustainability will become one of the most critical challenges of the coming decade. Furthermore, the IEA notes that the energy market disruption caused by the war in the Middle East has made the global energy future less certain.
