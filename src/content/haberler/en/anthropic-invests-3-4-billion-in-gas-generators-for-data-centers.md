Anthropic has taken a significant step to secure the massive energy infrastructure required to train and operate its artificial intelligence models. The company has entered into a deal to purchase $3.4 billion worth of large-scale gas generators for its data centers. Signed in late September, this agreement was made with OnePWR Solutions, a power supplier based in San Antonio, Texas. Covering a total capacity of 1.7 gigawatts, this investment stands out as a concrete example of the trend among AI firms to build their own infrastructure to mitigate energy shortages.

This move addresses one of the most significant bottlenecks facing the AI sector: the insatiable demand for electricity. The training and inference processes of large language models require intensive power consumption that often strains the capacity of traditional data centers. By purchasing these generators directly and taking over the installation work, Anthropic aims to minimize uncertainties in its energy supply chain. OnePWR Solutions had previously ordered these generators from Caterpillar; however, Anthropic took over the existing orders and assumed responsibility for the necessary installation work.

From a financial perspective, this investment is directly linked to Anthropic's preparations for its stock market listing. The company's recently amended IPO filing revealed $895 billion in commitments, with almost the entirety of this capital allocated to computing power and infrastructure. This massive budget proves that in the race for AI dominance, physical infrastructure has become just as critical a competitive arena as software development.

A similar trend is observable across the industry. Other AI firms are increasingly turning to gas turbines to get their data centers operational as quickly as possible. For instance, Elon Musk’s xAI has faced legal challenges regarding the use of gas turbines at its Memphis site. Anthropic’s strategic move highlights the immense capital AI companies are willing to deploy to achieve energy independence and underscores the intensifying competition in the infrastructure race.
