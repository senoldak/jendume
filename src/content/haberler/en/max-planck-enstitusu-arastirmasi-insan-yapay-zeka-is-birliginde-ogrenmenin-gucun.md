Human-AI collaboration has long been heralded as the future of work, with the expectation that combined efforts would outperform either working alone. However, previous research often painted a different picture, suggesting that human-AI teams frequently performed worse than the better of the two working independently. This fueled doubts about the true value of human-AI synergy. To investigate further, researchers at the Max Planck Institute for Human Development led a reanalysis of a prominent 2024 review study spanning 74 previous investigations into human-AI interaction. Published in the Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences, the new study reveals that the potential of human-AI collaboration was previously underestimated because learning effects were largely ignored. The research team discovered that out of the 74 studies reviewed, only 10 provided participants with outcome feedback, and none experimentally varied learning opportunities. By applying meta-regression techniques, the researchers found that when participants had access to both explanations of the AI's recommendations and clear outcome feedback, the human-AI tandem consistently outperformed the better partner working alone. Conversely, explanations provided without feedback were associated with distinctly negative synergy. These findings indicate that explanations improve collaboration primarily when humans are given the opportunity to learn and verify the AI's reliability. The authors emphasize that future AI systems must be designed with human learning in mind from the very beginning.
