Industrial environments, traditionally seen as cold steel and clockwork machinery, are being reimagined as living organisms through AI. The technology known as 'living digital twins,' championed by companies like AVEVA, does more than just mirror a plant's real-time status; it utilizes autonomous agents to intervene in physical operations. While earlier digital twins focused on simulation and 'what-if' scenarios, these new versions connect directly with control systems to execute closed-loop optimizations.

At the heart of this shift is agentic AI, which enables industrial systems to move beyond diagnostics and act on their own accord. These systems develop 'reflexes' for industrial homeostasis, making constant small adjustments to keep a factory healthy and humming. However, this autonomy is not unchecked. Human judgment remains instrumental in defining what a 'healthy' state looks like and flagging when AI systems fall out of alignment. This ensures that while the AI handles the pulse of the factory, humans maintain strategic oversight.

The rapid adoption is fueled by the decreasing cost of sensors and IoT devices, which stream telemetry data into user-friendly software platforms. The industry is showing significant interest: a 2026 survey revealed that 66% of manufacturers are using or plan to use agentic AI in their operations. Despite the enthusiasm, 65% of executives rank operational risk and business disruption among their top concerns. Consequently, for the foreseeable future, AI is expected to work primarily in an advisory mode, with full autonomy reserved for narrow, repetitive tasks.

Governance and regulation are also evolving to keep pace. The EU AI Act classifies certain AI components in critical infrastructure as high-risk, imposing strict requirements for human oversight. Organizations are establishing responsible AI governance boards to ensure transparency and accountability. As these technologies mature, factories are moving away from siloed systems toward holistic coordination, integrating physical AI like drones and robotics to create an interconnected flow of operational information.
