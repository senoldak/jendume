Cisco develops the networking and security infrastructure that organizations utilize to link and safeguard their systems. As malicious actors leverage artificial intelligence to scale automated attacks and craft exploits at rapid speeds, enterprise infrastructure defense must evolve. To address this challenge, the company pairs its vast network telemetry with frontier AI reasoning to outpace emerging threats.

OpenAI's strict framework regarding enterprise data privacy, which focuses on zero data retention and defined customer data boundaries, allowed the organization to deploy advanced intelligence across mission-critical systems with confidence. The security strategy shifts from a single perimeter model to controls distributed across microservices, containers, and clusters. By placing a compact security stack in front of every microservice and Kubernetes container, the company reduces potential attack pathways and enables more selective system isolation.

Combining network intelligence with OpenAI models allows the system to correlate signals at machine scale. Two signals that appear harmless in isolation can together reveal a meaningful pattern. This capability addresses a pressing challenge for defenders: closing the gap between how quickly attackers exploit a vulnerability and how long organizations take to implement a patch.

Through initiatives like Daybreak and GPT-6 Astra, the company helps security teams find and resolve vulnerabilities more efficiently while integrating into existing scanning workflows. As software agents grow increasingly autonomous, security teams also need to distinguish malicious interference from unintended agent behavior using comprehensive telemetry data.

Through the Daybreak Defense Network, Cisco and OpenAI are exploring ways to help security teams triage exposure faster and enforce appropriate controls in Cisco Cloud Control, ultimately shifting the advantage back to defenders.
