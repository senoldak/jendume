Sweden’s Government Offices, which coordinate the operations of the prime minister and ministries, have restricted personnel from utilizing OpenAI products and Anthropic models on professional workstations. The measure aims to guarantee that personnel rely strictly on verified and authorized applications. Rather than accessing the restricted web-based chatbot interfaces, staff members are being routed to Microsoft’s Copilot, an alternative that the administration states has undergone safety evaluations. According to digital services leadership, deploying artificial intelligence software without an established service agreement can compromise institutional data security. Despite the broad institutional restriction, certain personnel retain the ability to interact with ChatGPT and Claude through Intric, a Stockholm-based enterprise providing assistant architecture that previously secured an agreement with the Government Offices and operates on domestic servers without depending on external cloud providers. Additional public entities in the region, such as tax authorities and institutional care administrations, have also introduced corresponding limitations on conversational software, while enforcement branches evaluate similar procedures. These widespread measures highlight a growing emphasis on strict data governance across administrative bodies. Furthermore, prior to this decision, the administration had already limited the use of DeepSeek, demonstrating an ongoing review of multiple external technologies. Similar cautious approaches have been observed internationally, reflecting broader regulatory debates on how large organizations handle third-party digital assistant platforms and cloud dependencies.
