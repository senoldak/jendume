Mercedes has taken a significant step in autonomous driving technology by announcing new systems that cover both city and highway driving. This new technology offers hands-free driving capabilities while intensifying competition in the sector. Developed as a direct response to Tesla's FSD technology, this system is powered by Nvidia's Drive AV software.

The system consists of two main components. The city support feature, called "MB.Drive Assist Pro," can manage intersections, traffic lights, and lane changes based on routes entered into the navigation system. However, as a safety measure, unlike Tesla's system, it requires hands to remain on the steering wheel. On the other hand, the highway assistant allows for hands-free driving at speeds of up to 137 km/h on approved highways and can perform automatic overtaking.

This development is critical for the integration of autonomous driving into daily life. Aiming to reduce the burden on drivers, these technologies seek to make traffic flow safer and more efficient. The system's response to unexpected situations, such as road work or pedestrians in complex city traffic, will determine the success of the technology.

In a strategic shift, Mercedes has decided to suspend its previous Level 3 autonomous driving projects to focus on Level 2 systems, which face fewer legal hurdles. This approach allows the technology to reach end-users more quickly. The software will be tested in a program starting next month in California and will be available across a wide range of models by 2027. Users will be able to add these features to their vehicles via subscription models through online stores.

The system is equipped with 27 different sensors and 254 TOPS of processing power. This hardware increases the vehicle's capacity to perceive its surroundings and make real-time decisions. A special security layer called "Halos" aims to minimize the error margin of the software.
