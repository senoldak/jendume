Scientists from the Technion - Israel Institute of Technology have conducted a new study examining the relationship between neural network learning processes and their internal structures. Led by Professor Omri Barak and research student Yoav Ger, the research utilized mathematical models to demonstrate that networks exhibiting identical external responses can follow different trajectories during learning. The paper, published on the arXiv preprint server, was selected for an oral presentation at the NeurIPS 2026 conference. The study focused on hidden structural properties that do not affect a network's current performance but influence how it learns in the future.

During their investigation, the team concentrated on specific kinds of feedback-driven computational frameworks that simulate neural information processing. They formulated mathematical expressions enabling researchers to track how systems evolve during training using minimal parameters. Their evaluation successfully separated attributes governing immediate execution from those dictating adaptation capacities. Such underlying traits remain invisible during standard operational checks but surface prominently when systems undertake unfamiliar challenges.

Within one comparative scenario highlighted in the project, two distinct internal architectures generated identical outputs upon receiving matching stimuli. Nevertheless, subjected to identical protocols, their developmental paths diverged notably; progress plateaued temporarily in one instance while advancing steadily in the other. Consequently, training dynamics themselves function effectively as analytical instruments, exposing variances undetectable through static operational observation alone.

The findings additionally confirmed that under specific environments, these concealed attributes retain historical footprints from prior training phases. Systems initially conditioned on alternative objectives before tackling shared benchmarks achieved comparable success metrics, yet their underlying configurations still allowed investigators to trace their developmental lineage. Derived through computer simulations, these insights offer valuable frameworks for future biological experimentation.
