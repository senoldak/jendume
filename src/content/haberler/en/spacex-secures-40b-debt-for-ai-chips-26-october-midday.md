SpaceX has completed a massive $40 billion debt financing deal to purchase graphics processing units, the essential chips required to run advanced AI systems. Announced by Elon Musk on X, the financing was secured thanks to the company's investment-grade credit rating and robust revenues from Starlink and rocket launches. The package, reportedly consisting of $10 billion in bank loans and $30 billion in investment-grade debt, is intended to cover the costs of chips arriving in the first and second quarters of next year. SpaceX's AI unit has become the company's largest business segment, with third-quarter revenues surging to $7.56 billion from $813 million in the same period last year. Amidst this growth, Elon Musk warned that debt markets are tightening for companies lacking substantial revenue streams outside of AI data centers. Musk predicted that many of the more than 200 companies currently renting out AI computing capacity will face consolidation and failure due to high debt and capital scarcity. Stocks in the sector faced significant pressure as major players saw their shares drop following Elon Musk's warnings about potential industry-wide collapses. In contrast, Tsinghua University researchers have open-sourced AKV, a new cache engineering system that reduces AI agent operational costs by an average of 27.7% while slightly improving task scores. Unlike traditional methods that summarize history, AKV allows agents to drop specific data entries and reuse the remaining cache, significantly optimizing how large language models process information. Testing on models showed that cost per task dropped significantly, with the system now available under the Apache-2.0 license for the global research community.
