China's commerce and science ministries released a draft update targeting the nation's list of banned and restricted export technologies. The catalog focuses on twenty-three critical artificial intelligence technologies across large models, software agents, compute clusters, and data processing systems. Artificial intelligence tools capable of autonomously altering malware to attack critical infrastructure face a strict total export ban, whereas software vulnerability discovery tools encounter substantial new limitations. Large artificial intelligence models face export restrictions under two distinct scenarios. The first scenario regulates models trained directly on designated important data originating from domestic rules. The second scenario targets foundational core technologies encompassing model weights, source code, system architecture, and advanced training methods. These specific controls trigger automatically once a model reaches ten to the power of twenty-six floating-point operations of training compute, features at least two trillion parameters, or secures a position in the global top ten. To prevent harming domestic open-source momentum, authorities introduced a simultaneous fast-track regulatory process. Publishing restricted artificial intelligence technologies and weights openly qualifies developers for automatic licensing. Comparable expedited pathways apply to domestic firms operating data centers abroad or collaborating with international research partners. Accessing restricted models through standard API calls, web applications, or lightweight local versions remains exempt from these export rules. However, deploying restricted technologies onto foreign local servers mandates a standard export license. Companies must register affected exports through the ministry portal until final regulations take legal effect.
