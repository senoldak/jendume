Following US President Donald Trump's decree that artificial intelligence should be referred to as Super Intelligence, technology companies have begun adopting the new branding in their official communications. An analysis revealed that corporations such as Microsoft, SpaceX, Meta, Tesla, and IBM used the term Super Intelligence or the abbreviation SI in their earnings season documents and reports. One SpaceX filing reportedly used the term eighty-five times, while Microsoft's annual report highlighted the arrival of a new era.

The rebranding effort stems from a series of actions by the administration, starting with a social media poll and culminating in an executive order banning the term artificial intelligence in government documents. Major technology leaders quickly complied with the directive, reflecting a notable alignment with the administration's new terminology guidelines.

SpaceX and Tesla CEO Elon Musk enthusiastically adopted the moniker, even altering his company's internal nomenclature to reflect the change. Meanwhile, OpenAI CEO Sam Altman expressed skepticism about the broader utility of the rebrand, suggesting that the public would likely continue using the traditional terminology regardless of official mandates.

This corporate shift illustrates the complex relationship between technology giants and political authorities, showing how swiftly industry standards and lexicon can be influenced by governmental decrees and executive orders.
