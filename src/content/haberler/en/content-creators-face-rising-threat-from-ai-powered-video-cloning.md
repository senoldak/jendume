Musicians, dancers, and comedians are increasingly discovering that their original videos are being cloned by AI-generated characters. This practice, known as "reskinning," is creating significant ethical and copyright challenges in the digital space. In this process, AI tools are used to replace a real person in a video with an AI avatar while keeping the original movements, facial expressions, and background intact. The result is a video that looks and moves exactly like the original, but features a completely different, AI-generated persona.

This development is deeply concerning because it allows bad actors to steal years of hard work in mere seconds. While some accounts use these clones simply to gain views or attention, others are exploiting them for financial gain, often by driving traffic to adult content subscription sites. For instance, a professional guitarist reported that her performances were being reskinned with over-sexualized AI characters to promote external services. Similarly, dancers and automotive content creators have found their likenesses altered while the rest of their content remains identical.

Although social media platforms are beginning to explore ways to limit repeat offenders and implement new detection tools, the problem persists as new accounts appear almost immediately after old ones are removed. This creates a constant, exhausting cycle for creators trying to protect their work. Experts describe these cloning activities as a byproduct of an online culture focused on rapid, often unethical, monetization.

Performers' unions and advocacy groups are now arguing that current copyright laws are insufficient to address this modern threat. They are calling for new legislation that grants creators explicit rights over their digital likenesses and provides stronger mechanisms to prevent unauthorized AI replicas. Until such legal frameworks are established, creators remain vulnerable to having their identity and effort repurposed without consent.
