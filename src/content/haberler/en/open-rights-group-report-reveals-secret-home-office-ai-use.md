The UK Home Office has reportedly embedded artificial intelligence across its operations in near-total secrecy to reinforce restrictive immigration policies. According to a report by the Open Rights Group, AI technologies are now involved in tasks ranging from determining the age of asylum seekers and detecting potential sham marriages to reviewing the contents of new arrivals' phones.

The report highlights that AI is utilized for border surveillance through live facial recognition, drones, and satellites, as well as for summarizing asylum interviews. Critics argue that relying on automated systems reduces complex human experiences to technical problems, risking the replication of societal discrimination and bias, particularly within aggressive anti-immigration frameworks.

Open Rights Group is urging the government to enforce strict regulations, ban undemocratic uses of AI that threaten human rights, and establish a public register of government AI tools. Lawmakers supporting the findings emphasize the lack of accountability and transparency regarding how these automated systems impact vulnerable populations.

In response, a Home Office spokesperson stated that artificial intelligence serves merely as an assistive tool to protect borders and does not replace human decision-makers. The department maintained that trained caseworkers make all final asylum decisions, while AI is used strictly to navigate interview records and policy information under established quality-assurance processes.
