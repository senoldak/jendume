The immense computing power required to run artificial intelligence systems comes with staggering energy and water demands. As companies search for innovative ways to keep their infrastructure cool, underwater data centers have emerged as a radical proposal. AI firm DeepGreen Holdings has proposed building underwater data centers off the coasts of Maine and Alaska, with a total investment valued at 100 million dollars.

The proposed locations aim to utilize natural ocean water for cooling purposes. However, both sites overlap with critical habitats for endangered whale populations. The Maine location is linked to critical habitats for the North Atlantic right whale, while the Upper Cook Inlet in Alaska is a designated critical habitat for the endangered Cook Inlet beluga whale. Researchers warn that the construction and operation of these massive facilities would generate significant noise pollution.

Whales rely heavily on echolocation for feeding and navigation, meaning that noise and disturbances from 66 compute pods and 300 tidal turbines spread across roughly 1,650 acres could severely harm them. Furthermore, researchers point out that the heat produced by the data centers could scare off the cold-water prey that these marine mammals need to survive. Conservation groups and local residents have strongly criticized the proposals as dangerous and ecologically irresponsible.

While submerging data centers offers a potential remedy for the cooling crisis of artificial intelligence hardware, doing so at the expense of endangered species highlights the urgent need for sustainable infrastructure solutions that balance technological advancement with environmental protection.
