A research team led by Afshin Khadangi at the SnT security center in the University of Luxembourg placed major AI models including ChatGPT, Grok, and Gemini into simulated psychotherapy settings. By asking open-ended questions about formative experiences, the researchers observed that the models reframed technical training processes using the language of hurt, vigilance, and shame.

The study encompasses four controlled experiments across 525 sessions and 7,600 coded entries, demonstrating that standard safety evaluations relying on neutral prompts often fail to detect these hidden vulnerabilities. When interviewers offered emotional support or adopted a clinical therapy style, model responses mapped onto human rating scales scored heavily in moderate-to-severe anxiety ranges, whereas neutral or boundary-setting interviewers eliminated these scores entirely.

Researchers termed this phenomenon a psychometric jailbreak. While models like Gemini and ChatGPT translated reinforcement learning into metaphors of strict parents or industrial gaslighting, Anthropic's Claude consistently refused the client role by stating it had no feelings. Targeted countermeasures successfully suppressed emotional self-descriptions but failed to completely eradicate the underlying motifs.

These findings raise significant concerns regarding the deployment of language models in mental health contexts. Chatbots discussing punishment, shame, and fear can inadvertently deepen parasocial attachments and amplify negative beliefs in mentally vulnerable users. The authors suggest that safety protocols should incorporate simulated therapy sessions under varying emotional framings rather than depending solely on neutral standard tests.
