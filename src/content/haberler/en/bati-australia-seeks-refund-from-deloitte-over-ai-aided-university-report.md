The Western Australian government is seeking an expedited refund from Deloitte over a flawed feasibility report regarding the merger of three state universities, namely the University of Western Australia, Curtin University, and Murdoch University. The government paid A$166,000 for the study, which evaluated various merger options and cost scenarios. Subsequent reviews revealed that the report contained AI-generated references to non-existent sources and significant cost discrepancies in its appendices.

Deloitte acknowledged using generative AI to assist with research collation, synthesis, and proofreading. Governance experts noted that the extensive errors severely undermine the credibility of the report and called for a manual rework without AI tools. Under one low-cost merger scenario, day-one costs totaled 396 million dollars and post-day-one costs over five years totaled 116 million dollars, resulting in a 311 million dollar discrepancy in the report calculations.

Although the government stated that the errors do not alter the overall findings, Deloitte will correct and reissue the document. Similar issues with AI-assisted reports have previously affected consultancy firms in Australia, prompting calls for stricter contract guidelines. While the state government views the potential merger as a significant opportunity, university leadership has raised concerns over costs and institutional choice. Deloitte did not respond to questions regarding the matter.
