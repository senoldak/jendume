Franco-Italian chipmaker STMicroelectronics expects fourth-quarter revenue of $4.15 billion, up about 10.5 percent on the third quarter and above analysts' forecasts. The company noted that artificial intelligence computing hubs and orbital hardware builders are contributing significantly to a broader market rebound. Third-quarter revenue rose 17.9 percent to $3.76 billion, as detailed in the official earnings statement.

Chief Executive Jean-Marc Chery noted that in an environment where demand remains well above supply, the company continues to expand capacity aligned with its strategic roadmap. Projections place full-year revenue at roughly $14.5 billion, representing a 22.8 percent increase. Management highlights artificial intelligence server facilities and orbital communication networks as primary growth drivers, with annual sector revenue anticipated to reach approximately $1.3 billion.

Gross margin rose to 37.3 percent from 33.2 percent a year earlier, helped by a better product mix and less idle factory capacity. ST expects a gross margin of about 39 percent in the fourth quarter. Because supply is tight and input costs are rising, the company is raising prices on some of its products.

The results arrive during a strong period for chipmakers capitalizing on AI demand. Samsung posted a record profit, SK Hynix reported surging earnings, and rival Infineon committed 1.1 billion euros to a plant in Austria.
