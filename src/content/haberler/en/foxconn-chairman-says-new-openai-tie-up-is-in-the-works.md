Foxconn is actively working on a new partnership with OpenAI, as confirmed by chairman Young Liu during an annual technology event in Taipei. Liu stated that he had met with OpenAI chief executive Sam Altman earlier that same day. While specific details regarding the discussions have not yet been disclosed, leadership indicated that an official announcement will follow once all agreement terms are finalised.

This potential collaboration builds upon the existing relationship between the two entities. In November 2025, the companies announced a joint effort to design multiple generations of AI data centre racks, with Foxconn planning to manufacture components such as cooling systems and power equipment in the United States. That previous arrangement provided OpenAI with early access to test the hardware alongside an option to purchase.

AI hardware has rapidly become a central pillar of Foxconn's overall business strategy. During the second quarter, the cloud and networking division, driven heavily by AI servers produced for Nvidia, accounted for 51 percent of the company's total revenue, crossing the halfway mark for the first time in its history.

Addressing broader market concerns regarding a potential artificial intelligence bubble and rising borrowing costs, Liu pushed back against such worries. Foxconn anticipates double-digit growth in the upcoming year and continues to expand its manufacturing facilities in Houston, Texas, to meet the surging demand for AI infrastructure.
