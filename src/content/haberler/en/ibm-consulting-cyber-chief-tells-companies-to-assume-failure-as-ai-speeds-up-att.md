Artificial intelligence is dramatically transforming the cybersecurity landscape by enabling attackers to operate at machine speed. Mark Hughes, Global Managing Partner of Cybersecurity Services at IBM Consulting, points out that while threat actors leverage AI to automate target research and vulnerability discovery, most organizations continue to patch systems at human speed. This growing disparity creates significant security risks as automated attack chains outpace traditional engineering remediation workflows. Hughes notes that autonomous agents could soon automate even larger portions of the attack lifecycle, introducing complex challenges around identity and access control.

To address these evolving threats, initiatives like the collaboration between IBM and Red Hat combine artificial intelligence with engineering expertise to identify and fix vulnerabilities in foundational open source software. Backed by a commitment involving over 20,000 engineers, the program has already uncovered and resolved hundreds of unknown flaws in widely used Java libraries, highlighting the critical need to secure foundational infrastructure. Hughes stresses that open source projects often rely on limited resources, making it harder to match the new speed of threats such as PHP2Shell vulnerabilities. Organizations must prepare for disruptions by assuming failure, prioritizing risks effectively, and shifting toward autonomous security operations to protect critical systems from advanced threats.
