Swiss robotics startup coprod has raised 5.45 million euros (approximately $6 million) in a pre-seed funding round to develop modular hardware components that allow robotic parts to work together. Led by Founderful and PROTOTYPE, the round included participation from General Catalyst and Hugging Face co-founder Thomas Wolf. Founded in late 2024 by EPFL alumni, the company aims to eliminate the technical bottlenecks known as 'integration hell,' where combining parts from different manufacturers often takes years of debugging and custom software development.

Coprod's modular approach focuses on 'co:products,' a family of compatible hardware modules covering sensing, actuation, power, and battery systems. These modules are unified under the co:system platform, which utilizes a real-time EtherCAT control layer. This allows developers to access all hardware components through a single API using C++ or Python, maintaining the high-performance standards required for safe and reliable robotic operation. The company claims its infrastructure can reduce development time for small teams from years to just a few months.

With the new investment, coprod plans to establish a fully equipped prototype workshop in Lausanne. This facility, featuring CNC machines, lasers, and electronic assembly tools, is designed to shrink development cycles from two weeks to 24 hours. The company's hardware is already being utilized in the field, most notably in the first Swiss student-built humanoid robot developed by the ETH Robotics Club, which recently took its first steps in Zurich.

Looking ahead, coprod aims to have its full suite of components tested, certified, and on the market by the end of 2027. While research and development will remain in Switzerland, the company targets large-scale production in Shenzhen and Texas. According to McKinsey, the market for actuators, sensors, and control systems for humanoid robots is expected to reach $28 billion by 2030. Coprod's vision is built on the belief that task-specific hardware will always outperform general-purpose robots in speed, cost, and reliability.
