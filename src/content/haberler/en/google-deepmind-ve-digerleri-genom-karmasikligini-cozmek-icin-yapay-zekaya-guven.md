Interest in turning to artificial intelligence models to unlock the secrets of human DNA in genomic research is growing steadily. Genomic foundation models such as Evo 2, Genos, and AlphaGenome developed by Google DeepMind are trained on vast datasets consisting of billions of DNA building blocks. These algorithms aim to predict how differences in DNA sequences affect biological processes and organism traits, including disease risks.

However, biologists point out that the human genome is far from being a simple blueprint or computer code. Less than two percent of the human genome consists of genes that code for proteins. The remaining portion presents a complex structure that regulates when and how genes are activated. Karen Adelman, a biologist at Harvard Medical School, emphasizes that gene regulation differs significantly from the straightforward logic found in bacteria, noting that human cells utilize an intricate decision-making mechanism where multiple signals interact simultaneously.

Although AI algorithms attempt to predict these processes by drawing correlations from known cases, critics debate whether this approach fully captures biological reality. Complex regulatory mechanisms and the three-dimensional knots of DNA continue to present a difficult puzzle for artificial intelligence to solve.

These developments highlight both the potential and the limitations of artificial intelligence in biology. While computational models provide researchers with immense data-processing power, achieving a true understanding of life's fundamental operations still requires human effort and profound scientific analysis.
