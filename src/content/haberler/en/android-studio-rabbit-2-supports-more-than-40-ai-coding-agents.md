Google has officially moved the Rabbit 2 version of Android Studio to the stable channel, introducing a significant shift in how developers interact with artificial intelligence. The standout feature, "Bring Your Own Agent," breaks the mold of being tied to a single AI assistant. Developers can now choose from over 40 compatible coding agents, including Claude Agent, Codex, Junie, and Google Antigravity, to assist in their daily tasks.

This integration is powered by the ACP protocol, which allows these agents to access Android Studio’s core tools such as build systems, logs, and emulators. By selecting an agent through the settings and authenticating with the provider, developers can tailor their environment to their project's specific needs or their organization's existing service agreements. Google’s approach emphasizes choice, offering a platform that accommodates various AI providers rather than enforcing a single solution.

Beyond agent support, Rabbit 2 introduces AI-enhanced debugging capabilities. The LeakCanary integration now utilizes AI agents to investigate the root causes of memory leaks and suggest specific code fixes. Furthermore, the "Change Review" feature allows for a more organized inspection of AI-generated code, grouping related changes by topic rather than simple alphabetical order, which streamlines the peer-review process.

In addition to AI features, the update stabilizes Android Studio’s XR emulators. This allows developers to build and test experiences for smart glasses and virtual reality headsets without requiring physical hardware. These emulators can use 360-degree photos or live camera feeds to simulate real-world environments, providing a comprehensive testing ground for the next generation of spatial computing applications.
