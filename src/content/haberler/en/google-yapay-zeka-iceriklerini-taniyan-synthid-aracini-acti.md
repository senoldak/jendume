Google has opened its SynthID Detector tool to the public, providing a web-based service designed to spot digital content created or edited by artificial intelligence. The tool checks for invisible digital watermarks embedded in media created by Google and its industry partners.

Originally introduced in 2023, SynthID technology has stamped billions of media files with hidden signatures over time. The newly released web detector makes it easier for everyday users to check if a piece of media was generated by AI. The system can also recognize content produced by models from other major tech companies such as OpenAI, NVIDIA, and Kakao.

Despite its usefulness, the tool has clear limitations. Because different companies rely on varying standards and watermarks can sometimes be removed, the detector does not yet function as a universal verification system. Furthermore, it cannot always distinguish whether a file was entirely created by AI or merely edited with its help.

Experts note that until the broader tech industry establishes unified standards, users must remain cautious about digital media. Google's move represents an important step toward increasing transparency in an increasingly synthetic world.
