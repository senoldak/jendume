London-based autonomous driving software developer Wayve has announced it will train its artificial intelligence models on CoreWeave's cloud infrastructure. Under the multi-year agreement, the startup will deploy Nvidia Blackwell processors on the cloud platform to train, test, and simulate its AI Driver. Financial terms and the exact duration of the partnership were not disclosed by either company.

Wayve does not manufacture vehicles; instead, it licenses its software to automakers and ride-hailing firms. The software continuously learns from driving data gathered across more than 70 countries. Having utilized Microsoft's Azure cloud for model training since 2022, Wayve's new collaboration expands its computational resources. The startup originally originated from an AI model-tracking tool platform.

According to Silvius Rus, Wayve's senior vice president of engineering, the AI Driver improves by executing a data cycle of gathering real-world driving experience, processing training sessions on it, evaluating results, and deploying only the software that outperforms existing solutions on the road. Uber initiated passenger transport services in Wayve-driven cars in London utilizing a safety driver in every vehicle, while Mercedes-Benz plans to integrate the AI Driver into its production cars.

For CoreWeave, this partnership represents a strategic push beyond chatbot developers into physical AI applications such as cars and robots. The infrastructure provider has expanded its clientele through agreements involving major industry players like Meta and Jane Street. The announcement coincided with an approximate 3% fall in its shares prior to the market opening alongside ongoing debt concerns.

Valued at $8.6 billion in February, Wayve counts prominent technology giants like Microsoft and Nvidia among its backers. Such cloud and hardware partnerships remain critical for providing the massive computing power required to advance and deploy safe autonomous driving technologies.
