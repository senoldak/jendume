Representatives from Meta, TikTok, and Google have united at an Indonesian parliamentary hearing to oppose a proposed revision to the country's copyright law. Discussed on November 11, 2026, the bill aims to mandate that digital platforms compensate publishers for displaying news content while establishing specific rules for artificial intelligence. If enacted, Indonesia would become the first nation in Southeast Asia to formally integrate AI regulations into its copyright legislation.

The tech companies argue that these measures would ultimately restrict user access to information and negatively impact publishers. Google representatives stated that forcing platforms to pay for displaying news links would limit the public's ability to access news. Meta officials warned that such a policy could backfire, potentially reducing traffic and revenue from advertising and subscriptions for the publishers themselves. TikTok focused on the AI-specific provisions, arguing that disclosure requirements should not apply in instances where AI merely assists in content creation rather than fully generating it.

This debate represents a new front in the global struggle over news content and AI. Similar legislative battles have previously occurred in Australia and Canada, where platforms were forced to either negotiate deals or block news content entirely. Indonesia's move is particularly significant because it seeks to codify these rules into statutory law, moving beyond previous presidential regulations. The tech industry fears that if Indonesia, the world's fourth most populous country, sets this precedent, it will provide a template for other legislatures across the region. The ongoing tension highlights the complex challenge of balancing technological innovation with traditional copyright protections.
