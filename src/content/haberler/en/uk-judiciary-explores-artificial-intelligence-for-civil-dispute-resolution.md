Artificial intelligence is increasingly finding its way into courtrooms and legal workflows, prompting debates about the future of justice. Geoffrey Vos, one of the UK's senior judges, highlights that AI tools are frequently used for administrative tasks and navigating large volumes of legal documents. During a visit to courts in Shenzhen, China, Vos observed civil disputes being adjudicated with heavy assistance from AI, suggesting that global courts should consider similar approaches.

However, this technological shift comes with notable challenges. Lawyers have occasionally submitted hallucinations generated by AI, and litigants are flooding courts with claims drafted by chatbots. Despite these concerns, Vos views the surge in court filings as an indicator of broader access to justice, noting that legal procedures have historically been difficult to navigate for everyday people.

County court cases in the region have seen a ten percent annual rise, driven largely by individuals turning to AI models like ChatGPT, Claude, and Gemini to draft claims and legal documents. While some of these claims require careful filtering, they often reflect genuine grievances that citizens want addressed.

Looking ahead, simpler civil disputes such as personal injury claims and low-value negligence cases could potentially be resolved with artificial intelligence. Meanwhile, complex commercial and sensitive family law cases will likely remain under the purview of human judges to maintain absolute integrity.

As the legal profession adapts to a new technological era, courts are also grappling with AI-generated deepfakes and false evidence. Legal experts emphasize that producing fraudulent digital evidence carries the same severe penalties as traditional forgery, ensuring that the foundational rules of justice remain intact.
