The rapid expansion of artificial intelligence technologies is driving an unprecedented demand for data centers. However, the intense electricity required to power these facilities is bringing unexpected environmental and health consequences. A new academic study reveals that the energy demands of these data centers are degrading air quality and causing health issues in regions far removed from the physical locations of the facilities.

Public concern regarding data centers typically focuses on the immediate vicinity of the sites, such as noise levels or local energy consumption. Yet, this research demonstrates that the interconnected nature of the national power grid allows pollution to travel across vast distances. Because data centers operate around the clock, their electricity demand triggers power plants across the grid to ramp up production. Many of these plants, particularly older facilities burning coal and natural gas, release significant amounts of nitrogen oxides and sulfur dioxide into the atmosphere.

Researchers utilized 2023 data to develop a modeling framework that tracks how these emissions transform into air pollution and impact human health. The model estimates that the electricity demand from data centers in 2023 resulted in approximately 650 premature deaths. Notably, these health impacts are not concentrated in states like Virginia or Texas, where data centers are most prevalent, but rather in regions where older, dirtier power plants are forced to increase output to meet the surge in demand.

Agricultural regions in the Midwest are particularly vulnerable, as atmospheric conditions there facilitate the formation of fine particulate matter when combined with pollutants like ammonia. This mismatch highlights that data centers should not be viewed solely as technological infrastructure, but also through the lens of public health and environmental justice. As new facilities are planned, the study suggests that developers and policymakers must consider not only land and energy prices but also the indirect air quality impacts these facilities impose on the broader power grid.
