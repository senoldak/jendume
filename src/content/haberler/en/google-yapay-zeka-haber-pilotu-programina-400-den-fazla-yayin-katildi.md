Google announced that more than 400 publications worldwide are now participating in its news AI pilot program, which pays publishers to license content for experimental features within Google News. The latest partners to join the initiative include major outlets from across the media landscape.

The licensing program compensates participating news organizations for delivering enhanced content used to develop new AI features. In addition to major international outlets, local publishers have also joined the pilot, including regional newspapers in Charleston, Texas, and Wyoming. The pilot initially launched with several prominent institutional partners.

Licensed articles feed AI-generated story previews and in-depth summaries on publisher pages within the Google News app. Another feature currently being tested is a custom audio briefing podcast where users can select topics like World, Gaming, or Sports. Google noted that the pilot does not pay based on how much content contributes to an AI answer, as that model belongs to a separate program for AI Overviews and Gemini.

For major broadcasters, the agreements mark strategic partnerships following corporate transitions. Representatives noted that the deals align with network strategies to protect intellectual property while exploring new ways to engage audiences through generative AI tools.

These developments come as publishers increasingly push back against AI companies using their content without permission. Meanwhile, Google has also updated how links appear in its AI search features, placing inline links higher up in responses and testing additional thumbnail links.
