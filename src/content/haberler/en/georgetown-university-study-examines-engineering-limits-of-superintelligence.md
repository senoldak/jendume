The possibility of artificial intelligence systems achieving recursive self-improvement and leading to human extinction has sparked intense debate among researchers. Employees and executives at leading artificial intelligence companies have raised concerns that automated research and coding agents could accelerate development toward an uncontrollable artificial superintelligence. While some view these scenarios as existential threats, others argue they are used for marketing or regulatory lobbying purposes by incumbent laboratories.

However, from an engineering perspective, the technical feasibility of superintelligence deserves closer scrutiny. Research and academic seminars at Georgetown University highlight how major language models began facing diminishing returns after years of rapid scaling. The original GPT-1 model featured 117 million parameters, while GPT-2 scaled this figure to 1.5 billion parameters, with subsequent versions expanding significantly further. Following these scaling phases, engineers shifted focus heavily toward post-training reinforcement learning techniques, which reward models for successful responses in structured domains like programming and mathematics.

Despite rapid advancements in code and math, progress in other areas remains bottlenecked by a lack of accessible training data. Experts note that while artificial intelligence can tackle advanced theorems, it struggles with tasks dependent on broader human-generated contexts. This fundamental limitation suggests that recursive self-improvement may encounter strict boundaries rather than triggering an unstoppable intelligence explosion, as human-generated data remains a primary constraint across multiple disciplines.

Examining these technical obstacles helps distinguish between speculative thought experiments and practical engineering realities. Understanding these fundamental limits guides how society should approach the future of advanced artificial intelligence without losing sight of actual engineering challenges and potential design flaws.
