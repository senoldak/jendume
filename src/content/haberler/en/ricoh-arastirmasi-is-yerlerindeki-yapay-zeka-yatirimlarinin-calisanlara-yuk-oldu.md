Corporate enthusiasm for intelligent automation is facing growing resistance from frontline employees. A new research report from Ricoh highlights a sharp disconnect between leadership expectations and the daily reality of the workforce. While 91% of business decision-makers believe their artificial intelligence investments are delivering anticipated value, 53% of workers report that these tools actually introduce friction and create additional work instead of streamlining their tasks.

The study examined four core workplace categories, evaluating artificial intelligence tools alongside communication platforms, data access utilities, and workflow management systems. Among these areas, artificial intelligence tools were found to deliver the least impactful productivity boost. Furthermore, 55% of surveyed employees stated that the time required to manage poorly implemented technology directly interferes with their overall productivity.

Integration failures represent a major challenge across modern work environments. Although 90% of executives feel confident that their internal systems and tools are well connected, 76% of workers voice a need for platforms that integrate more effectively. This friction is particularly severe among hybrid employees, with 25% experiencing collaboration difficulties due to disconnected infrastructure, compared to 16% of purely in-office personnel and 14% of fully remote workers.

Because official enterprise solutions often fail to meet daily needs, workers are turning to unapproved digital workarounds. Nearly half (45%) of respondents admit to using shadow artificial intelligence tools at least once a month, presenting significant governance hurdles for leadership. Management appears aware of the underlying problem, as 81% of decision-makers acknowledge that company investments in emerging technology outpace the support provided for worker adoption.
