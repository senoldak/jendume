Vero Security has secured an 11 million dollar seed funding round led by TLV Partners for software designed to uncover workplace artificial intelligence tools and enforce safety protocols during their execution. Additional participants in the financing round included GTM Capital, Maple Capital, and several individual angel investors. The New York and Tel Aviv-based startup announced it will channel the capital into advancing its artificial intelligence security engineering platform, broadening enterprise technology coverage, and strengthening market presence.

The primary offering evaluates prompts and operational commands while workplace intelligence agents execute tasks, automatically blocking any actions that violate established corporate regulations. For instance, the system can prevent a software development agent from reading authorization tokens that are unnecessary for its workflow. Security departments establish the overarching guidelines, and the platform translates these rules into active operational safeguards that adapt as intelligence tools evolve. Industry research indicates that among corporations generating significant revenue, most assign intelligence security responsibilities to personnel who already manage separate primary roles.

Statistics show that within corporations generating at least 10 billion dollars in revenue, 65 percent employ someone overseeing intelligence safety, yet only 18 percent have established this responsibility as a dedicated position. In remaining organizations, safety duties are simply appended to existing job descriptions. Co-founder and Chief Executive Officer Elad Tessler noted that security leaders have grown weary of administrative dashboards that merely highlight organizational vulnerabilities without resolving them. Tessler previously directed product development at Astrix Security, an enterprise acquired by Cisco.

The marketplace for enterprise intelligence safety solutions is becoming increasingly competitive. Zenity secured 125 million dollars in August, Tel Aviv-based Bay obtained 50 million dollars, and Pluto Security launched with 42 million dollars in October. Corporate safety teams frequently struggle to monitor all artificial intelligence applications deployed across their networks. Alongside the financial announcement, the startup is introducing a complimentary online educational program and community dedicated to intelligence safety professionals, containing zero promotional material.
