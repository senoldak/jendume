OpenAI, a leading force in the artificial intelligence sector, has launched a new naming strategy for its datacentres, which have been facing increasing public scrutiny. The company announced that it will now refer to these facilities, known for their massive energy and water consumption, as "compute campuses" instead of datacentres. This move comes at a time when datacentres have become a heated political topic in the United States due to rising utility bills and environmental impacts.

An OpenAI spokesperson stated that the new terminology better describes the scale and role of the infrastructure being built for AI. The company emphasized that these facilities are not merely server rooms but long-term investments in computing, energy infrastructure, and skilled labor. However, critics view this linguistic shift as a public relations effort designed to mask the significant resource consumption issues associated with these sites. The rebranding aims to soften the image of facilities that are often seen as resource-guzzling industrial hubs.

Public unrest regarding datacentres has been reflected in recent studies and reports. In many regions, citizens are organizing protests, blaming these facilities for depleting local resources and driving up the cost of living. Analysis shows that pro-datacentre claims in media outlets have increased by 420% since August 18, suggesting an intensive effort by the industry to manage public perception. This surge in positive coverage followed a political memo warning that public animosity toward the industry could hurt candidates in upcoming elections.

This rebranding is seen as part of a broader trend in the tech world where controversial policies or structures are repackaged with softer language. OpenAI's new approach is considered a component of its wide-ranging strategy to ensure that the physical presence of AI infrastructure gains social acceptance amidst growing environmental and economic concerns. Furthermore, reports of law enforcement monitoring social media for opposition to these centers and at least 37 arrests related to protests this year indicate a tightening of security around AI infrastructure.
