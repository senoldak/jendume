AI chatbots are rapidly taking over as the primary way people navigate the internet and conduct shopping research, raising serious consumer protection concerns. A new study conducted by Podqi has found that leading industry chatbots such as ChatGPT, Gemini, and Perplexity frequently direct shoppers to dangerous storefronts that impersonate real retailers, revive defunct brands, or sell counterfeit products.

The study involved testing about 151 different brands, ranging from small businesses to major names like Lululemon and Birkenstock, by asking specific questions regarding official regional websites and international shipping. The findings highlighted significant vulnerabilities in how AI models evaluate and recommend commercial storefronts, with smaller brands being particularly susceptible to being replaced by scam sites in chatbot responses.

According to the test results, Perplexity performed the worst, displaying fraudulent links in 15 percent of its outputs. ChatGPT placed in the middle with an 8 percent rate, while Gemini was the least likely to show dubious storefronts at 4 percent. Overall, 57 percent of the answers featuring a hazardous link endorsed it as a valid shopping destination, and 34 percent did so without any cautionary comments. Only 9 percent of the responses actively warned shoppers away from the dangerous links.

While traditional search engines can also be manipulated, AI chatbots present their responses as definitive answers while remaining opaque about their sourcing mechanisms. Security experts emphasize that because users tend to trust these tools uncritically, individuals must verify sources before entering sensitive personal or credit card information into unfamiliar online stores.
