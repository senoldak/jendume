London-based financial giant Barclays has decided to part ways with 5,000 employees as part of an artificial intelligence-supported restructuring process. Following the decision, 1,000 people have already lost their jobs, while an additional 4,000 employees are expected to be laid off by the end of the year. This transition primarily affects the bank's operations in the United Kingdom, with nearly 60 percent of the job cuts taking place in the software department. Furthermore, similar measures will be implemented in certain operational units operating in India.

These developments have intensified debates regarding the impact of artificial intelligence and digital transformation on employment within the banking industry. Bank management emphasized that these steps are part of a 3-year cost-saving program. CEO C.S. Venkatakrishnan stated that investments in technological infrastructure simplify operations, describing the role of artificial intelligence as the icing on the cake. Barclays management aims to achieve an additional 1.1 billion pounds in savings by 2027 through this transformation.

On the other hand, the bank reported a profit of 2.3 billion pounds for the three-month period ending in September, which fell short of market expectations. Meanwhile, labor unions strongly reacted to the move. Unite union general secretary Sharon Graham argued that the bank was using artificial intelligence as an excuse to discard workers, stating that the priority should be reskilling staff rather than leaving them unemployed. Analysts predict that such AI-driven transformations in the financial sector could spread to other banks as well.
