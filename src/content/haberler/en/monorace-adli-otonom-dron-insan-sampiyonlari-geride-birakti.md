Experts working at Delft University of Technology in the Netherlands have designed an artificial intelligence-based aircraft capable of outperforming the world's best human pilots. Named MonoRace, the autonomous system successfully navigated the racing track without relying on global positioning systems or human intervention. Utilizing only a single camera and internal motion sensors, the vehicle completed the course in a short time and drew significant attention. According to details shared by the researchers, the system proved unmatched in the autonomous drone organization held in Abu Dhabi. Demonstrating a superior performance compared to the times recorded by three former human world champions competing in the event, this technology opens the door to a new era in aviation.

The fundamental element that makes this aircraft extraordinary is the modesty of the hardware it utilizes. Unlike traditional systems, it requires no pre-made maps, external position tracking infrastructures, or complex stereo camera arrays. Instead, a single lens positioned at the front of the body and internal components measuring movement enable the perception of the surrounding environment. The brain of the MonoRace model processes raw data coming from the sensors through an advanced neural network architecture. The artificial intelligence algorithm instantly recognizes the gates on the track, calculates the instantaneous position and speed of the vehicle, and then generates commands directing the four propellers.

All these complex mathematical operations are repeated 500 times per second, while the entirety of the calculations is executed directly on the drone's own hardware without the need for an external computer. Weighing approximately 966 grams, this lightweight aircraft successfully cleared the course consisting of 11 gates over two laps in the Abu Dhabi competition, achieving a time of 16.56 seconds. The highest speed reached by the vehicle during the speed tests was recorded at approximately 102 kilometers per hour. Experts noted that the vehicle could complete sharp turns smoothly even at this speed.

The research team chose the reinforcement learning method to train the MonoRace model. The artificial intelligence experienced the most efficient flight maneuvers on its own by performing millions of trials in a virtual simulation environment. During the training process, the drone's weight, battery level, air resistance, and propeller conditions were continuously altered. Thus, it was ensured that the system did not remain dependent on a single fixed environment. This acquired experience establishes an important foundation for autonomous vehicles with different hardware to achieve similar successes in the future.
