A recent survey by Pew Research shows that around 50 percent of surveyed US adults have used an AI chatbot at some point, with 8 percent reporting they utilized various digital assistants to research candidates and policies prior to the elections. However, much of the information provided by these chatbots is out of date, hallucinated, or sourced from partisan websites.

Even prominent models developed by OpenAI, Anthropic, and Google have fallen short when answering questions about elections. Research by Forum AI found that nearly 40 percent of election-related answers given by mainstream chatbots contained at least one factual error. For instance, Google's platform misstated the mail-in ballot deadline in Pennsylvania, while competing models confused a North Carolina lawmaker with a former school superintendent.

Part of the core issue is that chatbots rely on an increasingly problematic pool of online data. NewsGuard research indicates that mainstream chatbots cite partisan websites disguised as mainstream news nearly 50 percent of the time when asked about midterm policies. OpenAI’s model cited these sites in over 70 percent of relevant searches, while xAI’s platform performed best by citing them in 30 percent of cases.

Experts note that consumers are largely unprepared to navigate elections in an era dominated by advanced software. Because these models are predictive and ingest data from questionable online sources, they frequently generate inaccurate information when factual answers are missing, raising serious concerns for public discourse.
