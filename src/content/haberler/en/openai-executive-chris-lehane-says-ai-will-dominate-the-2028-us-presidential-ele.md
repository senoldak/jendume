OpenAI Chief Global Affairs Officer Chris Lehane has released a comprehensive memo stating that artificial intelligence will be the primary issue of the 2028 US presidential election. The report emphasizes that political campaigns and candidates must outline a clear roadmap in this field, balancing rapid technological growth with robust control mechanisms.

The memo notes that the construction of data centers has become a focal point of political debate, particularly in states like Michigan, Ohio, Pennsylvania, and Texas. A significant portion of voters reportedly supports ongoing AI development while demanding stronger safety measures.

Following the electoral events, OpenAI is preparing to launch a new policy push in Congress advocating for enforceable safety standards and mandatory rules. The company also proposes community agreements and state laws giving local residents a say in data center developments. Operations at the corporate technical site in Piketon, Ohio will require thousands of qualified professionals to support infrastructure expansion.

The national security dimension and the technology race with China are also highlighted in the report. Lehane compared opposing a moratorium to past significant legislative votes. Polling cited in the memo shows that 66% support continued development, including a majority who demand enhanced protective measures, while a smaller portion favors a temporary halt.

Experts point out that the future of AI infrastructure must be carefully managed in terms of energy consumption and societal impact. OpenAI also wants its data centers called compute campuses to better describe the scale and role of the infrastructure.

Ultimately, the discussion around AI infrastructure is reshaping political alliances and public discourse, setting the stage for a new social contract in the digital age.
