Canadian publisher Les Éditions du Boréal has announced it is ending its professional relationship with writer Thélyson Orélien. The decision came after an independent investigation confirmed that artificial intelligence was used in the writing process of his novel. The author, whose book was removed from France's prestigious Prix Goncourt literary prize list last month, had previously denied the allegations.

The publisher stated that new information confirmed over recent days indicated artificial intelligence was utilized. Boréal noted that it had initially tried to work with the author to gather evidence of innocence, but after those efforts proved inconclusive and the author declined to provide further information, an outside party was hired to analyze various drafts of the novel. Pangram AI detector tests had previously indicated a high probability of computer-generated text.

Author Thélyson Orélien has maintained his innocence, stating that he only used tools for research and foreign idioms, and insisting that no sentence in the book was created by a machine. He stated he began writing the book years before modern generative artificial intelligence services became widely available. The migration-themed novel had been scheduled for translation in more than twenty countries.

This incident underscores a broader trust crisis within the literary and artistic community regarding the integration of artificial intelligence into creative workflows. As publishers and award committees face mounting challenges in verifying originality, the case highlights an urgent need for transparency and new standards regarding artificial intelligence assistance in publishing.
