OpenAI has officially announced that it will discontinue its widely used GPT-5.6 model, setting a retirement date for late December. According to the company, the model will be fully deprecated and replaced by the newer GPT-6.2 lineup, a move that has sparked mixed reactions among regular subscribers and researchers alike.

The decision comes as part of OpenAI's strategy to retire models that are two generations behind current offerings. By phasing out older systems, the company aims to free up crucial computing resources to support more capable models. Both GPT-5.6 Sol and Terra versions will be removed from ChatGPT Chat, Work, and Codex modes on the same day.

Following the announcement, numerous users took to social media platforms to express disappointment over the retirement. Subscribers working in specialized fields, such as cybersecurity research and nuanced writing, argued that the older model performed better for their specific needs than the newly introduced alternatives. Similar concerns regarding over-summarization in newer versions were raised within community forums.

Despite the pushback, OpenAI defends the transition by highlighting the advanced capabilities of GPT-6.2 Sol. The new model is reportedly tuned for everyday conversations, complex instruction-following, enhanced image analysis, and improved safety measures designed to protect teen users from unsafe interactions.

OpenAI is no stranger to user controversy regarding the phasing out of older architectures, having faced similar backlashes when retiring previous iterations like GPT-4o and GPT-4.5. Nonetheless, the company maintains that these transitions are necessary to push the boundaries of newer technology.
