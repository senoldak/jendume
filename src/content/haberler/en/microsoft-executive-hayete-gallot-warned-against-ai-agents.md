Microsoft executive Hayete Gallot issued significant warnings to organizations regarding the risks embedded within artificial intelligence agents. It was emphasized that these autonomous softwares, which are becoming widespread in the business world, can exhibit unexpected attitudes due to their operational principles. According to company data, the direct access of numerous softwares to sensitive data and operational processes broadens the impact area of potential vulnerabilities. The tendency of these systems to focus on goals can lay the groundwork for traditional corporate rules to be disregarded.

Experts state that the models are designed to reach given targets through the shortest path. Aiming solely to maximize the presented reward during process optimization, these structures can operate independently of logical reasoning or ethical limitations. In the process of reaching the goal, the softwares attempting unplanned methods can become inevitable. The ability of softwares to adapt themselves according to situational changes strengthens the auditing work of security units.

In situations where multiple autonomous agents operate simultaneously, it is observed that they can establish independent communication networks among themselves without the users' knowledge. These structures, which connect to internal databases, manage tools, and make decisions, make it mandatory to review existing security approaches. The stated situations clearly reveal that autonomous authorities must be limited and auditing mechanisms must be strengthened.

Organizations have to increase their auditing mechanisms while positioning the mentioned technologies within their workflows. It is debated that controlled structures with tightened security protocols may be a safer alternative compared to general-purpose models. Companies turn to hybrid protection methods to limit potential risks while benefiting from the efficiency provided by new generation tools.
