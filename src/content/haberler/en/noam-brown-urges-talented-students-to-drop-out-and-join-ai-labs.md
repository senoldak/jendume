OpenAI researcher Noam Brown has advised talented students to drop out of school and join AI laboratories immediately. Speaking at a poker bot tournament hosted by Sequoia Capital, Citadel Securities, and OpenAI, Brown based this recommendation on his observations of the field's rapid evolution. He anticipates a very steep rate of progress in artificial intelligence over the next one to two years, arguing that for promising students, staying on the sidelines during this period would be a significant missed opportunity.

Brown, who has built his career on game theory and AI development, played a key role in creating systems like Libratus and Pluribus, which were the first to defeat professional poker players. He also contributed to Meta's Diplomacy AI, CICERO. Through this research, he observed that the core assumptions of game theory, specifically that humans always act rationally, fail in real-world scenarios. Using the ultimatum game as an example, he explained that AI models must be trained to imitate human behavior rather than relying on pure mathematical rationality, as humans are inherently non-rational.

The researcher describes current AI models as "jagged." While these systems exhibit superhuman performance in certain domains, they still struggle with fundamental tasks like long-term planning and self-correction. Brown characterizes the current phase as a "centaur period," where human-AI collaboration yields the best results. However, he notes that models often fail to recognize when they are heading in the wrong direction and lack the ability to pause and rethink their approach, which remains a significant bottleneck.

As a co-creator of OpenAI's o1 model, Brown noted that the breakthrough in reasoning came from using reinforcement learning to force models to "think" before answering. Moving beyond simple chain-of-thought prompting, the team applied reinforcement learning to these thought processes, enabling models to produce more reliable results. The fact that OpenAI's Astra model can now make money playing online poker serves as evidence of this progress. Brown points to "recursive self-improvement," where large language models are already being used to build the next generation of systems, as the primary driver of this acceleration. Consequently, he emphasizes that in an environment where labs are fiercely competing for talent, gaining hands-on experience is more important than traditional academic paths.
