Mistral AI has released its new trillion-parameter model, Mistral Large 4, also known as Le Chonk, as an open-weights model. This release comes despite warnings that the model could potentially increase cyber attack risks. The company acknowledged that during the public preview process, it observed live traffic and noted that the model exhibited potential capabilities that could assist in cyber attacks. Making the model weights accessible globally has reignited the debate between openness and safety in the AI industry.

While the company claims a 96.6% success rate in rejecting harmful prompts, security experts emphasize that hosting the model locally brings significant responsibilities. Pierre Stock, the VP of Science at Mistral, noted that while there are security measures integrated into the model weights, updates will only apply to the managed API. This means that users running the model on their own servers will not automatically benefit from future security patches.

This development redefines the balance between openness and safety in the AI world. Security experts emphasize that local hosting shifts the entire responsibility for security onto enterprises. While API-based versions receive automatic patches, those running the model on their own infrastructure must manage their own security protocols. As the European Union continues to oversee regulations, the audit processes for models with potential systemic risks are ongoing. Although Mistral announced it would publish technical reports on its evaluation methods, it remains unclear how the model will be classified under these regulations.

For corporate users, this model offers data sovereignty but mandates additional security measures, such as rigorous penetration testing and strict access controls, to mitigate potential vulnerabilities. Experts advise that enterprises should treat this tool not as an independent decision-maker, but as a component restricted by intensive supervision mechanisms. Isolated environments, the principle of least privilege, and human-in-the-loop code verification are critical to minimizing potential cyber risks.
