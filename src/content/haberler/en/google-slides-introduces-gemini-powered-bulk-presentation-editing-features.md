Google has expanded the workspace of its Gemini AI model within Google Slides, introducing a new capability that lets users modify multiple pages or an entire presentation through a single prompt. According to the company's announcement, operations are initiated from the side panel, where the system determines which pages require updates and applies edits directly to local slides. As a result, the generated output is not just a static image.

With this update, users can collectively update fonts and colors across an entire file, refresh content based on another document, or merge several pages together. For instance, aligning an existing presentation with the visual style of another project or expanding specific topics using a source file stored in cloud storage becomes entirely feasible. This approach aims to minimize repetitive tasks associated with editing individual pages.

However, applying prompts to the entire file does not mean the output is instantly ready for publication. Reviewing modified pages remains essential, particularly for corporate presentations containing dates, prices, and product details. Specifying sections to be updated and information to be preserved before launching the bulk process can make reviewing the final result much easier.

The rollout of the feature started in late October and may take more than two weeks to appear for eligible accounts. Supported subscription tiers include specific business and educational plans. The company stated it will offer higher promotional limits for multi-slide presentation creation to workspace customers until December 1, 2026, after which per-user limits will apply. Since operations are treated as user edits, undo and version history remain fully accessible.
