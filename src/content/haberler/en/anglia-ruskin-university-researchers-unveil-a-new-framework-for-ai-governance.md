The rapid advancement of artificial intelligence technologies continues to spark intense global debate regarding safety, regulation, and ethical control. Addressing these complex challenges, researchers Dr. Kirti Prakash and Dr. Ian van der Linde from the Advanced Computing Research Centre at Anglia Ruskin University have introduced a novel governance proposal. Published in the journal AI and Ethics, the framework moves beyond the traditional debate of simply accelerating or slowing down AI development.

The authors argue that treating AI risk as a single problem is insufficient because AI capabilities cannot be easily contained within a single jurisdiction or company, and existing models can often be replicated without retraining frontier systems. To tackle this, they introduce the Four As framework, which stands for Action, Access, Authority, and Allocation.

The model addresses four fundamental questions: whether humans can effectively constrain consequential actions, who should be permitted to access potentially harmful capabilities, which judgments may be delegated to AI systems, and how the resulting benefits and burdens should be distributed across society. By matching each ethical obligation to an appropriate form of governance, the framework assigns specific roles to developers, regulators, and public institutions.

Experts emphasize that as automated AI research accelerates, establishing robust governance becomes increasingly urgent. Recent policy initiatives in the United Kingdom, such as specialized biosecurity labs utilizing trusted access to advanced models from leading tech companies, illustrate how modern governance focuses heavily on deployment and secure access rather than merely restricting technological creation.
