For global companies, artificial intelligence is a matter of sovereignty. Where data resides, who holds the encryption keys, and who operates the system are critical design decisions, especially in highly regulated industries. A recent analysis has evaluated how cloud providers stack up against these "sovereign AI" criteria. Google leads with a score of 3.45, followed by Oracle at 3.25. IBM and Microsoft Azure both scored 3.0, while AWS trails with a score of 2.7, largely due to limitations in offering isolated, customer-managed environments. These scores do not tell the whole story. A significant portion of the surveyed senior technology executives have yet to perform essential security tests, such as "disconnection" or "key revocation" scenarios. This reveals a gap between theoretical compliance and practical readiness, leaving many organizations vulnerable. Companies are caught in a balancing act between the capabilities of advanced AI models and the necessity of adhering to local regulations. While many organizations mandate that AI inference must occur within regional borders, this often comes at the cost of accessing the most powerful models. Sovereignty is not a simple "purity test" but a complex risk management process. Instead of relying solely on provider scores, organizations must implement compensatory controls and thoroughly evaluate legal risks to ensure their sensitive workloads remain secure and compliant. Even providers with lower scores remain popular due to their service quality, but organizations must proceed with a clear understanding and management of these inherent risks.
