AI developer Anthropic has officially rolled out an updated usage policy designed to restrict sustained and needless abusive behavior toward its Claude chatbot. The new guidelines do not target everyday user frustration or creative writing disagreements, but rather focus on extreme cases of repeated cruelty toward the models. Under this policy, Anthropic has empowered Claude to end conversations with persistently abusive users on platforms like Claude.ai and Claude Code, a capability initially tested for AI welfare research.

The policy update coincides with fresh research shedding light on how frequently users lose their temper with conversational AI. According to an analysis of over eight million messages by AI visibility platform Searchable, 33 percent of American chatbot users have sent at least one swear word or insult to ChatGPT, Gemini, or Claude. The primary trigger for derogatory language was chatbots failing to follow instructions, followed by incorrect answers and misunderstood requests.

Interestingly, the research revealed that swearing at an AI does little to resolve the underlying problem. Only 24 percent of complaints containing abusive language were resolved by the chatbot's subsequent response, compared to 35 percent of complaints expressed in plain language. Experts suggest that pointing out specific errors gives the AI something concrete to fix, whereas insults offer no constructive path forward.

Anthropic's new rules do not demand endless politeness or require users to say please and thank you. Instead, the company draws a clear line between normal frustration and repeated, unnecessary abuse. While users increasingly expect flawless performance from capable models, understanding how to communicate effectively remains key to a productive AI experience.
