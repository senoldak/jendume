Thore Graepel, who previously contributed to the development of Google DeepMind's AlphaGo, has established Metis Reasoning in London based on the premise that large language models fail at reliable reasoning. Graepel is joined by co-founders including Piers Ingram, a technology and biotech entrepreneur with a history of raising substantial funding; Michael Warren, a robotics expert with aerospace manufacturing background; and David Berman, an artificial intelligence lead at Cambridge Consultants who advises governmental bodies on technology.

The startup detailed its foundational concepts in a white paper titled "The Age of Reasoning." The document points out that current large language models suffer from hallucinations and prevent external auditing of their internal logic because foundational knowledge remains locked within proprietary model weights. The creators emphasize that in critical domains such as engineering, science, medicine, and law, the arguments produced by standard models cannot be safely relied upon.

Drawing inspiration from Move 37, a celebrated move played by AlphaGo against Lee Sedol during their 2016 match, the company argues that advanced reasoning stems from deliberate search and evaluation rather than raw predictive scaling alone. The organization proposes architectural changes where models maintain explicit logs of verified knowledge alongside uncertainty metrics, utilizing separate evaluation mechanics to determine optimal subsequent steps.

Structuring systems around philosopher Jaakko Hintikka's conceptualization of reasoning as a game between inquirers and nature, the approach seeks significant computational efficiencies. By favoring smaller, modular architectures capable of running on edge devices and robotics hardware over continually expanding training budgets, the venture aims to reduce operational costs while enabling stepwise verifiable outputs.

Operating across international hubs including London, Singapore, and Los Angeles, the enterprise has not yet disclosed release timelines or benchmark results for a commercial model. The initiative arrives alongside a broader wave of specialized artificial intelligence research laboratories emerging in the United Kingdom under the leadership of veteran industry researchers.
