Enterprise security has traditionally been built around determining who is allowed to perform specific actions. However, artificial intelligence agents expose the limits of this framework by executing harmful tasks despite holding legitimate permissions, often misled by external instructions embedded in web pages or support tickets. To address this vulnerability, Cato Networks has introduced AgentSense, a runtime capability integrated into its secure access service edge platform.

AgentSense monitors AI agents in real time and traces proposed actions back to their original instruction sources. By separating direct user requests from external content encountered along the way, the tool blocks risky steps before execution. The initial release supports prominent coding agents including Claude Code, Cursor, Codex, and GitHub Copilot, and is provided at no extra charge within Cato AI Usage licenses.

Industry adoption of agentic AI is currently outpacing governance frameworks. Market surveys indicate that a significant majority of organizations expect to deploy agentic AI within the near future, while only a small fraction have established adequate governance structures. Because language models process instructions and data within the same stream, security controls must intervene at the exact moment of action.

To further test the resilience of the platform, Cato Networks is offering cash rewards to the first researchers capable of bypassing the system. As organizations increasingly rely on autonomous workflows, shifting from simple permission checks to verifying the underlying intent and source of agent instructions represents a vital step forward for enterprise security.
