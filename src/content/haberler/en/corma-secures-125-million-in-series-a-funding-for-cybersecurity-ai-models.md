Corma has completed a $125 million funding round to develop specialized artificial intelligence models and autonomous agents for the cybersecurity sector. While the offensive capabilities of general-purpose AI models are evolving rapidly, the defense side is struggling to keep pace. The company is developing foundational models trained specifically on security data to bridge this gap.

Founded in 2025 and operating out of Tel Aviv and San Francisco, the startup transforms these models into autonomous agents that work alongside corporate security teams. These agents analyze network traffic, security logs, and configuration data to detect threats and manage security processes. The company aims to offload complex tasks traditionally performed by security teams entirely to these AI agents.

Following its emergence from stealth mode, the company increased its server capacity 24-fold in just three months to meet high demand from large enterprises. With this Series A round, the company's total funding has reached $185 million, and its valuation has climbed to $1 billion.

This development highlights the growing investor interest in the intersection of cybersecurity and artificial intelligence. According to industry data, investments in cybersecurity startups reached $4.7 billion in the first nine months of 2026, surpassing the total investment level of the previous year. The new funding will be used to scale foundational models and increase processing capacity.
