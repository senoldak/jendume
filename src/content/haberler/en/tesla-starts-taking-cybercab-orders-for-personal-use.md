Tesla has announced that it has started sending out invitations to select customers to purchase the Cybercab, its fully autonomous vehicle designed without a steering wheel, gas pedals, or brakes. While the automaker previously focused on selling vehicles to entities that could build its robotaxi network, this latest move introduces personal ownership options for the autonomous car.

The vehicle is priced at $29,990, but customers are required to pay a monthly fee of $299 for an Autonomy Subscription to operate the car, with the first month offered for free. Tesla notes that these vehicles will initially only operate within designated geofenced locations where the company currently provides its robotaxi services, limiting their use to specific regions.

The transition of the Cybercab to personal use represents a major shift in the automotive industry, as fully autonomous vehicles move beyond corporate-run robotaxi fleets into the hands of individual consumers. This step could reshape how personal transportation and vehicle ownership are viewed in the near future.

Meanwhile, the rollout faces regulatory scrutiny. The National Highway Traffic Safety Administration previously opened an audit query into the self-certification process used by Tesla for vehicles lacking conventional manual controls. With estimated deliveries targeted for December 2026, the progress of personal Cybercab deployments remains closely watched by both consumers and regulators.
