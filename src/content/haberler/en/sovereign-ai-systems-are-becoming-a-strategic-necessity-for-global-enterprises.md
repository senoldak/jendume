Global enterprises, particularly those in highly regulated industries, are entering a new phase in managing their artificial intelligence systems. This approach, known as "sovereign AI," addresses fundamental questions about where data resides, who holds the encryption keys, and which models interact with sensitive information. Organizations are now evaluating how much control they need over the systems that produce their intelligence and the potential jurisdictional, operational, and commercial risks of ceding that control to third-party providers.

Recent studies reveal a significant gap between buyer priorities and operational reality. While technology decision-makers in regulated sectors express high concern regarding legal exposures, many have yet to implement rigorous testing for disconnected systems or exit scenarios. Instead of deploying dedicated sovereign offerings, most organizations opt to add compensating controls to their existing platforms, primarily due to the cost and burden of managing additional environments.

The "inference" stage of AI, where the model processes data to generate results, has become the focal point of this architectural debate. A vast majority of surveyed leaders now require inference to occur within sovereign or regional boundaries. This creates a strategic tradeoff: companies must decide whether to accept the risks of operating outside these boundaries to access superior model capabilities or to prioritize local compliance with potentially more limited tools. Sovereign AI index scores show the varying capabilities of major cloud providers.

Google leads the index with a score of 3.45, followed by Oracle at 3.25. IBM and Microsoft both score 3.0, while AWS follows at 2.7. Huawei and Alibaba currently score 1.2. These metrics serve as a starting point for evaluation, but they do not dictate the final choice. Organizations must weigh these scores against their specific workload requirements and local legal mandates. Sovereignty is a deliberate process of risk assessment, requiring companies to understand their exposure and implement necessary controls to mitigate it.
