The chatbot platform Character.AI initiated Chapter 11 bankruptcy proceedings, revealing plans to sell the company and its extensive user data assets to address ongoing financial challenges. A newly formed entity named Smirks and Pangs Acquisition LLC emerged as the initial bidder seeking to acquire the data for $10 million. The attempt by Character.AI to keep the backer's identity hidden sparked immediate backlash, leading 42 state attorneys general to move to block the sale over severe concerns regarding the privacy of sensitive information belonging to underage users who interacted with the platform. Following a judge's order requiring transparency, the prospective buyer was identified as the New York-based investment firm Octave Advisors.

State attorneys general continue to push back against the auction, pointing out that the database under scrutiny encompasses the private chat histories of more than 21 million American accounts, with 2.1 million belonging directly to minors. Meanwhile, Disney has submitted a separate stalking horse bid of $20 million to license Character.AI's intellectual property and hire the majority of its workforce as part of a parallel transaction. As the auction process moves forward despite mounting legal resistance, the controversy involves regulatory and ethical challenges surrounding the handling of user data when AI startups fail.
