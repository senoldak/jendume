As artificial intelligence tools and advanced calculators become widespread in daily life, the place of foundational sciences in school curriculums is once again under discussion. Responding to recent arguments that children should focus primarily on fluent speech and communication skills, mathematical society presidents argued that this should not come at the expense of mathematics education. Experts highlighted that mathematical reasoning is vital for testing the reliability of answers produced by AI in seconds and for distinguishing accurate outcomes.

Mathematics education goes far beyond performing numerical calculations, equipping individuals with the ability to solve unfamiliar problems, analyze evidence, and make logical inferences. In an era where technology-generated information is surging, public numerical literacy stands out as a fundamental requirement to question these answers and make informed decisions. Government curriculum and assessment reviews similarly recognize that core subjects will remain pivotal, highlighting the growing importance of critical thinking alongside digital skills in an AI-shaped world.

From an economic perspective, mathematical sciences continue to generate substantial value. Analyses by academic institutions show that millions of people work in mathematical-science occupations, contributing significantly to overall economic value. In this context, ensuring that young generations develop strong mathematical and communicative skills simultaneously is deemed essential for individual potential, future productivity, and societal prosperity.

Ultimately, experts conclude that educational systems in the age of artificial intelligence should embrace a balanced approach. Just as language skills are valuable for social cohesion, mathematical reasoning forms an indispensable foundation for navigating the complexities of a technology-driven world.
