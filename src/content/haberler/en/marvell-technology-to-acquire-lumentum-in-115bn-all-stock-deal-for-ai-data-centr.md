Marvell Technology has agreed to acquire Lumentum in an all-stock transaction valuing the optical components maker at $115 billion, including debt. Announced on Monday, the deal represents the largest equity-based acquisition of 2026 and the largest ever in the global technology sector outside of the SpaceX and xAI merger. Under the terms, Lumentum shareholders will receive 4.5 Marvell shares for each share they own.

The valuation translates to approximately $1,132 per Lumentum share, representing a premium of about 23.9% over the closing price on October 28, the final trading day before talks were reported. Upon completion, Marvell shareholders will own roughly 67% of the combined entity, with Lumentum shareholders holding the remaining 33%. Both corporate boards approved the transaction unanimously.

Copper connections are reaching their physical limits as AI systems expand, forcing data centres to adopt optical links. Lumentum produces high-performance lasers and optical transceivers, while Marvell designs custom AI chips and network switches. The combined company expects to target a $550 billion market and anticipates achieving more than $1 billion annually in cost savings.

The transaction requires regulatory and shareholder approvals and is expected to close in the first half of 2028. Matt Murphy will remain as chairman and CEO of Marvell, while three Lumentum directors, including CEO Michael Hurlston, will join Marvell's board. Earlier this year, Nvidia invested $2 billion in each company.
