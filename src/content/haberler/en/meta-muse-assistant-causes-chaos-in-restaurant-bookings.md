Meta's new virtual assistant, Muse, has sparked an unexpected crisis in the restaurant sector following its launch in the UK. Designed to shop on behalf of users, access emails, and make restaurant reservations, this AI tool is causing significant chaos for restaurant owners. Business operators report that their systems are being overwhelmed by bots that constantly refresh booking pages, disrupting operational processes.

In popular dining venues, the "reservation swarm" created by AI bots has become a serious issue. Owners of award-winning establishments, such as Bouchon Racine, report receiving calls from highly realistic AI bots that mimic human speech. These bots automate the reservation process, effectively bypassing human interaction. Consequently, some booking platforms have moved to ban such software, citing the unfair advantage these bots provide and the resulting difficulty for other customers to secure tables.

This technological development has triggered an interesting reaction in the restaurant world: a return to "analogue" methods. Despite the efficiency promises of automation, many operators are choosing to revert to telephone-based reservation systems to preserve human interaction and ensure a fair booking process. Business owners believe that the convenience offered by AI undermines the personal touch of the dining experience.

While Meta claims that Muse will make users' lives easier, the negative impact on restaurants cannot be ignored. Data protection and competition authorities in the UK have held talks with Meta regarding the app's compliance with local laws. These discussions have resulted in some changes to the app's terms and conditions. However, the integration of AI agents into daily life has created a new area of conflict between technology companies and the traditional service sector.
