Websites and public digital services across parts of Europe have recently gone dark as administrators deliberately shut them down to avert security risks. Government portals and digital ID login systems in the Netherlands and Finland, alongside specific Wikimedia services, were temporarily taken offline. Rather than direct cyber-attacks, these shutdowns stem from severe security vulnerabilities found in PHP, a foundational programming language used by about 70 percent of tracked websites.

The underlying vulnerabilities effectively gave malicious actors an open window to infiltrate online stores, access passwords, and harvest payment information. The PHP Foundation acknowledged that its developers first identified the flaw capable of disrupting the internet in 2024, yet fixing it was delayed because it was deemed too costly and complex, representing a structural failure caused by inadequate funding and resources.

Artificial intelligence fundamentally changed the scope of this threat. Researchers working alongside OpenAI and Wiz demonstrated how easily AI tools can identify and exploit such software weaknesses. Malicious hackers quickly followed suit, with Cloudflare reporting millions of exploit attempts against online systems within a short timeframe.

Automated systems driven by artificial intelligence are drastically lowering the cost of discovering flaws across legacy code bases. Documented internet-wide vulnerabilities escalated from approximately 48,000 in 2025 to over 84,000 in 2026. Scoring 9.2 out of 10 on severity scales, this latest PHP flaw demonstrates the mounting pressure on under-resourced open-source maintainers tasked with safeguarding global infrastructure.
