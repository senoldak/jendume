METR has conducted a detailed review of the "Risks from automated R&D" section within Anthropic's August 2026 Risk Report. This assessment is part of a broader evaluation of AI R&D acceleration, where METR examined private data to understand how models might influence the pace of scientific discovery. While METR agrees with Anthropic that models deployed by July 15, 2026, posed a low risk of causing acute catastrophic outcomes, they argue that the company's conclusion regarding "early warning thresholds" is based on contestable judgments that lack clarity.

Anthropic's Responsible Scaling Policy (RSP) establishes two specific capability thresholds for automated R&D. The first is the "Full Substitution Threshold," which triggers if a model can fully replace the entire set of research staff at competitive costs. The second is the "2x Acceleration Threshold," defined by a dramatic increase in the pace of AI progress attributable to automation. Anthropic maintains that these thresholds have not been crossed, citing evidence that human researchers are still essential to their operations. However, METR suggests that the public report understates the level of acceleration actually occurring.

This debate is significant because it touches on the core of AI safety: the potential for recursive self-improvement. If AI models can automate the research process itself, the speed of technological progress could become uncontrollable. Such acceleration could shift power structures between nations, potentially enabling disruptive advances in areas ranging from cyberwarfare to energy. METR's critique highlights the necessity of transparency in how AI labs define and report their safety metrics. As these models become more capable, the judgment calls made by developers regarding "early warning" signs will become increasingly vital for global security and the long-term alignment of these powerful systems with human interests.
