AstraZeneca and Oracle will use artificial intelligence and real patient health data to find participants for drug trials more rapidly. The two firms signed a non-binding term sheet, and the financial terms have not yet been disclosed or finalized. AstraZeneca brings its expertise in drug science and clinical trials, while Oracle contributes cloud servers, artificial intelligence tools, and anonymized hospital patient data. Patient recruitment is widely recognized as one of the slowest parts of developing a new medicine, causing trials to run longer and incur higher costs. The companies aim to help physicians identify potential study candidates sooner while utilizing the data for early disease detection and drug safety monitoring. The initial program will operate in Abu Dhabi alongside M42, an enterprise located in the United Arab Emirates, focusing on patients with uncontrolled high blood pressure. Subsequent projects are planned in the United States, the Middle East, and Europe, targeting conditions including oncology, cardiovascular disorders, nephrology, respiratory illnesses, and uncommon medical disorders. AstraZeneca emphasized that patient identities will remain confidential and each project will only proceed following strict legal, privacy, and ethics reviews. Furthermore, the pharmaceutical firm collaborates with alternative artificial intelligence enterprises like Insitro to discover novel targets for hepatic and cardiac conditions.
