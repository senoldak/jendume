Google is expanding advertising coverage for its AI Mode search experience to a broader global audience, officially announcing that the feature will roll out across international markets. During the company's third-quarter earnings call, executives acknowledged that this new Gemini-based search experience generates lower revenue compared to traditional search methods. Consequently, even as query volumes grew rapidly, the financial returns failed to match the scale of engagement.

Following the earnings report, Alphabet shares fell by approximately 12 percent, wiping out over 500 billion dollars from Google's total market value. Stock prices hovered around 280 dollars, remaining just a few points above the low value recorded at 273.14 dollars. Financial analysts noted that this steep decline marks the second-largest loss following Nvidia's 589 billion dollar drop in 2025. Investors are scrutinizing massive capital expenditures directed toward data center infrastructure, worrying whether heavy investments could leave tech giants vulnerable if market conditions shift.

Market observers point out that the drop is fundamentally tied to third-quarter earnings falling short of expectations rather than solely originating from the AI search mode itself. Traditional search ads still account for roughly half of the company's total revenue, keeping pressure on management to balance old revenue streams with new computational costs. Investors frequently question whether Google can monetize AI search sufficiently before conventional search demand declines.

Despite these financial hurdles, company leadership expressed satisfaction with early advertising trials and ongoing platform engagement. Google highlighted that AI Mode and AI Overviews keep users on the platform three times longer than traditional search, despite sending less overall traffic to the open web. Users in the United States are beginning to see multi-turn ads during follow-up queries, and the company reports that sponsored product listings achieve click-through rates comparable to organic results. Furthermore, Google plans to expand ads into additional regions including Brazil, Mexico, and the United Kingdom during the fourth quarter.
