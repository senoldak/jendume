Music streaming platforms are taking proactive measures to manage the tidal wave of artificial intelligence-generated tracks flooding their systems. Google-owned YouTube Music has announced that it will actively flag songs created primarily through generative AI, attaching descriptive labels and pop-up windows to its player page. Meanwhile, music streaming service Deezer is going a step further by introducing a dedicated toggle before the end of October 2026, allowing users to exclude AI-generated music entirely from their feeds.

Both companies noted a clear user demand for transparency regarding artificial tracks. Deezer Chief Executive Officer Alexis Lanternier stated that the explosion of AI content impacts the entire music ecosystem and emphasized the importance of fostering a future centered around transparency, artist respect, and user choice. The streaming service described the situation as a critical challenge, noting that AI tracks can negatively impact human creators' revenues and make up a massive share of problematic streams.

Deezer reports that it has already detected and removed over forty-two million AI-generated tracks from its recommendation system and tagged more than fourteen million albums as machine-made. The platform's detection tool processes around ninety thousand fully AI-generated tracks submitted daily and recently earned recognition among top inventions. However, Deezer has also acknowledged that its detection system still faces challenges regarding audio manipulation and emerging generation models.

While YouTube Music currently focuses on labeling rather than active removal, Deezer's upcoming toggle offers listeners absolute control over their audio experience. These developments highlight how major streaming platforms are evolving to balance technological innovation with the protection of human creativity in the digital music industry.
