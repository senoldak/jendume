Booking.com Chief Security Officer Marnie Wilking issued a warning at the Web Summit in Lisbon regarding the impact of AI on cybersecurity. According to Wilking, AI has the capability to scan a platform's code and find minor bugs that may have remained unnoticed for five or ten years. The shift is that AI can now figure out how to 'chain' multiple harmless flaws together to find a way through systems or perpetrate large-scale fraud.

Wilking noted that attackers ramped up efforts in the travel and hospitality sector following the post-pandemic travel surge. The release of ChatGPT in late 2022 provided these attackers with the tools to send phishing messages at scale, featuring flawless grammar and specific personal details. By picking up hints from social media posts about upcoming trips, attackers create highly convincing messages that are increasingly difficult for users to distinguish from legitimate communications.

To counter these evolving threats, Booking.com has implemented mandatory two-factor authentication (2FA) for both its partners and guests. The platform utilizes machine learning to verify if a login attempt is normal by checking the device, browser, and IP address. If any anomaly is detected, the system triggers additional security steps before allowing payments. Wilking observed that as major platforms strengthen their defenses, attackers are shifting their focus toward smaller property management systems and travel agencies within the wider ecosystem. AI has initiated a high-stakes 'cat-and-mouse game' in cybersecurity.

Booking.com’s own security teams are now using AI to scan their code repositories for vulnerabilities before attackers can find them. Wilking emphasized that regardless of whether society chooses to slow down AI development, malicious actors will continue to utilize the technology, making it imperative for defenders to stay ahead using the same advanced tools.
