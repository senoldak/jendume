Michigan Senate candidate Abdul El-Sayed has unveiled a comprehensive reform plan titled "AI Under Democracy," aimed at overseeing the development and societal impact of artificial intelligence. El-Sayed's proposal advocates for public appointees or democratically elected members to hold a majority of seats on the boards of influential AI companies, ensuring that public interest is prioritized in corporate decision-making. This model aims to grant the public veto power over critical decisions, such as datacenter development or biological weapons manufacturing.

One of the most notable aspects of the plan is the restriction of financial control that tech giants exert over AI ventures. The proposal calls for legacy companies like Google, Microsoft, Meta, and Amazon to divest or abandon their investments in frontier AI companies such as OpenAI and Anthropic. El-Sayed argues that the current ownership structure creates an untenable friction between revenue-generation prerogatives and public safety.

Furthermore, the plan focuses on the environmental and societal impacts of datacenters, proposing to slow down the volume of developments, facilitate local job creation, and freeze residential utility costs. El-Sayed has been particularly vocal about large-scale datacenter projects in Michigan, emphasizing the need to contain the environmental and socioeconomic footprint of AI infrastructure.

Public opinion polls indicate growing concern regarding AI. Surveys show that a significant majority of Americans are worried about AI posing a threat to human existence and support the idea of AI companies transferring a portion of their stock to public sovereign wealth funds. El-Sayed's approach is rooted in the "first, do no harm" principle, arguing that AI development must be subject to democratic oversight mechanisms to ensure it aligns with the public interest.
