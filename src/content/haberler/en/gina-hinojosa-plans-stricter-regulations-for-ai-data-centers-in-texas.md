Texas has historically been recognized as one of the territories most welcoming to digital infrastructure, with numerous new facilities emerging rapidly over the past few years. However, mounting public opposition and political shifts are poised to change this landscape. Speaking in an interview, Texas Governor-elect Gina Hinojosa stated that any work with artificial intelligence companies must be a partnership that benefits Texans, noting that data centers have moved into mostly rural communities without adequate rules to protect residents.

Aimed at changing this situation, Hinojosa campaigned with the pledge of enforcing a temporary standstill on new facility approvals. Emphasizing that local populations must be included in discussions regarding whether and how these projects are established, she asserted that companies have been taking advantage of Texas and that this practice will stop under her administration.

Public sentiment in Texas appears aligned with her stance. A public opinion survey indicated that three quarters of participants residing in Texas objected to the construction of facilities in proximity to their homes. This resistance is not unique to Texas, as opposition to facility expansion grows across the United States, potentially raising questions about the future plans of major artificial intelligence companies. For instance, Anthropic noted in recent regulatory filings that its infrastructure supply chain is highly complex and any delays could significantly impact operations. While the exact shape of Hinojosa's new approach remains unclear, the rapid expansion in Texas appears to be coming to an end.
