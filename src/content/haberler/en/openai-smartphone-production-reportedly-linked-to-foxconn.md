OpenAI is moving forward with plans to develop its first AI-focused smartphone, reportedly securing a partnership with industry giant Foxconn. According to reports from supply chain sources in China, Foxconn’s subsidiary, FIH Mobile, has been tapped to handle the assembly of this device. While an official announcement has yet to be made, this development confirms OpenAI's intention to transition from the software realm into the hardware ecosystem.

This device is expected to offer more than standard smartphone functionality, promising a user experience where artificial intelligence is deeply integrated into the hardware. Unlike traditional smartphones, this device is anticipated to feature advanced environment-sensing AI capabilities and a custom-designed processor. This represents a shift, moving AI from cloud-based systems directly into our pockets as an assistant that understands the physical world. For users, this means digital assistants could evolve from passive tools into proactive companions that observe their surroundings and offer real-time solutions.

The target release window for the device is set for late 2027 or 2028. Foxconn’s extensive experience in manufacturing iPhones suggests that OpenAI has chosen a capable partner to bring this project to life. These claims, previously hinted at by analyst Ming-Chi Kuo, are now being reinforced by supply chain reports. While details regarding production volume, target markets, or specific technical hardware specifications remain unconfirmed, the tech world is monitoring this initiative.

This move is part of a trend where AI companies are no longer content with just developing software but are actively building their own hardware ecosystems. If this project comes to fruition, it could redefine the smartphone market, where hardware and software are centered around AI. With this initiative, OpenAI aims to change how users interact with artificial intelligence.
