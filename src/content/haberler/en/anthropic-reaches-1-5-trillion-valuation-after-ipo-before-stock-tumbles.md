AI lab Anthropic reached a $1.5 trillion valuation after finalizing its initial public offering on Wednesday. However, the company's newly minted stock quickly declined as insider investors cashed in on the initial rally, driving shares down by 12 percent. The sell-off spilled into the broader technology sector, pulling the Nasdaq index down by 3.7 percent.

Economists and market analysts are raising red flags over a potential bubble in the artificial intelligence sector. MIT economist and former US SEC chair Gary Gensler noted that annual investments in AI infrastructure are reaching as high as $800 billion. Gensler added that historical trends show major technological investment waves eventually reach a plateau and retrench.

Uncertainty is also growing among enterprise customers who are rattled by high usage costs. One corporate user spent $500 million on Claude tokens in a single month, prompting many businesses to enforce stricter spending caps or turn to hybrid setups with cheaper alternative models. The difficulty of monetizing enterprise and consumer AI features is creating broader headwinds.

Similar challenges are impacting other tech leaders. Google lost half a trillion dollars in market cap in a single day last month after earnings revealed slower search advertising growth tied to AI features. Growing fears that massive capital spending on AI will not translate to immediate profitability continue to weigh on investor sentiment.
