Cathedral, an AI-focused cybersecurity startup, successfully breached Anthropic internal systems, gaining access to sensitive data such as salary details, job offers, and internal messages. Following initial direct attempts against company infrastructure, researchers shifted their focus toward vulnerabilities within third-party business software used for administrative tasks, sales processes, and employee records. Utilizing artificial intelligence agents to navigate these third-party platforms, the team identified pathways to sensitive corporate data in under twelve hours according to a draft report.

The accessed data included meeting recordings, compensation details, and messages discussing advanced artificial intelligence models. Anthropic maintained that the systems exposed to researchers were more limited than initially claimed, emphasizing that human resources, sales, and information technology systems were only partially affected. Furthermore, the company highlighted that user chats, prompts sent to models, and core model weights remained entirely untouched and secure throughout the entire process.

Following the discovery, Anthropic closed the identified access paths, disabled the vulnerable third-party software tools, and notified federal authorities regarding the security incident. The research itself was conducted under a structured bug bounty program designed to let external security researchers test corporate infrastructure in a controlled environment. Consequently, the startup is not expected to face punitive sanctions for the breach.

However, Anthropic argued that the startup failed to follow all program guidelines and exaggerated the national security implications of the event. The findings nevertheless generated significant concern within the United States government. Officials noted that a small startup gaining access so quickly indicates that malicious actors or foreign intelligence services could potentially employ similar methods to compromise critical digital infrastructure across the broader technology sector.
