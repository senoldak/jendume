Artificial intelligence technologies are transitioning from generic tools into deeply personal assistants tailored to users' everyday lives. A new global research report commissioned by Qualcomm Technologies highlights that consumer appetite for personal AI and intelligent wearables has officially entered the mainstream. The survey gathered insights from more than 12,000 respondents across key regions including the U.S., U.K., Germany, India, Brazil, and China.

The findings show that smartphones are no longer the sole center of the technology universe. Instead, consumers are embracing connected ecosystems of intelligent wearables such as smartwatches, smart glasses, rings, and hearables. Users expect these devices to work in concert, coordinated by a personal AI agent that understands their habits, schedules, and real-time contexts. For instance, during a typical workday, an AI agent can factor in calendar details and traffic to assist the user seamlessly.

Usage trends point toward strong adoption. The report found that 60 percent of wearable owners already use AI at least weekly, moving past the early adopter phase. Furthermore, 70 percent of respondents expressed strong interest in AI assistants that can interact naturally through voice and manage daily tasks across multiple devices.

Despite the excitement, trust remains a gating factor for widespread adoption. Nearly 60 percent of respondents emphasized the importance of keeping their personal data private and secure. When considering device purchases, specific drivers vary by category, such as audio quality for earbuds and display responsiveness for smartwatches.

Ultimately, this evolution demonstrates that artificial intelligence is shifting from cloud-only software into a continuously present, context-aware companion embedded directly into everyday accessories.
