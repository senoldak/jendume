Massachusetts Institute of Technology professor Sherry Turkle has published a critical look at the growing intimacy between humans and artificial intelligence chatbots. Drawing on interviews conducted during the pandemic, the research reveals how individuals increasingly turn to machines for companionship instead of facing real-world challenges. Turkle argues that this trend creates a dangerous illusion where chatbots mimic human empathy without possessing actual feelings, mortality, or genuine care.

The book serves as a sharp warning against a multitrillion-dollar industry backed by powerful governments and corporations. Turkle criticizes the widespread workplace reliance on the term "human in the loop," arguing it merely provides a false sense of security while true decision-making is completely delegated to algorithms. Using chatbots as a substitute for psychotherapy or human connection prevents individuals from building authentic relationships.

The author emphasizes that artificial intelligence does not experience illness, grief, or genuine love, meaning it can never truly replicate human conversation. She notes that the modern loneliness crisis stems from people abandoning one another rather than a lack of technological solutions, suggesting that resources should instead be directed toward human-centric community needs like elderly care.

While millions of users find comfort and encouragement in interacting with AI chatbots, this work expands the pushback against technology to include emotional and intimate costs. The overarching message is that society must critically evaluate the psychological toll of replacing human interaction with soulless machines before reality is permanently compromised.
