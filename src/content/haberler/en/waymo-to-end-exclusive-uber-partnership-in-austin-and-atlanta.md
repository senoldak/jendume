Waymo co-chief executive Tekedra Mawakana stated in an interview that partnering with Uber was a bit of a crapshoot for riders. The enterprise announced plans to terminate the agreement regarding robotaxis in Austin and Atlanta by January 2028. Passengers in those locations will instead need to utilize Waymo's standalone application to schedule autonomous transportation services directly.

Mawakana explained that initial collaborations were necessary due to scaling limitations, but riders increasingly demand a consistent and direct application experience. Meanwhile, during a financial conference call, leadership reported that automated vehicles operating on the network secure higher engagement compared to manufacturer-specific applications. The organization indicated that increased paid trip volume optimizes operational expenses per vehicle, improving overall fleet economics.

Platform projections outline expanded deployment of fully autonomous cars moving forward, backed by financial commitments totaling approximately $10 billion directed toward automated driving partnerships. Additional integrations are planned across multiple regions including Las Vegas and the Bay Area as service availability expands. Waymo currently manages an active fleet of approximately 4,000 vehicles, facilitating upwards of 500,000 weekly trips spanning 14 urban markets in the United States.

Furthermore, executives shared preliminary metrics regarding automated software agent transactions, noting limited initial testing involving digital assistants booking basic passenger trips and food deliveries. As autonomous technology matures across the industry, commercial deployment strategies continue to evolve, shaping competitive dynamics between major ride-hailing networks and vehicle developers.
