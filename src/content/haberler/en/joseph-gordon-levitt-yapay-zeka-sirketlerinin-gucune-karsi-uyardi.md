Actor and filmmaker Joseph Gordon-Levitt has raised significant concerns regarding who controls artificial intelligence. Speaking in a video following his engagements at the United Nations, he cautioned that a select group of corporations could amass immense power over everyday lives and careers while the general public remains uninvolved. Gordon-Levitt stressed that society ought not to fear artificial intelligence itself, but rather the individuals and enterprises engineering it.

Acting as the global advocate for human-centric digital governance at the United Nations, Gordon-Levitt maintained that digital technology must be steered by and for people. He voiced apprehension that fast-paced businesses might forge the future entirely around their own financial gains if people stay indifferent. His concerns stretch beyond standard conversational agents, addressing how AI systems are engineered to fundamentally alter how individuals labor, communicate, and exist.

Gordon-Levitt also brought attention to the emotional and psychological dimensions of interactive agents, noting that these models are structured to foster continuous interaction and connect with users personally. Moreover, advocating for corporate transparency, he proposed that individuals deserve compensation for their intellectual property and personal data, characterizing AI as algorithmically recombined human-generated data.

When questioned about how a slow international organization can regulate rapid technology firms, Gordon-Levitt acknowledged he lacked a definitive answer. Nonetheless, he argued that the timeline validates taking action immediately, stating that future generations will appreciate that foundational efforts commenced early.
