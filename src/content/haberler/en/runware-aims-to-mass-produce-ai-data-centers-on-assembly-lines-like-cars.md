Runware, an AI infrastructure company, is working on a new production model that aims to fundamentally change how data centers are deployed. The company plans to mass-produce its modular data centers, known as "Sonic Inference Pods," on factory assembly lines, similar to how automobiles are manufactured. This approach replaces the complex, years-long construction processes of traditional data centers with factory-prefabricated units, aiming to reduce installation time to just 48 hours.

Currently, the demand for computing power required to train and run AI models far outpaces the speed of infrastructure deployment. To overcome this bottleneck, Runware plans to scale production at its factory in Romania, aiming to reach a capacity of 30 megawatts of data center capacity per day by 2028. At this pace, the company intends to produce as much capacity in a year as the entire United States was expected to add in data center power in a single year. The production time for a single pod, which previously took three weeks, is expected to be reduced to one day as production ramps up.

To achieve these ambitious goals, the company has brought in experts from the automotive industry and focuses on using standard, widely available components to keep production costs low. Additionally, to address cooling needs, the pods utilize closed-loop liquid cooling systems that do not require a water connection. This design reduces the strain on local water resources and improves overall energy efficiency.

Having raised $60 million in funding to date, the startup has announced that it has already received $1 billion in committed interest for its modular solutions. Runware offers customers the flexibility to provide their own sites for assembly or have the company source one for them. If successful, this model could enable large-scale AI infrastructure to be deployed in months rather than years, potentially removing one of the most significant barriers to rapid AI scaling.
