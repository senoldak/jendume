Spotify announced that it has removed more than 50 million low-quality and spam tracks from listener recommendations throughout 2026. According to data shared by the company, this process is a separate application from the 75 million plus spam tracks it completely deleted from the platform due to severe or repeated rule violations. The changes in the recommendation system rely on reviewing tracks that listeners skipped, ignored, or reported they did not want to hear.

The company does not rely on a single indicator for its decisions. Listener engagement, feedback, upload behaviors, track information, and data from multiple third-party artificial intelligence detection tools are evaluated together. At the same time, Spotify acknowledges the limitations of AI detection technology, emphasizing that it will become harder to make this distinction as generative models continue to evolve.

These steps follow the AI Persona badge decision announced in August. The badge explains whether there is a real person behind the profile rather than the specific tools used to create the music. Spotify does not take these identities into its editorial or algorithmic recommendations by default.

These measures taken by the platform represent an important threshold in the moderation of AI content within the digital music market. Aiming to protect artist rights, this process seeks to improve the user experience and prioritize human effort.
