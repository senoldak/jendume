Software giant Microsoft has issued voluntary buyout offers to approximately 60,000 employees across four nations. Announced by Chief People Officer Amy Coleman, the program targets eligible staff in the U.S., Canada, India, and the U.K. holding a rating of seventy or below on the corporate evaluation scale. Participation remains entirely voluntary, and eligibility depends on specific organizational requirements and business units rather than geographic location alone.

The voluntary exit initiative comes as Microsoft increases its capital expenditures to support growing artificial intelligence operations and data center expansion. The company projects its capital expenditures to reach 175 billion dollars, driven heavily by data center buildouts and custom silicon development. By offering voluntary buyouts, Microsoft aims to reshape its workforce without resorting to mandatory mass layoffs while reallocating funds toward its AI strategy.

Eligible employees who choose to depart will receive severance packages based on their length of service, alongside stock vesting, continued healthcare coverage, and outplacement support. According to reports, staffers at level sixty-four and below will receive one week of compensation for every half-year spent at the firm, while those at levels sixty-five to sixty-seven will receive two weeks. Employees have until the early part of December to accept the terms.

Microsoft frames the initiative as a way to adapt to changing organizational demands while supporting workers through career transitions. Although the initial eligibility pool covers about a quarter of the global workforce, the final number of departures will depend entirely on individual employee decisions in the weeks following the announcement.
