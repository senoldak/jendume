The explosion of extreme wealth and oligarchic threats in the age of artificial intelligence has been brought to the forefront by an international group of academics and writers who published the Paris Manifesto. The signatories pointed out that the wealth of the richest 0.0001 percent of the population has surged from 3 percent of global GDP in 1987 to 17 percent. This situation highlights the staggering imbalance created by wealth concentrated in the hands of a tiny fraction of the global population, consisting of just 3,000 households.

The manifesto emphasizes that artificial intelligence and information technologies represent humanity's collective heritage, opposing the capture of these accumulations by a handful of corporations without permission. The declaration argues that democracy can reverse oligarchic dynamics and stresses the need to secure tax fairness and access to information. It notes that access to information forms the bedrock of democracies, and corporate control over media and digital platforms threatens this foundational structure.

Key principles proposed in the text include a wealth tax floor and one-time catch-up taxes on the super-rich to address past tax inequalities. These proposals build upon reports commissioned by the G20 under the presidency of Brazil, which suggested an individual tax floor of 2 percent for fortunes exceeding 100 million dollars.

Furthermore, the manifesto suggests adopting non-profit foundation models in the development of artificial intelligence. The signatories argue that, much like popular examples such as Wikipedia, Signal, or Firefox, technology decision-making mechanisms should be guided by the workers operating in this field. Raising its voice on a global scale, this initiative calls for a transnational coalition to defend democracy and overcome extreme inequality.
