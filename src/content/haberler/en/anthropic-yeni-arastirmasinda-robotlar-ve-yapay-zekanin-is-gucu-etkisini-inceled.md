Anthropic has released a new research study examining the combined impact of artificial intelligence models and robotics on the workforce. According to the findings, robots and AI models together could take on about 80 percent of the work Americans do. While AI models alone can handle about half of all work, adding robots pushes that figure to approximately 81 percent.

The study highlights that AI and robots target very different types of jobs. Unlike software-based AI tools, robots reach physical and lower-paid roles. In transportation, for instance, fewer than 15 percent of tasks are exposed to AI models alone, but with robots added, that figure jumps to about 90 percent. Nine of the ten most exposed jobs are vehicle drivers, led by taxi drivers.

Cost remains the primary barrier to broader adoption. Currently, robots are cheaper than people for roughly 300,000 US workers, mostly consisting of packers and packagers. If robot prices were to fall by 20 percent, that number would rise to about 2.8 million workers. However, at past rates, reaching 10 percent of physical tasks would take approximately 40 years.

Anthropic stresses that high exposure ratings do not equate to direct job losses. The ratings were generated by Claude using web searches, noting that cleaning and maintenance work would see high exposure. Nevertheless, practical limitations such as cleaning robots being slower and multiple times more expensive than humans continue to challenge rapid deployment.
