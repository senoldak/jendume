National Grid has taken a major step toward building Britain’s biggest electricity substation on Teesside to supply power to a neighboring datacentre as the UK races to expand its artificial intelligence infrastructure. The transmission company agreed to acquire property measuring 29.5 hectares situated at Dorman Point, which is managed through a regional public-private partnership structure. This commercial agreement followed earlier official announcements indicating that authorities had selected the Teesworks location for a large artificial intelligence datacentre initiative featuring architectural planning applications covering nearly 500,000 square metres. Regional civic leaders noted that securing rapid electrical power transmission remains a primary hurdle for modern digital infrastructure projects, arguing that establishing up to 3.2 gigawatts of local capacity will enable the area to compete effectively on an international scale. Meanwhile, national authorities released preliminary policy guidelines defining digital data facilities as foundational economic pillars comparable to traditional transportation networks. Nevertheless, accelerated construction timelines have encountered localized community opposition and public demonstrations across various regions. Engineering services providers such as Balfour Beatty are reportedly slated to execute the primary electrical substation construction works for the overarching utility development. Furthermore, additional local power generation capacity is expected to attract substantial industrial investments to the north-east region, supporting future digital growth and long-term economic development initiatives across the United Kingdom.
