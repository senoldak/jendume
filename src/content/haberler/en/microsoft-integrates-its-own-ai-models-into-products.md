Microsoft is shifting its strategy at the Ignite conference by replacing third-party AI models in its enterprise software with its own newly developed alternatives. The company introduced seven new models under the "MAI" brand, specifically designed to lower costs for customers who rely on "always-on" AI agents. This move marks a significant effort by Microsoft to make its internal AI operations more efficient and economically viable.

The new lineup covers a wide range of specialized tasks, including coding, cybersecurity, image editing, and speech processing. For instance, the MAI Code 2 model is designed to deliver performance comparable to industry standards in coding tasks while significantly reducing operational costs. Microsoft emphasizes that the primary focus of this new model family is to optimize the price-to-performance ratio for enterprise users.

In the cybersecurity domain, MAI Cyber 2 Flash demonstrates high success rates in identifying vulnerabilities. Microsoft highlights that this model operates with a much lower reasoning budget compared to other existing solutions. Furthermore, in the field of image editing, MAI Image 2.7 stands out with high performance scores, while the newly introduced MAI Realtime 1 model for speech processing is built to handle interruptions and background noise effectively.

This strategic shift is a critical step for Microsoft as it aims to scale AI adoption among corporate clients. By reducing reliance on external models, the company intends to offer more sustainable pricing for workflows that require intensive computational power. These models are being integrated directly into Microsoft's existing platforms, enabling users to achieve faster and more cost-effective results in their daily enterprise operations.
