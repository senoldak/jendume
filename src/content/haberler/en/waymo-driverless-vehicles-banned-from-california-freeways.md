California’s Department of Motor Vehicles has officially barred Waymo from running driverless cars on any freeway across the state. The restrictive order took effect on November 11 and was later made public. The regulatory agency stated that the company's freeway operations pose an imminent hazard to public safety, covering both driverless passenger rides and testing activities.

Following the directive, the company paused its freeway rides in every market of operation, while its robotaxis continue to run on standard surface streets. The agency cited multiple recent incidents in the Bay Area where vehicles entered active construction zones and attempted to bypass traffic controls, despite prior restrictions and corrective measures implemented earlier in the year.

The regulatory ban will remain in place until the company provides sufficient evidence that its vehicles can properly detect and respond to construction zones, emergency vehicles, and complex road conditions. A company spokesperson emphasized that safety is a foundational priority and noted that representatives are working diligently with state officials to safely resume freeway operations.

The company confirmed that the recent encounters resulted in no injuries or collisions. While freeway services are suspended, the network continues to facilitate over half a million trips per week on surface streets. This regulatory action arrives as the firm prepares for future fleet expansion targets in the coming years.
