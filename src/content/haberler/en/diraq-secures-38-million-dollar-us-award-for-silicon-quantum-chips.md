Six months after Washington promised funding to Diraq, the official contract has been signed. The quantum computing company announced that the US Commerce Department has finalized its award of up to 38 million dollars under the CHIPS program. The funding is designated for manufacturing and testing Diraq's quantum chips on American soil, as well as designing the large processing units that a functional quantum computer will require. Reports indicate this money emerged from a roughly 2 billion dollars CHIPS Act quantum round. Andrew Dzurak, founder and CEO of Diraq, emphasized that the upcoming decade will rely heavily on quantum technology as a foundational advancement. Diraq's approach relies on making its silicon spin qubits using the same processes and materials as standard phone and laptop processors, allowing ordinary foundries to produce them. The enterprise works toward establishing a framework capable of supporting up to 1,000 error-corrected configurations based on 150,000 physical components by the end of the decade. Two years later, it targets millions in systems roughly the size of four server racks. Earlier in October, Diraq reached the final stage of DARPA's quantum benchmarking program, securing an initial 51 million dollars with up to 300 million dollars more if its machines prove commercially useful. Founded in 2022 as a University of New South Wales spinout, the company now maintains a team exceeding 130 members distributed across operations in Palo Alto, Chicago, Los Angeles, and Albuquerque.
