As artificial intelligence models become more advanced, the question of who stands behind digital content and takes responsibility for it is sparking intense debate. In a recent essay, Massachusetts Institute of Technology Professor Deb Roy points out that public policy feedback channels are increasingly vulnerable to AI-generated submissions that lack human accountability.

While mass-produced feedback used to leave clear fingerprints such as templated text or duplicated phrasing, large language models now generate fluent and unique responses that make it nearly impossible for reviewers to distinguish human perspectives from synthetic noise.

Roy argues that public comment is a pillar of democracy that requires real people with a stake in the outcome to stand behind their words. Without clear accountability and answerability, the vital connection between citizens and lawmakers risks breaking down in the age of generative technologies.
