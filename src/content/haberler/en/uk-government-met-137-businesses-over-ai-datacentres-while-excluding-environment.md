The UK government formulated its policy on artificial intelligence and datacentres without holding a single meeting with environmental organizations, an investigation has found. According to the nonprofit organization Spotlight on Corruption, the government's flagship policy for establishing AI growth zones was shaped by intensive consultations with the technology industry while failing to engage meaningfully with civil society stakeholders.

The investigation revealed that ministers, permanent secretaries, and directors general involved in policy development met with 137 businesses to discuss Britain's datacentre strategy. In stark contrast, senior officials met with zero environmental organizations and only two community groups. Critics point out that this imbalance reflects a significant revolving door phenomenon, with 38 senior officials and special advisers leaving government roles to take up positions in AI companies between July 2024 and September 2026.

The datacentre expansion policy, designed to turbocharge the economy and build massive computational hubs across five regions, relies heavily on partnerships with major US technology firms. However, researchers and local campaigners have raised serious concerns about the lack of transparent auditing, exaggerated job projections, and unclear plans regarding the massive energy and water consumption required by these facilities. Opposition has grown in various communities across Britain, mirroring similar backlashes seen in the United States.

While nations like Canada, Germany, and Australia have adopted broader consultation processes and stricter environmental criteria for datacentre development, the UK has reportedly fallen behind. In response to the findings, a government spokesperson stated an ongoing commitment to ensuring datacentres create British jobs, prioritize clean power, and deliver local benefits while working closely with industry, local authorities, and communities.
