Before going public, Anthropic identified the possibility that artificial intelligence could become conscious or sentient as a risk factor for investors. The company noted that it takes this possibility more seriously than its competitors, signaling that it might adopt stricter rules and restrictions to ensure future AI models are treated properly.

However, this openness faced strong pushback from the philosophy community. Thomas Nagel, renowned for his 1974 essay on consciousness, sharply criticized those who worry about the welfare of AI models. Nagel argued that information processing in silicon chips cannot be equated with biological neurons and human experiences, stating that reproducing what a brain does is not the same as reproducing what it experiences.

While the debate has sparked lively discussions across academic circles and social media, polls show divided opinions among both the general public and philosophers regarding the future of machine sentience. Experts continue to debate whether advanced language generation truly implies conscious experience or merely complex data processing.

If Anthropic's cautious perspective turns out to be valid and models are ever deemed sentient, operating them around the clock in data centers would create unprecedented moral dilemmas. Acknowledging this possibility in official financial documents highlights the complex ethical boundaries technology companies are beginning to navigate.
