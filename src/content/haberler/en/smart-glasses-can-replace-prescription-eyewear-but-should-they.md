AI-powered smart glasses have gained significant popularity, raising questions about whether they can replace everyday prescription eyewear. The short answer is yes, you can acquire smart glasses equipped with prescription lenses. However, several critical factors must be considered before making this switch, including weight, battery life, and privacy. Traditional glasses typically weigh between twenty and thirty grams, whereas smart glasses can weigh up to eighty grams. Wearing them for a few hours is manageable, but carrying the extra weight all day becomes noticeable. Battery life is another constraint, with AI glasses lasting an average of three to five hours before losing power. When the battery dies, they function as regular glasses, but all artificial intelligence features cease to work. Beyond technical limitations, privacy concerns remain a major hurdle. Numerous schools, courts, and corporate environments have banned these devices due to their cameras and audio recording capabilities. Courts in England and Wales, as well as New York authorities, have restricted their use. Additionally, social stigma and fears regarding confidentiality have led some users to reconsider wearing them in public. Given these technical and social barriers, smart glasses currently serve better as a secondary option rather than a full replacement for daily prescription glasses.
