Microsoft has unveiled its latest foundational artificial intelligence model, named MAI-Base-2, designed to offer significantly higher efficiency than existing open-source alternatives. Rather than a fully finished chat assistant, the new release functions as a robust base model that developers can further train with reasoning capabilities and safety guardrails. In internal benchmark evaluations covering science, programming, and math queries, the model outperformed leading market competitors.

The system utilizes a mixture of experts architecture, meaning it activates only a fraction of its total capacity for any given prompt. Out of its total two trillion parameters, approximately eighty billion are engaged during processing. By relying entirely on mixture of experts layers instead of older design modules, the architecture achieves up to twelve times greater hardware efficiency under specific operational measures.

Training the new model required thirty-eight days using sixteen thousand Nvidia GB200 chips. The extensive training dataset encompassed fifty trillion tokens, with a notable portion dedicated to an integrated computer vision module that enhances scientific chart comprehension. Developers implemented gradient clipping and optimization tools to streamline the training workflow and prevent common technical disruptions.

Microsoft plans to license the model's weights to trusted partners through its Azure cloud platform while leveraging the foundation to power upcoming proprietary language models. This development marks a notable step forward in balancing massive model scale with practical hardware efficiency and resource optimization.
