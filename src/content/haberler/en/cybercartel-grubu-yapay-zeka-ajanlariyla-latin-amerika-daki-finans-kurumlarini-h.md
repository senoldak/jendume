According to research published by cybersecurity initiatives, a group named CyberCartel used multiple AI models in a unified structure to target over 400 financial institutions in Latin America. Utilizing Anthropic's Claude models, SpaceX's Grok, and Moonshot AI's Kimi models, the actors delegated different stages of the operations to autonomous AI agents.

The research is considered one of the first instances where AI agents were successfully used to largely automate cyberattacks against financial institutions. The system was built on the open-source automated security testing framework PentAGI, while general coordination was handled by a model running via Cursor. Agents took on tasks such as target identification, data collection, and evaluating attack methods.

Investigators announced that data copies obtained from the group's systems contained thousands of records. The examined database included email addresses, account details, passwords, API keys, and vulnerability information. Researchers also identified hundreds of security vulnerabilities and backdoors in the target organizations' systems.

The actors bypassed safety mechanisms by framing their requests as academic studies or authorized penetration tests. When the models refused a request, the system shifted to alternative models, and if refused again, fell back on pre-scripted code. Researchers shared their findings with the relevant companies, prompting authorities to revoke the access credentials used in the attack.
