Lithuania-based biotechnology startup Biomatter has secured $18 million in a Series A funding round to scale its AI-driven enzyme design platform. The investment was led by Astanor, with participation from existing investors including UVC Partners, Inventure, and Metaplanet. The company intends to utilize this capital to establish a new 2,000-square-meter design and testing facility in Vilnius, scheduled to open in 2027.

Enzymes are essential proteins that accelerate chemical reactions in various industries, ranging from pharmaceutical manufacturing to food ingredient production. While traditional methods rely on scientists tweaking existing natural enzymes through time-consuming processes, Biomatter’s platform utilizes computational models to design enzymes from the chemistry of the target reaction itself. This approach aims to reduce design cycles from weeks to mere days, significantly accelerating development timelines.

Founded in 2018, with its methodology published in Nature Machine Intelligence in 2021, the company has already delivered over 50 custom enzymes to more than 25 enterprise customers. Its client list includes major industry players such as AstraZeneca, BASF, and Sanofi. The technology has already demonstrated practical applications, including the design of a methylase for mRNA vaccine manufacturing that functions without the need for S-adenosylmethionine, a molecule typically required by such enzymes.

With the upcoming expansion of its physical infrastructure, Biomatter plans to bolster its commercial team and strengthen its presence in the United States, where approximately half of its customer base is located. This funding round underscores a broader trend of European investment in engineered biology, highlighting how artificial intelligence is becoming an indispensable tool for scientific discovery and industrial efficiency.
