The challenge of supplying power to AI data centers has emerged as a central debate in the artificial intelligence era. Brook Porter, a partner at G2 Venture Partners (G2VP), emphasizes that the industry must look beyond traditional grid constraints. While many companies are currently securing natural gas plants to meet immediate needs, Porter argues that solar power paired with long-duration energy storage offers a superior path for speed and scale.

According to G2VP's analysis, the global capacity for natural gas is limited to approximately 60 to 70 gigawatts per year, whereas solar capacity has already surpassed one terawatt. By leveraging Wright’s Law, which is the principle that costs decrease as production volume increases, solar and modular storage technologies can be manufactured at a massive scale more efficiently than fossil fuel infrastructure. This modularity is key to meeting the rapidly growing demand for AI compute power.

The venture firm identifies two primary paths for future data centers: small, on-grid facilities and larger, off-grid centers powered by renewables. Going off-grid allows developers to bypass grid connection delays and complex permitting processes, significantly accelerating time to market. This independence is becoming increasingly attractive as the U.S. grid faces unprecedented growth demands after years of stagnation.

Investment in data centers is estimated to top $4 trillion by the end of the decade. While lithium-ion batteries have historically been too expensive for round-the-clock power, new technologies from companies like Form Energy and Antora Energy are bringing costs down. Porter suggests that while these technologies are still a few years from broad adoption, data center developers must act now to stay ahead of the massive wave of AI infrastructure requirements.
