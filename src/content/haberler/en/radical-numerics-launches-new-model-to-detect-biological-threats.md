Radical Numerics has introduced Omnii Defense, a new model designed to detect biological threats. This technology is capable of operating on standard laptops integrated with USB-based gene sequencers. The fact that it does not require an internet connection allows researchers in the field to identify biological hazards even in challenging conditions with limited infrastructure. This capability is considered a critical step in the field of biosecurity.

There is a technological arms race in the biosecurity space, similar to what is seen in cybersecurity. While traditional methods search for exact matches against libraries of known organisms, this new model learns patterns within biological sequences. This allows it to detect pathogens that have been genetically modified or have never been seen before. The model assigns a "threat score" between zero and one to a genome sequence to determine the risk level.

In experiments, even when data for the Bundibugyo virus, which causes Ebola outbreaks, was removed from the training set, the model successfully detected 33 percent of the pathogenic sequences. When the entire genus of the virus was removed from the training data, this rate was 22 percent. Traditional tools failed to indicate any potential risk in these scenarios. These results demonstrate that the model can recognize threats it has never encountered before.

The company's founders come from the research team behind the DNA foundation models Evo and Evo-2 during their doctoral studies at Stanford University. Previously, they used the genome language model Omnii to create custom bacteriophages targeting a drug-resistant strain of E. coli, which was used to treat a patient with special permission. Currently limited to vetted external testers, the tool is undergoing performance evaluations in collaboration with national laboratories and government agencies. At a time when concerns about AI-engineered biological threats are rising, such defense mechanisms are of critical importance for public health.
