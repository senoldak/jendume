As artificial intelligence company Anthropic prepares for what could be the largest IPO in history, CEO Dario Amodei has named the central weakness of his business model. Speaking at an investor event in New York alongside JPMorgan CEO Jamie Dimon, Amodei stated that nobody knows how the company's massive compute investments will pay off. Compute must be bought one to two years ahead, long before anyone knows whether future revenue will justify the cost.

Amodei explained that buying too little compute restricts resources for customers and new technologies, while buying too much carries the risk of financial overextension. According to company reports, Anthropic has committed massive funds mostly toward compute and chips, with a significant portion coming due by September 2027. To soften this dilemma, the company relies on strong gross margins, cash reserves, and the ability to source additional capacity from spot markets as needed.

Amid these strategic financial challenges, Anthropic plans to raise substantial capital through its upcoming IPO and private placements, driven by surging popularity and revenue growth of its AI products. Amodei noted that the company's annualized revenue run rate hit significant milestones by late October. Looking further ahead, he expressed optimism that AI could eventually help cure major diseases within the coming decades.
