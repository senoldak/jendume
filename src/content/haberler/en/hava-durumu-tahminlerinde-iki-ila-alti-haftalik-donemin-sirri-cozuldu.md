Weather forecasting beyond the two-week mark has long been considered unreliable, a period often referred to in meteorology as the predictability desert. To tackle this, a research team including scientists from MIT developed a machine-learning model designed to correct errors in existing forecasting systems.

By applying this error-correction layer to leading forecasting models, the team managed to double the predictive power for the two-to-six-week timeframe. The approach outperformed traditional climatological averages for the first time in a prominent international forecasting competition.

Beyond routine forecasting, the new technique significantly improves the prediction of extreme weather events such as floods and cold spells. This advancement gives agricultural and energy sectors crucial time to match supply with demand and brace for potential climate disruptions.
