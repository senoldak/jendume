Private equity giant Blackstone is positioning itself at the center of the artificial intelligence boom, investing heavily through strategies ranging from massive data center loans to frontier model investments. Speaking at an industry event, firm veteran Jas Khaira addressed growing market anxieties regarding the debt burdens accumulated by AI infrastructure operators.

Unlike previous tech cycles like mobile or software, the transition to artificial intelligence requires staggering amounts of capital investment. With $1.3 trillion in assets under management, Blackstone has played a pivotal role by pioneering financing structures that allow massive loans to neoclouds using customer contracts and GPUs as collateral, starting with deals like CoreWeave.

Recently, surging debt burdens among neocloud operators—with CoreWeave's debt ballooning to about $50 billion—have triggered market jitters amid rising interest rates and higher credit spreads. These concerns have spilled over into the stock market and forced some planned public offerings to be scrapped. However, Khaira downplayed fears of a widespread credit crisis, arguing that the situation is distinct from shaky banking credits because the underlying infrastructure is backed by the world's most creditworthy companies.

Beyond financing data centers, Blackstone has also formed a $1.5 billion joint venture called Ode with Anthropic to help portfolio companies adopt AI technologies. Khaira noted that while return on investment is tangible, it currently follows an uneven pattern.
