Federal prosecutors in the United States have charged a former OpenAI employee with attempting to conduct corporate espionage by allegedly planting a hidden recording device in a secure executive conference room.

According to court filings, the individual spent weeks querying various AI chatbots and productivity agents for technical advice on how to set up a covert audio recording device and plan the surveillance operation. The suspect reportedly used internal company tools and partner accounts to gather information on high-level meetings discussing sensitive research and superintelligence technology.

OpenAI's security team discovered the thumb-sized recording device hidden behind a television during a countersurveillance sweep. Subsequent investigations, including reviews of chat logs and security footage, linked the device to the employee, who was fired following his arrest. OpenAI stated that there was no indication of any unauthorized access to core model weights, source code, or customer data.

The case brings to light the physical security challenges confronting the artificial intelligence sector. As frontier labs race to develop advanced systems, protecting the people, facilities, and intellectual property behind these breakthroughs has become a matter of national and global significance.
