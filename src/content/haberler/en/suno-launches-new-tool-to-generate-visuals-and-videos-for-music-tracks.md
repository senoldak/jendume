Suno has expanded its music-focused AI platform with a new feature called "Visuals." This tool enables users to generate images, album art, and short videos of up to 15 seconds that align with their music. The technology features lip-syncing capabilities that match lyrics, allowing users to start with existing characters or customize clips using text prompts. Integrating visual content with music streamlines the creative workflow for artists and casual users alike.

However, Suno is navigating significant legal and ethical challenges while launching this feature. The company has implemented filters to block prompts that attempt to replicate the likeness of artists or public figures and is embedding hidden watermarks into all generated videos to ensure accountability. The company notes that these guardrails are intentionally designed to differ from other platforms, underscoring the growing necessity for content safety and ethical usage in the generative AI landscape.

The company is striving to establish legitimacy amidst ongoing legal battles, including recent lawsuits from the estates of Elvis Presley and Eminem. To address copyright concerns, Suno has previously launched music models trained on licensed tracks from major labels like Warner and BMG. Valued at $5.4 billion in June, the company is currently rolling out the tool on the web, with iOS and Android applications expected in the coming weeks. As other industry players like ByteDance have adopted similar guardrails, Suno’s move highlights the ongoing tension between creative freedom and the need for safety in generative AI.
