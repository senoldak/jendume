The U.S. Treasury Department has proposed a new rule to crack down on American high-tech investments in countries such as China, Russia, North Korea, and Cuba. Announced on Thursday, the proposal expands outbound investment restrictions to cover artificial intelligence—referred to by the Treasury using the term superintelligence—along with supercomputing, hypersonic systems, and biotechnology. The program builds on executive actions initiated in 2023 and expanded by a congressional law passed in December 2025.

Washington aims to prevent U.S. capital from accelerating military-relevant capabilities abroad. Legal experts note that the new rule will require companies to implement more robust compliance programs to manage investment risks. According to the proposal, the restrictions will apply to investments exceeding $50,000, while excluding many financial investments such as standard stock purchases.

In Beijing, the Chinese Ministry of Foreign Affairs criticized the move. Spokesperson Mao Ning stated that overstretching the concept of national security and taking discriminatory measures against specific countries serves no one's interests, adding that China will monitor developments and safeguard its own interests. The world's two largest economies have long been locked in a technology rivalry, which recently saw the U.S. Treasury penalizing a company for an unreported investment in a Chinese artificial intelligence firm.

The Treasury will review public comments before issuing a final rule. Analysts view the proposal as an initial step toward a more rigorous enforcement framework for outbound national security investments.
