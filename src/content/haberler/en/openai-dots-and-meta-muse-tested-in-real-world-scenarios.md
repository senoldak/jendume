Autonomous agents have become a major focus in the artificial intelligence sector, with tech companies racing to release tools that automate everyday tasks. While early versions struggled to meet expectations, modern AI agents show significant improvements in browser control, task execution, and proactivity. A recent day-long experiment put six major AI agents through rigorous testing to evaluate their strengths and weaknesses.

The tested tools include Meta’s free Muse model, OpenAI’s Dots priced at one hundred dollars per month, Google’s Gemini Spark at twenty dollars monthly, xAI’s Grok Bot at thirty dollars, and Anthropic’s newly released Claude Duet. Each assistant operates slightly differently, utilizing virtual browsers to handle tasks like scanning emails, tracking travel deals, and managing daily shopping decisions.

During the evaluation, the agents were tasked with compiling tech news briefings and generating headlines based on specific guidelines. Meta’s Muse and OpenAI’s Dots spent the allotted time thoroughly researching topics, whereas other agents delivered faster but simpler responses. Although the generated pitches received mixed reviews from professional editors, the test highlighted the varying capabilities among current market offerings.

Experts note that while these personal agents offer immense utility by integrating with calendars and email accounts, they also require a high level of trust. Because the software remains in its nascent stages, users must weigh the benefits of automation against potential privacy and security concerns.
