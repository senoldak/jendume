In a detailed security framework published on October 10, 2026, Microsoft Chairman and CEO Satya Nadella argued for a fundamental shift in how artificial intelligence safety is engineered: because frontier models are probabilistic and do not always produce the same output for the same input, safety must live in predictable surrounding systems rather than inside the model alone.

Nadella proposed treating both proprietary and open-weight models as potential internal risks. Under this architecture, the AI model is strictly separated from the software harness that executes actions, ensuring that permissions and security boundaries are enforced externally.

## Human Oversight and the “Emergency Brake”

Central to the proposal is what Nadella terms an “emergency brake”—a mechanism allowing authorized human operators to pause or completely shut down an AI system mid-task if unexpected behavior occurs.

The framework also emphasizes traceability through human-readable action logs, strict least-privilege access from the outset, continuous independent auditing, and model diversity so critical operations never depend on a single unverified system.
