Microsoft reported $45.2 billion in capital expenditures and finance leases for the quarter, missing the average analyst estimate of $50.7 billion according to market data providers. Following the report, shares of neocloud providers such as CoreWeave and Nebius dropped nearly 5% in extended trading. The figures point to infrastructure partners taking longer to deliver data center capacity as tech giants pour money into artificial intelligence infrastructure. Market participants are closely watching rising interest rates and debt financing costs that affect smaller cloud infrastructure companies.

In the same earnings report, Microsoft announced that Azure revenue jumped 48% from a year earlier, outpacing the increase in capital expenditures. The company noted that growth was driven by timing differences between the receipt of goods and payment alongside a lower mix of finance leases. Alphabet also reported capex figures below expectations, totaling $51.36 billion against analyst predictions of $55.09 billion.

Industry observers and research analysts have warned that rising debt costs and tightening financial markets could severely impact neocloud companies that lack independent revenue streams from AI data centers. Predictions from market researchers indicate that a significant majority of these smaller infrastructure providers could face consolidation or closure by the end of the decade. Meanwhile, other industry players like Oracle have attempted to steady market sentiment by issuing strong long-term revenue forecasts and positive free cash flow projections.
