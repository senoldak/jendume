Palantir has addressed questions regarding the AI-powered battlefield system Maven, which is utilized by NATO's military command. Louis Mosley, head of Palantir's UK and European operations, stated in an interview in Brussels that neither the company nor the US government can remotely shut down the system. Mosley emphasized that the software features no remote shutdown mechanisms and can be operated independently by customers. Maven combines data from satellites, radar, and sensors to give military officers a clearer battlefield picture and accelerate decision-making. NATO acquired the system for its Supreme Headquarters Allied Powers Europe in March 2025 following a six-month procurement process. The alliance declared the system fully technically capable on June 22, 2026, while the contract value was not disclosed by either party. These remarks come amid growing European scrutiny over reliance on US defence technology, with countries like Germany and France expressing hesitation due to dependence on American suppliers. Thomas Daum, head of the German army's cyber command, previously ruled out using Palantir's technology over database access concerns. Similarly, France is transitioning its domestic intelligence agency to ChapsVision in search of a homegrown alternative. Mosley noted that many military and commercial customers already operate the software independently, leaving European governments to weigh whether technical independence is enough to address broader control concerns.
