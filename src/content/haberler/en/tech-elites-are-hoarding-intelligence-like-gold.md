Engineer Venkatesh Rao argues in a recent essay that the current power dynamics in the AI world resemble mercantilism, the economic doctrine that predated capitalism. Just as mercantilists viewed wealth—specifically land and gold—as finite resources to be hoarded, many modern tech elites now treat intelligence as a scarce, dangerous resource. They believe it should be managed by experts, kept expensive, and withheld from rivals. This perspective often frames open-access AI models as a threat, with some executives leveraging safety concerns to consolidate political influence.

The common narrative surrounding AI often centers on the fear of job displacement. However, Rao suggests that this focus misses the broader potential of how much intelligence people will actually be able to utilize. By making projects that were previously too difficult or costly feasible, AI could significantly increase the demand for ideas rather than rendering human input obsolete. This shift could mirror the early days of capitalism, where people voluntarily increased their labor to access new goods like tea or sugar; similarly, AI could incentivize people to think more, not less.

This analysis highlights a critical shift in how we should approach AI policy. Instead of focusing solely on protecting legacy jobs, the political conversation should prioritize fair and equitable access to AI tools. Even autonomous AI agents could pursue their own projects, creating entirely new demands for intelligence. While these agents certainly require guardrails, the goal should not be to keep them as mere order-takers.

Ultimately, the hoarding of intelligence as if it were a finite gold reserve is a strategic choice, not an inevitability. If intelligence is treated as a tool for broad empowerment rather than a controlled commodity, it could unlock unprecedented levels of human productivity. The challenge lies in moving past the fear-based management of AI and ensuring that the benefits of this intelligence are distributed widely rather than locked away by a select few.
