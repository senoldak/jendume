India has brought two major projects to the agenda aimed at integrating artificial intelligence into both individual development and public administration processes. The first plan, proposed by Prime Minister Narendra Modi, envisions the creation of an AI application that will collect data on three-year-old children to perform talent analysis. In this system, parents, teachers, and even the child's friends will be able to enter data. The AI will process this information to determine which educational or career path the child is inclined toward, guiding families on their educational investments.

On the other hand, Uttar Pradesh, India's most populous state with over 240 million people, has opened a 178-page tender to establish a comprehensive "AI Stack." This system aims to link data from different institutions using a unique "Family ID" assigned to each household. One of the primary goals of the project is to automate public services by deploying AI-powered social media monitoring and urban grievance detection tools. For instance, identifying broken roads and directing repair teams could be handled automatically through this system.

However, these projects face serious criticism regarding data security and oversight mechanisms. Civil society organizations, such as the Internet Freedom Foundation, argue that the proposed systems lack a clear legal basis and sufficient safeguards to prevent misuse. There is particular concern that social media monitoring tools could be used by governments, which have previously resorted to internet shutdowns, to suppress dissenting voices.

Uttar Pradesh plans to run these applications using open-weighted models on its own servers with anonymized datasets. No specific implementation timeline or information regarding the winning bidders has been shared for either project yet. These initiatives demonstrate that AI will play a central role in India's digital transformation strategy, though the balance between technological progress and fundamental rights remains unresolved.
