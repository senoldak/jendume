Companies are utilizing more artificial intelligence than ever before, yet they are refusing to increase their spending on it. A new report from Ramp reveals that AI spending flatlined over the late summer months even as token volumes increased, signaling that intense price competition and savvier customers may be capping revenue growth for major players like OpenAI and Anthropic. According to the data, token spend grew by only 1.5% across August and September, a sharp drop compared to July's 42% growth from the previous month.

As Anthropic gears up for its initial public offering, the data suggests that investors might be overly optimistic in assuming AI companies will maintain breakneck growth speeds indefinitely. Ramp lead economist Ara Kharazian noted that the slowdown is not driven by a lack of demand, as usage volumes remain high, but rather by market competitiveness. Both OpenAI and Anthropic have implemented significant price cuts and enterprise discounts, which have successfully locked in customers but severely dented revenue growth targets.

Furthermore, companies are becoming increasingly thrifty and skilled at managing their AI usage. Advanced organizations are implementing stricter budgets, utilizing default routers, and shifting tasks to cheaper lightweight models to avoid running up high bills on expensive systems. Meta, Nvidia, and various open-source providers are also adding to the competitive pressure by offering low-cost alternatives.

Amidst these headwinds, market valuations have begun to adjust. Reports indicate that Anthropic's potential valuation dipped from an early October estimate down to a lower range, while OpenAI has also adjusted its financial targets. These developments suggest that the era of unquestioned hypergrowth in AI spending may be giving way to a more cost-conscious buyer's market.
