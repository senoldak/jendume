The US Department of Homeland Security has instructed civilian federal agencies to purge DeepSeek products from their IT infrastructure and halt any future purchases. Issued under a 2018 supply chain security law, the directive extends to third-party software built on DeepSeek models. Officials cited risks that sensitive government data could potentially be accessed under Chinese security laws and pointed out multiple threat vectors within the models.

The order was signed by Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin and published on the Federal Register. Alongside the removal from government computers, DeepSeek is barred from participating in federal contracting. Several military and civilian branches, including the Pentagon IT agency, the Commerce Department, and the Navy, had already restricted the chatbot, while states like New York, Tennessee, and Virginia implemented similar prohibitions.

This measure follows a prolonged period of scrutiny in Washington regarding Chinese artificial intelligence developers. US officials have previously raised concerns over data collection practices and techniques such as model distillation. Representatives for DeepSeek did not immediately provide comments on the new federal restrictions, and the Chinese embassy in Washington also withheld immediate comments.

Such sweeping federal actions signal a broader tightening of controls over foreign artificial intelligence technologies within critical infrastructure. As governments around the world increase their oversight of software supply chains, the rules governing how AI models are developed, distributed, and integrated into public systems are undergoing fundamental shifts.
