The construction of solar energy farms is becoming significantly faster and more efficient through the deployment of AI-powered robotic solutions. One of North America’s largest infrastructure builders has decided to integrate robotic arms developed by Gritt into its utility-scale solar projects. These systems, which can be bolted onto existing construction equipment like skid steers and forklifts, automate the labor-intensive process of lifting and placing solar panels.

This development offers a critical solution to the skilled labor shortage currently challenging the construction industry. While traditional manual methods allow a crew to install approximately 800 panels per day, the AI-powered robotic systems can increase this capacity to between 3,000 and 4,000 panels daily. This massive leap in productivity enables renewable energy projects to be completed in shorter timeframes, accelerating the expansion of essential energy infrastructure.

The technology is not merely experimental; it has been rigorously tested on active construction sites, where it successfully facilitated the installation of megawatts of solar panels. This partnership marks the second major collaboration for the robotics firm in recent months, following successful trials with other industry leaders.

As the construction sector faces the retirement of skilled workers, firms are increasingly turning to automation and robotics to maintain operational efficiency. This trend is driving significant investment across various construction domains, including welding and drone technology. Rather than replacing human workers, these AI-driven robots are designed to augment the expertise of field teams, allowing them to perform demanding tasks with greater speed and precision. As these systems become more prevalent, they are poised to become a standard component of large-scale energy infrastructure projects, fundamentally changing how renewable energy sites are built.
