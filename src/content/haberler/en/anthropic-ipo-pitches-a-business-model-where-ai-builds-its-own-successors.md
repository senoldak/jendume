Anthropic is set to launch its initial public offering, marking a significant moment for the AI industry. The company is asking investors to buy into a business model where its AI products are not just tools, but active participants in building their own successors. According to filings with the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission, Anthropic’s Claude models are now directly accelerating the development of future versions, a concept known as recursive self-improvement.

This approach represents a departure from traditional software companies, which rely on human engineers to build and scale products. Anthropic claims that its AI is fundamentally changing how the business operates. In September 2026, Claude wrote more than 90% of the contributions to the company’s code base. Furthermore, the daily volume of code contributed per engineer has surged to more than 45 times the 2024 average.

While this efficiency is striking, experts note that it transcends the traditional concept of companies using their own software. However, the model raises questions about autonomy and control. Anthropic acknowledges that human supervision remains essential, as errors in current models could propagate into future iterations. The company is not yet granting Claude the authority to make consequential research decisions without human oversight.

For investors, this IPO is a bet on whether AI can truly become a self-building entity. With a target valuation of $1.4 trillion, the company is positioning itself at the forefront of a shift in how technology companies develop products. Whether this vision of AI-driven self-improvement can deliver exponential gains or if it is primarily a pre-IPO marketing narrative remains a central point of debate among market observers and industry experts.
