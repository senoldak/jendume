As the complexity of AI models continues to rise, the data centers powering them are facing new, significant challenges. One of the most pressing issues is that processors (GPUs) often sit idle, waiting for data to be moved from memory, rather than actively processing it. This creates both a loss of time and substantial energy waste. Given that more than 80 percent of scale-up network traffic in AI infrastructure consists solely of memory transactions, resolving this bottleneck has become critical.

Companies like Marvell Technology and Penguin Solutions are turning to photonics—light-based technology—to overcome this hurdle. By replacing traditional copper cabling with optical signals, this new approach moves memory away from the immediate vicinity of the processor and into a broader, shared pool. Penguin Solutions' "MemoryAI Photonic Appliance" solution places high-bandwidth memory (HBM) tens of meters away from the GPU, allowing multiple server racks to share that memory pool effectively.

The primary promise of this technology is energy efficiency. When compared to copper-based data movement methods, this photonic-based system can reduce the energy consumed during data transmission to one-quarter of current levels. In an era where AI agents are generating constant data requests and large language models (LLMs) require massive memory capacity to maintain context, such shared memory pools are becoming a necessity.

Low GPU utilization in data centers currently signals that the system's potential is not being fully realized. Photonics technology addresses this by allowing data to remain in a shared space rather than constantly moving physically, thereby eliminating unnecessary traffic. This development is seen as a significant step not only toward faster AI models but also toward building more sustainable, energy-efficient data centers.
