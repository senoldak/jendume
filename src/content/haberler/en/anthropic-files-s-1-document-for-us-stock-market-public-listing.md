AI company Anthropic has officially filed for a public listing on the US stock market, offering the public its first audited look at the company's financials and a detailed overview of business risks. The S-1 filing outlines rapid expansion alongside soaring operational costs, providing investors and the public with a comprehensive view of the laboratory's economic standing.

According to the regulatory document, the company brought in $16.3bn in revenue and lost $80bn in the first half of 2026, largely driven by a $72.4bn one-off charge. In comparison, the firm generated $4.585bn in revenue and experienced a loss of roughly $33.9bn throughout 2025. These figures illustrate the immense capital required to sustain operations and drive explosive growth in the competitive artificial intelligence sector.

Securing necessary computing power constitutes the vast majority of the company's expenses. Anthropic spent nearly $9bn on AI infrastructure during the first half of the year, a significant increase from the $2.3bn spent during the same period in the previous year. Building and maintaining adequate data center capacity remains central to the company's ongoing financial expenditures.

The prospectus highlights the company's strong focus on enterprise clients, noting that its top 5 customers account for 25% of its total business. While leadership emphasizes that enterprise deployment yields the greatest economic impact, this customer concentration introduces notable business vulnerabilities if key clients depart.

Alongside financial metrics, the filing addresses broader implications, including advancements in biotechnology, governance structures, and ongoing challenges regarding safety measures and cyber-security. The document discusses potential risks ranging from misuse by malicious actors and autonomous cyber-attacks to regulatory hurdles and limits on technical safeguards, painting a complex picture of the future of frontier artificial intelligence.
