A federal judge has denied a motion by xAI seeking to dismiss a lawsuit that accuses the company of failing to obtain necessary permits to operate the gas turbines powering its Colossus data center near Memphis. The Southern Environmental Law Center, suing on behalf of the NAACP, claimed the AI company installed and operated at least 35 combustion turbines and other air pollution sources without required preconstruction or operating air permits. xAI had argued that the lawsuit should be dismissed because the turbines are mobile sources exempt from permitting requirements and that plaintiffs lacked standing. However, Judge Debra M. Brown ruled that the plaintiffs have statutory standing under the Clean Air Act and that the claims will proceed.

The court also rejected xAI's arguments regarding the constitutionality of citizen suits. Judge Brown wrote that because plaintiffs sue on their own behalf and demand penalties that remedy personal harm, they do not exercise executive powers. NAACP representatives welcomed the decision as an important victory in holding the company accountable for environmental impacts in local communities. A Department of Justice request to dismiss a similar case was also rejected.

This legal battle unfolds amidst growing public scrutiny and regulatory pushback against the rapid expansion of power-hungry AI data centers across various states. New York recently passed restrictions on new facilities, and other state and local authorities are weighing comparable limits. The public backlash has also prompted major companies like Amazon and Microsoft to change their policies, highlighting the tension between technological growth and environmental compliance.
