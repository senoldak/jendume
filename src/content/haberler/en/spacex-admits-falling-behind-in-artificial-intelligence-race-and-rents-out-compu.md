Elon Musk has made notable admissions regarding SpaceX's position in the artificial intelligence sector. According to reports based on internal communications, Musk renamed a company group chat in August to reflect that the firm could not compete. Musk warned employees against inefficient use of graphics processing units, stating that misuse of GPU resources could lead to terminations.

SpaceX has shifted its strategy by leasing the infrastructure it built for its own artificial intelligence projects to competitors. Having established high-capacity infrastructure, notably the Colossus data center in Memphis, the company directed excess capacity to industry players. Claude developer Anthropic and Google have emerged as SpaceX's primary clients.

Reports indicate that Anthropic pays approximately $3.1 billion monthly to lease computing power from SpaceX, while Google pays around $800 million per month. These massive agreements have allowed SpaceX's computing infrastructure unit to generate more than half of the company's total revenue.

As computing power leasing grew, SpaceX scaled back investments in its own artificial intelligence projects. An artificial intelligence-powered companionship feature was shut down in September, and the team working on the visual and video generation tool Grok Imagine was reportedly disbanded. Resources allocated to projects conducted with Tesla were also shifted to other areas.

During the DealBook Summit, Elon Musk stated that Anthropic intends to lease significantly more computing power from SpaceX. Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei also noted satisfaction with the quality of the rented infrastructure, emphasizing a desire to continue the collaboration. This situation clearly demonstrates how massive hardware and infrastructure demands in the artificial intelligence market are reshaping corporate dynamics.
