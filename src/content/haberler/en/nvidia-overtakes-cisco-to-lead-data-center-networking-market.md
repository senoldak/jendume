Known for its graphics processing units, Nvidia has captured global leadership in the data center networking market. Generating 17.5 billion dollars in networking revenue, the company surpassed its rival Cisco, which recorded 17.3 billion dollars in total product revenue during the same period. This strategic transformation, initiated by the acquisition of Mellanox in 2019, has borne fruit thanks to integrated systems optimized for artificial intelligence workloads. The company no longer just sells processors; it now dominates the switching and connectivity technologies that form the communication backbone of massive data centers.

Analysts note that the foundation of this success lies in a co-design strategy. The organization designs processors, network interface cards, and switches not as independent components, but as a single structure optimized for specific workloads. This approach provides a major advantage for operations working with massive datasets, such as drug discovery. Offering computing and networking components as a unified system greatly simplifies troubleshooting and optimization processes.

Although long associated with InfiniBand, Nvidia has also made a major leap in the traditional networking market with Spectrum-X Ethernet switches. Massive installation projects demonstrate trust in these Ethernet-based solutions. Through software optimizations, the company has managed to deliver low-latency features with broader compatibility.

With the launch of Blackwell-generation processors, networking revenues experienced a significant surge. Switching technologies used in rack-scale systems account for a substantial portion of revenues, while new licensing programs enable other chipmakers to integrate these connectivity technologies into their own products.
