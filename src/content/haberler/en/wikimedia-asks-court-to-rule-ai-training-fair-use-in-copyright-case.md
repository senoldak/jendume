The Wikimedia Foundation asked the court handling the copyright dispute to split the legal question in two. The foundation argued that building machine intelligence models using copyrighted material qualifies as permissible usage, whereas automated chat assistants duplicating web pages to answer inquiries do not share the same legal standing.

In its submission presented to the American judicial authorities, the organization stated that opposing litigants present overly simplified interpretations regarding legal exceptions. Wikimedia chief executive Bernadette Meehan noted that the institution adopted this stance to protect the unrestrictive and collaborative public knowledge base that supports Wikipedia operations.

The legal brief proposes that developing foundational models constitutes acceptable practice because the resulting systems introduce novel contributions without supplanting original source materials. Conversely, retrieval mechanisms that extract direct passages from web domains to construct conversational replies require strict compliance with established publisher distribution terms.

Wikimedia also emphasized that unregulated data extraction places heavy demands on infrastructure and discourages volunteer contributors. The foundation maintains that high-volume operators must adhere to public access thresholds or purchase enterprise solutions to safeguard the open knowledge ecosystem.

Wikipedia currently maintains more than sixty-seven million articles across three hundred languages, compiled by volunteer editors utilizing verified references. Foundation representatives stressed that expanding information access through machine learning remains an important objective, provided it honors the human communities essential for reliable knowledge creation.
