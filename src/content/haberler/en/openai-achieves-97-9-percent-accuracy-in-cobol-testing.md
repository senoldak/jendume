As the capabilities of artificial intelligence in software development continue to advance, significant results are being achieved in tests involving the migration of legacy programming languages to modern ones. Recent evaluations show that models operating with high reasoning effort achieve high accuracy rates in transforming older systems. According to information released by OpenAI, the model tested in COBOL evaluations reached an accuracy of 97.9 percent.

This process aims to transfer legacy COBOL programs into languages such as C++, Java, Kotlin, Python, or Rust while preserving their existing behaviors, utilizing hidden tests that require observable outputs to remain identical. To consider a program migration successful, passing all tests is required rather than simply compiling or starting to run. While the model's accuracy at low reasoning effort remains at 86.6 percent, operating costs also vary depending on model settings.

Experts emphasize, however, that high accuracy rates do not mean an entire bank's or public institution's software can be securely migrated on its own. There is a significant difference between rewriting code and replacing a live enterprise system. Business rules, data storage formats, and inter-system connections reveal that transformation is not merely a syntax issue and requires rigorous auditing.

Factors such as a lack of documentation in legacy systems and the departure of personnel familiar with the systems make the process even more complex. Experts point out the importance of running the same workflow in both old and new systems to compare results and thoroughly review code translations. This demonstrates that artificial intelligence serves as a powerful assistant in code writing and translation processes, yet human oversight remains indispensable for ultimate safety and verification.
