The Los Angeles Unified School District has quietly disabled artificial intelligence generated summaries in search on school-issued devices. This decision makes the district, which enrolls half a million students, the first to sideline the technology company over its automated features. School officials, parents, and student advocates have raised concerns about the impact of automated tools on learning, arguing that students might use technology as a shortcut instead of developing critical thinking skills.

Major school districts previously announced moratoriums on generative AI use to allow administrators time to establish formal digital policies. Meanwhile, the technology company has maintained that search products and artificial intelligence tools are deeply integrated and cannot be separated, leaving educational institutions to choose between restricting access to automated features and utilizing popular classroom search tools. In response to growing pushback, hundreds of employees signed a petition asking leadership to allow schools to turn off artificial intelligence features without losing search capabilities entirely.

As districts across the country reevaluate their digital safeguards and safety measures, the debate over corporate reach and student learning continues to intensify across various educational institutions. School administrators emphasize that student safety and age-appropriate experiences remain top priorities as automated tools continue to expand into everyday learning environments. Education technology experts point out that these ongoing discussions will shape how digital tools are deployed in classrooms, forcing technology providers to adapt to the demands of school boards and local communities.
