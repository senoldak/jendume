Apple has updated its App Store review guidelines to address non-consensual sexual content and explicitly include images produced by artificial intelligence tools under content moderation obligations. Released in a developer update on October 16, the changes follow instances of abuse on the platform and set clear responsibilities for developers to prevent and remove harmful material. The company emphasized that the longstanding ban on pornographic content remains in effect while expanding definitions to cover AI-generated violations and applications that change their purpose after review.

The updated section 1.2 of the guidelines covers user-generated content, defining non-consensual intimate imagery—including AI-generated media—as material that applications must actively prevent and remove. Developers who fail to provide adequate safeguards or address violations after notification face the risk of being removed from the marketplace. This provision ensures that software creators maintain rigorous oversight over user interactions within their products.

The updated rules subject AI content-creation applications to the same rigorous framework as social media platforms. Developers are now required to filter inappropriate content, provide user-reporting mechanisms, ban offending accounts, and publish accessible contact information. These mandates are designed to protect users and ensure swift resolution when inappropriate behavior occurs on the platform.

This regulatory update marks a significant step toward curbing the misuse of generative artificial intelligence technologies within consumer application ecosystems. By clarifying developer obligations, the company aims to foster a safer environment for all digital marketplace participants.
