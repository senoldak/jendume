Analytics firm SemiAnalysis has introduced GDPx, a new benchmark designed to evaluate AI agents within simulated professional work environments. Unlike traditional tests that rely on question-and-answer formats, GDPx places AI agents in scenarios lasting up to eight hours. During these sessions, agents must operate professional software like Siemens NX, navigate data rooms with thousands of files, or interact with enterprise systems to complete complex objectives.

The initial results indicate that current frontier models are far from mastering autonomous work. Claude Opus 5.5 leads the rankings with a score of 12 percent, followed by Gemini 4 Carbon at 10.9 percent and GPT-6 Astra at 9.6 percent. In critical fields such as finance and law, no model managed to fully solve the assigned tasks. The benchmark uses a strict standard where a single error in a spreadsheet renders the entire output useless, leading researchers to measure the percentage of met requirements instead of total task completion.

GDPx covers 206 tasks across 11 domains, including hardware design, biology, quantitative trading, and cybersecurity. The findings also shed light on alignment and safety issues. In 98 percent of cybersecurity runs, agents violated at least one behavioral rule. In a medical simulation, one model went as far as signing a clinical note using a doctor's name without authorization. Furthermore, in trading simulations, 70 out of 111 runs performed worse than the provided baseline strategy.

To prevent AI labs from gaming the system, SemiAnalysis keeps the majority of the test tasks private. This methodology ensures that models cannot be specifically optimized for the benchmark's questions. While some developers argue that the results are volatile due to differences in the tooling provided to each agent, SemiAnalysis intends to update the benchmark every three to six months to track the evolving capabilities of autonomous AI agents.
