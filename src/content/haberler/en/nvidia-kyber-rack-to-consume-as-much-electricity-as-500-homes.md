NVIDIA is pushing the boundaries of AI hardware to unprecedented levels. The company's new Kyber rack, which will house the Rubin Ultra NVL576 system, has caused a stir in the tech world with an estimated power consumption of 600 kW. Based on the average continuous consumption of an American household (approximately 1.2 kW), this means a single rack will consume as much electricity as roughly 500 homes.

The power requirements for AI chips and systems have grown exponentially in recent years. In 2022, a high-density Hopper rack consumed about 40 kW, while the 2024 Blackwell NVL72 rack saw this value rise to around 120 kW. The Kyber rack demands approximately five times more power than the Blackwell model and 75 times more than a standard enterprise server rack. This situation presents a significant challenge for existing data center infrastructures.

The design of the Kyber rack is notable not only for its energy consumption but also for its physical footprint. Due to the intense power and cooling requirements, the rack is expected to carry an additional side unit. This configuration means a single Kyber rack will effectively occupy the space of two racks in a data center. Converting existing facilities to provide 600 kW per rack and manage the resulting heat is, in most cases, technically and economically impractical.

This energy density is forcing cloud service providers and data center operators to redesign their facilities from the ground up. NVIDIA plans to launch the Rubin Ultra NVL576 and Kyber rack in the second half of 2027. However, some industry reports suggest that potential design delays could push this date into 2028. In any case, the Kyber rack stands as concrete evidence that the AI era is entering a new, energy-focused phase.
