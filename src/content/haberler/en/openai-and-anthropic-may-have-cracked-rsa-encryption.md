It has been claimed that OpenAI or Anthropic might have unlocked the digital lock long expected to be broken by quantum computers. Quantum computing expert Scott Aaronson pointed out in recent statements that two major artificial intelligence developers may have discovered a fast method to factor massive numbers into prime components. Aaronson expressed that he was almost certain groundbreaking developments capable of shifting balances had occurred in this field.

The RSA encryption infrastructure, which plays a critical role in internet security, is built on fundamental mathematical operations. While multiplying two small prime numbers is straightforward, finding which prime factors were used in numbers reaching hundreds of digits is extremely difficult. The security of the encryption method relies precisely on this level of difficulty. Under normal conditions, it is assumed that only quantum computers with sufficient power could solve such complex problems. However, the latest claims suggest that standard computers could achieve similar results without requiring additional hardware investments.

Achieving such technical capability has the potential to directly impact many areas from banking transactions to electronic signatures. The timeline planned for updating security infrastructures could be brought forward much earlier than anticipated. On the other hand, a concrete algorithm capable of effectively disabling RSA encryption has not yet been shared with the public. There are also no official results verified by independent researchers yet.

The tech community is closely monitoring the course of these claims, while waiting with curiosity to see whether the relevant artificial intelligence developers will issue an official statement on the matter. Subject matter experts emphasize that if the claims reflect reality, a complete overhaul of global cybersecurity standards will become inevitable.
