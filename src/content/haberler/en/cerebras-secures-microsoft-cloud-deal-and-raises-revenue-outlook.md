Cerebras, a prominent competitor to Nvidia in the AI processor market, has revised its 2026 financial outlook upward and announced a significant partnership with Microsoft. According to the company's third-quarter results, core revenue surged 70% year-over-year to $230.9 million, surpassing analyst expectations of $214.7 million. Management now anticipates full-year core revenue to fall between $940 million and $960 million, up from previous estimates.

CEO Andrew Feldman confirmed that the previously undisclosed "top three hyperscaler" customer is Microsoft, with the deal signed in October. Revenue from this partnership is expected to start flowing in the first quarter of 2027. Cerebras already maintains relationships with Amazon and Oracle; the latter plans to offer Cerebras inference services within its cloud infrastructure starting in 2027. This shift highlights Cerebras' strategic move toward a cloud-based service model rather than relying solely on hardware sales.

A substantial portion of the company's growth is anchored by its relationship with OpenAI. Much of Cerebras' $26.1 billion in remaining performance obligations—contracted revenue not yet booked—is tied to compute deals with OpenAI. Feldman noted that while OpenAI's business with Cerebras grew during the second quarter, the chipmaker currently lacks the capacity for a broad rollout of OpenAI’s "Ultrafast" tier, emphasizing the industry-wide struggle with infrastructure availability.

To address these capacity constraints, Cerebras has contracted 700 megawatts of data center capacity to be delivered by the end of 2027, including new sites in Norway and Finland. The company spent $1.05 billion on property and equipment in the first nine months of the year and ended September with $7.8 billion in cash and short-term investments. Cerebras also announced that its Wafer Scale Engine 5 processor, which will power the upcoming CS-5 system, is already in testing for a planned release in the summer of 2027.
