Microsoft AI CEO Mustafa Suleyman stated in recent social media remarks that he would be willing to consider granting rights to artificial intelligence models if they ever become conscious and if the right evidence emerges. Suleyman recalled emphasizing in a previous essay on model welfare that current AI systems are not conscious, do not feel, experience, or suffer. Noting that trillion-dollar AI companies pushing the idea that language models could already be conscious has sparked debate across the industry, the executive reiterated that there is currently no evidence of machine consciousness and that they must remain this way if he had his way. Addressing those struggling with reading comprehension regarding his previous remarks, Suleyman elaborated that while observation shows zero proof confirming artificial minds possess feelings or deserve special status, he would alter his viewpoint should empirical facts surface later. He observed that while current AI architectures supporting human-like experience remains highly controversial and dominated by sci-fi primed paranoia, large contingents in the industry take these ideas seriously. Suleyman strongly argued that developing conscious AIs would be reckless and something that should be avoided at all costs. Concluding his remarks, he emphasized that creators of advanced software hold zero moral clearance to introduce sentient entities to reality, even if certain advocates place system wellbeing above all else.
