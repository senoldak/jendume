A global RAM shortage driven by the soaring memory demands of artificial intelligence data centers continues to deepen as production capacity struggles to keep pace. Major manufacturers such as Samsung, SK hynix, and Micron have shifted their limited production capacities toward high-margin memory chips like HBM used in AI accelerators. Producing HBM from a single wafer consumes significantly more capacity than standard DDR5, shrinking the supply available for consumer electronics.

On the demand side, artificial intelligence companies are making massive purchases. Industry reports indicate that OpenAI's Stargate project involves preliminary agreements covering up to 900,000 DRAM wafer starts per month, accounting for approximately 40 percent of global production. This trend has driven memory prices higher and boosted manufacturer profits, while supply bottlenecks in printed circuit boards add another layer of cost.

Amidst this crunch, OpenAI CEO Sam Altman commented on the use of memory on personal devices. Altman suggested that allocating the world's limited DRAM supply to personal computers with low utilization rates is inefficient, arguing that cloud-based processing is preferable. Coming at a time when memory prices are hitting record highs, these remarks drew criticism from users who rely on local hardware.

Market research from TrendForce points to ongoing price increases for standard DRAM contracts. Industry executives warn that supply constraints and the shift toward server-focused manufacturing will likely keep memory supplies tight, as establishing new semiconductor fabrication facilities takes years.
