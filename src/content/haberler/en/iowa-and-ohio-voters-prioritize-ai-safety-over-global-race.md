A recent public opinion survey conducted in Iowa and Ohio reveals a clear shift in voter priorities regarding artificial intelligence. The findings indicate that voters prefer the implementation of robust safety measures over the urgency of winning the global AI race. This shift demonstrates that AI discussions have evolved beyond the realm of tech companies and government strategy, becoming a pressing political issue that directly impacts public safety and daily life.

Approximately 67% of voters in both states believe that the United States should pass reasonable AI safety regulations, even if it means not always securing the top position in the global race. There is overwhelming consensus on specific protective measures: preventing dangerous AI models from falling into the hands of adversaries received support as high as 94%. Furthermore, proposals such as strengthening parental controls and requiring government-led safety testing before AI models are deployed garnered support from over 85% of respondents.

These findings present a notable contrast to the current administration's strategy, which frames leadership in "super intelligence" as a necessity for national security and economic prosperity. While the government focuses on maintaining a competitive edge, voters are prioritizing the management of risks associated with these technologies. This public anxiety is also influencing labor organizations, which are currently drafting new legislation aimed at addressing workforce displacement and the uncertainties introduced by AI in the workplace.

Growing local resistance to the construction of data centers further reflects this underlying concern. Voters indicated a higher likelihood of supporting Senate candidates who champion common-sense AI safety measures, suggesting that this topic could become a decisive factor in upcoming elections. The rapidly evolving dynamics of the technology sector are no longer defined solely by engineering breakthroughs, but are increasingly shaped by the public's demand for safety, accountability, and stability.
