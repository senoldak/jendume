A U.S. court has ruled that Apple will gain expedited access to OpenAI's hiring materials in a trade secrets lawsuit filed against the artificial intelligence developer. The court order allows Apple to view documents associated with interviews conducted with former Apple employees who applied for roles in consumer hardware and robotics.

The documents include interview checklists, guides, manuals, and records indicating whether discussions referenced Apple technology. The court gave OpenAI five days to turn over already collected materials and 14 days for any remaining documents related to the expedited request.

The lawsuit unfolds as OpenAI develops consumer hardware devices that could eventually rival traditional smartphones. Apple's complaint accuses former employees of using company laptops to download confidential materials regarding unannounced technologies and features before moving to OpenAI.

While the judge approved access to interview materials and specific messaging app logs, he denied requests for expedited access to broader research and development areas such as power management for now. The case underscores the intensifying legal friction surrounding talent acquisition in the artificial intelligence sector.
