SoftBank, the Japanese investment giant known for its massive bets on artificial intelligence, has reported a $7 billion loss for the quarter ending in September. This marks the company's first quarterly loss in two years, signaling potential instability in its aggressive investment strategy. The financial downturn has intensified debates regarding the viability of the AI data center boom and the high-risk debt strategies used to fuel it.

Reports indicate that a significant portion of the loss is linked to large-scale data center developments in Ohio, intended to support AI model training. While SoftBank initially attributed the losses to these infrastructure projects, it later walked back those statements, claiming the losses were unrelated to its Ohio dealings. Regardless of the specific attribution, the market reaction has been severe, with investor confidence wavering as the firm continues to pour capital into AI powerhouses.

The scale of the financial shift is stark when compared to previous performance. The company posted a $2.17 billion profit in the preceding quarter and a substantial $15.64 billion profit in the third quarter of 2025. Following the recent loss, SoftBank's shares have plummeted by nearly 50 percent since June, as credit rating downgrades and skepticism about the AI industry's long-term profitability drive investors toward safer assets.

To maintain its funding for AI labs, SoftBank has increasingly turned to high-risk debt, issuing over $11 billion in non-investment grade bonds—often referred to as junk bonds—in September alone. This move has made the firm a record-holder for the largest corporate junk bond offering ever. Despite CEO Masayoshi Son's public dismissal of the idea of an AI bubble, the company's heavy reliance on high-risk financing to sustain its AI portfolio underscores the precarious nature of the current AI infrastructure gold rush.
