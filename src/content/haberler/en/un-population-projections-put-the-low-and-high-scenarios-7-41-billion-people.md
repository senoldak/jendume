In July 2024 the Population Division of the United Nations (UN) published its new population projections. According to the institution's summary, the population expected for the world in 2100 is about 700 million people, or 6%, smaller than in the projections published in 2013. The same summary puts the probability that world population reaches its highest value within this century at 80%.

That usually travels as a single number: in the medium scenario world population peaks at 10.29 billion in 2084 and eases to 10.18 billion in 2100. Yet the same data file holds two more numbers. In 2100 the low scenario shows 6.99 billion people and the high scenario 14.40 billion. The two are 7.41 billion apart.

This article follows that gap through time. It begins in 2023, reaches the middle of the century, passes the peak in 2084 and ends in 2100. We make no population forecast of our own. We place the three UN scenarios and one probability interval from the institution side by side, and we calculate how wide the range is, where it opens up and how it changes once we look at individual countries.

## One starting point, three paths

Each country and region gets three main scenarios from the UN: low, medium and high. In the data file, values until 2023 are the institution's estimates and values since 2024 are projections. For the world the estimate is 8.09 billion people in 2023. In 2024 the projection shows 8.16 billion. After this point the three paths part.

Assumptions behind the scenarios are not covered here, and no judgement is passed on whether they hold. We only compare the published values.

Separation starts slowly. In 2030 the low scenario shows 8.46 billion people and the high scenario 8.68 billion. In 2040 the figures are 8.78 billion and 9.57 billion. The band between the two scenarios is widening, but against the size of the numbers it is still narrow; both lie above the estimate in 2023, which is 8.09 billion.

## At mid-century the range opens by more than a billion

At mid-century, in 2050 the low scenario shows 8.94 billion people, the medium scenario 9.66 billion and the high scenario 10.40 billion. The gap between low and high is 1.45 billion people. The medium scenario corresponds to a rise of 19.4% on the estimate in 2023.

A turning point shows in the low scenario. In 2050 the value is 8.94 billion; in 2060 the value is 8.87 billion. Over the same period the high scenario rises from 10.40 billion to 11.18 billion. Since 2050, the two paths head in different directions.

Separation continues in the decades that follow. In 2070 the low scenario is at 8.60 billion people and the high scenario at 11.98 billion. In 2080 the figures are 8.18 billion and 12.77 billion. In 2090 the figures are 7.63 billion and 13.57 billion. The low scenario sinks a little further each decade; the high scenario climbs a little further.

## The peak and the end of the century

Medium-scenario world population reaches its highest value, 10.29 billion people, in 2084. In 2100 it eases to 10.18 billion; the medium-scenario value at the end of the century thus stays 25.8% above the estimate in 2023.

Turn to the low scenario and the same comparison reverses. In 2100 there are 6.99 billion people, 13.7% fewer than the estimate in 2023. In the high scenario there are 14.40 billion, 77.9% more than the estimate in 2023. All three scenarios start from the same estimate; by the end of the century one sits below it and two sit above.

Midway is not quite where the medium scenario sits between the low and the high scenario. In 2050 it is 0.72 billion people from the low scenario and 0.73 billion from the high scenario, so the range is almost symmetrical on either side of it. In 2100 that balance is gone: the medium scenario is 3.19 billion from the low scenario and 4.22 billion from the high scenario. The distance from the medium to the high scenario is 1.3 times the distance from the medium to the low scenario.

Altogether, in 2100 the gap is 7.41 billion people. That is 5.1 times the 1.45 billion seen in 2050.

## The gap as a share of the medium scenario, by country

Worldwide the gap of 7.41 billion equals 72.8% of the medium-scenario population. For individual countries the ratio changes.

Among the countries shown the ratio is highest in China, at 86.6%. It is 84.5% in South Korea, 81.9% in Brazil, 81.2% in Türkiye and 80.1% in India. In Japan it is 69.0%, in Nigeria 68.1%, in France 64.8%, in Germany 64.7% and in the United States 61.5%.

Absolute numbers paint a different picture. In India in 2100 the figure is 991 million people in the low scenario, 1.51 billion in the medium scenario and 2.20 billion in the high scenario; the gap is 1.21 billion. In Nigeria the figures are 335 million, 477 million and 660 million. In Japan they are 53.42 million, 76.85 million and 106.41 million. In South Korea they are 13.97 million, 21.85 million and 32.44 million. In China the low scenario shows 405.58 million, the medium scenario 633.37 million and the high scenario 954.16 million people; the estimate is 1.42 billion people in 2023. In the United States the figures are 307.50 million, 421.28 million and 566.46 million, against an estimate of 343.48 million. In Brazil they are 106.43 million, 163.36 million and 240.28 million, against an estimate of 211.14 million. In Germany they are 50.52 million, 70.90 million and 96.37 million, against an estimate of 84.55 million. In France they are 49.04 million, 68.48 million and 93.43 million, against an estimate of 66.44 million. The estimate for Japan is 124.37 million.

Among the countries shown the absolute gap has its highest value in India, and the ratio has its highest value in China. The two measures do not say the same thing: one shows how many people wide the range is, the other how wide it is relative to the medium scenario. A high ratio says the range is wide; it does not say whether a country's population is large or small.

## Countries where the direction depends on the scenario

If we read the three scenarios against the estimate in 2023, in some countries even the sign of the change varies from scenario to scenario. In France the low scenario shows a 26.2% decrease, the medium scenario a 3.1% increase and the high scenario a 40.6% increase. In India the figures are a 31.1% decrease, a 4.7% increase and a 52.8% increase. In the United States they are a 10.5% decrease, a 22.7% increase and a 64.9% increase.

According to the UN projection of July 2024, Türkiye's low scenario shows a 51.3% decrease, the medium scenario a 25.1% decrease and the high scenario a 9.5% increase. In Germany it is a 40.2% decrease, a 16.1% decrease and a 14.0% increase. In Brazil it is a 49.6% decrease, a 22.6% decrease and a 13.8% increase.

Elsewhere all three scenarios point the same way. In Japan all three show a decrease: a 57.0% decrease, a 38.2% decrease and a 14.4% decrease. In South Korea the figures are a 73.0% decrease, a 57.8% decrease and a 37.3% decrease; in China a 71.5% decrease, a 55.5% decrease and a 32.9% decrease. In Nigeria all three show an increase: 47.2%, 109.2% and 189.6%.

This comparison shows in which countries the question of direction stays inside the range. It does not say which scenario will come about.

## Three paths for Türkiye

Türkiye's estimate is 87.27 million people in 2023. In the UN projection of July 2024 the medium scenario rises to 91.26 million in 2050, falls to 88.86 million in 2060 and shows 65.34 million in 2100.

The same UN projection puts the low scenario for Türkiye at 84.46 million people in 2050. In 2100 the low scenario shows 42.53 million. In 2050 the high scenario shows 97.99 million, and in 2100 the high scenario shows 95.57 million. The value in 2100 is above the estimate in 2023 in the high scenario and below it in the low scenario. In 2100 the gap between low and high is 53.04 million people, which is 81.2% of the medium scenario.

In the UN projection of July 2024 the low scenario for Türkiye falls every decade. In 2030 it shows 87.85 million people. In 2040 the figure is 87.06 million. In 2060 the figure is 79.14 million, in 2070 it is 71.24 million, in 2080 it is 62.01 million and in 2090 it is 52.28 million. The high scenario shows 98.95 million in 2060. In 2070 the high scenario shows 99.02 million. In 2080 the high scenario shows 98.05 million. The medium scenario shows 88.86 million, 84.36 million and 78.56 million in the same three years.

## A counter-reading: scenario range or probability interval?

World totals have a second UN product: a probability interval. In the same data file the 80% interval in 2100 has a lower bound of 9.41 billion and an upper bound of 11.03 billion people. The difference is 1.63 billion. The gap of 7.41 billion between the low and high scenarios is 4.5 times the width of this interval.

The two products differ: one is published as scenarios, the other as a probability interval. The practical point for a reader is that the word range does not mean one thing. Which range is meant determines the width that comes out. Reading the low and high scenarios as a plausible interval can mislead as much as reading the probability interval as a scenario gap.

Only the medium scenario has its peak in 2084. The institution puts the probability of a peak within this century at 80%.

## Uncertainty and measurement limits

Projections can also be compared with an earlier projection. The UN's summary states that the population expected in 2100 is 6%, or about 700 million people, smaller than in the projections published in 2013. The line between estimate and projection is not sharp either: values up to the year 2023 can also change from one revision to the next, and the estimates here are those of the revision published in July 2024. The range itself shifts over time; today's medium scenario need not be the medium scenario of the next revision.

Values here come from the total-population table of the revision of the UN Population Division published in July 2024; they refer to 1 July of each year. The table gives total population; it contains no breakdown by age or anything else. The countries we show are a sample: ten countries, and others could have been chosen.

Low and high scenarios are scenarios published by the UN; this article does not read them as limits on possible values. The country values are separately published scenarios; we did not add them up.

Comparisons in this article keep three distinctions apart: estimate versus projection, scenario versus probability interval, and absolute gap versus ratio. Mixing the three can produce sentences that seem to contradict one another from the same data file.

## How we calculated

Our source is the total-population table (WPP2024_TotalPopulationBySex) in the World Population Prospects data file that the UN Population Division published in July 2024. The file is public and downloadable; its address is in the sources section. For every country, region and scenario the table gives annual total population in thousands of people. We took the low, medium and high scenarios, and the lower and upper bounds of the 80% probability interval for the world.

Values were taken every ten years in 2030–2100 for the world and for Türkiye; for ten countries we took only the year 2100 and the estimate in 2023. The gap is the high-scenario value minus the low-scenario value. The ratio is that gap divided by the medium-scenario value and written as a percentage. The change on the estimate in 2023 is the percentage change between the estimate and the value of the scenario in question. The multiple is the gap in 2100, divided by the gap in 2050, or by the width of the probability interval.

Three numbers from the summary report (6%, 700 million, 80%) are taken as they stand and recorded in the evidence file with their page location. Values in the text are rounded to two decimals for billions and for millions, and to one decimal for percentages; calculations use unrounded values, and results are rounded to the decimals declared in the evidence file.
