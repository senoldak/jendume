The UK economy grew by 0.7% in the third quarter, marking its fastest expansion in two and a half years, largely fueled by the ongoing AI boom. This growth surpassed the optimistic 0.3% forecasts from Treasury analysts and City experts, positioning the UK as the second-fastest growing G7 economy this year.

Growth was primarily spearheaded by the IT and professional services sectors, which together contributed approximately half of the total rise in economic activity. Analysts describe this surge as a "datacentre story," highlighting the UK's status as a premier destination for AI infrastructure investment in Europe. This technological backbone has become a critical pillar of national economic resilience.

Estimates suggest that AI-related spending added between 0.3% and 0.4% to the UK's GDP growth this year. The government is actively seeking to harness this momentum by courting global business investment in AI to build on the groundwork laid by previous administrations. However, the sustainability of this growth engine faces potential headwinds from global market volatility.

Concerns regarding a potential bubble in AI stocks and a slowdown in technology investment following international political shifts could impact the UK's trajectory. Furthermore, rising energy bills and cooling real wage growth may pressure consumer spending in the coming months. Despite these challenges, the underlying demand for AI infrastructure remains a significant buffer against broader economic stagnation.
