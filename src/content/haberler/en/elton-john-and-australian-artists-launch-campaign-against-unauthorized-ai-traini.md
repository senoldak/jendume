Elton John made a surprise appearance at the 2026 Aria Awards to urge the Australian government to protect artists from the unchecked practices of AI firms. The British musician backed the "Not AI’s To Take" campaign, a major initiative launched by creative and media industries to ensure that AI companies obtain permission and provide compensation before training their models on copyrighted works. The campaign has garnered support from a wide array of sectors, including music, film, television, and publishing.

The core demand of the campaign is straightforward: AI developers must seek explicit consent and pay creators when using their intellectual property for training purposes. Artists argue that human creativity is the foundation of their industry and should not be exploited by machines without recognition or remuneration. This movement highlights a growing tension between the rapid advancement of generative AI and the fundamental rights of human creators.

The stakes are significant for the Australian economy. The creative industry is valued at 70 billion dollars and supports 600,000 workers. Campaigners warn that the changes sought by AI giants could jeopardize this entire ecosystem and the livelihoods of those who sustain it. By uniting under a single banner, these diverse groups are signaling to the government that the protection of human labor is non-negotiable.

The Australian government is expected to unveil a legislative package concerning AI and copyright in late 2026 or early 2027. While officials have pledged to ensure creators receive fair compensation, the path forward remains complex. AI companies have advocated for an "opt-out" model, where works are used unless the rights holder objects, whereas artists insist on an "opt-in" approach requiring prior permission. As the government weighs these options, the outcome will likely set a precedent for how copyright law adapts to the age of artificial intelligence globally.
