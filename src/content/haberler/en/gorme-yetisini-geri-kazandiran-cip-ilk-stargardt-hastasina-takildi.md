Science Corporation, founded by former Neuralink executive Max Hodak, has achieved a significant milestone by implanting its PRIMA vision-restoring chip into the first patient suffering from Stargardt disease, an inherited condition that gradually destroys central vision.

The procedure involves placing a tiny two-millimeter chip at the back of the patient's eye. Patients then wear camera-equipped glasses that capture the surrounding world and transmit visual data directly to the implant. The device translates these visuals into electrical signals, mimicking natural photoreceptors and sending signals along the optic nerve to the brain. Early rehabilitation sessions showed the patient successfully recognizing letters and reading words.

Looking ahead, the company aims to expand the chip's field of view and refine electrode sizes to achieve near-natural visual acuity. Beyond vision restoration, the broader mission explores utilizing brain-computer interfaces to address various neurological conditions, strokes, and ultimately expand human health span.
