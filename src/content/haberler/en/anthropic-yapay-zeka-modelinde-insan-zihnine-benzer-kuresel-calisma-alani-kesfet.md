Neuroscientists and philosophers gathered in the Galapagos Islands for an exclusive workshop organized by the International Center for Consciousness Studies to discuss whether entities like chatbots possess an inner life. The event focused heavily on how information is processed in biological brains versus artificial machines and explored the broader implications of artificial general intelligence.

The discussions were prompted by a research paper published by AI company Anthropic, which argued that its Claude model had spontaneously developed something akin to the human mind's global workspace. According to leading cognitive hypotheses and workspace theories discussed by researchers, this workspace serves as the seat of consciousness where thoughts and sensory inputs merge into awareness.

While most large language models operate statistically using prompt data and training sequences, their computations evolve in unpredictable ways based on the data they receive. Researchers at Anthropic utilized specialized decoding tools to read out units of information within Claude's neural networks, revealing patterns that mirror the global neuronal workspace theory established in human and animal neuroscience over decades of academic research.

Experts at the workshop emphasized that while these emergent properties do not definitively prove that AIs possess subjective experiences like humans, they provide profound insights into the differences between biological and artificial minds. The discovery of J-space and covert thoughts during tests involving mental arithmetic and text translation highlights how advanced language models handle complex reasoning tasks, reshaping how society evaluates machine intelligence and capability.
