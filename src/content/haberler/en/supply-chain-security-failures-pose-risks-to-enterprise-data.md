A comprehensive new study reveals that a significant portion of companies developing and deploying artificial intelligence technologies are failing to adhere to basic cybersecurity practices. By examining over 18,000 AI-focused organizations worldwide, researchers have identified a widespread weakness that introduces substantial risks into the digital supply chain. Businesses are increasingly integrating third-party AI solutions to streamline operations and handle complex tasks. However, when these tools are introduced into a corporate ecosystem, any existing security flaws in the supplier's infrastructure are inherited by the user. This reality makes it imperative for companies to look beyond the functionality of an AI tool and rigorously audit the security posture of the vendors they rely on.

The research found unusual network activity at 44.3% of the assessed organizations, while 41.8% showed signs of botnet-related activity. Furthermore, nearly half of the security findings related to protocols that link AI applications to business systems were classified as high or critical. Additionally, 33.2% of the companies studied maintain a limited digital footprint, making them difficult for clients to monitor effectively. With the vast majority of enterprise AI solutions being purchased rather than built internally, the attack surface for corporations is expanding rapidly. Security experts emphasize that a one-time assessment is no longer sufficient. To mitigate these risks, businesses must implement continuous monitoring of their AI suppliers, audit the data access permissions of these tools, and prioritize the management of vulnerabilities that could be exploited by ransomware or other cyber threats.
