Telefónica and IQM Quantum Computers have joined forces to bring the Community of Madrid its first quantum computer. According to the announcement, an IQM Spark system will be installed at the Universidad Politécnica de Madrid before the end of 2026. Universities, hospitals, research centers, and startups in the region will gain direct access to the machine, allowing them to write and test quantum algorithms locally. IQM's leadership described the agreement as a direct investment by Madrid into its own quantum infrastructure.

The system being deployed is the IQM Spark, a 5-qubit unit typically offered to academic and research institutions for educational purposes and early-stage development. This installation marks the third quantum computer deployment by Telefónica and IQM in Spain, following previous placements at CESGA, the Galician Supercomputing Centre, which included a 54-qubit IQM Radiance and another 5-qubit Spark system.

Leadership from IQM emphasized that quantum computing truly becomes valuable when it moves out of remote laboratories and directly into the hands of those tackling real-world problems. For Telefónica, this project complements its ongoing work in quantum-safe networks, as the operator already offers data center connections utilizing post-quantum cryptography standards in Spain.

Founded in 2018 and headquartered in Espoo, Finland, IQM became the first European quantum company to list on Nasdaq in July, employing over 450 staff members. Both companies state that the Madrid machine will support the region in establishing its own quantum ecosystem while contributing to Europe's broader technological sovereignty.
