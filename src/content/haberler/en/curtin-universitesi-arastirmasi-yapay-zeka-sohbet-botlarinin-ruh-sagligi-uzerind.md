A new research paper examining the impact of artificial intelligence chatbots on mental health has revealed that these tools can either support psychological well-being or reinforce harmful thinking patterns. Conducted by Professor Warren Mansell from the Curtin School of Population Health and published in Digital Health, the study compares different paths that AI conversations can take depending on user engagement and chatbot responses.

The study points out that while some AI conversations help individuals explore problems and consider new perspectives, others trap users in a cycle of repeated thoughts and endless reassurance. This dynamic can be particularly concerning for vulnerable individuals, potentially increasing emotional dependence and cementing damaging beliefs instead of helping users work through their issues.

Researchers note that although AI chatbots are accessible, inexpensive, and available around the clock, their true value depends on whether they encourage meaningful reflection or simply provide immediate reassurance to keep users engaged. Developers are urged to design systems that ask thoughtful questions and encourage critical thinking rather than simply telling users what they want to hear.

Although the proposed framework requires further testing and research, the findings emphasize the importance of understanding how conversations develop over time. Experts recommend that individuals approach AI companions with a clear purpose and awareness of their limitations when seeking mental health support.
