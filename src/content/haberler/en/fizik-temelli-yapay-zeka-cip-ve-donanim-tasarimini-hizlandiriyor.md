For hardware engineers, running simulations is essential to catching design flaws and avoiding costly deployment errors. However, orchestrating these simulations traditionally required specialized expertise and weeks of waiting. Aiming to transform this workflow, Vinci4D developed a software platform that lets engineers execute simulations just one day after a design change instead of two weeks later.

According to co-founder and Chief Executive Officer Hardik Kabaria, the company integrated core physics equations with custom-trained artificial intelligence models to build an out-of-the-box accurate tool. This physics-driven software operates up to 1,000 times faster than conventional simulation tools while maintaining higher accuracy. The technology addresses the intersection of advanced manufacturing demands and infrastructure reliability.

The startup works closely with Advanced Micro Devices, optimizing its software to run on AMD hardware platforms. This allows engineers to analyze printed circuit boards, chip packages, and 3D stacked integrated circuits with greater speed and precision. In October 2026, Vinci closed a $250 million Series B funding round at a $1.5 billion valuation, led jointly by Advent, Temasek, and Xora, with participation from AMD Ventures and other venture capital firms.

Emphasizing that hardware development relies on a collaborative ecosystem, company leadership notes that the rapid pace of future innovation will depend on joint industry efforts. These advancements mark an important step forward for semiconductor facilities and complex manufacturing environments.
