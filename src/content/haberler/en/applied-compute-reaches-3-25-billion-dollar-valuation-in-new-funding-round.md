Company financing rounds announced in early November highlighted the widening gap between valuations and revenues across the artificial intelligence and technology landscape. Companies prominent in funding rounds are being examined through their underlying business models and revenue proofs. According to its November announcement, Applied Compute reached an annualized run rate of 100 million dollars and secured 350 million dollars in funding at a valuation of 3.25 billion dollars. These figures indicate a revenue multiple of approximately 32.5 times, with investors including Kleiner Perkins and NVIDIA.

Other transactions across the sector reflect diverse financial structures. Revolut's secondary share sale valued the company at 115 billion dollars, while Beacon Software reached a valuation of 1.75 billion dollars through the consolidated revenue of its 45 portfolio businesses. The total of Beacon's expanded Series C round rose to 425 million dollars with a 50 million dollar contribution from the Canada Pension Plan Investment Board. Meanwhile, companies like Traversal and Northwood attracted attention with their funding amounts and valuations, though they were excluded from direct revenue multiple calculations due to unreleased exact revenue figures.

Additionally, multi-billion dollar valuation expectations concerning OpenAI and Anthropic, based on uncompleted rounds and unverified claims, continue to circulate. Experts emphasize that such unconfirmed targets must be evaluated separately from completed transactions. High valuation negotiations lacking revenue proof can pose risks to startup sustainability.
