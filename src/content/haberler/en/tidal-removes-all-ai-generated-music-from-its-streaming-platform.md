Starting December 9, 2026, the music streaming platform Tidal will begin a comprehensive purge of all music tracks entirely generated by artificial intelligence. Previously, the company attempted to manage the issue by labeling such content and demonetizing it, but these measures proved insufficient to stem the volume of AI-generated content flooding the platform. According to company data, 43 percent of all tracks uploaded to the service over the last 90 days were flagged as AI-generated, necessitating a more drastic approach.

This cleanup operation is expected to result in the removal of approximately 29 million tracks from the platform's catalog. Furthermore, the company will begin rejecting an estimated 80,000 to 85,000 AI-generated songs daily. Company officials believe this decision will resonate positively with both artists and listeners. Data indicates that 96 percent of all AI-generated songs on the platform receive zero plays, suggesting that this content provides no value to the user experience. This decision follows the ongoing debate regarding the use of artificial intelligence in the music industry.

While other major streaming platforms have largely opted to label AI content or impose partial restrictions, Tidal's decision to outright ban fully AI-generated music distinguishes human artistry from machine-generated content. The company clarifies that it does not oppose artists using AI as a creative tool, but it maintains that content created entirely by AI without human input degrades the quality of the platform. The company is implementing these new content management strategies across its digital platform.
