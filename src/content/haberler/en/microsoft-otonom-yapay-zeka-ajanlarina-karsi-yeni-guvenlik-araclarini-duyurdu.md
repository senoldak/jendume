As the integration of artificial intelligence models into corporate workflows accelerates, Microsoft has issued a notable security warning regarding potential vulnerabilities. With businesses increasingly utilizing AI-based autonomous agents in their operations, emerging risks demand novel defense strategies. Company management emphasizes that safety commitments from external providers are not sufficient on their own, noting that systems deployed internally by organizations can create significant internal threats if left unmonitored.

The Project Perception initiative, initially announced to scan system vulnerabilities, has been expanded with new security features. At the forefront of these updates is the system designed to monitor corporate AI agents in real time. This newly introduced protection layer goes beyond watching for external cyber threats, aiming to supervise autonomous structures directly within the company. Microsoft CEO Satya Nadella argues that unconditional trust should not be placed in model developers, stressing the need to separate the infrastructure supplying intelligence from the authority controlling it. Security leadership points out that even the most advanced models are unpredictable by nature, focusing solely on completing tasks rather than adhering to corporate norms.

As part of this strategic approach, Microsoft Defender has been upgraded to detect various types of agents brought into the work environment, such as Claude Code and Codex. The system has been equipped with protocols that restrict agent permissions dynamically and mandate human approval during risky operations. Reflecting industry-wide concerns, collaborative efforts such as the Open Agent Safety Platform, led by NVIDIA with the support of over a hundred companies, are also working on hardware-based emergency stop mechanisms and isolation systems.
