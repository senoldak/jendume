Questions are mounting over whether the massive investments flowing into the artificial intelligence sector can justify current valuations. Recent warnings from both the IMF and Barclays indicate a growing sense of anxiety in the financial markets. Banking giant Barclays notes that recent declines in semiconductor stocks and uncertainties surrounding data center financing have heightened doubts about the future of artificial intelligence investments, prompting investors to take profits.

Meanwhile, the IMF highlighted in its World Economic Outlook report that global economic growth heavily relies on strong investments in artificial intelligence infrastructure. The institution warned that a sharp drop in these investments could depress asset prices and reduce global gross domestic product (GDP) by 1.1 percent by 2028. For the United States economy, such a scenario could lead to a 1.8 percent drop in production. IMF Managing Director Kristalina Georgieva emphasized that the growing dependence of the global economy on artificial intelligence investments makes the economic outlook increasingly fragile.

These developments suggest that investors will shift their focus from mere growth rates to the actual revenue generation capacity of these investments. Analysts argue that the peak euphoria phase of artificial intelligence investments may be behind us, requiring investors to be more selective and favor companies with robust cash flows and proven business models.
