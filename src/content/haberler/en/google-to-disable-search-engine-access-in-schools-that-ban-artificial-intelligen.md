Google has announced a major update to its education policies, stating that it will completely turn off search services for student accounts in schools that reject its artificial intelligence features. According to the company, access to products like Gemini and search functions will remain off for K-12 student accounts unless school administrators explicitly turn them on. This policy update comes as educational institutions review their digital tool usage.

Company executives explained that search and artificial intelligence are deeply integrated, meaning administrators cannot easily separate the two functions. If a school decides to ban AI tools entirely, students will lose access to search services and see a notification indicating availability is restricted. Meanwhile, several jurisdictions including New York and Los Angeles have previously placed limits on generative AI tools in public school systems.

To address educational concerns regarding academic integrity, Google is introducing a Guided Learning tool designed to help students think critically rather than just providing direct answers. This feature breaks down complex problems into step-by-step explanations and will be tested in select United States schools before a broader rollout. Teachers will also be able to review transcripts of chatbot interactions.

The update follows previous friction between educators and the technology provider, particularly after certain artificial intelligence features were enabled by default in learning platforms earlier in the year. As public schools and state regulators continue to debate the role of automated tools in classrooms, these new controls will be rolled out in the coming months.
