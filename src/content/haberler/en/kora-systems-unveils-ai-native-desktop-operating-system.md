Cyanogen co-founder Kirt McMaster has unveiled a new desktop operating system built specifically for the artificial intelligence era by his startup, Kora Systems. Built on Linux and Nix, the operating system directly integrates AI models and autonomous agents into the user interface, enabling them to work across applications, access local files, and retain context across tasks. The software is slated for release in 2027 as a free desktop operating system supporting Intel, AMD, and Qualcomm Snapdragon processors.

Formerly known as Playtron, a company focused on gaming operating systems, Kora rebranded in July 2025 to pivot entirely toward AI integration. McMaster stated that web browsers fail to provide AI agents with sufficient system access to complete complex tasks, making an operating system a superior foundation. Developing the platform with a team of around 50 engineers, Kora utilized AI coding tools such as OpenAI's Codex and Anthropic's Claude, which McMaster noted accelerated overall development by at least fivefold.

While Microsoft Windows continues to dominate the global desktop operating system landscape with roughly 77% market share, Kora aims to serve a targeted niche. The company focuses on an estimated 45 to 50 million power users who heavily rely on AI tools and are willing to pay for premium access. McMaster projects this addressable power-user market will grow to over 200 million users by 2033.

Kora features a proprietary Cortex AI engine designed to process lightweight tasks locally while routing computationally heavy workloads to cloud models. The Palo Alto-based company has collaborated with Qualcomm for nearly a year to optimize performance for Snapdragon X2 Elite chips. Additionally, Kora partnered with Saudi Arabian AI firm Humain to build an enterprise edition called Humain OS. Backed by investors including Samsung Next, Square Enix, Alumni Ventures, Polychain, VanEck, and Circle, Kora plans to generate revenue via bundled subscription services.
