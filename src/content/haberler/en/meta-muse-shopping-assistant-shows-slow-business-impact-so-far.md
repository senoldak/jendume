Meta's AI shopping assistant Muse has gained millions of users since its launch, but its immediate economic impact on corporations remains limited. While Wall Street fears that proactive shopping assistants will disrupt brand loyalty and ignite price wars, executives across roughly 50 companies spanning travel, food, and banking report minimal immediate disruption from the technology.

Major brands such as DoorDash, Hilton, and eBay note that order volumes and bookings originating from Muse and other AI agents are very small or insignificant. Fitness chains and delivery services similarly report zero subscription cancellations stemming from the automated assistants, indicating that consumer habits are shifting gradually.

Meta CEO Mark Zuckerberg stated that more than 10 million people in the US and Canada have activated Muse, with over 25 percent using it for shopping tasks. Meta has formed partnerships with major retailers like Walmart, Best Buy, and Gap to streamline shopping and plans to charge businesses a fee for transactions facilitated by the assistant.

Despite the slow uptake, companies are already adapting their websites for AI agents. Restaurant chains like Chipotle are updating their website code to help agents navigate online menus and apply discounts smoothly after discovering minor technical hiccups. Meanwhile, retail giant Amazon briefly blocked Muse before expressing openness to working with external agents.

Security concerns also persist as financial institutions warn customers about sharing login credentials with AI assistants. Industry leaders suggest that while autonomous agents are still in their early stages, product quality, customer service, and brand trust will remain crucial factors in purchase decisions.
