Intel is moving forward with its domestic factory initiatives as the need for data centre processors used in artificial intelligence services outpaces available supply. The corporation has begun placing machinery inside its newest semiconductor facility, designated Fab 62, situated close to Phoenix. Concurrently, construction activity has advanced at the previously delayed manufacturing location in Ohio.

Fab 62 is positioned within the Ocotillo operational grounds in Arizona, adjacent to the already active Fab 52 structure. Commercial operations at this new location are targeted to commence during the early portion of 2028. Equipping a semiconductor facility represents the most expensive phase of development, given that individual fabrication tools frequently require hundreds of millions of dollars.

Within New Albany, Ohio, construction personnel successfully raised the initial primary structural roof support for the location's primary production building. This regional hub was originally envisioned to become a massive global semiconductor manufacturing center, though development schedules experienced prior delays and an adjusted launch window spanning 2030 to 2031.

Chief executive Lip-Bu Tan, who assumed leadership responsibilities in March 2025, previously indicated that expansion initiatives like the Ohio site would advance in alignment with verified market demand. The recent construction milestones reflect strengthening organizational confidence regarding future orders.

Incoming requests for data centre processors supporting artificial intelligence workloads continue expanding beyond current production velocity. Furthermore, the organization is directing capital toward Xeon processor manufacturing expansions in Ireland and has elevated overall capital expenditure projections to accommodate upcoming market requirements.
