As the artificial intelligence boom reshapes the global technology landscape, legacy software companies are facing unprecedented disruptions. Milan-based holding company Bending Spoons has emerged as a prominent buyer of once-high-flying software startups that have been sidelined by the rapid advancement of generative artificial intelligence. Backed by a substantial investment of over $900 million from Berkshire Hathaway, the company's aggressive merger and acquisition strategy is gaining significant momentum on international markets following its public debut.

The investment from Warren Buffett's conglomerate has bolstered market confidence, pushing Bending Spoons' valuation to approximately $30 billion. However, this aggressive expansion also underscores the harsher realities of the current tech climate. Recent acquisitions, such as workflow software provider Airtable purchased at a steep discount roughly ninety percent below its late 2021 peak valuation, have been accompanied by significant workforce reductions. As large language models and artificial intelligence agents displace traditional developer tools, discussions around a broader software industry correction have intensified across the sector.

Unlike traditional private equity firms focused strictly on short-term turnarounds, Bending Spoons takes a hands-on operational approach inspired by long-term value investing principles. By acquiring established platforms with high switching costs and optimizing their operations, the company is charting a unique course through the shifting economic realities of the artificial intelligence revolution. CEO Luca Ferrari and his co-founders have utilized profits from portfolio acquisitions to fund further deals, driving revenue past initial milestones while navigating the structural shifts brought on by modern software automation.
