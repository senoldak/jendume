In traditional factories, robots are typically programmed for a single specific task, meaning any change to the production line requires weeks of reprogramming and physical retooling. Founded by former Tesla engineers, Industrial Next aims to tackle this limitation using AI-powered foundation models that connect vision, motion, and task context.

The startup's approach relies on training robots within high-fidelity simulation engines that closely mimic real-world physics. This virtual practice allows robots to master high-precision and delicate physical tasks—such as threading delicate cables or peeling protective films—at or above human speed without traditional hard-coded scripts.

Industrial Next recently announced a partnership with BMW Group Technology Office to evaluate its AI-powered dexterous manipulation for manufacturing. The ultimate vision for the technology is to enable fully automated end-to-end production lines, from assembling small consumer electronics to building entire vehicles without human intervention.
