OpenAI is targeting a demonstration in 2027 as part of its humanoid robot development efforts. Shared by company executive Sam Altman, this timeline does not mean that the robots will be directly released for sale or made available to consumers. While global momentum in the humanoid robot market continues, widespread adoption of such systems is expected to take several years.

Coming to light in an interview conducted in September, this plan does not yet constitute the official launch of a new model. The company has not yet disclosed the robot's final appearance, retail price, or release date. Technical details such as the motors to be used, mobility capabilities, and payload capacity also remain unconfirmed.

OpenAI's interest in systems capable of operating in the physical world dates back several years. On October 15, 2019, the company shared research on solving a Rubik's cube using a robotic hand. At that time, neural networks trained in simulation were transferred to a physical robot, aiming to adapt the system to different conditions through reinforcement learning methods. However, this past research does not serve as a direct continuation or test result of 's humanoid robot project.

Experts point out that there are significant stages between a demonstration and daily use. While demo projects can showcase prototype capabilities under specific conditions, mass production and safe deployment require separate processes. Future announcements by OpenAI will clarify the scope of the 2027 demonstration and the stage the project has reached.
