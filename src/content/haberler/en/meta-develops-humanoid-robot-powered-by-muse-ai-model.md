Meta is reportedly developing a new humanoid robot powered by its "Muse" artificial intelligence model, aiming to bring AI technology into the physical world. According to leaked information, this mechanical assistant is designed to stand approximately 137 centimeters tall and weigh 48 kilograms. The robot's most notable features include its ability to mimic human facial expressions and maintain direct eye contact. This development is seen as part of a strategy by tech giants to introduce AI software into people's private living spaces in the guise of friendly and helpful assistants.

The development of these next-generation robots represents not just a technological advancement but also a strategic shift in financial models. Competing with rivals like Tesla and Boston Dynamics, Meta aims to overcome consumer reluctance to pay for abstract AI subscriptions by offering robots that provide physical services. Companies plan to market these robots through monthly rental models that include AI updates and maintenance services, rather than one-time sales. In this way, high-cost AI infrastructures will be transformed into a steady stream of revenue through home robots.

However, significant hurdles remain behind these technological displays. Contrary to social media videos showing robots dancing or performing complex movements, most current prototypes still operate via remote control or pre-defined codes. More time is needed before robots can operate fully autonomously and safely in the unpredictable and complex environment of a typical home.

Ultimately, Meta's move serves as a concrete example of how billions of dollars in AI investments will be commercialized. When robots truly become reliable assistants, consumers will begin to directly finance massive data centers and the AI ecosystem through these in-home helpers. This paves the way for AI to become not just a tool, but an economic way of life.
