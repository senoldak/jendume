Wherobots has unveiled Waldo, a new AI agent designed to transform workflows for teams working with location data. Spatial data analysis is inherently complex, involving the integration of satellite imagery, GPS logs, and geographic coordinates. Traditionally, setting up data pipelines and conducting analysis projects could take weeks, but Waldo aims to reduce this timeline to mere days.

Waldo is not a new large language model (LLM) built from scratch. Instead, it acts as an interface that leverages existing LLMs to accelerate spatial data flows. Users can issue natural language commands to locate data sources, generate queries, and visualize results. The system integrates seamlessly with existing enterprise data infrastructure, including Amazon Web Services Glue and Databricks Unity Catalog, meaning customers do not need to migrate their data.

The company highlights the efficiency gains with concrete examples. One customer successfully reduced a large-scale data merging process from a full week to under an hour. Another user is completing fleet analytics processes two to three times faster compared to manual methods.

Despite these gains, the technology is not yet flawless. Company executives openly acknowledge that Waldo does not guarantee error-free results for every command, emphasizing that human oversight remains essential. Users can review generated queries and visualizations, and even utilize Jupyter notebooks to verify individual processing steps.

Founded in 2022 by the creators of the Apache Sedona spatial analysis engine, Wherobots aims to make spatial analytics more accessible. Waldo is currently in public preview and is included in the Professional Edition without additional fees, allowing customers to pay primarily for the computing resources they consume.
