Palmer Luckey issued a critical warning at a technology summit on November 18, 2026, stating that AI-powered cyberattacks are rapidly outpacing current defense systems. As the founder of Oculus and Anduril Industries, Luckey argued that aging technological infrastructures are struggling to counter the new and complex attack capabilities enabled by artificial intelligence. This situation indicates that institutions and governments must fundamentally change how they protect their digital security.

Cyber attackers can find vulnerabilities much faster and more effectively by utilizing AI tools. Luckey noted that these attacks are carried out for both strategic and financial reasons, and that actors such as North Korea are actively employing these new capabilities. Recent global tech outages have highlighted the severe consequences of this defense gap. While government officials have attributed these outages to companies rushing to patch their systems against new vulnerabilities, the underlying issue lies in the mismatch between the speed of outdated systems and the speed of modern attacks.

This development represents a vital turning point for the future of digital security. According to Luckey, while attackers move very quickly, defense systems remain slow to adapt to technological advancements. This "speed mismatch" has become the primary weakness of the defense side. It is predicted that in the long run, the advantage will accrue to the side that most effectively uses AI for defense.

Luckey emphasizes that defense systems must "start running" against these new threats, meaning the pace of developing security measures must be urgently accelerated. In a world where everything is potentially hackable, using AI as a shield rather than viewing it solely as a threat is no longer an option but a necessity. Unless institutions abandon outdated systems and transition to AI-powered proactive defense mechanisms, they appear destined to fall behind in this digital race.
