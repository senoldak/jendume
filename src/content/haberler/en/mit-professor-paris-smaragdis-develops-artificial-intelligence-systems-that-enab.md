Massachusetts Institute of Technology (MIT) Professor Paris Smaragdis is conducting research on new artificial intelligence and machine learning systems designed to help machines perceive and understand the auditory world. Smaragdis builds his research on the premise that, much like computer vision, machines need an advanced sense of hearing. These systems, equipped with super-human hearing capabilities, can be used for critical tasks such as rapidly pinpointing traffic accidents by listening for squealing tires or identifying potential failures in industrial drilling.

The researcher's past work includes machine learning models for sound source separation and advanced audio processing technologies. Smaragdis and his team developed technologies using neural networks to separate individual sounds from complex audio mixtures. One of these technologies was utilized to clean up archival recordings for the 2021 documentary series 'The Beatles: Get Back,' a project that earned the team an Emmy Award in 2022.

Combining his musical background with computer science, Smaragdis has worked at institutions such as Mitsubishi Electric Research Labs and Adobe throughout his career. During this time, he developed technologies enabling AI systems to scan video soundtracks to capture highlights and tools allowing artists to manipulate sounds easily. After a long academic tenure at the University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign, the researcher joined the MIT faculty.

Tackling interdisciplinary challenges with his research group at MIT, Smaragdis aims to enable machines not only to hear sounds but also to understand their meanings. The smart devices and audio processing tools being developed aim to enrich daily life and transform the processes of creating and analyzing music.
