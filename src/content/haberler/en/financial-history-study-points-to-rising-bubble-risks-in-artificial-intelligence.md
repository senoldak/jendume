Artificial intelligence investments have come under renewed scrutiny through a comprehensive study examining historical financial data spanning 170 years. Conducted by Marco Grotteria, Afonso Alves Monteiro, and Constantine Yannelis, the research analyzed approximately 796,000 articles published between 1843 and 2026 using large language models, covering twenty-two historical financial bubbles across ten countries.

To identify speculative movements, the researchers utilized five key indicators, including speculative narratives, regulatory warnings, and corporate valuation concerns. Based on the monthly financial bubble index derived from these indicators, the probability of an artificial intelligence-driven financial bubble was calculated at ninety-seven percent as of September 2026.

One of the most striking findings of the study is that current market conditions share strong similarities with the dot-com bubble that peaked in March 2000. Rapid company valuations, massive data center investments, and heavy infrastructure spending are cited as primary drivers. For instance, leading AI firms such as Anthropic have recorded substantial long-term infrastructure spending commitments alongside significant net losses despite growing revenues.

Historical precedent suggests that speculative bubbles do not render the underlying technology worthless; past booms in railways and the internet ultimately left behind enduring infrastructure. However, the researchers warn that technology stocks may experience lower returns over the subsequent twelve-to-twenty-four-month period.
