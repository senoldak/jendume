Neocloud companies, which provide the essential infrastructure for artificial intelligence, are facing significant scrutiny from financial markets. Firms like CoreWeave and Nebius rely heavily on debt to fund the purchase of cutting-edge AI systems. However, investors are increasingly skeptical about their ability to manage this debt as market sentiment sours. The pressure is evident in the bond market, where yields on CoreWeave bonds maturing in 2031 have jumped above 16%, and credit default swaps have surged by 50% over the past month.

While company executives maintain that existing customer commitments and backlogs remain unaffected by political or economic shifts, capital markets have become notably more selective. The focus has shifted from the sheer size of contracts to whether these projects can be delivered, financed, and operated profitably. CoreWeave, which held $35 billion in debt as of June, faces the challenge of needing further capital to fuel growth over the next five years, a task made difficult by declining stock prices.

Despite the broader market anxiety, some players are finding stability. IREN, for instance, saw its stock rise after reporting that a significant portion of its backlog is tied to major tech partners like Microsoft. Analysts are now closely watching the upcoming quarterly earnings reports from major neocloud providers. These results are expected to be a litmus test for the industry, determining whether the massive investments in AI infrastructure can translate into sustainable profits and reassure creditors that the growth story remains intact.
