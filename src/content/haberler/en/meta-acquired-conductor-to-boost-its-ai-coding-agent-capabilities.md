Meta has officially acquired Conductor, a startup that enables software engineers to run several artificial intelligence coding agents at once. Charlie Holtz, the chief executive of Conductor, confirmed the deal and stated that the team will join Meta Superintelligence Labs. Financial terms of the agreement were not disclosed by Meta.

Launched in July 2025, Conductor's app allows developers to run coding agents from various labs, such as OpenAI and Anthropic, simultaneously in isolated project copies. Following the acquisition announcement, Conductor stopped accepting new sign-ups. The company announced that its cloud-hosted service will be shut down on November 20, while the desktop application will continue to receive updates and bug fixes until early 2027.

This move aligns with Meta's broader strategy in developer tools. The company previously launched its Muse Code coding agent in August, and its Muse assistant has already surpassed ten million users. The Conductor team will now collaborate with Nat Friedman and Alexandr Wang at the lab, promising new tools for users early next year.

The deal coincides with a broader industry trend where major players are snapping up developer-focused tools. OpenAI also recently announced the acquisition of Pierre Computer Company, underscoring how developers remain the primary power users in the ongoing competition against tools like Anthropic's Claude Code and OpenAI's Codex.
