NVIDIA and Cisco have jointly unveiled a USFP optical module design aimed at delivering higher connection density within artificial intelligence data centers. Designed to address surging data traffic driven by expanding graphics processor infrastructures, this collaborative effort targets 400 Gbps per lane in electrical connections. The roadmap outlined by the companies envisions scaling total capacity up to 12.8 Tbps in subsequent development phases.

The initial modules will deliver a total capacity of 6.4 Tbps across 16 full-duplex lanes within a footprint comparable to the OSFP size. According to project details, the following phase will double the lane count to 32. The USFP architecture aims to provide four times the bandwidth density compared to modules operating at 200 Gbps lane speeds, while also achieving roughly 1.3 times greater energy efficiency in optical connections.

This new connection framework extends beyond standard network switches to incorporate SuperNIC cards as well. To support these efforts, NVIDIA and Cisco are partnering with TE Connectivity and Amphenol for cage, connector, and module development. The team also plans to establish an open industry specification through a community-managed multi-source initiative.

The connector utilizes a two-stage mechanism that engages high-speed contact points only after the module is securely seated. This mechanical arrangement is intended to minimize contact wear and enhance signal integrity. Furthermore, the design supports heat dissipation of up to 100 watts without requiring a separate embedded cooling circuit inside the transceiver itself.
