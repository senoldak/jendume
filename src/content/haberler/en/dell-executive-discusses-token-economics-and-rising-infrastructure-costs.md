Enterprise artificial intelligence is entering a cost-conscious phase where hybrid infrastructure decisions dictate strategy. As inference spending climbs, business leaders are carefully weighing where models run, which models they use, and how data is handled across different environments. Speaking at the Dell Technologies New York Symposium, Kyle Dufresne, senior vice president of sales at Dell, highlighted a sharp shift in token economics and how these factors shape corporate buying decisions across various industries.

According to Dufresne, within a span of just three months, the share of tokens generated by frontier models shifted dramatically toward open models, largely driven by cost considerations. This change is prompting companies to run inference closer to where data is created, spanning clouds, data centers, and personal computers as business owners look for ways to reduce inference costs without curbing innovation.

The economics of AI are also raising strategic questions regarding corporate control. Chief financial officers across organizations implementing these structures are actively seeking ways to reduce exploding token costs. Dell has responded by aligning its AI Factory updates with solutions that help customers maintain control over their infrastructure and workloads while achieving tangible business results.

On the supply side, rising memory prices and component inflation are pushing technology costs higher rather than lower. With memory demand significantly outpacing expected supply growth for the upcoming year, enterprises are engaging in collaborative planning and early purchasing to optimize their capital use, finding that their cost of capital is often lower than component inflation.
