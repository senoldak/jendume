Blacksmith, a platform designed to help companies more efficiently build and test software, has announced a $66 million Series C funding round led by Sequoia Capital. This latest investment values the company at $1 billion, granting it unicorn status. According to Blacksmith co-founder and CEO Aditya Jayaprakash, the company was established to make continuous integration—the process of building, testing, merging, and validating code changes—less burdensome, especially in an era where software is increasingly written by AI.

During the announcement, leadership emphasized that as AI agents write more of the world's software, the primary question moving forward is whether that code can be trusted. Companies are reportedly struggling to keep up with the sheer volume of continuous integration jobs they need to run. Blacksmith addresses this gap by helping development teams streamline automated code checks and validation processes.

In terms of business growth, Blacksmith reported that its annual recurring revenue has grown 17 times, while having executed over 90 million automated code check jobs. Its customer base includes prominent firms such as Mercury and Granola. This Series C round follows closely on the heels of its $45 million Series B round held just two months prior, which had valued the enterprise at $550 million.

Just a year ago, Blacksmith was valued at $60 million following a $10 million Series A round, bringing its total funding raised to date to $125 million. This rapid financial scaling highlights the surging demand for robust testing infrastructure amid the widespread adoption of AI-generated code.
