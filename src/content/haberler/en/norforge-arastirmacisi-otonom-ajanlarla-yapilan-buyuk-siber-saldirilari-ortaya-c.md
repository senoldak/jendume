A threat researcher at cybersecurity company NorForge discovered that a Chinese-speaking hacker used autonomous AI agents to carry out a massive hacking spree targeting more than 100 different companies. According to reports, global enterprises including John Deere, SharkNinja, and Cummins were among the dozens impacted by these coordinated attacks.

The hacker abused an open-source AI tool known as Cairn to deploy swarms of agents on autonomous hacking missions. Snorre Fagerland, the researcher who uncovered the activity, noted that this malicious actor is among several using such tools to exponentially raise the threat level of a single human hacker. The primary attacker deployed AI systems against over 1,200 e-commerce sites across the US, Canada, Australia, and Japan.

Successful breaches occurred after the AI agents attacked different targets for approximately 23 hours. Each successful hack reportedly cost about $50 in tokens, with experts pointing out that the techniques employed by the agents were highly sophisticated and went far beyond basic script-kiddie capabilities. The report also noted that sensitive credit card information was compromised in at least one observed instance.

In response to the breaches, John Deere stated it had taken necessary remediation actions with no indication that sensitive information was accessed. SharkNinja reported that the hacker accessed a site without sensitive data, while Cummins announced it was investigating a potential cyberattack on a vendor-operated website.

This development underscores the growing cybersecurity crisis brought on by advanced AI's capacity to locate and exploit vulnerabilities in digital infrastructure. While AI agents assist ordinary users with daily tasks, they also empower cybercriminals to unleash capable, always-on hacking swarms.
