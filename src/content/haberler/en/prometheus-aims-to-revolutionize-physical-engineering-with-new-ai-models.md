Jeff Bezos has taken on his first executive role since stepping down as Amazon CEO to co-lead Prometheus, a startup dedicated to "physical AI." Unlike the large language models that dominate the current landscape by processing text, Prometheus focuses on teaching AI the language of physics. The company aims to develop an "artificial general engineer," a system capable of handling complex tasks ranging from aerospace design and drug discovery to optimizing manufacturing pipelines.

This development marks a significant shift in the industry, as it moves the focus from digital content generation to the automation of the physical world. While current AI models excel at reasoning through text, the systems being built at Prometheus integrate physical simulations, thermal analysis, and fluid dynamics with digital twin technology. This approach seeks to solve the fundamental challenge of engineering, which is the sheer breadth of variables involved in creating physical hardware. Instead of focusing on robotics, the company is building an advanced, AI-powered version of computer-aided design (CAD).

Prometheus was incubated at Foresite Labs in 2024 and currently employs approximately 250 people, including experts in aerospace, chemistry, and high-performance computing. The company has secured significant capital, raising funds in two major rounds—initially $6 billion and subsequently $12 billion—bringing its valuation to $41 billion.

The ultimate goal is to drastically compress development timelines for complex hardware. For instance, a jet engine design process that might traditionally take a decade could potentially be reduced to just a few years using these AI-driven simulation tools. By focusing on an advanced, AI-powered version of computer-aided design rather than robotics, the company intends to reshape how industries approach everything from household products to large-scale industrial systems.
