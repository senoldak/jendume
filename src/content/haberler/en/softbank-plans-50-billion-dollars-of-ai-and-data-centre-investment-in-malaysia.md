SoftBank Group chairman and chief executive Masayoshi Son has selected Malaysia for 50 billion dollars of investment focused on artificial intelligence, new technology, and data centres. Prime Minister Anwar Ibrahim made this announcement during the launch of a national cost of living plan in Malacca, noting that Son shared the plan during a video call. SoftBank itself has not officially announced the figure. The initiative follows a framework agreement signed in Kuching involving SoftBank, ride-hailing group Grab, and Petroleum Sarawak Berhad to explore a digital infrastructure platform covering AI compute, robotics, and workforce development. Reports indicate that SoftBank is expected to fund only a portion of the 50 billion dollars, with financing and other partners covering the rest. Prime Minister Anwar emphasized that the investment will generate new and better jobs while highlighting the role of political stability in attracting foreign capital. Malaysia previously recorded 385.7 billion ringgit of data-centre-related investments from 2021 to mid-2026, and OpenAI became an anchor customer for a local site in September. Son has been funding his broader AI ventures through a new bond and loans secured by the company's stake in OpenAI. This landmark commitment marks a significant step forward in Southeast Asia's growing technological ecosystem, establishing vital infrastructure for future innovations. As governments and corporations increasingly collaborate to build robust digital foundations, initiatives of this scale demonstrate the shifting geography of the global artificial intelligence landscape. Local leaders have repeatedly stressed that maintaining economic stability and fostering specialized skills remain essential for converting such massive capital inflows into sustainable, long-term national prosperity and technological leadership.
