Proton has launched its privacy-focused AI assistant, Lumo, as a desktop application for Windows, macOS, and Linux. This release marks a significant shift, moving Lumo beyond a simple chat interface and into an agent capable of performing tasks directly on a user’s computer. Users can now connect Lumo to local files and third-party services, such as email, cloud storage, and project management tools, allowing the AI to automate workflows across their digital environment.

At the core of this development is a commitment to data privacy. As consumer AI technology increasingly focuses on agents that perform tasks on behalf of users, the risk to personal data grows. Proton addresses these concerns by offering granular control over the AI's access. Users can decide which actions the AI agent takes automatically, which require explicit approval, and which are blocked entirely. The company pledges that conversations and information from connected apps are never used for personalized advertising or to train its AI models.

The new application supports local and remote Model Context Protocol servers, enabling secure access to external data sources. Additionally, users can schedule tasks and utilize a "Skills" feature, which allows them to save instructions, templates, and reference files for reuse across conversations. All data is hosted on European infrastructure and protected under Swiss privacy laws, keeping it outside the reach of certain international data access agreements.

To support this new service, Proton is also investing in its underlying infrastructure. The company has brought its first Nvidia B300 cluster, built on Open Compute Project standards, online in Switzerland. This move highlights the company's broader strategy to build a privacy-centric AI ecosystem that balances powerful automation with user control. By allowing users to manage permissions for each connector individually, the platform ensures that the convenience of AI agents does not come at the cost of personal data security.
