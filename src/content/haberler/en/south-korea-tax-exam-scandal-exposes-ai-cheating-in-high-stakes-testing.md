A major scandal has erupted in South Korea after a top-scoring candidate admitted to using generative AI to cheat on the national tax accountant exam. The individual, who ranked first in both rounds of the qualification tests held in April and July, confessed to using a microcamera to photograph exam questions and sending them to an AI tool, then checking the answers on a smartphone. The candidate's performance was exceptionally high, with a score of 397.5 out of 400 points, missing only one question out of 160.

This admission has rattled a nation where standardized tests are the primary determinants of social mobility and career paths. The timing of the scandal is particularly sensitive, as it occurred just days before the annual national college entrance exam, a high-pressure event for which students prepare for years. The presidential office has stated that it views the matter with extreme seriousness, convening emergency meetings to implement countermeasures and ensure the integrity of the upcoming university entrance tests.

The Prime Minister's office acknowledged public concerns regarding AI-assisted cheating, promising to oversee the examination process closely to ensure it remains fair and stable. This incident serves as a stark warning to educational authorities about the vulnerabilities of traditional testing environments in the age of advanced AI. As the Seoul metropolitan police agency investigates the case, the event underscores the urgent need for new surveillance methods and policies to combat AI-driven academic dishonesty. It highlights the growing tension between technological accessibility and the preservation of meritocratic systems.
