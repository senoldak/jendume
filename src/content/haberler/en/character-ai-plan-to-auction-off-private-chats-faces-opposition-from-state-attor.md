Chatbot developer Character.ai's plan to auction off private conversation histories as part of its bankruptcy proceedings has sparked a strong backlash from state attorneys general across the United States. A coalition of 42 state attorneys general has intervened in the bankruptcy court to halt the sale of sensitive user data. Officials stated that the dataset in question includes chat histories from more than 21 million U.S. accounts, with over 2.1 million belonging to minors, alongside other personal information such as IP addresses.

Character.ai, which filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy, had previously assured consumers that their personal information would remain protected under its privacy policy and would not be sold. So far, only one anonymous bidder has been disclosed for the auction. State officials are opposing the sale on the grounds that buyers will not be bound by the company's privacy policy and could freely resell the data without disclosure. Several states emphasized that the sale could also trigger violations of upcoming chatbot safety legislation set to take effect soon.

California Attorney General Rob Bonta stated that using bankruptcy proceedings to bypass consumer protections and break promises made to users is unacceptable. Other officials also criticized the company's business model and past safety issues that preceded its financial downfall. The company previously faced scrutiny over a proposed licensing deal with Disney, and following the departure of its co-founders and various lawsuits related to user safety, it now faces a major legal battle over the fate of its users' private data.
