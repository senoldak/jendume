A new artificial intelligence model called NovaAtom-Lite has achieved a major milestone in biomolecular structure prediction, outperforming AlphaFold 3 in antibody-antigen tests. This breakthrough is considered a critical step in accurately predicting how antibodies bind to specific proteins, which directly impacts the effectiveness of future treatments and drug discovery.

The primary objective of the model is to reveal the three-dimensional positions of different molecules forming a complex structure within a single estimation. Capable of analyzing protein chains alongside DNA and RNA structures, the model calculates the coordinates of every heavy atom rather than just estimating the general shape. This allows researchers to foresee how a small drug molecule fits into the binding pocket of a target protein.

Thanks to this technology, researchers can perform preliminary evaluations in a digital environment before resorting to expensive and time-consuming laboratory experiments. The model also supports small molecules, cofactors, metals, ions, and cyclic peptides. By offering reliability metrics such as pLDDT, PAE, and ipTM, it informs researchers about which regions of the predicted structure are trustworthy.

According to data shared by the company, the success rate across 172 antibody-antigen interactions rises from 74.3 percent with a single seed to 83.1 percent using 100 seeds. In stricter evaluations requiring a DockQ value of 0.80 or higher, the success rate increases from 41 percent to 46.5 percent.

Geodesic Intelligence has made NovaAtom-Lite available through the Geodesic API and NovaDDE platform. While AI models cannot completely replace lab experiments yet, more accurate predictions of molecular interactions help researchers prioritize promising drug candidates more efficiently.
