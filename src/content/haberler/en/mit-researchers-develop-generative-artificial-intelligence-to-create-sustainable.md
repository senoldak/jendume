A research team led by the Massachusetts Institute of Technology is embarking on a major initiative to develop next-generation weed killers using advanced generative artificial intelligence. Funded by the U.S. Advanced Research Projects Agency for Health under agreement number 140D042690030, the project aims to tackle the growing crisis of herbicide resistance in agriculture while safeguarding human health and environmental safety. Across the United States, more than 1 billion pounds of herbicides are utilized annually to prevent significant crop loss. However, many persistent weeds are gradually developing genetic mutations that render current chemical treatments less effective, raising concerns regarding potential long-term impacts on human health and local ecosystems.

The research coalition will focus on creating sophisticated generative artificial intelligence models capable of predicting novel chemical compounds and ribonucleic acid structures that disrupt targeted plant enzymes without harming humans or the environment. These computational tools will generate massive libraries of candidate compounds designed to achieve economical synthesis, maintain safety against genetic resistance, and minimize ecological damage.

Following the computational phase, subsequent teams will evaluate promising candidate molecules through high-throughput screening and controlled greenhouse studies. The initial greenhouse trials testing RNA interference candidates are slated for completion within the first twelve months of the program. The comprehensive research program also includes the development of engineered microbial systems designed to degrade agricultural chemicals before they can impact food supplies, soils, or natural waterways.
