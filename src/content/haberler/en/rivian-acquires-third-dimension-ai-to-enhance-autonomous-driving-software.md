Rivian has announced the acquisition of Third Dimension AI, an enterprise specializing in converting real-world optical imagery into spatial simulations. The automaker plans to utilize the technology to instruct and evaluate its driver assistance and autonomous driving software. While the financial terms were not disclosed, the startup's team has joined Rivian's autonomy and AI group led by James Philbin.

Founded in 2024, Third Dimension operates primarily in the UK and previously raised seed funding. The platform rebuilds real driving footage into controlled 3D environments where engineers can alter lighting, weather, and road user behavior. This allows Rivian's AI models to train on complex conditions without waiting for rare edge cases to happen on actual roads.

Rivian draws raw material from its own vehicle fleet, receiving hundreds of thousands of driving sequences daily. The company emphasizes that simulation is central to validating its autonomous systems before shipping them to customers. Rivian plans to release point-to-point supervised driving software and aims to launch robotaxi versions on Uber's app in 2028.

Other industry players, including Google DeepMind and Maxinsights, are also building similar simulation and data collection tools. Rivian's move highlights the growing importance of advanced simulation platforms in the race to achieve safe and reliable autonomous vehicle technology.
