International Monetary Fund (IMF) Managing Director Kristalina Georgieva has described the current state of the world economy as standing on a single leg: the AI leg. Speaking at an event attended by global financial officials and economists, Georgieva warned that the global economy is becoming dangerously unbalanced due to extreme concentration in the artificial intelligence sector. She cautioned that any instability in this specific sector could lead to broader financial stability troubles, as the market is currently driven by a circular financing loop where a small group of tech companies effectively pass capital back and forth.

According to IMF estimates, the AI-driven financial buildout is now valued at over $1 trillion. Perhaps more concerning is that approximately two-thirds of this valuation is composed of debt. Georgieva noted that while such a structure might be sustainable in a more diversified economy, the current reality is that spending on AI data centers has become one of the primary drivers of the US economy. This reliance implies that if AI spending were to drag, the resulting ripple effects from a debt spiral could slow the entire market to a crawl.

Economists are increasingly viewing the AI sector as a precarious structure, similar to a Jenga tower, where a sudden drop in investment confidence or a withdrawal from the field could send markets tumbling. The IMF emphasizes that with the AI bubble becoming a mainstream concern, market observers must remain vigilant for potential triggers that could destabilize the global economy. The current situation makes it difficult to distinguish between genuine real-world demand for AI and the financial momentum generated by this debt-fueled expansion.
