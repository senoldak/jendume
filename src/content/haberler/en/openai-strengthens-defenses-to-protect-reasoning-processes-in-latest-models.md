OpenAI has significantly strengthened its security walls to protect the "reasoning processes" of its AI models. Updates made specifically to the GPT-6.2 Astra model have enhanced defenses against "adversarial distillation" attacks, which aim to steal the internal logic and reasoning methods of the model. Such attacks are used to understand how an AI generates results and to use that information to train smaller, less capable models.

Technical improvements have dramatically reduced the success rate of these attacks. The success rate for extracting data from prior requests dropped from 54.0% in the earlier model to 0.2% with the new updates. Similarly, the success rate for attacks on active requests fell from 22.9% to 0.9%. These figures demonstrate the effectiveness of the new defense mechanisms implemented by the company.

Security measures are not limited to filtering model responses. OpenAI has structured this defense in two layers. The first layer utilizes targeted training and input-output filters for messages and tool calls. Additionally, API blocks have been implemented to prevent older models from reading protected reasoning processes. The second layer involves an active fight against coordinated attack campaigns. The company aims to dismantle attack networks by banning thousands, and sometimes tens of thousands, of accounts every week that engage in such violations.

This development marks a critical turning point in protecting intellectual property in the AI world. Not only OpenAI but also other major AI laboratories, such as Anthropic, are fighting similar systematic "distillation" attacks. These companies argue that a coordinated industry-wide response is necessary to prevent the theft of internal model logic. The security of AI models has become not just a technical detail, but a fundamental requirement for the future sustainability and commercial value of this technology.
