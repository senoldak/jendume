The future of robotics depends not just on advanced machinery, but on how well machines can understand the physical world. Founded in 2024 and based in the United States, Mecka creates extensive datasets by recording human movements to train humanoid and other robots. The company pays everyday people to record themselves using body sensors and smartphones while performing routine tasks like making coffee or repairing machinery.

These collected datasets are carefully analyzed and provided to robot developers, mirroring the data providers that fueled the rise of large language models, but tailored specifically for robotics. This approach allows AI-powered robots to learn natural, human-like responses to everyday physical challenges.

In its latest funding milestone, Mecka raised $60 million in a Series B round led by Sequoia Capital, with participation from tech giants such as NVIDIA, Microsoft's M12 fund, Qualcomm Ventures, and Samsung. Having reached a valuation of around $500 million last September, the startup will use this fresh capital to expand its data collection operations and accelerate robotic intelligence.
