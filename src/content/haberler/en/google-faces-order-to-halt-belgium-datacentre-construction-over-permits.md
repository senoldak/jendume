Regional officials in Belgium have instructed Google to suspend building operations on a massive facility located in the southern region because the American technology enterprise implemented unapproved adjustments to its original layout designs. Local inspectors visited the location and observed that structural work was almost finished despite the permit being refused. Officials stated that 300 workers were given an order to stop work, which was also confirmed to Google in writing. Google responded that it remains committed to an open and collaborative dialogue with regional authorities.

The dispute centers on Google's application to change its plans, notably switching from air to water cooling. Although initially approved, the modification was later revoked following protests and drought concerns raised by residents. Environmental group Greenpeace published a report featuring drone footage showing that construction continued regardless of the revoked permit. Google appealed the revocation to Belgium's council of state, with a legal ruling expected in January.
The vast building occupies about 3 hectares of a 50-hectare business park on a former coalmine site near Charleroi. Regional officials submitted a report to public prosecutors alleging a major offence regarding illegal construction. This case highlights the growing friction in Europe as tech giants race to build datacentres to support the expanding artificial intelligence industry.

Similar regulatory hurdles are emerging elsewhere in Europe. Finland launched a criminal investigation into environmental law breaches at a planned Google datacentre in the country's north, halting construction until environmental impact assessments are fully completed.
