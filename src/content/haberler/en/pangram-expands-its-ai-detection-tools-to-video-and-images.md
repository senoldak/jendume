Pangram, a developer specializing in software designed to identify machine-authored writing, has released new tools for flagging AI-generated images and videos. With this update, subscribers can upload video clips to assess whether synthetic technology was used to create or edit them. The system can distinguish between clips that are partially created by software and those entirely made by artificial intelligence, providing a heatmap to flag specific aspects likely to be synthetic. According to internal tests detailed by the organization, the video model can flag content with more than 98 percent accuracy.

Alongside video capabilities, Pangram has upgraded its image detection. The organization's Pangram Image 1.0 model is now available via a browser extension, which automatically scans images while browsing and flags potential synthetic content. In testing, the image model successfully flagged more than 99 percent of machine-made graphics. Chief Executive Officer Max Spero noted that developing the model required overcoming challenges such as modern smartphone cameras applying software postprocessing to real photos, requiring a robust system to distinguish between camera enhancements and fully synthetic visuals.

Pangram's new detection tools are now available to subscribers, while users with free accounts can check a limited number of images without signing up for a paid plan.
