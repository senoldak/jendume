The UK government will establish a specialized Wildfires AI Unit to help detect and fight fires more quickly following the country's hottest summer on record. Prime Minister Andy Burnham announced the plan at a wildfire summit, stating that the unit will operate inside the National Situation Centre within the emergency committee rooms. The initiative brings together academic institutions, technology firms, and government officials to leverage satellite monitoring and early warning systems. Major technology and industrial companies, including Google, Airbus, and VodafoneThree, participated in the summit to discuss technological solutions and how early warning systems could help mitigate disasters. Alongside the artificial intelligence initiatives, the government is updating its emergency response by integrating military transport planes and helicopters into wildfire operations for the first time, allowing fire services to request them for remote moorland and steep hillsides. Fire services will receive up to forty-five million pounds for specialized protective gear and dedicated wildfire teams, addressing the shortage of suitable outdoor equipment reported by a significant portion of rescue services. Furthermore, twenty-five million pounds will be allocated to restore dried-out peatland across England to act as a natural firebreak, with grant schemes supporting farmers in fireproofing their land. These comprehensive measures reflect a growing reliance on advanced technology, updated local limits, and proactive strategies to mitigate escalating climate risks after climate change made extreme summer heat significantly more likely.
