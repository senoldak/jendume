OpenAI has officially begun testing Private Inference, an alpha feature aimed at protecting business data during processing. The tool utilizes confidential computing to keep enterprise prompts and responses secure while interacting with powerful AI models. OpenAI executive Sam Altman announced the initial trials, which are currently underway with a select group of business customers.

The system runs workloads on specially isolated hardware combined with verifiable controls to ensure that sensitive enterprise data remains protected. This initiative marks the second component of OpenAI's privacy package for businesses, following the rollout of Private Safety Processing earlier in the year. The primary goal is to ensure that companies can leverage artificial intelligence without risking data exposure.

OpenAI representatives noted that the current test serves as a stepping stone toward a much wider rollout. The company is actively scaling up its computing infrastructure to support a broader release of the feature in the future. Meanwhile, competition in the enterprise AI security space is intensifying, with rival firms launching their own safeguards to protect business data during interactions.

Data privacy remains a critical hurdle for enterprise AI adoption. By addressing these concerns through hardware isolation and strict verification controls, AI providers aim to build deeper trust with corporate clients. These measures ensure that businesses can handle confidential workloads securely while maintaining compliance and privacy standards.
