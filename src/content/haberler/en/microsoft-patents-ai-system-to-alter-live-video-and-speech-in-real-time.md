Microsoft has filed a patent application for a new artificial intelligence system designed to alter speakers' words, voices, and faces in real time during live video or augmented reality presentations. The system utilizes AI agents to identify and modify or remove specific words based on set parameters, while simultaneously editing the speaker's mouth and facial expressions to match the changes seamlessly.

Proposed use cases for the technology range from real-time language translation and the removal of filler words to eliminating stutters, speech impediments, and local colloquialisms. The patent suggests that the tool could allow audiences to consume live events tailored precisely to their personal preferences without hearing unwanted terms or phrases.

Emerging at a time when deepfakes and digital disinformation are major global concerns, this patent raises significant questions about the future of live media and the modification of reality. While it could offer practical tools like instant translation, critics note it might push users further into personalized filter bubbles. The patent briefly mentions that original footage could be stored to maintain ground truth against potential misuse.

Microsoft has not yet issued a public comment on the filing. The system's potential applications span across lectures, presentations, television productions, and live sporting events, changing how audiences fundamentally engage with live footage.
