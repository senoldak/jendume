One of the world's leading wind turbine manufacturers is working on the idea of converting floating wind energy platforms into offshore data centers. The company is exploring the addition of computing capacity capable of running AI workloads to its OceanX design, which combines two turbines on a single platform. Speaking at an industry summit, company executives stated that the project is still in its early stages. Officials noted that the firm is focusing on two main issues in developing the OceanX platform: reducing costs and creating new value-added use cases such as AI data centers.

The manufacturer previously unveiled its first OceanX prototype with a total capacity of 16.6 MW. The two-turbine platform features 8.3 MW semi-direct drive wind turbines on each tower. The first OceanX unit was installed at an offshore wind farm off the coast of southern China. The platform has successfully weathered super typhoon conditions since its initial installation.

The company's strategy relies on placing specific computing capacity on each floating platform, allowing platforms in the same wind farm to form a joint data center cluster. According to company statements, AI companies could purchase the computing power offered at these facilities. Unlike bottom-fixed wind turbines, the wide surface area of the OceanX design provides a more suitable structure for housing data center hardware.

While adding data center hardware to the platforms will increase development costs, it will also provide a new revenue stream for the turbines. The additional income generated from data center services could help offset the high installation and operational expenses inherent to floating wind farms.
