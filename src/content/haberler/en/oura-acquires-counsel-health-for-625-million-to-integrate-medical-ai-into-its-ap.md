Wearable technology company Oura is making a significant move to reshape its health services by acquiring Counsel Health, an AI-native primary care provider, for $625 million in cash. This strategic acquisition aims to bridge the gap between biometric data collected from wearable devices and actual clinical expertise. Once the deal is finalized, users will be able to do more than just track their health metrics; they will have access to AI-powered health assistants within the Oura app, with the ability to escalate their concerns to board-certified physicians when necessary.

The system utilizes specialized AI agents capable of analyzing medical records, past conversations, clinical guidelines, and data directly from the wearable ring. These agents are designed to handle tasks such as taking medical histories, reviewing records, and detecting potential emergencies. Crucially, the process maintains a human-in-the-loop approach, where physicians provide feedback on the AI's performance, allowing each agent to improve its accuracy and safety over time. The companies plan to cover a broad spectrum of care, including preventive, acute, and chronic health management.

Financially, Oura is demonstrating strong growth. The company reported that its revenue grew by 153% to $533 million in the quarter ending September 30, with total annual revenue reaching approximately $1.75 billion. The company currently serves more than six million members worldwide.

While Oura maintains that its ring is not a medical device and is not intended to diagnose, treat, or monitor illnesses, this acquisition signals a clear shift in the company's ambition. By integrating Counsel Health's AI infrastructure, Oura is evolving from a passive data provider into an active participant in the clinical health ecosystem. Users will soon see their wearable data not just as a set of charts, but as a foundational element of a comprehensive, AI-supported healthcare experience.
