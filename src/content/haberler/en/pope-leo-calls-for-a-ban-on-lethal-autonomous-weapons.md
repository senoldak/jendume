Pope Leo XIV called for artificial intelligence to be disarmed, stating that choices affecting human existence, personal liberty, and physical coercion must always remain anchored in human supervision. In an official address released by the Vatican, he pressed government representatives gathering in Geneva during November to advance toward prohibiting lethal autonomous weapon systems. The gathered audience participated in a specialized assembly addressing the trajectory of automated intelligence, hosted within the Apostolic Palace.

The pontiff outlined five core principles, asserting that the evolution of automated systems relies entirely on human choices rather than autonomous momentum. He rejected suggestions to grant legal standing or personhood to digital systems, warning that such measures would obscure personal accountability and abandon victims to anonymous entities. Furthermore, he insisted that critical determinations regarding target directories and border management databases must never be delegated entirely to machines.

Addressing broader implications, the pope maintained that technology must prioritize collective welfare over narrow privileges. He underscored the necessity for robust international bodies equipped with transparent mechanisms, independent monitoring, and unified standards. Governmental delegates continue to deliberate on lethal autonomous capabilities under the framework of the relevant convention during scheduled review sessions in Geneva.

Previous statements from church leadership regarding automated warfare were also recalled during the addresses, emphasizing that no mechanism should ever possess the authority to terminate a human life. While differing viewpoints persist among technology developers regarding military applications of automated systems, the broader international discourse continues to focus heavily on establishing boundaries and ensuring strict human governance.
