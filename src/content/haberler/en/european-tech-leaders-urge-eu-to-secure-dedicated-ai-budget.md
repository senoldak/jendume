Founders of Europe's leading technology companies and prominent scientists have issued a call for the European Union to implement fundamental changes in its 2028-2034 budget planning to ensure the continent remains competitive in the global race for artificial intelligence. The open letter, signed by 59 influential figures including executives from ElevenLabs, Lovable, and DeepL, aims to bolster Europe's technological sovereignty.

The signatories argue that Europe currently competes with only a fraction of the capital, compute, and scaling capacity available to its global rivals. They emphasize that the EU's current long-term budget negotiations are at a critical juncture, warning that any priorities not secured now will likely remain unaddressed until 2035.

To overcome these hurdles, the tech leaders propose solutions across four key areas. First, they advocate for a "28th regime," a unified regulatory framework that would allow companies to incorporate once and operate under a single set of rules across all EU member states. Second, they urge the EU to steer pension fund and insurance savings into European scale-ups to provide necessary growth capital. Third, they call for a dedicated AI budget within the European Competitiveness Fund, suggesting that current IT budgets should prioritize European providers. Finally, they propose the establishment of well-funded, independent flagship AI institutes, modeled after the ELLIS network, to foster scientific excellence.

Asserting that Europe can no longer be satisfied with merely regulating technologies invented elsewhere, the signatories highlight the urgency of the situation with the warning that "we cannot win a heavyweight fight with featherweight budgets." This initiative represents a significant push to position Europe not just as a regulator, but as a proactive developer and leader in the global AI landscape.
