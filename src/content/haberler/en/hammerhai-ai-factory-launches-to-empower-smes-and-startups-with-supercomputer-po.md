The HammerHAI AI factory in Stuttgart is taking shape around a Hewlett Packard Enterprise system utilizing Nvidia Grace Blackwell chips, aiming to provide startups and small manufacturers with supercomputer-class GPUs. According to Bastian Koller, managing director of the High-Performance Computing Center Stuttgart, the facility focuses heavily on supporting small and medium enterprises that need guidance alongside raw processing power. The center implements a personalized advisory framework that assigns a dedicated support representative to every single participating organization, ensuring that technical deployment closely matches how actual users operate.

The facility is specifically designed to prevent vendor lock-in, allowing workloads to easily migrate between the local installation and major hyperscalers. This pragmatic approach to data sovereignty ensures that companies can use the computing boost without being permanently trapped in a single proprietary ecosystem. Rather than waiting years for fully independent regional processors, the center focuses securely on managing data flows. The Stuttgart location also operates within an ISO-certified environment featuring TISAX Level 3 certification tailored specifically for automotive workloads.

Energy efficiency plays a crucial structural role in the facility's architecture. Operating at approximately 700 kilowatts, the upgraded installation successfully multiplies the throughput capacity achieved by its 3.2-megawatt predecessor, and the center actively reuses its waste heat. By combining advanced hardware with a human-centric support network and workload portability, the initiative ensures that smaller enterprises can adopt powerful computing safely and efficiently.
