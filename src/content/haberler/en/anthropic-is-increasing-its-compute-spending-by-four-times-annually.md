The massive compute power required to train and run AI models is at the heart of the competition between technology companies. Recent data shows that Anthropic is increasing its compute and infrastructure spending by four times annually. This pace outstrips the three-fold annual growth rate of the sector's other major player, OpenAI. The company's spending, which was $433 million in 2023, reached $10 billion in the third quarter of 2026, climbing to an annualized rate of $40 billion.

Most of this spending consists of payments to cloud computing partners. The collaboration with SpaceX is particularly notable; in the third quarter of 2026, Anthropic paid $5 billion to SpaceX, accounting for roughly half of its total compute spending. The company's future plans indicate that these costs will continue to rise. Anthropic has made a spending commitment of $447.5 billion for the period from the end of 2026 through the end of 2030, which translates to an average annual investment of $100 billion.

Why does this matter? Progress in the AI world is no longer measured solely by software code, but by massive data centers and hardware capacity. The fact that companies are pouring such vast capital into compute power shows that the future capabilities of AI are directly tied to this infrastructure capacity. The fact that competitors like OpenAI are similarly allocating multi-billion dollar budgets proves that the AI race is not just a software war, but also an infrastructure and energy war. These massive investments provide the "brainpower" necessary for models to solve increasingly complex problems. However, this also brings new debates about the sustainability and cost management of AI technologies. Companies must transform this high-cost infrastructure into profitable business models.
