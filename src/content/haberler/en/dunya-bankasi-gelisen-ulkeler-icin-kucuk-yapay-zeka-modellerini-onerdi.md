The World Bank is urging developing countries to embrace small artificial intelligence models capable of running on mobile phones with low connectivity to maximize the benefits of the technology. While major Silicon Valley firms focus on highly complex consumer products, World Bank officials emphasize that AI can accelerate economic development in emerging markets if tailored to local needs.

Small AI relies on specialized models trained on narrower datasets for specific purposes. These systems can operate in local settings, in native languages, and with limited internet access. Officials highlighted examples such as farmers in agricultural schemes needing soil analysis in their own native languages where agricultural experts are otherwise scarce.

During the annual meetings of the World Bank and the International Monetary Fund held in Bangkok, Thailand, the impact of AI on jobs and growth emerged as a central theme. IMF leadership pointed out that governments must ensure reliable electricity, digital infrastructure, and basic safety protections to enable successful adoption. Furthermore, officials warned that a significant share of global jobs could be augmented or replaced by automation, affecting young people entering the workforce.

Under its leadership, the World Bank has increasingly focused on channeling private-sector funding into emerging economies, mobilizing substantial private capital over the past year despite cuts in traditional aid budgets. Cooperation between governments, international institutions, and the private sector is seen as vital for making these accessible technologies widespread.
