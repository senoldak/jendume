An analysis conducted for Australia's central bank reveals that the initial impacts of artificial intelligence on the labor market are disproportionately affecting young workers. While overall employment remains relatively stable, those entering highly exposed fields such as software development, clerical work, and customer service are experiencing greater difficulties. Since late 2022, job opportunities for individuals aged 15 to 24 and recent college graduates have lagged behind those of more experienced peers. Researchers emphasize that these findings do not establish direct causation, noting that post-pandemic economic shifts or changes in remote work patterns could also be contributing factors.

A Stanford University study from August 2025, which examined millions of US payroll records, confirms this trend. Employment for 22- to 25-year-olds in AI-exposed occupations has declined by 13 percent since late 2022, with a nearly 20 percent drop specifically among young software developers. In contrast, more experienced professionals in these same roles have maintained or improved their standing. A March 2026 study by Anthropic also identified a decline in hiring for this age group, with job-finding rates falling by approximately 14 percent. Anthropic reported that the proportion of junior hires in its own technical roles has decreased from 27 percent to 9 percent since early 2025.

Conversely, a large-scale study of 25,000 workers in Denmark found no measurable impact on wages or working hours, despite a 64 percent AI adoption rate. A January 2026 analysis from the University of Pittsburgh suggests that the downturn in exposed occupations began in early 2022, prior to the widespread adoption of generative AI, pointing to broader macroeconomic factors. Meanwhile, the World Economic Forum's 2025 report indicates that 77 percent of surveyed companies are prioritizing upskilling over layoffs. This shift suggests that organizations are focusing on adapting their existing workforce to new technologies rather than replacing them.
