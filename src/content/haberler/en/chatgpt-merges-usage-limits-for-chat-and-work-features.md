OpenAI is updating its ChatGPT subscription service to merge usage limits for its "Chat" and "Work" features. These usage allowances, previously tracked separately, will be combined into a single meter across the platform. The company has initiated a rollout to a small group of Pro subscribers and plans to expand this change to a broader user base throughout December.

The consolidation follows the escalating cost of infrastructure and the surging demand for computing power required to run advanced AI models. The company says unifying these limits will allow it to manage resources more efficiently and ensure a sustainable service model. However, the change has sparked concerns among some subscribers who fear that merging these limits could result in reduced access to advanced reasoning models, despite maintaining the same subscription fees.

Under the new system, usage of the "Instant" model will remain unlimited and will not count toward the shared allowance. This ensures that even after users exhaust their primary usage limits for more complex tasks, they can continue to utilize the faster, lighter models without interruption. The company states that it will continue to focus on improving efficiency to help subscribers maximize the value of their plans.

This shift reflects a broader trend in the artificial intelligence industry, where companies are increasingly consolidating their conversational and work-oriented tools into single, streamlined interfaces. Competitors have adopted similar strategies to manage the computational load required for modern AI. As the industry faces mounting pressure on computing resources, these adjustments are becoming a standard approach to balancing service availability with operational costs.
