South Korean artificial intelligence data centre firm Elice saw its shares plunge 32 percent on their first day of trading in Seoul, marking the worst debut on record for a Korean IPO of 100 million dollars or more. The company raised 201.1 billion won, equivalent to 151 million dollars, by selling 2.22 million new shares at 90,500 won each, which was the top of its pricing range. Opening at 75,700 won on the Kosdaq, the stock fell as low as 58,000 won before closing at 61,500 won. Despite strong retail demand that made it the largest IPO on the Kosdaq for the year, individual investors faced significant losses, dropping about 12 million dollars based on the closing price. Founded in 2015 by three KAIST doctoral students as a coding education platform, Elice transitioned into AI cloud infrastructure, building modular data centres and selling servers, networking, and liquid cooling solutions. The company's prospectus showed strong financial health with first-half revenue surging 200 percent to 34.4 billion won and an operating profit of 10.3 billion won. Chief executive Jae Won Kim stated that the company plans to use the funds to expand AI data centres both in South Korea and abroad in its pursuit of becoming a global full-stack infrastructure leader. However, the weak debut reflects a broader cooling of investor enthusiasm and growing volatility toward neocloud and AI infrastructure providers globally.
