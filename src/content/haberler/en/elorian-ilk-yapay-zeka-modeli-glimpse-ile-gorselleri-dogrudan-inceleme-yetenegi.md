Palo Alto-based AI startup Elorian, which previously raised a $55 million seed round at a $300 million valuation before launching any product, has released its first model called Glimpse as a research preview. Founder and former Google DeepMind researcher Andrew Dai pointed out that most current multimodal models look at an image only once, convert it into text, and reason from memory without truly seeing the details. In contrast, Glimpse can continuously return to the source during its reasoning process, cropping into specific regions, extracting video frames, or running code to measure what it observes.

Elorian describes this mechanism as a visual scratch space. While similar concepts have appeared in other systems, Elorian emphasizes that it built its entire model around this approach at a low cost. The company trained Glimpse using a small team and a few hundred GPUs. Glimpse is priced at $0.60 per million input tokens and $3 per million output tokens, positioning it competitively among mid-tier models.

According to Elorian's evaluations, Glimpse outperforms even advanced frontier systems in scientific chart reasoning and short-form video captioning. Initially, the startup is targeting companies handling large amounts of video data, such as robotics firms, scientific laboratories, and media organizations. In the longer term, Elorian aims to transition from visual perception to direct action, including controlling physical robots based on visual scenes.
