Massive data centers that form the foundation of AI technologies are facing significant social resistance. In recent ballot measures in the United States, the public demonstrated support for restrictions on data centers, with 40 out of 41 proposed measures being passed. This trend indicates that the environmental impact of AI infrastructure, particularly its strain on water and energy resources, is being closely monitored by local communities.\n\nAdvanced graphics processing units (GPUs) that power AI models generate significantly more heat than traditional servers. Cooling these chips requires massive amounts of energy and water. Experts point out that a modern data center can consume up to 10 million gallons of water per day. Two primary cooling methods are prevalent: "dry cooling" and "evaporative cooling." While dry cooling uses less water, it leads to high energy consumption, straining electrical grids; evaporative cooling, meanwhile, directly consumes water resources.\n\nCompanies are making various investments to address this issue. For instance, Meta is building a $100 million water treatment plant for its data center in Idaho, while an $80 million recycling plant was planned for SpaceX's "Colossus" data center in Memphis. However, the construction and operational processes of these facilities are not enough to fully alleviate local concerns. Furthermore, the fact that data center operators typically report water consumption figures company-wide rather than site-by-site fuels transparency debates.\n\nThis tension in the sector reveals that AI growth is limited not only by software and hardware capacity but also by the sustainability of physical resources. The energy and water needs of data centers have become central to debates on climate change and local resource management. Companies are forced to increase infrastructure investments to gain public trust while simultaneously developing more efficient cooling technologies. This has become a critical factor that will determine the future growth rate of the AI world.
