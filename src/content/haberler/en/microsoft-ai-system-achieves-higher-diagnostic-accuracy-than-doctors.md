A new artificial intelligence system developed by Microsoft researchers has outperformed human doctors in tests based on medical case scenarios. Published in the journal Science, the research evaluated the system using scenarios generated from medical case reports and emergency room records. Named Diagnostic Orchestrator, the system brings together multiple AI agents to mimic a medical team, evaluating patient symptoms, determining necessary tests, and identifying potential diagnoses.

In tests based on patient records from the medical center in Boston, the system reached an 80 percent diagnostic accuracy rate. By comparison, human doctors achieved an average accuracy rate of 47 percent in the same scenarios. Researchers note that while these results do not mean the system surpasses doctors in every clinical condition, it shows remarkable success in specific case evaluations.

The core approach relies on different AI agents assessing a medical case from multiple perspectives and complementing each other throughout the diagnostic process. Microsoft aims to develop systems capable of easing the repetitive and time-consuming tasks in medicine, allowing healthcare workers to spend more time with patients rather than replacing them.

Moving forward, Microsoft plans to test the system in real hospital environments. Dr. Dominic King, who leads Microsoft's health AI initiatives, stated that tests in collaboration with the Boston hospital are likely to begin within the coming months. These clinical validations will be crucial in determining the reliability of the technology in real-world settings.
