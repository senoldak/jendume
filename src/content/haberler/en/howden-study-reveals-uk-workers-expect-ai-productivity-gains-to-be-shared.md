A recent study conducted in the UK has shed light on how employees view the integration of artificial intelligence in the workplace. According to research by insurance group Howden, workers expect to share in the rewards if their employers achieve significant productivity gains or cost savings through AI. The study found that one in three UK workers expects a higher pay as an AI dividend.

The research surveyed over 2,000 employees and revealed a general acceptance toward AI, provided that its benefits are passed on to the workforce and not kept solely by the company. Beyond higher pay, workers impacted by AI also expect shorter working hours, greater job security, and bonuses if their company successfully unlocks AI benefits.

However, the report also highlighted significant concerns among employees. Half of the respondents worry they might be expected to shoulder even more work due to time savings, while another half fear AI could soon be used to assess their performance. Additionally, many workers are anxious that AI could be introduced faster than they can understand it, with 53 percent worrying they will lack the time to learn new skills alongside their existing workloads.

Howden Employee Benefits & Wellbeing Managing Director Cheryl Brennan stated that with the workforce being central to the AI-productivity journey, existing misalignments in expectations must be addressed. The report emphasizes that simply providing access to AI training is insufficient; companies must offer full support, including dedicated learning time.

While the UK Government has set plans to upskill 10 million workers in AI by 2030, uptake has reportedly been low. Howden reminds employers of their duty to workers, with Brennan concluding on the necessity for dialogue and action regarding pay, progression, and transparency to keep human workers in the loop.
