Running AI models, known as inference, consumes massive amounts of energy in data centers. To address this, researchers and startups are revisiting "optical computing," a method that uses light instead of traditional electronic circuits. Light is already used in data centers for networking; now, the goal is to utilize it directly for computation.

At the core of AI models lies the "matrix-vector multiplication" operation, which is repeated billions of times per second. In traditional computers, this is performed by electronic circuits, where energy costs increase quadratically as the matrix size grows. In optical systems, this multiplication is performed by passing light through a filter, a passive process that consumes significantly less energy. The total market valuation of companies in the optical computing sector has risen from $0.4 billion in 2022 to $2.22 billion.

Researchers at Cornell University suggest that optical systems could theoretically reduce energy consumption by a factor of 100,000. However, this technology is still in its early stages. The biggest challenge facing optical computing is a fundamental computer science principle known as "Amdahl’s Law." This law states that the speedup or efficiency gain of a system is limited by the portions that can actually be improved. In other words, optical systems cannot accelerate 100% of AI operations; they can only optimize specific parts like matrix multiplication. Nevertheless, startups like the UK-based Optalysys are aiming to revolutionize AI hardware by integrating this technology into existing network paths.
