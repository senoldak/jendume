Massive "AI factories" built to train and run complex models are no longer evaluated solely by their processing power, but by how quickly they can process data. Dell Technologies has taken a significant step to address the growing data storage and cooling needs of these facilities. At the SC26 conference, the company unveiled fully liquid-cooled storage units and software-defined storage solutions.

AI workloads are evolving from simple training phases to complex processes involving autonomous agents. This shift requires storage systems to be highly flexible. Dell is transforming its PowerEdge servers into flexible nodes that can switch roles—acting as file, block, or object storage—as workloads shift. This software-defined solution, called Dell Exascale Storage, allows companies to reconfigure their hardware investments based on evolving needs rather than being locked into a single storage type.

Another critical feature of the system is its cooling technology. The power density of modern AI systems has increased so significantly that data centers are now planning for fully liquid-cooled infrastructures. To meet this demand, Dell has developed a 2U, 32-drive node that is 100% liquid-cooled. This represents the first direct liquid-cooled storage solution designed for rack-scale AI environments.

These developments highlight that AI infrastructure is not just about processors and memory; physical processes like data storage and cooling directly impact system efficiency. For cloud operators, this flexibility enhances the long-term viability of hardware investments. By decoupling software from hardware, Dell enables faster integration of new server generations while helping data centers manage energy costs and performance bottlenecks.
