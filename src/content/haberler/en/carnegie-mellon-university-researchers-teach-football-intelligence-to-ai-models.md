Researchers at Carnegie Mellon University (CMU) have embarked on a project to teach artificial intelligence the intricate rules and strategies of American football through an exclusive partnership with the National Football League (NFL). Part of the NFL Forward Initiative, this project marks the first time the league has granted an academic institution access to its official film footage and on-field data. The research team aims to build foundation models capable of developing "football intelligence," comparable to the knowledge of an experienced fan or analyst.

The project utilizes massive datasets provided by the NFL's Next Gen Stats system. This data is sourced from RFID chips embedded in players' shoulder pads, equipment, and the ball, providing real-time location information powered by Amazon Web Services (AWS). To process several terabytes of information, the team is employing deep learning foundation models. These models are designed to synchronize video footage with numerical tracking data to understand the nuances of every play on the field.

The development process is structured into two primary phases. Initially, the researchers created two pretrained systems to serve as building blocks: one for video analysis and another for player-tracking data. The first goal is for the AI to identify what is happening on the field during a play. The second, more complex phase involves predictive capabilities. Researchers want the model to anticipate future moves, such as whether a team will execute a blitz or how a safety might rotate their coverage before the ball is even snapped.

This technology holds significant potential for both professional teams and the broader sports industry. For coaches and scouts, it automates the tedious task of manually annotating thousands of hours of game film, allowing them to focus on high-level tactical decisions. For fans, the model could identify if a specific play has been run before by another team or simulate potential outcomes of different tactical calls. The CMU team emphasizes that this investment in AI will enhance league efficiency and push the boundaries of how machines learn from multi-modal data.
