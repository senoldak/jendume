OpenAI's hardware team reportedly leveraged the GPT-6 Astra model to engineer a device allowing users to communicate with the Codex coding assistant, finalizing the project as an auxiliary task in less than seven days. According to OpenAI, Astra managed every phase independently, taking care of structural casing, circuit board assembly along with part selection and signal paths, as well as device software and the graphical interface. An external manufacturer subsequently built a small prototype run in under a month, although multiple redesign phases were necessary because Astra occasionally overlooked physical production limits, dimensional tolerances, and mechanical assembly specifics.

OpenAI also pointed out a substantial performance improvement. An unreleased internal variant achieved 0.875 on the BenchCAD 2.0 evaluation framework, whereas GPT-6 Astra scored 0.594 at its peak reasoning setting. This assessment verifies whether AI architectures can rebuild three-dimensional components and map out circuit board pathways. OpenAI has not yet disclosed further specifics regarding the internal variant.

The organization noted that internal engineering squads employ Codex and ChatGPT for computer-aided design files, manufacturing blueprints, and testing simulations across fields like robotics and server infrastructure. Throughout this timeframe, platform token consumption has reportedly multiplied twenty-three times starting from February. Furthermore, OpenAI is actively working on producing its own proprietary physical gadget, rumored to blend features of an intelligent speaker and a mobile phone.

This technical milestone highlights the expanding capacity of machine learning systems to optimize complex engineering workflows, while simultaneously emphasizing that physical manufacturing limitations still demand rigorous human oversight.
