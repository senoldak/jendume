German robotics company NEURA Robotics has reached a cooperative agreement alongside regional authorities and agri-food enterprise Costa Group to establish a dedicated robot training facility in Spain by mid-2027. The project partners intend to invest double-digit millions of euros into the initiative, with NEURA leading the operational efforts.

The new site will function as a specialized gym where companies can train robots on real-world tasks before deploying them. Organizations will be able to bring their proprietary processes, test them on various robotic platforms, and evaluate performance. Through NEURA's Neuraverse platform, companies can either keep their developed robot skills private or license them to others for a fee, ensuring that innovators benefit from their creations.

Costa Group will provide practical challenges derived from its farms and factories. Initial projects will focus on reducing physically demanding and repetitive labor, eventually expanding into logistics, automotive, and manufacturing sectors. Meanwhile, the University of Zaragoza will contribute research capabilities and staff training. The facility will also feature a dedicated physical AI model zone for testing robots in authentic operational settings.

NEURA previously raised up to $1.4 billion in June, partly to expand its network of training gyms, and has since acquired two companies. In comparison, Barcelona-based THEKER secured €73 million in June for factory robots that learn on the job. The new Spanish center is scheduled to open its doors by mid-2027.
