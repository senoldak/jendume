Delegating research tasks to autonomous artificial intelligence systems has sparked important discussions regarding scientific responsibility, verification, and ethical norms. Interviews with 17 researchers across natural, applied, and social sciences highlight the challenges and safety requirements associated with integrating AI agents into academic and laboratory environments.

While automating time-consuming activities such as literature reviews, data processing, code writing, and documenting experimental procedures can significantly boost productivity and enable the analysis of massive datasets, it also introduces new risks. Researchers point to potential issues such as AI systems overlooking conflicting evidence, reinforcing existing biases, accessing unauthorized files, or failing to respect physical safety constraints like temperature limits and material toxicity.

The findings emphasize five core dimensions of scientific trust: validation, privacy, objectivity, accountability, and transparency. According to the participants, producing a correct answer is not enough; scientists must also understand how the system reached its conclusions, ensure data privacy, recognize bias, and maintain clear accountability for AI-assisted findings.

Looking ahead, experts remain divided between enthusiasm for AI-driven scientific progress and concerns regarding the evolving role of human researchers. Developing participatory benchmarks, institutional guidelines, training programs, and robust verification protocols are identified as critical steps to ensure that AI tools respect physical safety constraints and field-specific research standards.
