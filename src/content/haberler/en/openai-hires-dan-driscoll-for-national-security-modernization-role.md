OpenAI has appointed Dan Driscoll, the 26th Secretary of the US Army, to the newly established position of Senior Counselor for National Security Modernization. In this role, Driscoll is tasked with advising the company on how advanced artificial intelligence can bolster national and cyber security. He will collaborate with OpenAI’s leadership, industry partners, and academia, with a primary focus on helping US allies adopt AI technologies responsibly. Driscoll’s background as a former Army officer and his legal education provide a unique bridge between technical innovation and government policy.

According to Driscoll, national security requirements are evolving more rapidly than at any point in the last half-century. He noted that AI would assist security professionals in reducing administrative paperwork, processing vast amounts of intelligence data, and delivering actionable answers more efficiently. During his tenure as Army Secretary, Driscoll managed nearly one million soldiers and over 265,000 civilian employees. He was also instrumental in driving AI modernization through initiatives like Project ARIA, which integrated AI tools into military budgeting, logistics, and tactical deployment models.

The hiring of Driscoll signals OpenAI's intent to secure a larger share of government contracts and influence the regulatory framework for AI in defense. This follows a February 2026 agreement where OpenAI began deploying its models on the Department of Defense’s classified networks. This partnership gained momentum after rival Anthropic faced setbacks with the Pentagon over contractual restrictions regarding surveillance and autonomous systems. Driscoll’s expertise is expected to enhance OpenAI’s Daybreak program, a cyber resilience initiative already being pitched to utilities and international partners like Ukraine, while helping internal teams understand the specific operational needs of security institutions.
