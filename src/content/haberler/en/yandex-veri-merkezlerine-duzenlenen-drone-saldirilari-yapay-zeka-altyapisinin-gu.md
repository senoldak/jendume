Ukrainian drone strikes targeting three data centers operated by Russian technology giant Yandex have laid bare the severe risks facing the physical infrastructure of the digital economy. The facilities, including one in Sasovo housing tens of thousands of servers and critical AI supercomputers, went offline, disrupting essential banking and retail services across the country. These attacks follow reciprocal strikes on digital infrastructure in Kyiv, illustrating a dangerous new dimension in modern warfare.

With military applications of artificial intelligence expanding rapidly—from drone navigation to battlefield intelligence analysis—the underlying computing power has turned into an inviting target. Experts point out that hiding a data center is practically impossible. Due to their immense size, extraordinary electricity consumption, and massive cooling requirements, these facilities are easily located and vulnerable to relatively inexpensive weapons like drones, mirroring earlier disruptions when Iranian drones hit Amazon Web Services facilities in the United Arab Emirates and Bahrain.

These escalating risks are forcing governments and industry leaders to rethink data center placement and security. The International Data Center Authority suggests shifting away from thousands of scattered sites toward a handful of heavily fortified digital hubs that are easier to defend. However, consolidating computing power also concentrates risk.

Beyond economic losses, the civilian consequences are profound. Hospitals, emergency dispatch systems, and daily commerce depend heavily on these networks. As the International Committee of the Red Cross notes, data centers utilized for military functions risk becoming lawful targets under the laws of war, raising critical questions about how to protect the civilian infrastructure intertwined with modern artificial intelligence.
