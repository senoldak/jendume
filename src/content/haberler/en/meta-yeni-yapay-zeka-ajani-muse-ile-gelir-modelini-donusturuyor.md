Meta is entering a new operational phase with Muse, its personal AI agent introduced a few weeks ago. Speaking during the company's third-quarter earnings call, CEO Mark Zuckerberg stated that the agent has achieved clear product-market fit and the focus is now on scaling. Available in the US and Canada, Muse has already attracted more than 10 million users since its early September rollout.

Operating Muse is significantly more expensive for Meta compared to its other services, prompting the company to accelerate its monetization plans. Meta is developing systems that will enable the company to charge businesses fees for transactions facilitated by its agent. Shopping is currently the most common use case, with users spending an average of 200 dollars per purchase through Muse.

Zuckerberg forecasted that financial returns generated by Muse could eventually outpace the earnings the company currently achieves from its existing apps. Noting that the average annual revenue per American user for their current family of apps is several hundred dollars, Zuckerberg expects Muse to reach an even higher level due to the volume of commerce taking place. The agent is also central to Meta's plans to boost AI subscriptions through its smart glasses.

Meta previewed Muse on its smart glasses, announcing that the agent will be available on the devices next month. Users will be able to interact with the agent, manage tasks, and add items to to-do lists. While Meta did not specify if all glasses features would require a separate subscription, Zuckerberg's statements indicate that certain Muse capabilities on the hardware will be paywalled.
