Samuel King, a bioengineering PhD candidate at Stanford University, has accomplished a groundbreaking feat showcasing the potential of AI in biology. King successfully used generative AI models to propose genetic blueprints for microscopic viruses.

 Conducted in 2025, this pioneering work demonstrates that artificial intelligence can extend far beyond generating text or images, stepping into the fundamental building blocks of life itself. While not yet an example of fully realized AI-generated life, this milestone points toward a future where biological research is fundamentally transformed.

 This achievement has sparked widespread interest across the scientific community, opening new horizons at the intersection of AI and biology while simultaneously raising essential ethical and safety questions. Experts emphasize the growing need to carefully monitor and regulate AI-designed biological agents.
