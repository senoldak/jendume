A new legislative measure aimed at preventing artificial intelligence-generated advertisements from misleading consumers has been enacted in the U.S. state of Pennsylvania. Signed by Governor Josh Shapiro, the regulation establishes new rules regarding the use of synthetic content in commercial advertising. The measure serves as a localized response to transparency issues arising from the widespread adoption of artificial intelligence technologies in everyday life and marketing.

Named the Artificial Intelligence Deceptive Advertising Act, the legislation targets synthetic advertising content that creates false perceptions about the nature, origin, or reality of a product or service. Under the regulation, visual, video, and audio files produced or significantly altered by artificial intelligence must be clearly disclosed when presented to consumers. It is required that the disclosure be presented during the consumer's first encounter with the synthetic content and within the same medium in a clearly noticeable manner.

However, the law does not impose a blanket labeling requirement on all AI content. Contents that are clearly fictional, parodies, satires, commentary, criticism, and text-only contents are among the exceptions to this requirement. Additionally, a 60-day transition period is provisioned for the implementation of the law. For those who violate the rules, penalties such as fines, attorney fees, and court costs may be imposed by courts.

Being a state-level regulation rather than a federal law, this measure represents concrete steps taken by local governments regarding artificial intelligence ethics and oversight. Initiating a new compliance process for the advertising industry and content creators, the practical application of the law will become clearer through future judicial decisions and implementation processes.
