Exams in South Korea have long served as the ultimate career-defining equalizer in a society intensely focused on merit. However, widespread allegations that high-scoring candidates are utilizing AI smartglasses and electronic devices to cheat have plunged the national testing system into severe disruption.

Authorities recently took the extraordinary step of broadcasting air-raid-style emergency alerts to mobile phones across the country to deter unauthorized electronic devices during the suneung, the grueling eight-hour university entrance exam. The education ministry reported that numerous candidates were caught with prohibited devices, leading to the resignation of Education Minister Choi Kyo-jin amidst the fallout. Meanwhile, President Lee Jae Myung offered substantial financial rewards for reliable tips regarding exam fraud.

Sociologists point out that the immense economic pressure to secure a stable livelihood drives extreme competition, turning exams into what many consider the last bastion of fairness following the economic anxieties triggered by the 1997 financial crisis. Yet, experts argue that multiple-choice testing formats centered around memorization are entirely unsuited for the age of AI, creating a vulnerability that fosters cheating.

Educational reformers emphasize that testing formats must evolve now that AI can solve standard exam questions instantly. As discussions around computer-based testing and systemic modernization continue, South Korea remains locked in a tense debate over how to preserve educational integrity in an era defined by disruptive technology.
