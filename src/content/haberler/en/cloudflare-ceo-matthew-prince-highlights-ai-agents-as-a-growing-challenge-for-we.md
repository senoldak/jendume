Cloudflare CEO Matthew Prince addressed the growing challenges posed by artificial intelligence agents during the company's third-quarter earnings call. Prince noted that certain customers view AI agents as a burden for their businesses and actively seek ways to keep these automated tools away from their websites. According to company projections, agent traffic could eventually reach a scale one thousand times greater than human clicks.

Prince illustrated the issue using a restaurant analogy. While an agent might scan and download menus from one thousand different restaurants on the internet, the user ultimately visits only a single establishment, leaving the other 999 businesses to bear processing costs without gaining any value. For website operators, the core problem is that this heavy traffic fails to convert into revenue. Furthermore, traditional container-based cloud architectures may struggle to keep pace with the cost demands of this emerging agent-driven landscape.

The meeting also highlighted Cloudflare's financial performance. The company's revenue grew by 39 percent year-over-year, reaching a record 782.5 million dollars. Management emphasized that demand is rapidly increasing for products designed to protect websites from unwanted AI traffic or allow them to monetize it. Notably, a significant portion of the web has adopted Cloudflare's content protection tools since July 1.

Addressing security concerns, Prince stated that vulnerabilities such as PHP2SHELL may have been exploited by state-sponsored actors before being publicly disclosed. As Cloudflare scans historical data to detect prior exploits, its broad customer base allows the firm to identify and patch security flaws early. Following the earnings announcements and discussions, Cloudflare shares rose by approximately 15 percent in after-hours trading.
