The cryptic, 1,009-page handwritten notebooks of Kurt Gödel, one of the 20th century's most influential logicians, have been unlocked with the help of artificial intelligence. A research team led by logician Jan von Plato at the University of Helsinki had been struggling to decipher the notes, which were written in the 1940s using an obsolete German shorthand. Progress was painfully slow, with researchers managing to process only about one page per week until they enlisted the help of the AI model Claude.

Within just a few days, Claude provided a comprehensive digest of the entire material, revealing that Gödel had proved hundreds of theorems that were previously unknown to the scientific community. The AI identified 663 theorems within the workbooks. When these results were verified using Coq, a program designed to check the validity of mathematical proofs, approximately 400 were confirmed as stated, while another 200 could be validated with minor adjustments. This discovery confirms that Gödel was far ahead of his time, having anticipated significant mathematical results decades before they were formally recognized.

Notably, the AI successfully reconstructed Gödel's proof regarding the independence of the axiom of choice, a topic Gödel had claimed to have solved in the 1940s but could not reconstruct at the time. Von Plato emphasized that the AI's ability to connect pieces across a thousand pages allowed it to see the work as a cohesive whole, a feat impossible for human researchers who typically process information page by page. This capability highlights the transformative potential of AI in historical scientific research.

Researchers believe that the notebooks contain dozens of additional themes in logic and foundations of mathematics that have remained hidden for over 80 years. With the continued assistance of AI, the team plans to explore these remaining sections, as well as Gödel's notes on physics and cosmology, potentially opening the door to further scientific breakthroughs.
