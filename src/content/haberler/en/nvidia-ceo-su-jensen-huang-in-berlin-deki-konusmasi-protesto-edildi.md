During Nvidia's GTC conference in Berlin, CEO Jensen Huang's keynote speech was interrupted by protesters holding a banner with a Greenpeace logo. The demonstrators questioned Huang about Nvidia chips found in Russian drones used in the conflict in Ukraine. Security staff escorted the protesters out while Huang urged them to be gentle, stating that while the issues were important, he needed to finish his presentation.

According to reports from tech outlets, the interruption occurred about twenty minutes into the talk. Activists demanded to know what Nvidia was doing regarding technology found in military hardware. Ukrainian officials and forensic teams previously reported discovering Nvidia Jetson Orin modules in Russian cruise missiles and AI-powered drones. Sanctions imposed after the invasion bar Russia from buying such hardware.

In response to earlier reports, Nvidia stated that its Jetson Orin modules are consumer-grade products sold to developers and startups for beneficial applications. The company noted it does not sell chips to Russia, did not design them for military use, and cannot track the resale of used hardware.

After the interruption was handled, Huang resumed his presentation, introducing Nvidia's OpenShell software for AI agents, new data center designs, and a reference humanoid robot. The event continues to draw attention to broader environmental and supply chain debates surrounding major tech firms, emphasizing the complex challenges facing manufacturers in global markets.
