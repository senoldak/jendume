The rapidly growing energy demand of artificial intelligence technologies and large data centers is driving major transformations in the energy sector. In the US, backed by tech giants like Microsoft and Google, plans are underway to revive three previously shut down nuclear power plants and feed them back into the electrical grid. Once the projects are completed, a total capacity of 2.25 gigawatts is targeted to be added to the grid.

The first facility to be recommissioned, the Palisades Nuclear Plant in Michigan, had entered a decommissioning process after stopping operations in 2022. Operated by Holtec with financing from the US Department of Energy, the plant is targeted to start electricity production around March 2027, marking a first for a decommissioned plant being brought back to life. The facility has a capacity of approximately 800 megawatts.

The second project involves Unit 1 of the Three Mile Island facility in Pennsylvania, which will reopen as the Crane Clean Energy Center and generate 835 megawatts of electricity. Operator Constellation Energy plans to invest $1.6 billion in the project, while the US Department of Energy provided a $1 billion loan. Microsoft will purchase the generated electricity under a long-term agreement.

The third project covers the Duane Arnold Energy Center in Iowa. Closed previously, the facility is targeted to go live through a collaboration between Google and NextEra Energy.

Previously shut down due to high operational, maintenance, and labor costs, these plants have become attractive again due to the soaring electricity demand triggered by artificial intelligence investments. Unlike solar and wind energy, nuclear facilities can generate uninterrupted electricity for long periods regardless of weather conditions, offering a critical solution for continuous data center operations.
