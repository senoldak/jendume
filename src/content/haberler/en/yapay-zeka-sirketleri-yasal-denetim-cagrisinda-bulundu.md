During a crucial parliamentary session in the United Kingdom, executives from leading artificial intelligence companies appeared before lawmakers to discuss safety and governance. Representatives from key firms stated that voluntary commitments are insufficient for managing frontier risks and advocated for a formal statutory framework to regulate the industry.

Meanwhile, security officials warned about the rising risks associated with advanced technologies, particularly concerning biosecurity threats. Government officials conceded during the hearing that current security evaluations rely almost entirely on the voluntary cooperation of developers, with no legal power to prevent the release of dangerous models.

Former researchers who testified at the session also expressed skepticism regarding industry self-regulation, urging policymakers to establish robust oversight mechanisms that do not depend solely on corporate goodwill.
