Quantum contextuality, one of the most mysterious features of quantum mechanics, has moved from theoretical debate to a measurable and usable technology. Researchers have successfully completed a logic puzzle that classical computers cannot solve, using superconducting quantum processors. In the "magic square" game, where classical strategies can achieve a maximum success rate of 88.89%, quantum processors surpassed this limit, achieving a success rate of 98.3%.

The key factor determining the future capacity of quantum computers to train AI models or solve complex problems is particle entanglement and this property of contextuality. Researchers have proven with this experiment that quantum contextuality can be used as a resource in complex systems. This demonstrates that quantum hardware is a machine that can outperform classical computers in real-world tasks. The experiment was conducted on systems scaling up to 105 qubits.

In a classical logic framework, it is impossible for numbers in a grid to satisfy both row and column rules simultaneously, as it is a paradox. However, in the quantum world, values are not fixed until they are measured. During the experiment, researchers observed that in games repeated 100,000 times, quantum processors achieved a level of coordination that classical strategies could not reach.

This study provides a new benchmark for quantum hardware. This method, which can catch subtle errors that random-circuit tests miss, has become a tool for measuring error rates and improving hardware quality. In the future, such quantum advantages could multiply the processing power of AI algorithms, paving the way for calculations that seem impossible to be performed in seconds.
