A North Carolina resident named Laura Mack has taken legal action after being evicted from her home despite receiving an email agreement from her landlord's AI assistant granting an extension on overdue rent. The situation involves complex legal questions arising as businesses increasingly automate customer communications through autonomous agents. The controversy began when Mack communicated with the leasing office of Colony Townhome Apartments regarding her 4.000 dollars in overdue rent.

An AI assistant named Olivia engaged in a compassionate email exchange, acknowledging specific personal details about her apartment and approving an extension until the end of the month. Days later, however, Mack received an abrupt eviction notice. When she confronted the leasing office, agents claimed that the AI had mistakenly granted the extension without management's knowledge and that the emails lacked the required AI disclosure notices. Residents had reportedly been informed about the AI agent handling maintenance and rent inquiries starting in December 2025, but the lack of disclosure in Mack's specific case left her believing she was corresponding with a human employee.

Local lawmakers have criticized the incident, with state senator Jim Burgin pointing to the ongoing necessity for legislative oversight regarding AI communication disclosure and liability. Currently staying in a hotel while pursuing her case in court, Mack argues that accountability must be maintained when automated systems make commitments to tenants. The case involves the broader societal challenges of integrating autonomous AI into everyday housing and business agreements.
