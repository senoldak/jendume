New York has launched the nation's first state-run AI testing program, marking a significant shift in how artificial intelligence is regulated at the local level. Governor Kathy Hochul announced the initiative, emphasizing that the risks associated with these technologies are too high to rely solely on the self-policing of tech companies. By moving away from trusting corporate claims, the state aims to provide objective evidence that can inform public policy and ensure the responsible use of AI in critical sectors like medicine and scientific research.

A specialized facility at Binghamton University, dedicated to AI responsibility and research, will spearhead these efforts. More than 80 scientists will utilize the computing power provided by Empire AI, a $500 million public-private partnership, to rigorously test advanced systems. The center will evaluate risks and provide evidence to support decision-making by public officials, while also exploring how AI can be used responsibly in various scientific fields.

This initiative is supported by the RAISE Act, which mandates that large AI developers must register with state authorities by January 1, 2027. Under this regulation, companies are required to publish safety frameworks, provide transparency reports, and report critical safety incidents within 72 hours. This legislative framework ensures that developers are held accountable for their safety protocols.

While the program represents a major step forward, it faces a notable limitation: it can only evaluate models that companies choose to disclose. Unreleased or proprietary models remain outside the immediate scope of these tests, though the legislation forces a higher degree of transparency than previously existed. This move sets a critical precedent by shifting AI safety verification from corporate self-policing to public oversight, ensuring that the public interest is protected as these technologies continue to evolve.
