A major US healthcare technology provider has partnered with Anthropic to allow patients to query their medical records using the Claude AI model. This integration, built on the Model Context Protocol (MCP), enables patients to securely connect their health data to the AI, allowing them to ask specific questions about their lab results, medication history, immunization records, and diagnostic reports.

The primary goal of this tool is to help patients better understand the information provided by their healthcare professionals. For instance, a patient can ask about their most recent blood test results or review past visit history without needing to contact their doctor for basic clarifications. However, it is critical to note that the system does not provide medical advice. Company representatives emphasize that this tool is not a substitute for a clinician’s professional judgment, and patients should always consult a qualified healthcare professional regarding diagnoses, treatments, or changes in their care plans.

Regarding data privacy, the agreement includes strict protections. The provider states that the data accessed through this connection cannot be used to train Anthropic’s models or improve its products. Users must authenticate their accounts to use the service, can select which specific health information the AI can access, and retain the ability to revoke access at any time. The feature is currently available to users subscribed to specific premium tiers.

This partnership reflects a broader trend in the health-tech industry, following similar collaborations between Microsoft and Mayo Clinic, as well as Boston Children’s Hospital and OpenAI. By offloading routine administrative queries to AI, these systems aim to reduce the burden on clinicians, potentially giving doctors more time to focus on complex patient care. While the long-term impact on patient outcomes remains to be seen, these tools represent a significant step toward integrating AI into personal health management.
