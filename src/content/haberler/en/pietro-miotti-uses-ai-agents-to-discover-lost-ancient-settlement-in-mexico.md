Researcher Pietro Miotti utilized artificial intelligence during a vacation in Peru to explore whether forgotten ancient settlements could be located using modern technology. Following discussions with models such as Gemini, ChatGPT, Claude, and Grok, Miotti was directed to NASA's 2013 LiDAR datasets. He subsequently deployed forty-eight AI agents to scan a twelve hundred square kilometer area in Mexico. LiDAR technology, which uses laser pulses to create three-dimensional maps of terrain and reveal surface shapes, played a vital role in analyzing landforms hidden beneath dense forest canopy. During the initial examinations, the agents identified a flat plaza, platforms, and a pyramid-like elevation standing eight to twelve meters high.

Miotti compiled these findings into a Spanish report and submitted them to the National Institute of Anthropology and History in Mexico. In its October twenty-first response, the institution stated that no archaeological site was previously registered at the reported coordinates. While this strengthens the possibility that these mysterious structures have not yet been officially documented, archaeologists must conduct on-site inspections to reach a definitive conclusion. If confirmed, this discovery will mark a notable milestone for the application of AI agents in scientific research. Utilizing large language models alongside geographical datasets demonstrates how modern computational tools can assist researchers in navigating vast and dense forest areas to uncover hidden historical sites.
