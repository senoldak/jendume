Apple is shifting its artificial intelligence strategy by arguing that Siri does not need to win the intelligence race against competitors like ChatGPT. Instead of focusing on building the most advanced foundational model, the company is prioritizing personal context already stored on user devices.

In a recent interview, Apple leadership emphasized that the company is comfortable collaborating with Google to utilize Gemini technology for the new Siri. Rather than trying to out-build rival frontier models, Apple is concentrating on how these technologies function directly within its hardware ecosystem.

Siri's primary advantage lies in its ability to securely access personal information scattered across apps, including messages, emails, calendar events, and photos. While other AI assistants are expanding cross-app capabilities, Apple's tight integration with iOS aims to make these interactions feel seamless and natural.

Privacy remains a cornerstone of this strategy, with requests processed locally on the device or through Apple's Private Cloud Compute infrastructure without retaining user data. Furthermore, Apple is deliberately avoiding making Siri an autonomous agent or a human-like companion, opting instead for predictable and restrained interactions.

The ultimate test for Apple will be whether this context-driven approach is accurate and secure enough to make iPhone owners rely on Siri rather than reaching for external AI chatbots.
