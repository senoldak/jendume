A new generation of "computer-use" AI agents is being deployed to assist healthcare teams by interacting with software applications just as a human would. These agents can click, type, and navigate between different systems to manage multi-step workflows, such as processing MRI authorization requests or coordinating patient discharge plans. Healthcare is often fragmented, requiring staff to manually move information between disconnected systems. By automating these repetitive tasks, the technology reduces administrative burden, allowing medical professionals to focus on patient outcomes rather than data entry.

The performance of these systems has been evaluated across both clinical and administrative tasks. The GPT-6 Astra model demonstrated high reliability, achieving a 99% safety rating in clinical evaluations. In independent benchmarks for administrative workflows, GPT-6 Astra scored 94.3%, while GPT-6 Luna scored 91.8%. Notably, the Luna model reduced costs by 98%, bringing the price per task down to 8.5 cents. These results highlight significant progress toward capable and affordable agents for high-volume administrative operations.

Major healthcare organizations, including CVS Health, Abridge, and Cigna Healthcare, are already piloting these tools. The systems are designed with strict security measures, including HIPAA compliance, human oversight, and audit logs. A key advantage is the ability to operate within legacy software that lacks modern API integrations, bridging gaps in existing digital infrastructure. Organizations are using these agents to clear months-long backlogs of document processing or to handle thousands of daily consults, enabling staff to prioritize work that requires their specific expertise. Data remains within enterprise-controlled infrastructure, governed by internal AI policies.
