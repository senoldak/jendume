Dyania Health’s Synapsis AI has successfully reviewed the medical records of over 7,800 heart-valve patients at the Cleveland Clinic. The system, which operates entirely on the hospital’s own servers, processes up to 16 years of patient history, including complex doctor notes and scan reports that are often difficult to parse. By keeping the data within the hospital’s network, the project addresses critical privacy concerns regarding patient information.

This technology is significant because it automates the labor-intensive process of chart review, which would traditionally take human clinicians years to complete manually. By linking findings across years of records, the AI helps build predictive models that estimate the risk of valve replacement failure at three, five, and ten-year intervals following surgery. This capability offers a promising step toward enhanced outcomes and more personalized care for patients with complex cardiac histories.

As major technology companies increasingly enter the healthcare space with AI-driven tools, this approach highlights a growing focus on data sovereignty and security. While the tool demonstrates the ability to create comprehensive datasets and predict risks, it is currently positioned strictly as a decision-support tool. Human doctors retain final authority on all clinical decisions, reviewing the evidence provided by the AI before taking action. Dyania Health acknowledges that while the initial results are promising, the tool still requires further external validation to ensure its efficacy and safety in broader clinical settings. This development underscores the potential for AI to assist in medical research and patient care, provided it remains under the careful supervision of medical professionals.
