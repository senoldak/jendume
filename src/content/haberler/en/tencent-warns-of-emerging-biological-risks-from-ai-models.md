Tencent's chief AI scientist, Yao Shunyu, has issued a significant warning regarding the potential for AI models to pose biological risks in the near future. Speaking at a science event in Hong Kong, Yao stated that the capabilities of AI models will soon reach a threshold where they could cause harm in the field of biology, much like they already can in cybersecurity. This development underscores the growing concern that AI technologies are expanding their impact beyond digital systems into physical and biological domains.

Yao emphasized that Chinese companies releasing open-source models, which can be downloaded and modified by anyone, must take safety protocols extremely seriously. He noted that managing biological risks could prove to be significantly more complex and challenging than managing cyber risks. These comments were made just days after Tencent launched its new flagship model, Hy4. In its launch announcement, the company stated that it intentionally excluded material on exploiting software vulnerabilities from Hy4's training data, focusing instead on defensive security data.

The company reported that while Hy4 possesses strong foundational knowledge of biology, it is less capable than leading models in designing experiments, a conclusion based on internal expert reviews. Tencent plans to release the model's weights and a report on its safety testing in the near future. According to benchmarking data, Hy4 currently ranks as the second-best model in China.

While acknowledging that US firms like Anthropic make a strong case for keeping top-tier models closed, Yao argued that a hybrid approach—utilizing both open and closed models—might be the most effective strategy, as open models can also assist defenders. This ongoing debate highlights that AI safety is not merely a technical challenge but a critical strategic issue for the future of global security.
