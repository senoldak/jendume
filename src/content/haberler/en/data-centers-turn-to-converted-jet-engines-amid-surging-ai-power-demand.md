The rapid expansion of artificial intelligence data centers is pushing power grids to their limits, creating a severe shortage of electricity. With companies facing years-long waiting lists for grid connections and new power turbines, some data center operators are now generating their own electricity using converted jet engines. Pulled from retired aircraft and rebuilt to run on natural gas, these engines offer a temporary fix to an escalating energy crisis.

Connecting large data centers to the traditional power grid can take years. For instance, hooking up 100 megawatts in Northern Virginia takes approximately seven years according to the International Energy Agency. These prolonged delays have already disrupted operations for major tech firms and data center developers, prompting a search for faster alternatives. Analysts note that converted jet-powered units allow facilities to become operational in a fraction of the time.

Prominent figures and companies are already adopting this approach. Elon Musk's xAI has installed converted jet-engine turbines in Mississippi to power its data center infrastructure. Similarly, OpenAI integrated gas turbines at its Abilene site, while data center builder Crusoe has ordered multiple converted units to accelerate its campus development.

However, relying on converted jet engines comes with significant trade-offs. These engines are less efficient and produce more emissions than turbines specifically designed for conventional power plants, requiring more units to generate equivalent power. Experts warn that while these engines solve immediate power shortages, environmental and efficiency concerns may mount as the artificial intelligence boom continues.
