OpenAI has officially announced support for Apple's email and calendar services for its ChatGPT chatbot. Thanks to the new iCloud Mail and iCloud Calendar plugins, users can now use the AI assistant with their iCloud accounts in the same way they do with Gmail and Google Calendar. To enable these plugins, users must log in with an Apple account and generate an app-specific password in order to establish a secure connection.

This new feature is accessible across desktop, web, and iOS platforms for both free accounts and paid ChatGPT subscribers. Furthermore, users on OpenAI's plan designed for professionals can also utilize these plugins through the company's autonomous agent known as Dot. Users can issue simple prompts to search through emails, catch up on inboxes, check what is next, or update calendar events directly from the chat interface.

The functionality of these plugins differs slightly from interacting directly with native Apple Mail and Calendar apps on macOS. Because the iCloud Mail and iCloud Calendar plugins can only process messages and events tied to Apple services, they are specific to the iCloud ecosystem and are not useful if you do not use iCloud.

This move by OpenAI also highlights the complicated relationship between the two tech giants. The artificial intelligence developer was the initial conversational software to feature built-in compatibility with Siri, though subsequent decisions by the hardware maker to pursue alternative partnerships suggested shifting dynamics. Legal actions concerning intellectual property added further complexity to the situation. Yet, alongside hardware releases including updated desktop and portable computers, the manufacturer also revealed productivity suite enhancements featuring conversational tools driven by the technology provider, indicating an ongoing operational connection between the entities.
