Cowbell has unveiled a standalone liability insurance policy specifically designed for small and midsized businesses that deploy autonomous AI agents. Set to launch on January 1, 2027, the policy addresses the growing problem of AI agents executing financial transactions or modifying records without human oversight. When businesses allow AI to operate autonomously, they expose themselves to unique risks. An agent might misinterpret approval statuses, pay a supplier twice, or file tax returns using outdated data.

This insurance covers these unrecovered funds, investigation costs, and even legal defense for claims involving algorithmic discrimination or bodily injury. It also protects against "LLMjacking," where unauthorized parties use stolen credentials to run AI workloads at the business's expense. Regulatory proceedings and certain fines are also included in the coverage, providing a comprehensive safety net for companies navigating this new technological landscape.

The underwriting process relies on "Cowbell Factors," which assess observability, autonomy, and governance. Businesses can connect their existing tools—such as Gemini, Claude, and ChatGPT—to the platform to monitor their risk scores. While the company previously offered AI coverage for larger enterprises, this new product is tailored for smaller organizations. Cowbell, which has raised over $200 million in funding to date, aims to mitigate the double exposure businesses face when their AI agents go rogue while simultaneously dealing with customer claims.
