Google has introduced new "agent skills" for AdMob, enabling mobile app developers to automate their advertising workflows. This update allows AI coding agents, including Claude Code, Codex, and Cursor, to handle the integration of ad code into mobile applications, verify setups, and identify errors before launch. By delegating these tasks to AI agents, developers can bypass the tedious process of navigating through dashboards and manually copying ad IDs, significantly streamlining the development lifecycle.

This development highlights a shift where AI agents are moving beyond simple code generation into operational management. Alongside these agent skills, Google is developing "Ask AdMob," a Gemini-powered chat assistant designed to sit within a developer’s account. This tool will assist in managing ad slots, suggesting ad networks to maximize revenue, and setting brand safety rules using plain language rather than complex block lists.

The integration of external agents aligns with a broader industry trend toward standardized agentic workflows. In August, OpenAI and several industry peers agreed on a unified standard for agent skills and server protocols. Google’s move is also a strategic effort to retain app developers within its ecosystem, particularly as competition intensifies with Meta, which is poised to overtake Google as the world’s largest advertising company this year.

While these tools are currently in testing, Google plans to expand the agentic capabilities throughout 2027. Crucially, the system maintains a "human-in-the-loop" requirement, meaning developers must approve any changes suggested by the AI before they go live. This approach aims to balance the efficiency of automation with the necessary oversight for managing complex advertising environments.
