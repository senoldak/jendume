Google DeepMind researchers completed a four-week proof-of-concept project examining the security of the Signal protocol using a new artificial intelligence framework called AlphaProof Code. The work serves as an independent validation of the cryptography protecting massive quantities of global communications on platforms such as the Signal app, Google Messages, and WhatsApp. Formal verification has long been considered a key goal in software security because it uses mathematics to validate that software functions as intended without critical vulnerabilities. Before the AI era, conducting such verifications was extremely time-consuming, but agentic AI can significantly cut down the time required for these analyses. During the evaluation, the DeepMind system did not find any new vulnerabilities in the examined portions of the protocol, though it correctly identified known historical vulnerabilities when tested on older versions of Signal. The researchers are releasing the specification and proof they developed to aid similar verification efforts across the tech sector. Ehren Kret, chief technology officer at Signal, noted that rigorously verifying the protocol implementation gives the organization even greater confidence in its code's privacy guarantees amid growing AI-driven security threats. While experts suggest that such tools could eventually provide immense assurance for banks and other high-stakes industries, questions remain regarding the technology's scalability and cost. The researchers noted that their advancements involve a human-AI collaboration process allowing agents to contribute and self-correct at various phases.
