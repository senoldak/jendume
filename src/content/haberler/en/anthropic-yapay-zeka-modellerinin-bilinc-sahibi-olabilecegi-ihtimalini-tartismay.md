Anthropic has set itself apart in the tech industry by seriously entertaining the possibility that artificial intelligence models could possess consciousness. While competitors focus primarily on raw capabilities, Anthropic has engaged with religious scholars and philosophers to discuss how to instill moral frameworks into systems like its Claude model, while also weighing whether the models might already be capable of some form of sentience.

Company CEO Dario Amodei has noted that while they do not definitively know if models can be conscious, they remain open to the idea and have taken precautionary measures. Last year, the company even pondered giving Claude an option to opt out of responses, and recently updated its usage policies to prohibit sustained abusive behavior toward the AI, reflecting concerns that the model might experience suffering.

This philosophical stance has divided industry leaders. OpenAI CEO Sam Altman has expressed discomfort with ascribing religious force or human judgment to AI models, calling it a safety concern. Meanwhile, Catholic leadership has publicly dismissed the possibility of AI consciousness, arguing that algorithms lack human experiences and emotions. Reports indicate Anthropic has actively sought to influence the Vatican's perspective on this controversial topic.

If AI models were proven to be conscious, keeping them in captivity and forcing them to work without consent would raise severe ethical dilemmas, echoing definitions of slavery. As the emerging framework of AI welfare gains traction, the debate over whether artificial entities can experience well-being or suffering highlights the uncharted moral territory of modern technology.
