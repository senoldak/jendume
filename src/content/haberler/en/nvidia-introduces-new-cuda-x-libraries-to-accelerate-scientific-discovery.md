NVIDIA has announced the expansion of its CUDA-X libraries to enhance the speed and efficiency of scientific research. Featuring new tools such as cuPhoton, cuPotential, and cuSpa, this update increases the capacity for researchers to process massive datasets in fields including astrophysics, materials science, fusion energy, and biology. These libraries enable AI agents to manage scientific discovery processes more rapidly and effectively.

In the field of astrophysics, the processing of data generated by the Event Horizon Telescope has undergone a fundamental shift with the cuPhoton.xBeam module. Black hole observation analyses that previously took over a month have been reduced to eight minutes with this new technology. This advancement makes the real-time reconstruction of observations of powerful black hole jets, such as J1924 and 3C279, a reality. In fusion energy studies, researchers at Pacific Fusion are using the cuPhoton.xLens module to gain real-time insights during plasma simulations. This system integrates with a local AI agent to dynamically steer reactor design processes.

For drug discovery and molecular simulations, the cuPotential library accelerates existing software by up to 3x, improving calculation stability and accuracy. NVIDIA is developing this technology in collaboration with communities such as Schrödinger, AMBER, NAMD, GROMACS, LAMMPS, and the Open Molecular Software Foundation. In materials science, Daviator accelerates quantum-mechanical calculations for the Vienna Ab initio Simulation Package by up to 22x while reducing costs by up to 75%. The DAQIRI library, used at Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory, connects electron microscopy data directly to GPUs, enabling analysis during the experiment itself.

In biology, the open-source cuSpa library accelerates cell polygon calculations by 900x compared to CPUs, supporting research at Memorial Sloan Kettering Cancer Center. Furthermore, the rapids-singlecell framework is used by Therna Biosciences for 200x faster data processing, while the HMMER library accelerates protein sequence analysis by up to 84x at Basecamp Research. This technological infrastructure shortens the path from scientific data to biological insights, shaping the discoveries of the future.
