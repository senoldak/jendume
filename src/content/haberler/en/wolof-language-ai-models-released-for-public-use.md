The Wolof language, spoken by approximately 16 million people in West Africa, now has a stronger presence in digital tools. Five new AI models capable of understanding and writing in Wolof have been released for public use. Built upon the foundation of the Qwen3 architecture, these models are available on the Hugging Face platform and can be used free of charge for non-commercial purposes. This release expands digital inclusivity and access to technology for underrepresented linguistic groups.

The development process involved training the models on public datasets that were automatically translated into Wolof. Ranging from 0.6 billion to 14 billion parameters, these models were fine-tuned to ensure they can effectively comprehend and generate text in the language. To maintain high standards, Wolof-speaking annotators conducted rigorous quality checks, performing blind, arena-style comparisons to rate the accuracy and naturalness of the models' responses.

This initiative represents a commitment to making technology accessible to diverse populations. The project was initially announced in late 2024, with the team eventually selecting the Qwen3 architecture due to its superior performance for their specific language-modeling requirements. By launching these models, the organization has also joined a broader AI language partnership, which aims to provide AI access to 3.4 billion people in their native languages.

Looking ahead, the roadmap includes developing similar voice and text support for other languages such as Pulaar, Darija, Dyula, and Mossi. These efforts work toward bridging the digital divide in West Africa and ensuring that local languages are integrated into the evolving AI ecosystem.
