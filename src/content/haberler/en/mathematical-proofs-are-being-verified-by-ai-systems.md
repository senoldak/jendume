The most complex and lengthy proofs in the world of mathematics are being digitized and verified by AI-driven systems. A recent breakthrough successfully verified a 15,000-page work widely considered the longest mathematical proof ever written by humans. During this process, AI utilized a complex network structure to break down the proof into manageable milestones, independently verifying each step.

Why does this matter? Verifying mathematical proofs is a process that pushes the limits of human cognition. Combining results from hundreds of articles spanning decades creates a workload that exceeds what any single mathematician can absorb. By translating this vast web of knowledge into code, AI not only identifies errors but also establishes a reliable foundation for future mathematical research.

The initiative also included the formal verification of a 7,500-page project, which serves as a cornerstone for modern algebraic geometry. For this project, a hierarchical system of specialized AI agents was designed to audit thousands of interlocking definitions and theorems in parallel. While the first proof was expressed in 43 million lines of code, the second project resulted in a 16-million-line code library.

This achievement is viewed as a turning point for the future of mathematics. Mathematicians can now access this verified, massive library to build new theorems. This capability of AI does more than just preserve past work; it provides a new digital infrastructure that will accelerate human mathematical discovery. Experts suggest that this development represents one of the most significant steps toward formalizing all of mathematics, offering a launchpad for humans to explore and understand mathematics in innovative ways.
