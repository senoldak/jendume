Neuralink has advanced its brain-computer interface technology by developing new software prototypes designed to integrate into everyday communication. According to the company, engineers conducted a seventy-two hour intensive session with a speech restoration study participant to test various communication enhancements. Among the tested features is the ability to route speech generated by the implant directly into an iPhone phone call audio stream. The mobile application, named Bloom, connects the brain-computer interface output to the call's microphone feed to deliver clear and consistent audio to the recipient.

The team also experimented with an optional greeting message at the beginning of calls to inform the listener that the caller is using an assistive communication device. Additionally, the engineers explored automatic detection of speech intent and early integrations with voice-enabled AI assistants. These prototypes represent early exploratory steps toward making neural signals practical tools for daily interaction. The mobile application itself has been under development over the past few months, benefiting from real-world usage feedback with the trial participant.

The company emphasizes that these features are not yet finished products available for general use, and the hardware remains strictly experimental. Research and development activities rely heavily on clinical trial participants, while regulatory approvals and long-term reliability assessments are ongoing. Engineers also prepared real-time visualisations of signal intensities to provide feedback to the participant, while Grok integration offered an early exploration of triggering agents through thought.
