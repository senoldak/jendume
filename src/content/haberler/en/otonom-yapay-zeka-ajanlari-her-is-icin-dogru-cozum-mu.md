Artificial intelligence agents are rapidly becoming ubiquitous in the enterprise, handling everything from writing code to customer communications. However, the risks inherent in agentic behavior are grabbing headlines just as much as their success stories. Built on large language models, these systems are fundamentally nondeterministic.

While traditional software always produces predictable results for given inputs, AI agents rely on probability to make decisions. This unpredictability gives them their unique power and agency, but it also opens the door to undesirable actions and costly failures. Experts argue that deploying agents for every business process is a flawed strategy.

Organizations must weigh the risks by calculating error budgets and ensuring that expected business benefits vastly outweigh potential costs of failure. As the initial hype settles, the future of AI agents will likely rely on using them sparingly and purposefully alongside traditional, deterministic software.
