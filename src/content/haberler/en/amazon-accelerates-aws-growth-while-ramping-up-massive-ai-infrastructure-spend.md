Amazon is doubling down on its artificial intelligence infrastructure, with its cloud computing division AWS showing significant momentum. Ahead of its third-quarter earnings report, analysts expect AWS sales to grow by 38%, surpassing the 37% gain recorded in the previous quarter. This would mark the fifth consecutive quarter of revenue acceleration for the cloud unit. CEO Andy Jassy highlighted the surging demand for AI computing power, stating that the company's cloud capacity is largely sold out for next year, with substantial commitments already secured for 2028.

To meet this unprecedented demand, Amazon is significantly increasing its capital expenditure. The company recently raised its 2026 spending forecast to $220 billion, up from an earlier estimate of $200 billion. Market analysts predict this figure could climb above $280 billion by 2027. While these investments are crucial for building the data centers required for AI, they are impacting the company's liquidity. Amazon reported a negative free cash flow of $7.6 billion for the twelve months ending in July, a sharp reversal from the $18.2 billion positive cash flow seen a year earlier. Estimates suggest a further cash outflow of $9.9 billion in the current quarter.

While investing heavily in hardware, Amazon continues to streamline its corporate operations. The company has laid off more than 57,000 employees since 2022, representing about 16% of its corporate workforce. Most recently, it cut fewer than 250 positions in its device software and services team to align with its product roadmap. Strategically, Amazon is also protecting its ecosystem by blocking third-party AI agents like Meta's Muse and Perplexity from scraping its store. Instead, it is promoting its own AI assistant, Alexa for Shopping, which has reached over 400 million users and seen an eightfold increase in the use of its agentic features since the start of the year.
