Apple's executive leadership is intensifying pressure on its software division to accelerate the company's artificial intelligence plans. Recent reports indicate that the CEO has held closed-door meetings with the software chief to address concerns that Apple's current pace is failing to match the urgency displayed by its rivals. While Apple has focused on incremental improvements to Siri, there is now a clear directive to move beyond these limitations and develop more advanced, agentic capabilities.

Currently, Siri operates primarily on a request-based model. However, Apple aims to transition toward a system capable of handling ongoing tasks, tracking goals, and maintaining continuity between conversations—features already present in competing AI models. The company is also notably lacking a "computer-use" tool, a technology that allows AI to read screens, move cursors, and interact directly with applications, which is a key area where competitors have already established a foothold.

On the technical front, the iOS 27.3 beta release demonstrates Siri's growing ability to perform precise actions within Apple's own ecosystem, such as organizing Safari tabs, sorting files, and managing messages. Support for third-party applications is expected to arrive in the spring with iOS 27.5 or 27.6, which would enable Siri to perform tasks like booking tables or placing food orders through external services.

To facilitate this shift, Apple has restructured its development workflow for next year's operating systems, including iOS 28, iPadOS 28, and macOS 28. The development milestones, which traditionally split time equally between coding and integration, have been adjusted to provide engineers with six weeks of coding and two weeks of integration. This structural change is intended to streamline the development process and allow for faster feature deployment, signaling a broader effort within the company to adopt a more agile approach to AI innovation.
