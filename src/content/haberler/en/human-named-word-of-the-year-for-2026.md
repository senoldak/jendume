Cambridge Dictionary has officially named "human" as its word of the year for 2026. This selection highlights how artificial intelligence is not merely a technical advancement but a transformative force reshaping our language and our very definition of humanity. The dictionary defines "human" as possessing the qualities, faults, and feelings that people have, in contrast to gods, animals, or machines. The rapid rise of AI has forced a re-evaluation of this definition.

As AI-generated content permeates the digital landscape, the term "human-made" has gained significant traction. People are increasingly using this phrase to distinguish their own creative efforts from machine-generated output. This shift underscores how technology is altering our perception of what it means to be human. It reflects a growing public discourse regarding the risks of outsourcing our critical thinking to algorithms and the necessity of maintaining a clear boundary between human cognition and machine processing.

Dictionary editors noted that this year's shortlist clearly reflects the pervasive influence of AI. For instance, the term "agent"—used to describe AI systems capable of executing tasks without direct human supervision—was among the shortlisted words. These developments demonstrate that technology is influencing not only our workflows but also how we conceptualize the world around us.

Looking back at recent trends, the 2025 word of the year, "parasocial," highlighted the one-sided emotional bonds people were forming with AI chatbots. The selection of "human" for 2026 suggests that the conversation has evolved from focusing on emotional attachments to exploring the fundamental essence of humanity and the importance of preserving the distinction between the human and the artificial. As technology continues to advance, the question of what it means to remain "human" is becoming one of the most significant philosophical and practical debates of the digital age.
