Technology company Amazon has joined forces with X-energy and American Electric Power to secure the surging electricity demands of its growing data center infrastructure. Under the agreement, a new advanced nuclear energy facility will be established on the site of a former coal-fired power plant in the state of Indiana. Once operational, the facility is designed to supply up to 960 megawatts of zero-carbon electricity directly to the power grid and Amazon's data centers.

The core of the project involves the deployment of 12 small modular reactors in the Fairbanks area within Sullivan County. Developed by X-energy, these units belong to the fourth-generation high-temperature gas reactor class, serving as an innovative alternative to traditional large-scale nuclear plants. While licensing and development processes continue, each reactor unit is targeted to deliver an 80 MWe power capacity.

A particularly notable feature of these reactor designs is their flexible cooling architecture. The units can utilize air, water, or a hybrid combination of both for cooling purposes. This adaptability ensures that the reactors can be operated even in regions with restricted water resources, minimizing any potential strain on local community water supplies.

This initiative in Indiana marks the second collaborative nuclear energy project between Amazon and X-energy, following a previous announcement regarding a similar facility planned in Washington state. Through ongoing partnerships with regional electric utility providers, the companies collectively aim to bring over 5 gigawatts of new nuclear energy capacity online across the United States.
