As artificial intelligence technologies integrate into daily life, chatbots and autonomous agents are increasingly adopting human-like faces and avatars. Various technological systems utilize facial features to help users feel more comfortable with their presence. According to Pew Research Center data, about half of adults in the United States use AI chatbots, and 38 percent of employed adults use them for tasks at work.

This trend brings up a lingering question: Does giving AI a face make it more acceptable, or does it simply build unearned trust? Ariel Hitron, CEO and co-founder of Second Nature, emphasizes that making people feel understood is far more important than physical appearance. Users already engage in personal conversations with conversational tools without requiring any specific visual interface on the screen. What truly matters is whether the AI comprehends the user and responds relevantly.

A human-like persona can help when practicing conversations on training platforms, but it also raises expectations. If the AI's expressions or timing feel unnatural, users may enter the uncanny valley, which can cause discomfort. While a face can draw someone into a conversation, the overall quality of the interaction keeps them engaged. Interestingly, human behavioral responses to these avatars reveal a strong tendency to treat them socially.

Tests and platform data show that users often laugh at AI avatar jokes and respond as they would in human conversations. Across tens of thousands of sessions on the platform, more than four out of five users thank the AI avatar. A third of them apologize to it, and nearly half say goodbye before ending the interaction. Experts stress that the industry must maintain transparency and ensure these systems are clearly labeled, as human social instincts activate regardless of whether the other side is truly human.
