Alexandr Wang, Meta’s Chief AI Officer and head of its Superintelligence Labs, has issued a stark warning regarding the trajectory of artificial intelligence and the potential for losing human control. Wang’s assessment follows a landmark achievement in mathematics: the proof of the Riemann Hypothesis, a problem that had resisted human solution since 1859. The proof was constructed using Anthropic’s Claude and certified by the Lean and Mathlib communities, marking what Wang considers a definitive turning point.

According to Wang, raw intelligence is no longer the primary barrier separating AI from the world's most capable humans. He argues that the remaining gaps are purely practical—involving specific knowledge, integration, and experimentation. Once these gaps are bridged, Wang expects AI to surpass humans in nearly every field, including the very research required to build AI. He compared the complexity of the Riemann Hypothesis to the development of the atomic bomb, suggesting the former is a far more significant intellectual milestone.

The primary concern for Wang is the issue of control. He finds it difficult to envision a scenario where humans remain in charge of systems that are 1,000 times smarter than themselves or capable of recursive self-improvement. He criticized the industry's tendency to view superintelligence as inevitable, urging for immediate and rigorous work on safety, alignment, and security rather than treating them as secondary problems.

This sentiment is echoed by other leaders in the field. OpenAI Chief Scientist Jakub Pachocki recently proposed shared limits on AI development based on the industry's ability to interpret its models. Meanwhile, US Senator Bernie Sanders is advocating for legislation that would ban superintelligence outright. Currently, the proof that sparked this debate exists as a 17-million-line Lean library verified by machines, though a human-readable version has yet to be produced.
