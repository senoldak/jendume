The UK Security Service MI5 has established a specialized unit called the AI Threat Assessment Centre to address growing security risks associated with advanced technology. Announcing the initiative at headquarters in London, security officials emphasized that humanity is facing actors capable of out-thinking human levels for the first time in history. The new center brings together experts from various cybersecurity and law enforcement agencies to provide the government with a unified assessment of intelligence.

Officials noted that artificial intelligence is already supercharging cyber conflicts, enabling targeted propaganda, and being exploited by bad actors for reconnaissance purposes. While current concerns focus heavily on cyber attacks and misinformation, intelligence leaders warned about the potential future misuse of technology in relation to advanced biological threats and drone capabilities.

Concurrently, a parliamentary hearing brought executives from major artificial intelligence companies before lawmakers to discuss safety standards. Representatives from leading labs faced questions regarding voluntary safeguards, unforeseen autonomous agent behaviors, and safety incidents that led to delayed model releases. Former researchers from prominent AI labs voiced concerns about internal safety cultures and the adequacy of risk management as capabilities scale rapidly.

In response to these growing challenges, the parliamentary committee announced a new inquiry focused entirely on artificial intelligence safety and regulatory frameworks. Intelligence leaders also advised technology startups to expect foreign espionage and strengthen their cybersecurity protocols, highlighting a broad governmental push to adapt to the realities of advanced technologies.
