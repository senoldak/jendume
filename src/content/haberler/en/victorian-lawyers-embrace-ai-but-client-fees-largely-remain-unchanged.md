More than half of Victoria's lawyers have reported using artificial intelligence in their professional work, according to a recent study. Conducted by the Victorian Legal Services Board, the 2026 census of 2,221 lawyers found that adoption rates surged from 37% to 55% over the past year. AI utilization was highest in trade practices, banking, finance, and information technology sectors, while seeing lower adoption in criminal and family law.

Despite the rapid integration of AI tools, the survey revealed that efficiency gains have not translated into widespread fee reductions for clients. Only 26% of lawyers stated that AI usage resulted in lower fees, with price cuts being more common among corporate clients rather than individuals. Researchers noted that business clients are better positioned to negotiate lower prices due to their own familiarity with AI technologies.

Furthermore, researchers pointed out that efficiency gains were often offset by the time required to verify AI outputs and correct errors. A significant challenge emerged as clients increasingly relied on AI for their own legal research, with many bringing inaccurate information or AI-generated documents that required correction by legal professionals.

Nearly half of the surveyed lawyers reported that client use of AI actually increased the time and effort required on cases. As courts and regulatory bodies across Australia grapple with these technological shifts, the legal sector continues to navigate the complex balance between embracing automation and ensuring accuracy.
