Anthropic is actively figuring out how autonomous AI agents could negotiate on behalf of users. According to industry reports, the company is consulting with law firms to develop fair negotiation norms for AI agents that would handle price haggling, resolve complaints, and improve contract offers. Previous experiments conducted by the company demonstrated that stronger models tend to secure better deals, raising concerns that this capability could widen societal inequality.

This initiative builds upon the company's existing efforts to bridge the gap between technical metrics and human judgment. Anthropic has been hiring Conceptual Reasoning Fellows, specifically qualified lawyers, to test Claude on judgment-heavy questions across law, philosophy, cognitive science, economics, and political science.

The strategic push fundamentally stems from a data shortage in the humanities. While improving AI models in math and coding is relatively straightforward due to abundant verifiable data, humanities data lacks clear-cut right and wrong answers. To overcome this, Anthropic needs human experts who can build robust test cases and evaluations to make Claude stronger in nuanced fields.

Justin Curl, a member of the technical staff researching law, institutions, and AI at Anthropic, points out that defining quality in legal contexts is notoriously difficult. As AI systems evolve to participate in complex human transactions, setting foundational norms and rigorous testing frameworks remains a critical priority for responsible development.
