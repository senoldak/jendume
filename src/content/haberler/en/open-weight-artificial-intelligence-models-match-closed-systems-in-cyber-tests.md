Vals, a company that measures AI capabilities, has tested how effectively different AI models can execute complex, multi-step network instructions across mock corporate networks. The findings reveal that the performance gap between restricted laboratory models and open-source models available to the public is rapidly closing.

The research suggests that closed models no longer hold a definitive edge in cyber skills. This challenges the strategy of frontier labs that have restricted access to new models in an effort to slow the proliferation of advanced hacking capabilities. Previously, leading labs argued that gated access would buy valuable time for security defenders to prepare.

During testing across simulated corporate networks, OpenAI's advanced cyber model Daybreak completed 81 percent of assigned tasks, while the Chinese open-weight model DeepSeek followed closely behind by completing 73 percent of objectives. The research also highlighted significant cost differences; running an attack using open-weight alternatives costs substantially less than utilizing proprietary frontier models due to the ability to run them on local hardware.

While experts continue to debate whether AI fundamentally alters the nature of cybersecurity defense or simply accelerates existing threats, the rise of capable open models changes how developers and organizations must approach security. Although real-world criminals often rely on older tools, the reality that open systems can match proprietary tech underscores a shifting security landscape.
