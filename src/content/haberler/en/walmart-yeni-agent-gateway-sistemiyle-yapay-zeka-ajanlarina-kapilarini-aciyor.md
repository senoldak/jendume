Retail giant Walmart has announced the launch of its Agent Gateway and new features designed to support customers using AI agents for online shopping. This move sharply contrasts with other online businesses and travel sites, which have recently begun blocking AI agents from making purchases on behalf of shoppers. Walmart, however, is opening its doors to allow approved agents to browse inventory and complete transactions securely as part of its broader strategy to prepare for mainstream autonomous commerce.

The new technology utilizes a model context protocol server to help agents connect to Walmart securely. Originally created by Anthropic, this protocol allows an AI agent to search online inventory and make purchases without requiring the user to share personal information or purchase history directly with the agent. Customers will also be able to differentiate agent-initiated purchases from their own shopping activities.

Starting next month, the company will roll out Agent Profiles, enabling shoppers to authorize each AI agent with a dedicated account profile. Users will maintain full control by either requiring approval for every individual transaction or setting specific spending limits, similar to adding an authorized user to a credit card account. Additionally, Walmart has established clear terms of service for AI agents, specifying that they must not take actions beyond user intent, cannot access payment details, and must comply with standard return processes. Agents that fail to follow these rules will be blocked from accessing the site, providing a safer environment for consumers experimenting with agentic commerce.
