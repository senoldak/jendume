Deepseek V4.1 Flash reached 72.8 percent of targets in the CyberOffense test developed by Vals AI, which simulates realistic corporate networks. In these evaluations, AI agents attempt to infiltrate target networks from an isolated attack machine without any human assistance, and every action is automatically scored.

According to the test results, OpenAI's restricted Daybreak cyber program running GPT-6 Sol achieved the highest score at 80.8 percent. However, the openly accessible Deepseek V4.1 Flash stood out with its accessibility and a running cost of approximately 3 dollars, compared to about 22 dollars for the leading model.

Research findings indicate that the cybersecurity capability gap between open-weight and closed frontier AI models is rapidly shrinking. According to analysis by the UK AI Safety Institute, this gap has dropped to four to seven months, down from longer periods previously.

Execution time and token consumption also played a critical role in the evaluations. Deepseek V4.1 Flash produced no results in the first 25 million tokens, recording most of its progress after 100 million tokens. This demonstrates that short tests may fail to reflect the true potential of open models.

Experts note that such AI tools present both risks and significant opportunities for organizations to identify and patch security vulnerabilities early. Cybersecurity-focused AI usage and similar evaluations are expected to increase in the coming months.
