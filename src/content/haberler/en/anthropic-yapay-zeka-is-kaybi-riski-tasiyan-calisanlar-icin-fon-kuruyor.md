Artificial intelligence company Anthropic is launching a new public benefit fund designed to address potential job market disruptions. The fund will explore making direct payouts to certain populations of workers who face the risk of losing their jobs due to rapid advancements in artificial intelligence. Shareholders still need to approve the initiative, which would also allow for large donations to traditional nonprofit organizations once greenlit.

Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei has repeatedly warned about the social and economic upheaval that fast-moving AI systems could cause. The company previously committed 200 million dollars to research the impacts on the labor market. Given the pace at which technology is evolving, company leadership argues that bolder approaches are now required. The fund could potentially receive additional shares as the company's valuation rises above set baselines over time.

Structurally, the fund is set up as a wholly owned limited liability company subsidiary rather than a tax-exempt nonprofit, giving Anthropic more flexibility in how funds are distributed. The company has promised that the capital will not be used for lobbying or political donations either domestically or abroad. Further details regarding valuation triggers and governance are expected to be announced in the coming months.

This announcement comes as Anthropic prepares for a major initial public offering. Recent presentations in San Francisco indicate that the upcoming IPO could raise up to 100 billion dollars and value the company at approximately 2 trillion dollars. While competitors like Google rely on traditional corporate philanthropy, Anthropic's inclusion of potential direct payments goes a step further in addressing economic displacement. Meanwhile, Elon Musk responded to the news by suggesting alternative distribution models involving SpaceX shares.
