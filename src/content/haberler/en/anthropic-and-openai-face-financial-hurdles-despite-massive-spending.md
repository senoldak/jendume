As Wall Street prepares for what could become historic initial public offerings, the financial realities of frontier AI labs are drawing intense scrutiny. While SpaceX has already gone public, Anthropic and OpenAI are expected to follow, though investors increasingly question whether these labs can generate enough value to justify their astronomical spending.

The scale of expenditure is staggering. Anthropic lost more than 8 billion dollars last year despite generating 4.6 billion dollars in revenue, and has committed over 500 billion dollars to computing power and artificial intelligence infrastructure. Meanwhile, OpenAI expects its revenue to surge from 36 billion dollars to 350 billion dollars by 2030, while still projecting a cumulative cash burn of nearly 280 billion dollars over the same period.

History suggests that inventors of breakthrough technologies rarely capture the largest share of value. Much like electricity and the internet in past centuries, the greatest economic gains often accrue to those who build complementary assets, products, and platforms on top of the new foundation. As AI models become commoditized infrastructure, chipmakers, cloud providers, and specialized businesses with proprietary data stand to capture significant value.

Ultimately, the race to build frontier artificial intelligence is producing extraordinary technology, but the biggest commercial winners may be companies that learn to reinvent themselves around this new foundation rather than simply building the models themselves.
