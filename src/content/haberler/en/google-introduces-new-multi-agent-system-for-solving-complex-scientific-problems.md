Google has unveiled "Antigravity /teamwork," a new multi-agent system designed to tackle long-standing scientific challenges in fields like mathematics, chemistry, and software engineering. Built upon the Gemini 4 model family—including Argon, Carbon, and Hydrogen—this system enhances human-AI collaboration to solve open problems that have remained unsolved for years.

The core innovation of /teamwork lies in its ability to partition complex tasks into specialized, manageable segments. Traditional language models often struggle with long-horizon tasks, losing track of details or producing unpredictable results. Antigravity /teamwork addresses this by assigning specific files and tools to dedicated agents, ensuring that each step of a process is focused and verifiable. The system employs "falsifiers," such as the Lean 4 proof checker, to automatically verify candidate solutions against physical measurements or logical constraints.

This methodology was recently demonstrated through the successful proof of the Courtade–Kumar conjecture in information theory. By collaborating with the system, researchers generated a formal proof consisting of 50 million lines of code. This achievement highlights the system's capacity to move beyond simple text generation, enabling it to construct rigorous mathematical proofs and optimize complex software systems.

This development marks a significant shift in scientific research. Researchers can now define theoretical constraints and allow multi-agent systems to explore large search spaces, test designs, and verify logic chains. This interactive discovery loop combines human expertise with machine efficiency, accelerating the pace of scientific innovation. By making these agentic tools available to external researchers and developers, Google aims to foster a collaborative ecosystem where complex problems are solved through the synergy of human and artificial intelligence.
