As digital scams and cybercrime continue to scale globally, automated defense mechanisms are leveraging artificial intelligence to turn the tables on fraudsters. Australian company Apate has built a vast network of AI bots designed to mimic potential victims, trapping phone scammers in lengthy conversations and infiltrating scam chat groups to collect real-time intelligence.

The system utilizes hundreds of thousands of bots with diverse personalities and conversational traits, making it difficult for scammers to realize they are talking to software. By keeping fraudsters occupied for hours on end—and gathering critical data such as money mule accounts and fraudulent URLs—the platform drains their operational resources and prevents them from targeting actual humans.

Researchers at institutions like ETH Zurich are also incorporating advanced language models into virtual security traps to keep automated cyber attackers engaged significantly longer. By exploiting the psychological patterns of scammers, these new defensive tools are shifting the paradigm in digital security.
