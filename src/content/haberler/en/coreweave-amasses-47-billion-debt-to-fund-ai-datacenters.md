CoreWeave, a major provider of artificial intelligence computing infrastructure, has accumulated $47 billion in debt as it expands its global network of 52 data centers. The former crypto-mining business has transformed into a neocloud provider, renting graphics processing units (GPUs) to major AI developers including OpenAI and Anthropic. However, this aggressive expansion has placed the company under severe financial scrutiny. During the most recent quarter, CoreWeave generated $3.6 billion in revenue but recorded a net loss of $709 million. Creditors recently priced its risk of default over the next five years at more than 60 percent, with over $10 billion of its debt coming due next year. The company's financing relies heavily on GPU-backed debt, where Nvidia chips and customer contracts serve as collateral for loans. Nvidia, which holds roughly an 11 percent stake in CoreWeave, plays a central role in propping up this model. In addition to investing directly, Nvidia has guaranteed to buy unsold compute capacity worth billions of dollars. This circular financial arrangement allows AI developers to access massive infrastructure without building facilities themselves, shifting the capital risk onto specialized providers. The business model faces significant headwinds as hardware depreciates quickly compared to traditional real estate assets. As interest payments near $1 billion per quarter, CoreWeave stands as a primary indicator for the stability and risks of the ongoing AI infrastructure boom.
