London-headquartered payments technology company Paysend has adopted a selective approach to artificial intelligence deployment. CEO Ben Chisell believes that simply handing out the latest AI tools to every employee can sometimes magnify poor habits like laziness or weak judgment. Consequently, Paysend requires staff to earn access to AI tools, often reserving them for top-performing employees who have proven what they can achieve with them.

While many financial and technology firms are rolling out AI licenses to tens of thousands of employees at once—such as Lloyds Banking Group and Grant Thornton UK—recent government research indicates that increased productivity does not always translate into higher revenue. Like Chisell, other industry leaders argue that AI should be used to expand ambition rather than merely cut costs or reduce team sizes.

At Paysend, new hires are given a month to experiment with AI tools and demonstrate their tangible contributions. Through this disciplined approach, Chisell estimates the company can deliver around 25 percent more engineering work with the same-sized team. For instance, when a new search engine optimization lead requested Claude, a month of testing revealed he only needed it for faster brief creation, prompting him to build a dedicated content-brief agent instead of using a general-purpose tool.

As businesses navigate the integration of automated systems into daily operations, leaders emphasize that technology should never replace human responsibility. Maintaining human ownership ensures that AI serves as a powerful amplifier of talent rather than a superficial fix for complex organizational challenges.
