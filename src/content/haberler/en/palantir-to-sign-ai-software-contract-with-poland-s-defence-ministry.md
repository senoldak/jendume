Poland's defence ministry will conclude an agreement with Palantir Technologies to acquire software utilizing artificial intelligence to assist armed forces in decision-making processes. The official signing ceremony is scheduled to take place at the Polish Army Museum in Warsaw, involving the company's local subsidiary. The procurement aims to enhance the operational capabilities of military units by integrating automated data processing into critical defense operations. The software will provide structural decision support across various branches of the military.

This upcoming agreement represents the initial binding phase of a collaborative framework that originated with a cybersecurity pact in December 2024. Defence leadership and corporate executives previously endorsed a formal statement of intent encompassing artificial intelligence, information technology, and cybersecurity following extensive testing procedures that commenced in December 2023 across multiple military branches. These preliminary evaluations included numerous meetings, exercises, and system demonstrations to validate the platform's reliability.

Officials chose not to disclose the financial valuation or specific operational units designated for deployment, and the initiative has encountered domestic scrutiny. Civil society organizations and privacy advocates have formally petitioned national human rights authorities regarding data protection standards, prompting administrative inquiries. In response to public debates, defense authorities emphasized that NATO forces and Ukrainian armed institutions already utilize similar technological solutions within their operations.

Poland's strategic direction diverges from several European partners, as nations like France and Germany pursue regional alternatives to the software, and the Netherlands seeks independent technical solutions within a two-year timeframe. Concurrently, Warsaw continues to strengthen bilateral defense technology cooperation with Washington through joint research initiatives, further aligning its military technological ecosystem with allied standards.
