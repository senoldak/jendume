Nobel Prize-winning computer scientist Geoffrey Hinton has warned that the uncontrolled development of artificial intelligence could lead to a Chernobyl-style energy infrastructure disaster. He stated that autonomous AI agents could launch cyberattacks on government and corporate systems, potentially paralyzing global financial systems or power grids. Hinton emphasized that such an event would serve as a wake-up call for world leaders and the public regarding the dangerous power of this technology. He suggests that a massive brownout caused by an AI-powered attack could force society to finally pay attention to the risks involved. While he clarifies that he is not an AI doomer, he consistently warns that the threats of job loss and engineered cyberattacks are becoming increasingly salient as rogue hacking sprees demonstrate the technology's potential for harm.

Separately, Anthropic is preparing for an initial public offering on the Nasdaq while facing growing public backlash against data centers. Although the company is targeting a valuation between $1 trillion and $1.5 trillion, this figure is lower than the $2 trillion target previously floated. The negative sentiment from voters toward AI data centers in recent US elections creates a new risk factor for investors. With local communities opposing data center-related ballot measures in 40 out of 41 cases, the public's distrust is becoming a significant hurdle. More than half of voters indicated that data centers were a crucial factor in their political support. As it moves toward becoming a public company, Anthropic will need to navigate this public pressure against the profit-driven approaches of tech giants. While becoming a public company will force greater transparency, industry analysts suggest the company will face heightened scrutiny regarding its infrastructure footprint.
