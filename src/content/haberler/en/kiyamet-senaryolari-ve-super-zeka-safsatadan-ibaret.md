Michael I. Jordan, widely regarded as a foundational figure in machine learning, has pushed back hard against the prevailing doom rhetoric surrounding artificial intelligence. In a recent critique, the UC Berkeley professor emeritus and Inria researcher called warnings about existential threats pure science fiction.

Jordan criticized tech companies calling for regulations as engaging in theater to maintain control while projecting a virtuous image. He also dismissed the panic surrounding young researchers resigning over apocalyptic scenarios, describing their actions as naive performances disconnected from rigorous engineering discipline.

Addressing claims about superintelligence and brain emulation, Jordan argued that such hypotheses lack credible steps and belong strictly in the realm of fiction. He further cautioned that massive data center investments represent an enormous gamble rather than a guaranteed path to technological breakthroughs.
