USA Co., the largest newspaper chain in the United States, has partnered with data analytics giant Palantir to better monetize its audience and content through automated tools, including push-button videos and AI-written article summaries. However, this aggressive push toward automation has triggered significant resistance from newsroom employees and the NewsGuild.

NewsGuild-CWA president Jon Schleuss points out that replacing working journalists with automated systems undermines the ethical responsibilities inherent to a free press. Tensions escalated after a newly deployed reader-facing chatbot misidentified a murder case reporter as a suspect. Guild members at USA papers have demanded an immediate end to the Palantir deal, calling for contractual protections against AI-driven layoffs and errors.

While company CEO Mike Reed stated that journalists will not be replaced by AI, employees note that this promise has not yet been formalized in their contracts. Amid ongoing layoffs across the media landscape, the upheaval has fueled a massive organizing surge within the union, reminiscent of its founding era during the Great Depression.

Industry observers emphasize that the core issue revolves around reader trust. Without rigorous human fact-checking and adherence to journalistic ethics, reliance on automated word calculators risks exacerbating misinformation and eroding the foundation of a democratic society.
