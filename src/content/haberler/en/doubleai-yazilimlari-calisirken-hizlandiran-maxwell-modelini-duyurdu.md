Tel Aviv-based startup doubleAI, co-founded by Amnon Shashua, has announced its new AI model called Maxwell and opened it for early access, positioning it as the first of a new category known as code execution models. While traditional coding agents write source code, Maxwell monitors running programs and rewrites the underlying machine code to fit the hardware and data it encounters. The original source code remains completely unchanged throughout the entire process.

The system operates on both CPUs and GPUs without requiring human review for the changes made while the code runs. According to the company, Maxwell made data pipelines more than 38 times faster on real-world workloads and accelerated AI inference kernels by more than 4.5 times, while cutting one early customer's compute costs by 67%. Additionally, LTX, a company building open-weight multimodal models, found that Maxwell doubled the speed of its model inference.

doubleAI plans to charge customers a share of their compute savings. The startup expects major AI labs with massive computing bills, such as OpenAI and Anthropic, to benefit the most, noting that even a small percentage saving could amount to hundreds of millions of dollars for them. Meanwhile, renting computing power continues to become more expensive as market prices rise steadily.

The two-year-old company, which has about 40 employees, has raised a total of $220 million, including a $200 million Series A round co-led by Lightspeed and Bessemer Venture Partners. The startup also allocated roughly half of its total funding to acquire 1,000 advanced chips for its internal operational needs and long-term development.
