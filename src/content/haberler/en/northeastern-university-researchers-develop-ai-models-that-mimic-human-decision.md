Researchers at Northeastern University have examined how artificial intelligence models can replicate human decision-making processes, flaws and all. The study demonstrated that AI agents can produce much more realistic simulations by modeling human responses. Historical events, such as panic buying during past shortages, show how individual human choices can trigger large-scale disruptions across supply chains, prompting researchers to seek better predictive tools.

The research team tested how AI models made decisions in supply chain simulation games. Agents that previously made unusual choices were given cognitive grounding reflecting human psychology and demand estimation tendencies. As a result, the models produced outcomes closely mirroring human behavior. The experiments proved that AI can be guided not just by rigid mathematical rules, but also by human-like feelings of pressure and uncertainty.

The study was conducted across 231 sessions, observing that the AI bots moved significantly closer to the human benchmark. Such simulations offer major advantages for policymakers and businesses. Crisis scenarios that would be risky or costly to test in the real world can be safely evaluated beforehand using artificial intelligence, helping officials test interventions such as price shifts or transit changes.

The findings also pave the way for making the collective behavior of AI agents more human-like, helping developers better understand and guide complex systems. By improving simulation accuracy, researchers aim to prevent costly real-world mistakes and design better systems for society.
