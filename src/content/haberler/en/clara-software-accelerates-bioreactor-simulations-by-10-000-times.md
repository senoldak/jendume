Researchers at Graz University of Technology have introduced CLARA, an AI-supported software toolbox designed to revolutionize the control and simulation of industrial bioreactors. In large-scale bioreactors, even the smallest variations in temperature, pH levels, or oxygen content can determine the success or failure of a process. While traditional computational fluid dynamics (CFD) methods provide highly accurate results, they are computationally intensive, often taking months to simulate processes that occur over days or weeks. CLARA addresses this by accelerating these simulations by up to 10,000 times, significantly boosting industrial efficiency.

At the core of CLARA is an AI-supported clustering process that utilizes unsupervised machine learning methods. The system automatically groups millions of data points into a manageable number of interconnected zones, ranging between five and 50. This automation transforms complex modeling tasks, which previously required extensive manual expertise, into mathematically precise operations. The software maintains high accuracy even in complex multiphase flows where solid, liquid, and gaseous materials are mixed.

A critical feature of this approach is its focus on physical consistency. While simplified models often suffer from inaccuracies in mass balance, CLARA's algorithms guarantee constant mass conservation. This ensures that vital processes, such as identifying dead zones with insufficient mixing or modeling species exchange between gas and liquid phases, remain realistic and reliable.

The research team highlights that the potential applications for CLARA are diverse, ranging from reactors for CO₂ capture to processes in battery cell production. The technology holds significant potential for developing digital twins capable of mapping and optimizing processes in real time. CLARA is now available as an open-source tool in a public repository, and it is already being utilized in both doctoral research and industrial applications.
