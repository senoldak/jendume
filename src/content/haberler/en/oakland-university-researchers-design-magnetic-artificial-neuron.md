Researchers at Oakland University have designed a new electronic neuron that mimics the operational principles of biological neurons. Unlike conventional electronic neurons, this design functions naturally through the physical dynamics of magnetic properties, eliminating the need for complex control circuitry. The system is built by pairing a standard electronic transistor with a nanoscale magnetic tunnel junction (MTJ). This approach is based on a "CMOS+X" architecture, where hardware does not merely store data but actively participates in the computational process.

Modern AI models face significant efficiency challenges due to their reliance on massive data centers and high energy consumption. The method proposed by the Oakland University team aims to enable computers to operate with significantly less power. While the human brain functions on approximately 20 watts of power, a typical AI model requires hundreds of times that amount for inference. By mimicking biological systems, researchers envision a future where AI can operate locally on devices without needing to rely on massive data centers.

Magnetic tunnel junctions are already widely used in magnetoresistive random access memory (MRAM) technologies. The researchers utilized the ability of magnetic layers in these devices to change orientation via current flow to generate the short electrical pulses, or "spikes," that biological neurons use for communication. The transistor controls the current flowing through the magnetic device, creating voltage pulses through resistance changes.

In simulations, these magnetic neurons successfully demonstrated neuron-like behaviors such as threshold spiking, latency, and synaptic integration. The research team reported that the network offers response latencies on the nanosecond scale and successfully completed standard machine learning benchmarks, such as the XOR gate. While the work is currently simulation-based, future steps involve physical fabrication and laboratory testing. This technology offers a potential roadmap for power-constrained applications, such as industrial sensors, wearable devices, and robotic systems, where data often arrives intermittently.
