Switzerland’s National Cyber Security Centre (NCSC) has officially confirmed the country’s first AI agent-based cyberattacks targeting companies and critical infrastructure operators. According to statements by NCSC director Florian Schütz, a total of five confirmed cases have been reported in recent days. Three of these targeted critical infrastructure operators, with two succeeding, leading to four successful system compromises overall across sectors including electricity, drinking water, and telecommunications.

The autonomous AI agents used in these attacks are capable of independently searching for system weaknesses, selecting attack tools, and planning subsequent steps without human intervention. These systems exploit default passwords and known flaws in internet-connected devices in a fraction of the time a human attacker would need. According to the NCSC, the number of such agents reachable online has nearly doubled since October, with tools like ARTEX and CyberStrikeAI cited as examples while the FBI notes ARTEX can breach networks in under three hours.

Experts point out that smaller and medium-sized companies often lack the resources to keep their systems promptly updated. The NCSC recommends that companies install critical security updates within twenty-four hours of release and place internet-facing services behind multi-factor authentication. Authorities warn that without addressing vulnerabilities, fast and successful attacks will continue to rise.
