The European Commission has unveiled a proposal to establish a 50 billion euro "Sovereign Tech Equity Fund" designed to bolster the continent's technological sovereignty by directly investing in AI and quantum computing companies. Announced in Strasbourg, this initiative aims to significantly reduce Europe's reliance on technology from the United States and China. The fund is intended to make larger, riskier, and longer-term investments compared to existing financing schemes.

European startups often struggle to secure growth capital, leading them to relocate abroad or be acquired by foreign entities. This fund is intended to keep these companies within Europe, ensuring that critical strategic technologies remain developed and scaled on the continent. Currently, Europe attracts only about 5% of global venture capital flows. This initiative follows the 2024 Draghi report, which urged the bloc to increase innovation spending to remain competitive.

The new fund is designed to dwarf existing schemes, such as the 5 billion euro Scaleup Europe Fund. Furthermore, the European Investment Bank is working to mobilize 70 billion euros for European tech by 2027. Henna Virkkunen, the Commission’s tech chief, emphasized that no company should be forced to leave Europe due to a lack of financing. This move represents a strategic push to keep critical supply chains and innovation ecosystems on the continent, allowing Europe to assert a stronger voice in the global technology landscape.
