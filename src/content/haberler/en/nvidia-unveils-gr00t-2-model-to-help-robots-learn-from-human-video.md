Nvidia introduced its new artificial intelligence model, GR00T 2, at the Conference on Robot Learning in Austin, Texas. The model is designed to help robots acquire delicate hand skills by learning partly from human instructional videos. Nvidia describes the technology as an open world action model capable of predicting how its actions will change its environment before it moves. The architecture relies on a 2-billion-parameter transformer built on Cosmos-Predict2.5-2B.

In company tests, GR00T 2 successfully performed 40 out of 50 tasks at least once without prior training. When shown a task once, the average success rate was 71 percent, and it reached 95 percent when shown five times. The training dataset includes 100,000 hours of first-person human footage, which Nvidia has released for public use. This allows robots to learn skills from humans and transfer them between robotic systems with different physical bodies.

Major industrial players such as Foxconn, Siemens, and Nexuni are currently testing the technology. Foxconn, which generates most of its revenue from AI servers, is utilizing the model to assist in building them. Siemens is evaluating its potential for factory operations, and Nexuni is testing it for laundry tasks, though none have committed to production use yet.

Alongside this release, Nvidia launched Isaac Lab 3.0, an open-source tool for training robots in simulation that trains up to 2.5 times faster in certain tests. Ken Goldberg, a robotics professor at University of California, Berkeley, noted that agentic robotics represents the most exciting technological development since the internet in 1995. Earlier iterations of GR00T are already set to power upcoming humanoids, such as LG's scheduled release.
