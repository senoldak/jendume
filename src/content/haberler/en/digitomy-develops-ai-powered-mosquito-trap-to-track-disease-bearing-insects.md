Digitomy, a startup originating from the University of South Florida, has engineered a smart trap to help mitigate virus outbreaks in regions facing severe public health threats. The smart system integrates patented artificial intelligence capabilities and has demonstrated over 98 percent accuracy in identifying mosquito species during rigorous laboratory evaluations. Research foundational to this system has progressed at the university since 2018. Conceived by Associate Professor Ryan Carney alongside Professor Sriram Chellappan, the mechanism utilizes machine intelligence to determine mosquito species, gender, and whether the insects have consumed blood. Integrated with cloud computing infrastructure and mapping interfaces, the platform assists public health authorities in monitoring vector populations for precise intervention.

Conventional mosquito monitoring relies heavily on manual evaluation by specialists under microscopes, a procedure that typically spans two to three days. The integrated intelligence within the device accelerates this workflow, allowing administrators to review trap contents on the same day. Positioned on low tree branches or ground level, the trap utilizes illumination or chemical attractants to lure insects, while an internal fan pulls them inside. Upon landing on an adhesive surface, embedded optical lenses capture images of the specimens, enabling the analytical software to classify the species against an extensive reference database. Laboratory computations indicate exceptional energy efficiency, consuming minimal electrical power to classify an entire adhesive card compared to standard digital sharing activities.

The academic team has advanced the system through strategic collaborations with regional vector control districts such as Anastasia and Hillsborough. Collected metrics have also been incorporated into specialized layers of a global observation dashboard, merging automated data with hundreds of thousands of community science reports to facilitate multi-region population tracking. Additionally, Digitomy is establishing a cloud-hosted learning environment designed for vector control professionals and citizens to refine species identification proficiency through automated feedback. Investigators are presently collaborating with the university technology transfer administration to license the system to established trap manufacturers and transition the innovation toward broader commercial availability.
