Apollo Global Management, one of the world's largest investment firms, has published a new analysis examining the financial impact of artificial intelligence investments across the corporate world. According to the research, companies outside of the top seven technology giants in the S&P 500 have yet to secure tangible financial returns from their investments in AI, indicating that the technology's impact on productivity growth across mainstream businesses remains limited.

Torsten Slok, the Apollo analyst who conducted the research, noted that while the Magnificent Seven have experienced real profits from AI, the profit margins and revenue per worker for other companies have remained flat. While average revenue per employee at major tech firms has risen significantly over recent years, that figure has hovered around the same level within the remaining S&P 493 companies since 2019.

The research points out that mainstream corporate America is starting to view token spending and agentic AI not as growth drivers, but as cost centers that weigh on margins. Companies' reluctance to pay for the latest models means that AI spending is currently confined to a small fraction of the economy, limiting its broader macroeconomic effects.

Experts explain that business intelligence heavily relies on judgment, and AI still faces significant challenges in understanding corporate culture, accountability, and ethical trade-offs. The ultimate trajectory of the AI boom will depend on whether mainstream companies can successfully transform cheap intelligence into higher revenue growth and profit margins.
