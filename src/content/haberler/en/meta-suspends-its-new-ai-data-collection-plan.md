Meta has suspended its controversial new terms of service that would have allowed the collection of content from websites outside of Facebook and Instagram for training artificial intelligence models. The proposed updates triggered significant backlash from journalists and publishers who opposed the inclusion of their work without explicit permission, creating substantial legal and public pressure.

The policy was originally scheduled to take effect on October 30, but amid mounting opposition, the implementation date was postponed to November 20. As tensions continued to rise, Mark Zuckerberg's company ultimately decided to halt the changes. The company announced that the updates are on hold for now, existing rules will remain in effect, and no new timeline has been provided, leaving the future of the initiative uncertain.

The decision was influenced not only by public backlash but also by legal actions in Europe. In Norway, the situation went to court and the new rules were temporarily blocked. Meanwhile, the UK competition authority launched an investigation into the matter. Experts suggest that regulatory bodies will maintain strict scrutiny over such data harvesting practices and that technology giants will continue to face strict legal hurdles.

Meta's retreat underscores the significant hurdles the AI industry faces regarding data privacy and copyright regulations. Whether the company will revise its terms and reach a compromise with regulators remains to be seen in the upcoming period, while publishers continue their efforts to protect their intellectual property rights.
