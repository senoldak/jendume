Michigan state prisons are set to implement a major new artificial intelligence system designed to classify incarcerated people based on security levels and potential risk. Previously, inmate screenings and housing assignments were conducted by committees of facility staff appointed by wardens. Under the new initiative, the Michigan Department of Corrections is outsourcing or heavily informing this decision-making process through software developed by Recidiviz, a non-profit technology organization that originated as a data project at Google.

The software platform is designed to identify individuals eligible for state-offered programs, reclassification, and release, while also coordinating access to resources such as transitional housing and employment. Starting with a pilot program in several men's facilities, the state agency points out that the software application is projected to enhance the department capacity to evaluate incarcerated persons future behavioral threats by roughly 30 percent.

Housing designations are highly consequential for inmates, determining their access to programming, resources, and the general population environment throughout their sentences. However, the partnership has raised significant concerns regarding data privacy and algorithmic bias. Investigations into similar risk-analysis programs in other states have revealed that historical data can contain inherent racial and socioeconomic biases, potentially leading to flawed classifications and harsher outcomes for vulnerable individuals.

While the Michigan system focuses on housing assignments rather than parole decisions, critics warn that relying heavily on software could introduce systemic errors rather than resolving correctional challenges. The rollout serves as a notable case study on the expanding intersection of artificial intelligence and the criminal justice system.
