Torus has launched "Foundry," a system designed to bring AI computing power directly to existing factories and business sites, supported by a new $500 million funding round. This development comes at a time when large-scale data centers are struggling to secure sufficient power and facing increasing resistance from local communities regarding energy usage.

The core concept of the system is to shift the paradigm by "bringing chips to power" rather than bringing power to chips. Foundry units operate by utilizing spare capacity at existing transformers, eliminating the need for new grid connections. Each unit integrates power, storage, cooling, networking, and computing into a single prefabricated system. These units range from 1 to 20 megawatts in capacity, and the timeline from contract to operational status is only 12 to 16 weeks.

The company aims to accelerate AI inference processes with this infrastructure. Industry data suggests that demand for inference in data centers is expected to grow by 35% annually through 2030. Torus reports that thousands of GPUs are already running within these units and plans to deploy more than 47,000 GPUs by the end of 2027.

Another significant feature of this solution is its focus on minimizing environmental impact. Foundry units utilize closed-loop cooling systems that require no water. Furthermore, the system contributes idle computing power to medical research through volunteer computing projects. By aiming to increase computing capacity without raising energy bills or creating pollution, this approach offers a new model for the future of AI infrastructure.
