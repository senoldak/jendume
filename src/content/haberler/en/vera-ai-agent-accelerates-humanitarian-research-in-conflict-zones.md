Humanitarian organizations operating in conflict zones are increasingly utilizing a new multi-agent AI system named VERA to streamline critical data analysis. Developed by Prosper Global in collaboration with Cloudera, this system aims to significantly shorten the research cycle for analysts working on the front lines of humanitarian crises. The system operates on Amazon Web Services infrastructure and leverages models from Anthropic to aggregate data from sources such as ReliefWeb, the conflict tracker ACLED, and local news outlets.

This development is particularly significant because time is often the most critical factor during humanitarian emergencies. Rather than attempting to fully automate the final reporting process, VERA provides analysts with a rapid starting point, allowing them to focus on higher-level research and decision-making. By reducing the time spent on initial data gathering, humanitarian experts can make informed decisions much faster than traditional methods would allow.

The tangible results from the field demonstrate the efficiency gains provided by this technology. For instance, in Sudan, the desk research time for an agricultural report was reduced from five or six days to just two, resulting in a cost saving of approximately $1,500. Furthermore, in Colombia, an employee without a security background was able to produce an election security report in only a few hours using the system. Launched in June 2026, the system is currently operational in Sudan, Colombia, the Democratic Republic of Congo, and Ukraine.

This approach highlights how AI can serve as a collaborative "research partner" rather than a replacement for human expertise. By keeping human agency at the center of the process, the system ensures that complex crisis data is processed efficiently while reducing the operational burden on humanitarian workers. As the system evolves, it is expected to integrate additional resources, providing an even more curated and comprehensive research process for teams working in the world's most challenging environments.
