Anthropic has completed a historic initial public offering, securing 81 billion dollars and reaching a valuation of 1.5 trillion dollars. This financial milestone has generated significant excitement among investors, while simultaneously creating immense wealth for the company's leadership and early employees. Each of the seven co-founders holds stakes valued at approximately 20 billion dollars, and thousands of employees now hold shares worth millions of dollars, highlighting the scale of capital accumulation within the sector.

Despite the financial enthusiasm, the broader economic implications have sparked concern. With AI-linked corporations accounting for 49 percent of the S&P 500's market value, economists warn that a potential market bubble burst could severely impact retirement funds and individual savings. Following the IPO, Anthropic shares experienced a 9 percent decline, reflecting the volatility inherent in the sector.

The rapid pace of development has intensified debates over industry regulation. While Anthropic's leadership acknowledges risks such as misuse, economic disruption, and loss of control, other investors argue that the industry is moving too fast and requires a slower approach to ensure safety. Political resistance to regulation remains a significant factor, as some officials argue that imposing controls could stifle growth and negatively impact stock markets.

Meanwhile, experts continue to warn about the long-term risks associated with advanced artificial intelligence, emphasizing the need for serious consideration of safety guardrails. The gap between the financial success of the IPO and the growing societal concerns regarding the impact of AI on jobs and the economy remains a central point of contention in the ongoing debate over the future of the industry.
