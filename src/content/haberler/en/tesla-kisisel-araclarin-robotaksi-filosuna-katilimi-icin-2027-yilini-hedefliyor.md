During a corporate financial results meeting, a Tesla autopilot executive pointed to the second quarter of 2027 for customers to be able to include their own vehicles in the robotaxi fleet. The company plans to use the same autonomous driving software across Cybercab and Model Y models, aiming to roll it out with a new version. This step intends to enable car owners to integrate their automobiles into the company's passenger transport network to generate income.

However, this transition process involves more than just a software update. Vehicles need to comply with geographic boundaries set by the company and be integrated into the fleet management systems. Similar goals and timelines announced in the past experienced delays. Currently, the company operates a limited number of robotaxis in parts of certain states while testing fully driverless vehicles in select locations. The executive noted that after these preparations, customer vehicles could be included in the fleet.

Obstacles to integrating personal vehicles into the autonomous network go beyond software. Driverless passenger transportation requires specific local and state-level legal permits and comprehensive insurance frameworks in the regions of operation. Additionally, the fact that some older Model 3 and Model Y vehicles lack the necessary artificial intelligence processing power or hardware remains a limiting factor for the plan. Past experiences have shown that similar targets by company executives have sometimes missed their projected timelines.
