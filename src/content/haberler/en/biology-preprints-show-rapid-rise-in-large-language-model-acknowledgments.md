A recent data analysis focusing on the integration of artificial intelligence in biological research highlights a significant shift in how scientists utilize modern technologies. According to data shared by Epoch AI, the proportion of biology preprints acknowledging the use of large language models (LLMs) has grown exponentially since 2023. Over the span of three years, these acknowledgments have nearly closed the gap with specialized AI tools, which have seen a steady rise since 2021.

The findings show that specialized AI tools include models designed for protein structure prediction, image analysis, and genomics. Among these, AlphaFold remains the most commonly cited tool, appearing in about half of all preprints that mention specialized AI usage. Meanwhile, the application of large language models spans a more diverse range of tasks, including literature reviews, writing assistance, and coding support. By September 2026, about 4 percent of biology preprints explicitly acknowledged using LLMs for research or analysis, up from roughly 1 percent at the beginning of the year.

The research methodology involved scanning bioRxiv preprints published between January 2020 and September 2026. Automated checks and classifications were conducted using advanced language models to categorize the specific use cases of LLMs in academic texts, ranging from conceptual design to methodological contributions. While the study focuses on preprints rather than peer-reviewed papers, it offers valuable insight into the evolving norms of scientific disclosure.

This rapid integration of language models into biological sciences underscores a broader cultural shift in academia. As researchers increasingly rely on digital assistants for various stages of their work, the landscape of scientific publishing and discovery continues to transform.
