California-based startup BrightAI has announced a $97 million funding round to scale its platform, which utilizes AI sensors, robots, and drones to monitor critical infrastructure such as power lines, pipelines, and industrial sites. Led by Smith Point Capital, this latest round brings the company's total funding to $184 million. The financing also saw participation from existing customer GE Vernova, alongside Cobalt Capital, Marlinspike Partners, and Zero Infinity Partners.

BrightAI’s technology is designed to monitor infrastructure by attaching sensors to poles, pipelines, and tanks. The system runs AI directly on the devices, allowing it to flag potential issues without requiring a constant internet connection. For locations that are too remote or hazardous for human crews, the company deploys robots and drones for inspection. Additionally, a wearable AI assistant provides guidance to technicians during repair processes.

The core value proposition is predictive maintenance: fixing infrastructure before failures occur. According to the company, more than half of scheduled manual inspections often find no issues, while serious failures can go unnoticed until they escalate. BrightAI’s platform is currently operational at more than 50,000 sites, with a client base that includes major energy firms such as GE Vernova, Diamondback Energy, and Kodiak Gas Services.

As global power grids face increasing pressure from the rising energy demands of data centers, utilities are planning to invest $1.4 trillion in grid infrastructure by 2030. BrightAI is positioning itself to support this transition by expanding its footprint. With nearly 60% of its target market located outside of North America, the company is now opening new offices in Europe and Asia to meet the growing demand for physical AI solutions in heavy industry.
