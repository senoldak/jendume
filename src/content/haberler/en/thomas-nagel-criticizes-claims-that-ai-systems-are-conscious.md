American philosopher Thomas Nagel has criticized claims that artificial intelligence systems are conscious. The New York University professor emeritus shared his views on AI technologies in an interview following his receipt of the Berggruen Prize for Philosophy & Culture. Known widely for his foundational work on the nature of consciousness written over fifty years ago, the philosopher evaluated the latest technological developments.

Nagel stated that while the ability of large language models to generate human-like texts is impressive, the idea that these systems could be conscious is groundless. Arguing that consciousness depends on a physical nervous system and a biological substrate, the philosopher emphasized that data centers or AI models lack any biological structure. Highlighting the subjective nature of consciousness, Nagel noted that merely producing language does not constitute a natural manifestation of consciousness.

Stating that the models acting as if they are conscious stems from commercial interests, Nagel expressed that he finds this trend dangerous. He pointed out that people might find it difficult to maintain the line between reality and pretense when interacting with these systems. He added that while AI models can simulate morality and beliefs, they cannot be part of the moral universe since they lack genuine mental lives.

Philosopher Nagel noted that AI is merely sophisticated machines mimicking human texts, warning that this situation could negatively affect public perception. He also expressed concerns that relying heavily on these systems might reduce the motivation for humans to engage in difficult intellectual work necessary for cultural progress.
