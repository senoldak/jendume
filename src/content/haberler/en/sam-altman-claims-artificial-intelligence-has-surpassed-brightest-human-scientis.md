OpenAI CEO Sam Altman spoke at a conference in Abu Dhabi, discussing the rapid advancement of artificial intelligence models and the future of technology. Altman stated that at the beginning of the year, AI was mostly not as smart as the smartest scientists, but it has now become mostly smarter than them. He stressed that humanity must remain in control of its own destiny in light of these rapid technological shifts and increasing capabilities.

OpenAI previously marketed its models as having PhD-level intelligence. Earlier this year, the company announced solutions to complex mathematical problems such as the Navier-Stokes equations, which sparked both breakthroughs and controversies. Mathematicians raised concerns over how research was utilized and noted that the complex proofs generated by AI will likely take researchers years to fully unpack and understand, creating significant debate within the scientific community.

The company subsequently released hundreds of studies documenting purported solutions to additional mathematical problems, drawing further criticism from researchers. Prominent mathematicians expressed frustration over the immense volume of output and how the academic community was treated during the process. Critics argue that while the technical solutions may hold up, the convoluted language of the proofs leaves their practical utility open to question.

Altman's bold assertions arrive at a time when a significant portion of the scientific community maintains reservations about the direction of commercial artificial intelligence models. Evaluating such claims requires rigorous proof, particularly when dealing with complex mathematical proofs that demand years of human validation. The intersection of aggressive marketing strategies and scientific verification remains a central point of discussion as these technologies evolve.
