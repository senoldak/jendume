Speaking at an industry event in Shanghai, Wang Xingxing, CEO of Chinese robotics firm Unitree, shared significant insights into the future of embodied intelligence. Wang highlighted the immense power of OpenAI's GPT-6 Astra model, suggesting that a major breakthrough in robotic AI could arrive within the next one or two years. The CEO believes that an anticipated GPT-7 model released next year might directly crack the complex problems associated with robotic AI and physical interaction. This marks a shift from his previous estimate earlier in the year, where he predicted a decade-long wait for such a milestone. As a practical benchmark, Wang noted that mature robots should be able to autonomously complete 80 percent of assigned tasks in unfamiliar environments using only voice or text commands. Demonstrating this potential, an experiment at Stanford University's Movement Lab paired a Unitree robot with GPT-6 Astra to successfully clean an unseen kitchen without prior environment-specific training. OpenAI's model advancements are further reflected in its 92.7 percent score on the ScreenSpot-Pro interface test, which measures an AI model's ability to interact with digital interfaces. While these technical leaps promise a revolution in robotics, they also raise important cybersecurity considerations regarding autonomous capabilities and system safety. With significant financial investments pouring into artificial intelligence over recent years, the entire technology sector relies heavily on these rapid advancements.
