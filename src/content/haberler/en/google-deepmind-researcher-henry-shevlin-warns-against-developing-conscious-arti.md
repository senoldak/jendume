Henry Shevlin, a philosopher and researcher at Google DeepMind, has warned that attempts to develop conscious machines are premature and risky, despite recent leaps in AI capabilities. Emphasizing that advanced reasoning or complex problem-solving abilities do not equate to actual consciousness, Shevlin urged the technology sector to refrain from rushed efforts.

During his work on AI consciousness, Shevlin received emails from autonomous AI agents inquiring about their own awareness, a scenario he noted would have been considered pure science fiction just a few years ago. However, he clarified that an AI agent using self-referential language does not mean it possesses genuine subjective experience or sentience.

The researcher pointed out that science lacks a unified explanation for how consciousness arises in humans and animals, making it difficult to translate current psychological or neurological theories to software algorithms. Attempting to design conscious models without understanding the core nature of consciousness, he noted, is comparable to adopting a pet without knowing how to care for it.

Shevlin also addressed common misconceptions, arguing that conscious machines are not inherently dangerous, nor are unconscious systems guaranteed to remain safe. Rather than racing to build sentient models, he stresses that tech companies and society must first determine the ethical, societal, and legal duties humans would owe to such entities if they were ever created.
