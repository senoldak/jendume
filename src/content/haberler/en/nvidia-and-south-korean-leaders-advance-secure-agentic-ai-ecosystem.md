South Korean technology leaders are partnering with NVIDIA to enhance the security and performance of autonomous AI agents. Hyundai Motor Group is currently investigating the potential of NVIDIA NemoClaw blueprints alongside the NVIDIA OpenShell runtime environment. This initiative aims to establish a more robust framework for executing AI applications. The technology functions as a critical security layer, monitoring how AI agents interact with sensitive information and governing their autonomous decisions based on predefined organizational policies. As a core element of the NVIDIA Open Agent Safety Platform, it provides sandboxed execution to protect enterprise tools.

Another key part of the collaboration involves AIM Intelligence, a Korean AI security specialist. The firm is working to merge its Starfort agent control platform with the NVIDIA OpenShell infrastructure. This integration is designed to bolster employee productivity by combining runtime access controls with advanced detection of malicious instructions. By preventing data leakage, this partnership paves the way for AI deployment in high-stakes environments like the public energy sector. The system is engineered to ensure that AI agents remain within the boundaries of corporate regulations while performing tasks independently across industrial and robotics settings.

On the side of language and cultural alignment, LG AI Research has opened API access for its K-EXAONE-2.5 model. This specific model was trained using NVIDIA's Nemotron-Personas-Korea dataset, which is a specialized collection of 7 million fully synthetic personas. These profiles are meticulously grounded in official Korean statistics to ensure accuracy. This approach allows AI models to generate responses that reflect the unique cultural and statistical nuances of Korean society. The Nemotron family of open models and datasets provides the necessary tools for developers to create highly specialized and efficient agents.

NVIDIA's open models and security tools allow developers to build more specialized and efficient AI agents. These security protocols are vital for the expansion of autonomous systems, especially in industrial and robotics environments. These developments in South Korea serve as a concrete example of how localization and security go hand in hand in the global AI ecosystem. By providing a secure runtime with policy-based controls, these companies are setting a new standard for how autonomous agents can be safely integrated into complex enterprise workflows.
