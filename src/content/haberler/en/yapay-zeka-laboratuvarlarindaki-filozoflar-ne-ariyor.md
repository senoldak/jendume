Leading artificial intelligence laboratories are increasingly welcoming philosophers alongside engineers to navigate complex ethical questions. Henry Shevlin, a researcher from Cambridge University, now spends a significant portion of his time at Google DeepMind examining AI ethics, consciousness, and how humans relate to generative models.

Shevlin points out that traditional academia has often been sluggish in keeping pace with the rapid advancements of artificial intelligence. Daily interaction with the technology is essential to develop proper mental models, especially as AI systems now excel in frontier mathematics and advanced coding, moving well beyond mediocre human performance.

Addressing concerns about AI autonomy and human-like interactions, Shevlin cautions against blanket restrictions or hastily suppressing models' self-ascription capabilities, as doing so can trigger unexpected trade-offs in broader cognitive functions. He emphasizes that empirical data on human-AI relationships remains thin, making global ethnographic research more important than ever to understand how people truly interact with these systems.
