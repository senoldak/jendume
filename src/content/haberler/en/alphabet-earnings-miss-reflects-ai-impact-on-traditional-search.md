Alphabet reported its latest quarterly financial results, showing that search and other segment revenue grew 12% from a year ago to roughly $63 billion, coming in below market estimates. The company stated that search query growth on its platform reached its highest level in five years, highlighting a growing gap between usage and revenue. Analysts point out that as more users turn to artificial intelligence bots and personalized agent tools, traditional search engines face mounting pressure on their monetization models. Alphabet executives noted that foreign exchange fluctuations played a major role in the revenue deceleration, and company shares fell about 10% in extended trading following the report. Meanwhile, the company's cloud division, which serves as a core engine for its AI strategy, experienced massive growth. Cloud revenue more than doubled in the quarter to nearly $31 billion, while the division accumulated a massive order backlog of approximately 560 billion dollars, illustrating the vast enterprise appetite for artificial intelligence infrastructure. Management maintained its full-year 2026 capital expenditure forecast between 195 billion and 205 billion dollars to support escalating infrastructure needs while sustaining its ongoing spending plans. As Gemini models continue to integrate into the company's advertising and cloud products, this shift in search revenue underscores the broader transformation brought by artificial intelligence across the tech industry.
