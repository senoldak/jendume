Fireworks AI, a platform used by developers to access open-source AI models, has confirmed that customer data was exposed following a cyberattack on its cloud provider. The company stated that an attacker breached a development system, gaining access to billing information and specific datasets. One of the most prominent customers affected by this breach is the online document and note-taking platform Notion.

In a disclosure, Notion revealed that the breach affected approximately 1% of its customer base. The stolen data includes chat transcripts from Notion AI users who utilized the "Insights" feature. These transcripts contain every input sent to the model and every output received by the user. While the company noted that much of the data is unattributable, making it difficult to link specific logs to individual users, the exposure of conversational data remains a significant concern.

Fireworks AI believes the hackers were financially motivated, aiming to steal and sell large language model (LLM) tokens. By gaining unauthorized access to these tokens, third parties can utilize AI model processing power without incurring costs, effectively stealing resources from legitimate account holders. This incident marks the second security breach for Fireworks AI in just two months, following a previous event in October.

Following the discovery, Notion disconnected its systems from Fireworks AI on November 3, deleted stored data, and halted all connectivity between the two platforms. This event underscores the heavy reliance of tech companies on third-party AI infrastructure and how a single point of failure can compromise an entire chain of services. Experts emphasize that the security of the infrastructure through which AI data flows is just as vital as the security of the AI models themselves.
