Wells Fargo is rolling out an updated customer agreement stating that the use of any AI agent is entirely at the user's own risk. While these autonomous bots automate online tasks and shopping, they have also sparked widespread security concerns among financial institutions. Banks are pushing back against third-party tools that log into accounts using customer credentials.

Several major financial institutions have enacted new policies or warnings regarding AI agents. In late October, JPMorgan Chase banned third-party agents logging in with user credentials, while Bank of America, Citibank, and PNC introduced similar warnings. Online platforms and travel companies, including Amazon and United, are also blocking personal bots from making purchases on their platforms.

Experts note that while consumers are comfortable automating basic household purchases, they remain cautious about dynamic transactions. Questions regarding liability in case of automated mistakes remain unresolved, though credit card providers like American Express are exploring limited reimbursement protections for future agentic errors.

At the same time, banks are actively developing their own proprietary AI tools while locking out external agents. Wells Fargo reported that its generative AI tool has been used over a billion times, and other banks are deploying internal agentic assistants. Analysts remain skeptical about whether shopping agents solve a genuine consumer problem or simply serve as tools for affiliate revenue.
