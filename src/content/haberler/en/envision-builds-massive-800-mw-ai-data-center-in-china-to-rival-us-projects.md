Envision, a Chinese energy and technology firm, is constructing a colossal AI data center campus named Galaxy AI in Inner Mongolia. With a target capacity of 800 MW, the project stands as a direct competitor to major US initiatives like the Stargate campus in Texas. The first 120 MW phase of the project was completed in less than a year, demonstrating rapid deployment capabilities in the global AI infrastructure race.

A defining feature of the Galaxy AI campus is its energy independence. Rather than relying solely on the local power grid, Envision is integrating its own wind farms and battery storage systems. The company aims to source over 60% of the electricity for the initial phases from wind power, supported by 420 MW of dedicated wind capacity. To ensure stability during low-wind periods, four-hour battery storage systems are utilized, contrasting with the natural gas-heavy approach often seen in US data center expansions.

Technically, the facility implements the world's first 800V direct current (DC) power distribution system. By using solid-state transformers, the design eliminates multiple conversion steps typically required in traditional data centers. This reduces energy loss and minimizes the need for intermediate hardware like traditional UPS units. Furthermore, the campus employs a shared cooling loop for both liquid and air-cooled servers, optimizing thermal management for high-density AI workloads.

The project's financial efficiency is also notable. Field reports indicate that the cost of the first phase is approximately one-fifth of similar projects in the United States. Scheduled for full completion by the end of 2027, the campus is part of a broader 3 GW data center investment plan in the region, where tech giants like Alibaba and Huawei are also expanding their infrastructure footprints.
