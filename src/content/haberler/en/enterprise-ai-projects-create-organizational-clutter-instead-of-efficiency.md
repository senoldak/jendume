Companies are rapidly adopting artificial intelligence tools, but whether these tools fundamentally change how work gets done remains a significant question. A new analysis reveals that the biggest barrier organizations face in AI projects is the disconnect between tool access and operational transformation. Many companies are running numerous AI pilot projects without connection, creating an image of activity rather than measurable change in efficiency or speed.

The research highlights two critical issues: runaway buildout and the copilot ceiling. Runaway buildout describes organizations launching experiments without a common basis for assessing results. The copilot ceiling refers to situations where employees use AI assistants for superficial tasks like drafting or summarization but fail to alter business workflows in a substantial way.

The root of this issue lies in the fact that while organizations grant permission to adopt AI tools, they often fail to provide the authority to redesign the processes around them. Employees are expected to learn new methods while still being measured against unchanged workloads, which negates the time savings AI is supposed to provide.

The analysis is based on data from 2,100 meetings and conversations across 700 different organizations. In a study examining 332 distinct organizational patterns, the researchers identified the top 25 priority problem areas. These issues include the complexity of approval mechanisms and the use of unapproved AI tools for sensitive information.

Ultimately, an organization's AI success should not be measured by how many projects it funds, but by which business processes are improved, which decision-making mechanisms are accelerated, and which responsibilities are clarified. Companies need to shift their focus from how many experiments are we running to how have these experiments changed the way we operate?
