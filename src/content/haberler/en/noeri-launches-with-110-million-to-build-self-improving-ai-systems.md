Noeri, an AI lab founded by Stanford professors Yejin Choi and Carlos Guestrin, announced it has raised $110 million in a funding round led by Menlo Ventures. The round saw participation from strategic investors including Nvidia, Cisco Investments, and Snowflake, alongside venture firms like Madrona Venture Group and NEA. The Palo Alto-based startup aims to solve a fundamental limitation in current AI: most models stop learning once their initial training is complete.

The company's technology analyzes a business's specific data and workflows to continuously refine the system. Noeri's AI can adjust instructions, select the most appropriate tools, and determine which model should handle specific steps in a process. Crucially, every modification comes with a clear explanation and requires human approval, similar to a standard code review process. This approach works with both open-weight and closed models, ensuring customers maintain ownership of their data and custom workflows.

Beyond corporate applications, Noeri is targeting scientific research. The lab intends for its AI to form hypotheses, conduct experiments, and learn from results in fields ranging from molecular biology to complex engineering. By utilizing smaller, specialized models, Noeri aims to make high-level scientific discovery more affordable. Co-founder Yejin Choi noted that their specialized small AI models have already demonstrated the ability to outperform massive models like GPT-5 in specific microbiology research tasks.

The leadership team brings significant expertise to the venture. Carlos Guestrin previously co-founded Turi, which was acquired by Apple, and co-created the widely used XGBoost library. Yejin Choi is a MacArthur Fellow with a background at the Allen Institute for AI and Nvidia. Both founders emphasized their commitment to preventing the centralization of AI power, advocating for a future where advanced technology is not controlled by a single entity.
