Opera has announced a new web standard called WebAI, which allows users to run artificial intelligence models directly within its browsers without relying on the cloud. The rollout marks a central shift toward making locally run AI a core component of next-generation browsing experiences. The WebAI standard is designed to support a variety of open-weight AI models, including Google’s Gemma and Apple’s on-device foundation model on Macs.

Opera is initially making these capabilities available as an opt-in early preview in its flagship Opera One browser, with Opera GX and Opera Neon expected to follow soon. Users are required to explicitly choose to opt into the feature according to the company's announcements.

Running models locally offers distinct advantages for users, particularly regarding privacy and cost. Users can utilize AI features without transmitting personal information to corporate servers or paying for cloud-based subscriptions. Developers can also integrate AI features without maintaining complex infrastructure or paying for every request.

The system enables users to generate text, classify content, and understand information relationships directly inside the browser. As privacy concerns grow with increasingly powerful AI assistants handling personal data, local processing offers an alternative by keeping tasks on the user's own device rather than a corporate cloud server, granting users greater control over their computing environment.

However, there are trade-offs to consider. Running AI models locally places higher demands on computer hardware, which can affect overall system performance. Opera notes that model performance ultimately depends entirely on the specific capabilities of the user's device hardware.
