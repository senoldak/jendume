Artificial intelligence company Anthropic has detailed the escalating energy demands of its infrastructure and its growing reliance on natural gas in S-1 registration documents filed with the US Securities and Exchange Commission ahead of an initial public offering. The filings highlight that powering data centers increasingly involves turning to natural gas-fueled generation facilities, a trend that could significantly affect future growth plans and operating expenses. According to the documents, potential increases in natural gas prices and supply chain bottlenecks could pose major challenges to data center investments.

The rapid surge in electricity consumption by data centers across the United States is reshaping energy production investments, driving plans for new fossil fuel-based generation capacity on a scale not seen in over a decade. Because developers often face long delays in connecting to the power grid, many choose to install natural gas engines directly on-site. However, such alternatives can lead to supply bottlenecks for turbines and result in lower energy efficiency and higher pollutant emissions.

While natural gas generally produces lower carbon dioxide emissions during electricity generation compared to coal, it does not eliminate fossil fuel-derived emissions. Methane leaks from natural gas infrastructure create powerful greenhouse gas effects, and recent studies warn that the soaring electricity demands of data centers could reverse decades of progress in air quality. Furthermore, rising global temperatures increase the cooling costs for servers, adding further financial burdens to facility operations.

Anthropic also warns that major cloud service providers and data center operators will face intense competition for natural gas resources and pipeline capacity. With natural gas serving as a leading source of electricity generation in the US, the growing demand from data centers strains existing energy grids. Local communities are increasingly pushing back against new data center projects due to rising electricity prices and grid strain, raising the prospect of regulatory interventions and higher compliance costs for AI developers.
