Google has released a new framework called "Agile Deliberation," designed to help AI models better classify subjective concepts like "healthy food" or "clickbait" that lack clear, objective boundaries. While most traditional tools assume that users already know the exact definition of a concept, this new approach helps users discover and refine those boundaries through an interactive process. This development is a significant step toward making AI models more aligned with human perception, particularly in fields like content moderation and image classification. AI models are often trained on rigid rules, but human language and perception are inherently flexible. Agile Deliberation bridges this gap by using Gemini 2.5 Pro and Flash models to turn human feedback into structured, readable definitions. The system utilizes CLIP embeddings and FAISS indexing to process visual data efficiently.

The framework was evaluated through 18 live sessions, yielding impressive results. Compared to standard zero-shot performance, the framework achieved an average increase of 10.5 F1 points. In specific tests, such as identifying "paid to play" clickbait, the precision of the model improved significantly. Users reported that the process required significantly less effort and stress compared to manual prompt editing. The framework is designed to run in Jupyter or Colab environments and has been released as open-source on GitHub. This accessibility allows developers to easily integrate this technology into their own custom classification projects, moving beyond simple keyword-based filtering to a more nuanced understanding of visual content. Instead of spending hours on manual labeling, developers can now leverage this AI-assisted process to achieve faster and more consistent results.
