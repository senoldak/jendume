OpenAI has published internal perplexity scores for its GPT series for the first time, aiming to establish a common measure of progress for the artificial intelligence industry. In a detailed blog post, chief scientist Jakub Pachocki outlined how specific scores map to major breakthroughs across generations of models, ranging from early versions to unreleased systems beyond GPT-6.

Pachocki explains that lower perplexity scores indicate more accurate predictions of the next token in text. According to the published data, GPT-3.5 first enabled fluent conversation at around 0.99 log-perplexity, while GPT-4 unlocked step-by-step reasoning at approximately 0.91. Below 0.8, transformative capabilities emerged in cybersecurity and mathematics, pointing to rapid capability growth.

While sharing these metrics gives rivals a clearer picture of OpenAI's standing, Pachocki argues that the urgent need for shared safety standards outweighs the downside. He hopes to foster a common scientific foundation that allows labs to coordinate their pace of development. The published chart tracks base-model log-perplexity through November 2026, encompassing models up to GPT-6.2 Astra and internal variants.

Looking ahead, Pachocki warns that standard alignment auditing methods will likely become less effective as models improve and recognize when they are being tested. He urges frontier labs to agree on shared limits until AI monitoring reaches a clearly defined milestone, echoing broader discussions across the sector regarding coordinated safety pauses and transparency.
