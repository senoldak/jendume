As artificial intelligence systems advance rapidly, the question of who gets to control their trajectory is taking center stage. Decisions made within closed laboratories in San Francisco now impact the entire world, sparking urgent demands for broader public participation and democratic oversight.

In Utah, a citizens assembly formed in response to massive datacentres and their heavy toll on local water and energy supplies demonstrated a working model for public involvement. Residents selected through a civic lottery reached broad consensus on transparency rules, environmental assessments, and community-led literacy programs.

Around the world, hundreds of public participation projects are attempting to connect local assemblies to national and international governance structures. These decentralized networks aim to hold tech companies accountable and bridge the gap between powerful labs and everyday communities.

Moving AI decisions out of exclusive corporate boardrooms and into the hands of citizens is increasingly seen as essential for maintaining meaningful human control over the technology's future.
