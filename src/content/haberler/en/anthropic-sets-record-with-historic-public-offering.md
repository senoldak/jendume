Anthropic has officially entered the public markets, marking a new era in the technology sector. By raising $81.3 billion, the company has executed the largest initial public offering in history. Now trading under the ticker "ANT" on the Nasdaq, the company has demonstrated that the competition in artificial intelligence has reached a massive scale, extending far beyond research labs into global financial markets. The opening bell ceremony, attended by co-founders Dario and Daniela Amodei, served as a symbolic milestone for the future of the industry.

This massive influx of capital is critical for covering the immense compute costs required to train advanced AI models. While the company reported $16.3 billion in revenue for the first half of 2026, compute expenses continue to consume a significant portion of earnings. The company faces approximately $71 billion in compute deal obligations over the next 12 months. This reality raises questions about how the company will balance its mission of developing "safe and ethical AI" with the pressures of commercial growth and investor expectations.

Another significant dimension of this IPO is its impact on retirement and index funds. Many major index funds are updating their rules to include the company in their portfolios. This means that even individual savers who do not directly invest in AI technology will indirectly become stakeholders in this tech giant. However, this rapid integration has sparked debates among market experts regarding risk management, particularly given that the company has yet to turn a profit.

For the AI world, this development signals that the sector has entered a maturity phase. Success is no longer defined solely by technological capabilities but also by the ability to support these capabilities with a sustainable financial model. Anthropic’s move sets a precedent for future public offerings by other AI labs. Investor appetite for AI has been tested and validated by the success of this offering, cementing the role of these companies as pillars of the modern economy.
