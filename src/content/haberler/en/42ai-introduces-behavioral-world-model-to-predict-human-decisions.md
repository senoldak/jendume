Artificial intelligence technologies are expanding beyond information processing and text generation into the realm of predicting human behavior. Tel Aviv-based startup 42AI has introduced a behavioral world model designed to forecast how individuals will respond when circumstances change. While traditional AI systems typically focus on interpreting data or generating answers, this approach treats human behavior as a distinct prediction problem, analyzing underlying psychological tendencies and internal motivations rather than merely extrapolating from previous actions.

Developed over three years, the technology integrates insights from psychology, neuroscience, and genetics. The foundational training involved psychological assessments of thousands of individuals, combined with government datasets and biobank information covering more than 2 million people. The company’s customer datasets have since expanded to include nearly 30 million individuals. The system transforms these behavioral observations into mathematical trait representations, which are subsequently processed by a transformer-based world model to outline probabilities for potential decisions under varying conditions.

The startup has deployed its predictive system within financial brokerages, assisting firms in managing liquidity, margin requirements, and hedging exposure. Currently collaborating with 10 large brokerages, the company reports that the model demonstrates an F1 score ranging between 0.8 and 0.85 when forecasting the direction and volume of trades. The company has secured $62.5 million in funding to support its operations and ongoing development.

Beyond finance, the startup is exploring applications in gaming, fraud prevention, and cybersecurity, with a long-term vision of enabling robots and AI agents to better understand human behavior. Despite its innovative potential, the technology raises valid concerns regarding privacy and the ethical implications of behavioral profiling. While the founders point to internal ethics committees and regulatory compliance as safeguards, the ability to anticipate human choices underscores the growing need for robust frameworks in advanced AI development.
