A new initiative aiming to transform the logistics sector has unveiled a massive autonomous cargo aircraft project featuring a cockpit-free design. Focusing entirely on freight transport, this aerial vehicle stands out with a structure that completely eliminates the need for manned flight. With a wingspan of approximately 15.24 meters, the massive drone aims to create a significant alternative in logistics operations with its ability to carry up to 1,814 kilograms of payload.

Founded by engineers David Zagaynov and Parker Tenney, who previously worked at prominent aerospace and e-commerce companies, the company aims to make aerial freight transport more economical. The project leaders compare this system to autonomous trucks moving in the sky. Removing the cockpit area significantly increases the internal volume of the aircraft while completely eliminating the weight of a pilot and the load created by life support systems. This design choice ensures that the vehicle focuses solely on cargo capacity.

Before the main Egret project, the company started its work with an initial prototype named V0. The team later developed the carbon-fiber Seagull model capable of operating on water, showcasing this vehicle in demonstrations attended by institutions such as the US Navy and Special Operations Command. All these engineering experiences gained from previous prototypes formed a critical foundation for the design processes of the Egret model.

Although Egret does not house a human pilot onboard, it does not operate entirely independently without oversight. An operator stationed at a ground control station can securely manage the vehicle using live camera feeds and flight surface controls. This hybrid approach ensures that critical flight functions remain under human supervision.

Flight tests planned to be conducted in the state of Oregon will allow the project to claim the title of the largest cargo drone flown in US territory. Meanwhile, a 60 million dollar investment led by TQ Ventures will be channeled into the development activities of this autonomous aviation technology.
