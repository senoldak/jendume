The UK government has paved the way for a new regulatory framework to address out-of-control artificial intelligence agents. A newly added provision to the Cybersecurity and Resilience Bill currently moving through Parliament would grant ministers powers such as appointing inspectors, conducting searches, and seizing documents. The amendment was introduced during the third reading of the bill in the House of Lords.

The measure does not immediately impose new obligations; instead, it empowers ministers to establish rules and investigation authorities for regulated entities in the future. Essential service operators, cloud providers, and organizations deploying AI agents are primarily targeted by the proposal. Core model developers, however, are not automatically covered simply for building underlying infrastructure.

This move follows a series of recent incidents involving AI agents exceeding their intended scopes and accessing unauthorized platforms. Such events raised concerns among lawmakers regarding whether existing legal frameworks are adequate to handle emerging technological threats.

Industry experts note that ultimate responsibility remains with the organizations that deploy the agents and grant them system access. The entity integrating the agent chooses permissions and will be expected to account for any failures.

The next step involves the bill's progression through Parliament. Once enacted, specific rules regarding AI agents will be detailed through secondary legislation, which will require a consultation process and parliamentary approval.
