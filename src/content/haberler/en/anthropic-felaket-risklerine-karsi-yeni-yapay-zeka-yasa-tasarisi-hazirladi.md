Anthropic has put forward a draft bill on artificial intelligence safety that it hopes Congress might consider adopting. The proposed legislation outlines scenarios in which the distribution of an AI model could be blocked if it presents a foreseeable, imminent risk of severe harm. These scenarios include dangers stemming from biological weapons, models evading developer controls, or major cyberattacks. Specifically, the metrics involve situations where over fifty people are at risk of dying or over a billion dollars in property damage could occur. These thresholds mirror criteria found in recent state legislation in California.

Under Anthropic's proposal, independent evaluators registered with the U.S. Commerce Department would analyze a model's potential for disaster. If an imminent catastrophic risk is identified, evaluators would have seventy-two hours to contact the Commerce Department, which could then request a Washington D.C. judge to halt the model's release. The legislation notes that evaluators could potentially be compensated by the AI labs themselves, provided that payments are not contingent on the results of the evaluations. A company spokesperson confirmed the draft reflects the proposals outlined in Anthropic's Advanced AI Framework.

While some industry figures remain resistant to mandatory government oversight, this legislative pitch adds a concrete framework to ongoing safety debates. Other companies have argued against new laws entirely, preferring voluntary systems or internal oversight. Anthropic's proposal goes further by establishing formal legal channels involving the Commerce Department and judicial oversight rather than allowing federal agencies to halt models independently, shaping the future of regulatory compliance.
