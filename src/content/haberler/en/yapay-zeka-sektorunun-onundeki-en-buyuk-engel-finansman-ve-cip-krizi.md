Top executives from Intel Corp. and Broadcom Inc., two of the artificial intelligence industry's major chip suppliers, have warned about the growing challenges facing the sector, pointing to capital access and high operational costs as primary concerns during a financial conference in Singapore in October 2026.

Broadcom's leadership noted that the imbalance between the soaring demand for computing capacity and the industry's ability to supply data center chips could persist towards the end of the decade. While frontier generative AI models hold immense revenue potential, massive spending on training continues to weigh heavily on developers' financials. Executives emphasized that AI labs need to shift their focus toward securing enterprise customers who can realize a tangible return on their investments rather than relying solely on pilot projects.

Intel's leadership echoed these sentiments, stating that capital and the cost of capital remain the biggest near-term constraints, while long-term success depends on clear return-on-investment metrics. Additionally, the growing popularity of efficient, budget-friendly open-source models from various regions is reshaping market dynamics, pushing the industry to prioritize efficiency alongside raw power.
