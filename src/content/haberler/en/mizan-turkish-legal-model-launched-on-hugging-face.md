The Mizan-27B-Turkish-Legal-Decision-Model, designed to classify events in Turkish legal texts through specific questions, has been released on Hugging Face. Created by developer Alican Kiraz, the system avoids generating long chatbot-like responses and instead assigns probability values to predefined options. This approach establishes a clear distinction between legal text generation and option scoring. The system processes event summaries and structured questions together, providing a distribution across headings such as core legislation, jurisdiction, claim status, deadlines, and complexity.

Although the developer published general test results showing a 94.9 percent compliance rate, the context behind this metric is crucial. The model card explicitly notes that test labels were generated by another artificial intelligence model and that this figure does not mean the system achieves 94.9 percent accuracy in real-world lawsuits. In a smaller and more challenging subset of 20 cases containing 119 questions, full compliance was achieved in 93 instances, translating to approximately 78.2 percent. Experts emphasize that the general test rate and this difficult subset should not be used interchangeably.

Based on the Qwen3.6-27B family, Mizan adds a decision layer that evaluates options to a body adapted for legal tasks. However, high probabilities in the output do not guarantee a definitive legal outcome. The model card highlights that human review is necessary, especially for examples involving deadlines or exceptions. Therefore, the tool is not presented as a system that issues court rulings or replaces lawyers, but rather as an assistive evaluation layer.

Regarding usage terms, the repository carries a CC BY-NC 4.0 license, which restricts usage to non-commercial purposes. The model requires approximately 52 GB of VRAM for local execution, alongside a trial area accessible via web browsers. The training summary includes thousands of records and question examples, with the developer stating that the test set was kept separate from training data. These developments underscore how transparency and the boundaries of testing methods remain critical in the integration of AI within specialized fields.
