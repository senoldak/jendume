AI assistants are currently capable at drafting emails, planning schedules, or summarizing documents, yet they often hit a wall when it comes to executing real-world tasks. If you ask an assistant to reschedule a dentist appointment, it might draft a message for you, but it typically cannot pick up the phone to make the call or navigate a website to finalize the booking. Linq is addressing this gap by raising $90 million in new funding to provide AI assistants with the infrastructure to use "human tools" like phones and computers.

Led by Andreessen Horowitz, this funding round will allow the company to build the necessary infrastructure for AI agents to operate more autonomously. Linq’s platform equips assistants with the ability to send texts, make voice calls, and interact with software interfaces on a computer just as a human would. This means a simple user command like "pay rent" can be translated into an agent navigating through multiple screens and completing the transaction. Andreessen Horowitz co-founder Ben Horowitz joined the company's board in connection with the funding. The company has already demonstrated the scale of its technology by processing over 1 billion messages on its messaging platform.

This development marks a shift in the evolution of AI agents. Assistants are moving beyond being passive information providers to becoming active agents capable of completing tasks on behalf of the user. By interacting directly with business websites and phone systems, these agents aim to streamline daily routines. Through products like "HD Calling for Agents," Linq enables assistants to bridge the gap between text-based instructions and voice-based execution. As this technology matures, it paves the way for a future where your devices handle mundane errands, allowing you to focus on more complex work while your AI assistant manages the logistics of your daily life.
