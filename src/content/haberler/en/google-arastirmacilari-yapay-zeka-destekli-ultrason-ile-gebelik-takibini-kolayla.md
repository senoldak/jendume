The World Health Organization estimates that hundreds of thousands of preventable deaths occur each year due to pregnancy-related complications, largely because two-thirds of people globally lack access to diagnostic medical imaging. Traditional ultrasound relies heavily on specialized clinical training that requires a multi-year commitment, leaving millions in low-resource settings without care. To address this real-world need, Google researchers developed machine learning systems that interpret "blind sweep" ultrasounds.

Pinned in JAMA Network Open, the new study evaluated how effectively an AI model for gestational age assessment generalizes across diverse clinical settings and hardware. Frontline healthcare workers without specialized sonography training were able to perform scans by gliding portable ultrasound probes across the abdomen. The AI system ran live on mobile devices and was prospectively tested in clinics in Chicago and Nairobi without prior fine-tuning on the Nairobi dataset.

The research model was initially trained on data from a large group of participants and fine-tuned using a small pilot dataset. Results showed that the AI system was non-inferior to expert sonographers in determining gestational age in both clinical settings, proving its reliability across unfamiliar equipment and diverse patient populations.

Gestational age serves as the master timeline for prenatal screening and medical treatments. By combining minimal operator training with AI interpretation, this technology holds the potential to significantly expand ultrasound availability and improve maternal health outcomes worldwide.
