Think of three things people do online in a single day: connect at home, order something, carry out a task on an institution's website. All three are called using the internet, yet each is a separate step. The question is how many people are at each step between household internet and the tax declaration, and whether the numbers follow the same order in Türkiye and in the European Union (EU).

Eurostat, the statistical office of the European Union, measures these steps with the same survey for Türkiye and for the EU total. In 2025, 96.2% of households in Türkiye have internet; in the EU total the figure is 94.8%. At the first step, Türkiye's value is 1.4 points higher. At later steps the gap changes sign. The share who bought online in the last three months is 42.3% in Türkiye and 62.4% in the EU total. The share who dealt with public authorities online is 71.3% in Türkiye and 57.7% in the EU total. The share who submitted a tax declaration online is 9.7% in Türkiye and 38.2% in the EU total.

This article does not read those numbers as good or bad. It looks at what exactly each step asks, and whether the steps sit inside one another. The answer can be given up front: they do not behave like nested rungs of a ladder but like separate questions put to the same people.

## Which steps are we counting?

Eurostat compiles the survey on households and individuals that national statistical offices carry out. The individuals part of the survey covers people aged 16 to 74; the household part covers households with at least one member in that age range. From this survey we lined up five indicators.

First, the share of households with an internet connection. Second, the share of individuals who used the internet in the last three months. Third, the share who bought online in the last three months. Fourth, the share of individuals who dealt with public authorities online in the last twelve months. Fifth, the share who submitted a tax declaration online in the same period. The last one has been in Eurostat's series since 2024.

Household internet counts households, while the others count individuals. The difference looks small but matters: a household can be connected without everyone in it using the internet. So we read the change from the first step to the second not as a loss but as the difference between two denominators.

## A small gap at the first step, a change of sign at the second

Household internet in 2025 is 96.2% in Türkiye and 94.8% in the EU total. The gap is 1.4 points, with Türkiye's value higher. Three years earlier, in 2022, the two values were 94.2% and 92.4%, so both have risen.

Moving to individuals, the picture changes. The share who used the internet in the last three months is 89.8% in Türkiye and 93.8% in the EU total. Here Türkiye's value is 4.0 points lower. A higher value for household connection alongside a lower value for individual use can look contradictory; the contradiction comes from the different denominators: a connection is a feature of a home, while use is the behaviour of individual people.

People who have never used the internet are counted too: 9.1% in Türkiye and 4.4% in the EU total. This share shows who is outside the use step.

Spread over all individuals, the EU total has 93.8% who used the internet in the last three months, 1.8% who used it earlier and 4.4% who never used it. In Türkiye the same three groups are 89.8%, 1.1% and 9.1%. The earlier users are the sum of people who last used the internet between three and twelve months ago and those who last used it more than twelve months ago.

## Of those who are online, how many shop?

Step three is online shopping. The share who bought online in the last three months is 42.3% in Türkiye and 62.4% in the EU total. At this step Türkiye's value is 20.1 points lower than the EU total.

A second form of the same indicator is published as well: as a share of those who used the internet in the last three months. That share is 47.1% in Türkiye and 66.5% in the EU total. The gap persists when the denominator is limited to internet users. So the shopping gap does not come from people who never go online; it appears among people who are connected too.

There is also a distribution inside the group of shoppers, because Eurostat asks how many times people bought in the last three months. In Türkiye, 13.9% of individuals bought once or twice in the last three months, 14.1% three to five times, 5.7% six to ten times and 8.6% more than ten times. Together these four groups correspond to the share who bought online, 42.3%.

![Waterfall chart: In Türkiye, the two largest groups of online shoppers bought one to five times in the last three months: Frequency: Once or twice: 13.9%, Three to five times: 14.1%, Six to ten times: 5.7%, More than ten times: 8.6%; Total: Online shoppers: 42.3%.](/grafik/dijital-basamaklar/graf-6.svg)
*Türkiye, year 2025: individuals by shopping frequency in the last three months, per cent; the total is the share who bought online. Source: Eurostat.*

For the EU total, the same four groups are 21.3%, 20.9%, 10.5% and 9.5%. Türkiye's value is 7.4 points lower for one or two purchases; it is 6.8 points lower for three to five; it is 4.8 points lower for six to ten; it is 1.0 points lower for above ten times. So the gap shrinks as frequency rises. The share who bought six times or more is 14.3% in Türkiye and 20.1% in the EU total. The shopping gap is therefore not only a gap in never buying; it is wider among occasional shoppers and narrow among frequent ones.

## With public authorities, the gap reverses

At the fourth step the picture turns around. The share of individuals who dealt with public authorities online in the last twelve months is 71.3% in Türkiye and 57.7% in the EU total; Türkiye's value is 13.7 points higher. That a value low at the shopping step can be high for public services shows that the steps do not follow one another automatically.

Contact is broken down by type as well. The share of individuals who accessed their personal information on the websites of public authorities is 68.5% in Türkiye and 41.3% in the EU total; the gap is 27.2 points, with Türkiye's value higher. The share who requested official documents or certificates online is 26.6% in Türkiye and 20.8% in the EU total; the gap is 5.8 points, with Türkiye's value higher. The share who requested benefits or entitlements online is 8.1% in Türkiye and 18.1% in the EU total; this time Türkiye's value is 10.0 points lower.

The share of people who accessed personal information and who requested documents is higher in Türkiye, while the share who requested benefits or entitlements is lower. These three types cannot be added up into the overall contact share: one person may have carried out several types of transaction in the same year,.

Two questions in the same survey, put to the same people under the same heading of public authorities, separate the two sides in opposite directions. This shows there is no single number for the level of digital public services: each transaction is a separate behaviour.

## The widest gap is in tax filing

Step five is submitting a tax declaration online. In 2025, 9.7% of individuals in Türkiye and 38.2% in the EU total submitted their tax declaration online in the last twelve months. Türkiye's value is 28.5 points lower. It is the widest gap of the five steps.

Nothing in the data explains the source of this gap. The denominator is all individuals. A person who does not have to file, or who files by a route that is not online, is also in the denominator. A low share therefore does not mean a count of people who wanted to file online but could not; it is only the share of people who did what the survey asked about.

We put five gaps side by side in the chart: household internet 1.4 points, internet use −4.0 points, online shopping −20.1 points, contact with public authorities 13.7 points, tax declaration −28.5 points. The gap is positive at two steps and negative at three. A one-directional story does not come out of this data.

## What changed in three years?

Our comparison starts from the year 2022, chosen because the question on contact with public authorities began in its new form at Eurostat in the year 2022; the tax declaration indicator was added later, so it is outside this comparison. In Türkiye, household internet rose by 2.0 points over 2022–2025, internet use by 6.3 points, online shopping by 8.7 points and contact with public authorities by 5.3 points.

In the EU total, the rises over the same period were 2.3, 3.9, 6.3 and 7.0 points respectively. The step that rose most in Türkiye is online shopping; in the EU total it is contact with public authorities. All four steps rose on both sides, but the size of the rise varies from step to step. Household internet is the step with the smallest rise on both sides; the data do not show the reason.

At the shopping step, Türkiye's value was 33.6% in 2022 and 42.3% in 2025. In the EU total the same values are 56.0% and 62.4%. In 2025, Türkiye's value is 20.1 points lower than the EU total.

## A counter-reading: are the steps really a ladder?

The word step in the title is a convenience. The five indicators do not form a ladder, because their denominators differ and it is not clear in every pair who sits inside whom. Household internet counts households. Shopping and internet use ask about the last three months; contact with public authorities and tax declarations ask about the last twelve months. Even the time windows are not the same.

Tax declaration shares are also published among those who used the internet in the last twelve months: 10.7% in Türkiye and 40.4% in the EU total. On this denominator Türkiye's value is 29.7 points lower. For shopping, the share is published among those who used the internet in the last three months. Because denominators and time windows change from indicator to indicator, we do not turn the steps into one nested chain.

A third reading concerns age structure. Eurostat also breaks the indicators down by age group and by level of education; we took only the total for all individuals. So the possible influence of differences in age structure between the two sides does not show in the numbers here.

Another reading is to see the differences between steps as a natural feature of behaviour. A person who does not shop online may use a public authority's website, and the reverse is also true. These two steps form different clusters among the same people. So a high value at one step does not imply a high value at another.

## What these numbers do not say

First, the data rest on people's own reports; whether a transaction was actually made has not been checked against records. Second, the survey is run in each country by the national statistical office with the same question list, but sample size and method can differ between countries; Eurostat keeps those details in national reports. Third, in indicators such as the tax declaration, the rule that the denominator is all individuals also carries differences in filing conditions between countries into the number. How much of the gap comes from behaviour and how much from conditions cannot be separated in the data.

Fourth, this article shows a narrow cross-section: two sides, two years and five indicators. Other steps, such as online banking, online learning or health services, may give a different table. It should also be noted that indicator definitions change over time: the question on contact with public authorities has been published in its new form since 2022, so we did not compare it directly with years before the year 2022.

## How we calculated

- From four Eurostat data tables we pulled the values for the year 2025 for Türkiye and the EU total: household internet access, internet use by individuals, online shopping, and contact with public authorities. For comparison we also took the values for the year 2022.
- To build the split of internet use, we added the share who last used the internet between three and twelve months ago to the share who last used it more than twelve months ago; the result is the share who used it earlier. We show it together with the shares of recent users and of people who never used it.
- The three groups add up to 100% in Türkiye; in the EU total the published values add up to 99.9%. This small difference may come from rounding in the published values, but we cannot verify that. The square chart completes the squares to one hundred.
- We took the shopping-frequency groups as Eurostat publishes them. We calculated the step gaps by subtracting the EU total's value from Türkiye's value. A minus sign means Türkiye's value is lower.
- We wrote the change over 2022–2025 as the percentage-point difference between the two years.

We give shares and gaps to one decimal; the evidence file holds the two-decimal values that Eurostat publishes. We pulled the data from Eurostat's dissemination interface in October 2026.
