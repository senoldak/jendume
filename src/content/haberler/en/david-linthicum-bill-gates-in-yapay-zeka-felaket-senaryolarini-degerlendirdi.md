Global AI, cloud, and cybersecurity expert David Linthicum addressed Bill Gates's warning that AI could potentially cause a billion deaths, noting that while technically possible in theory, it is not realistic. According to Linthicum, for AI to cause such a catastrophe, it would need control over critical infrastructure, weapons, or biological systems without human intervention, requiring massive integration failures and poor governance.

Experts emphasize that superintelligent systems independently wiping out humanity remain mostly hypothetical. The practical risk is not magic AI, but poorly engineered AI connected to real business and government systems. Fraud, deepfakes, data leakage, and biased decisions represent the real threats faced.

To ensure autonomous agents remain under human control, permissions must be limited, systems isolated, and human approval required for high-risk actions. Linthicum notes that a simple kill switch is not enough; safety must be engineered throughout the entire system architecture rather than bolted on at the end.

AI is altering the balance between attackers and defenders by lowering the cost and raising the volume of cyberattacks like phishing and voice cloning. On the defense side, multifactor authentication, zero-trust access, and strong identity checks remain essential.

Regarding biological weapons, while AI can lower knowledge barriers and summarize biology, fully enabling amateurs to create mass-casualty weapons remains speculative due to wet-lab constraints and material unavailability. Model screening and biosecurity standards are vital safeguards.
