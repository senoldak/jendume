Tony Fadell, who helped create the iPod and iPhone, has joined Smartex as chair, bringing veteran hardware experience to the AI-powered textile inspection startup. Alongside taking leadership, Fadell has invested about $10 million in the company. Smartex is launching two core products aimed at modernizing manufacturing quality control.

The first product, Smartex Inspect, attaches directly to existing textile machinery to examine material as it moves along the production line. The second product, Smartex IQ, converts data gathered from those inspections into actionable supply chain insights. According to the company, the system operates three times faster than a human inspector.

Global fashion retailer H&M serves as an investor and one of the initial customers for the new products. Industry executives note that apparel brands typically order about 5 percent more fabric than necessary to account for potential defects. Smartex estimates that eliminating this excess waste could save H&M roughly $100 million annually.

To power its core recognition capabilities, the system relies on a training library containing 50 billion fabric images. The technology successfully adjusts for varying lighting conditions, machine speeds, and subtle color shifts to match each client's specific quality standards. Founded in Porto in 2018, Smartex employs around 65 people and has previously installed 800 of its legacy systems across 10 countries.
