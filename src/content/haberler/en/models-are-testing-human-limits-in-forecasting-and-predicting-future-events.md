Newly developed AI agents have reached a new milestone in processing complex data and forecasting future events. Haifeng Xu, a computer scientist at the University of Chicago, and his colleagues began testing the forecasting skills of commercial models to examine how systems understand the world. During testing, models successfully guessed outcomes such as the NBA Championship winner, with GPT-5 performing the best, followed by Grok 4, Claude Sonnet, Gemini, and Llama.

The research team developed Prophet Arena to evaluate forecasting performance. Similarly, researchers at university and industry settings launched Prediction Arena, allocating $10,000 for advanced systems to place wagers on prediction markets across a 57-day period. Although the models consistently lost money in the experiment, researchers observed evidence of them improving over time.

Experts note that systems achieve optimal forecasting results through custom orchestration structures where individual agents handle distinct tasks like reviewing news and aggregating data. Despite this progress, human experts known as superforecasters still maintain an edge due to a deeper understanding of real-world dynamics. However, contests like the Metaculus Cup show that automated models can sometimes outperform even the best human predictors.

All of these predictive experiments suggest that industries predicated on trading may soon be transformed by automated agents. In the future, human traders may take on a new role supervising and coordinating AI agents capable of forecasting future events. As these systems improve, forecasting is becoming a powerful new way to measure progress in machine intelligence.
