Privacy-focused messaging platform Signal has introduced new security restrictions on iOS and Android to prevent operating system-level AI assistants from scanning user conversations. The app is restricting screenshot capabilities to stop AI agents embedded directly into mobile software from capturing private chats and sensitive user data.

Signal President Meredith Whittaker warned that agentic AI integrations are transforming the digital software environment into one that is increasingly hostile to user privacy. She emphasized that AI tools built into operating systems to scan on-screen information compromise Signal's core mission and its ability to independently guarantee message confidentiality.

Under the new security measures, a "Block Siri AI" setting is enabled by default on iOS 27. This stops Siri AI from scanning Signal content while simultaneously blocking screenshots within the app and the App Switcher. On Android devices, Signal automatically triggers its Screen Security setting whenever it detects an AI assistant trying to scan or capture the screen. Users can manually toggle off these protections through pop-up prompts, though Whittaker cautioned that doing so exposes their messages to OS agents.

Whittaker acknowledged that restricting screenshots is only an incomplete fix, as it cannot prevent AI agents from accessing messages already stored in the phone's filesystem. Signal took similar steps on Windows last year against Microsoft Recall, but macOS currently lacks effective mechanisms to restrict AI access without severe functionality compromises. For Mac users, Signal recommends manually adding the app to the Excluded Apps list within Siri settings.

Signal is urging major technology companies including Apple, Google, and Microsoft to provide developers with clear, documented opt-out mechanisms for AI data access across screens, files, and notifications. The organization is also calling for greater transparency on how AI agents process user data, noting that accessibility permissions are increasingly being exploited by third-party agents as a back-door route to private conversations.
