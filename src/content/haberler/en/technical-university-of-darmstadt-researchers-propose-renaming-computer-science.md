Forty researchers from the Technical University of Darmstadt have issued a joint position paper calling for computer science to reinvent itself. They argue that the discipline should be renamed "Computer and AI Science" because artificial intelligence has become a core technology for building and operating future computer systems. According to the authors, AI's ability to accelerate software development and automate complex processes makes traditional computer science fundamentals more important than ever.

The paper identifies three fundamental transitions: the lowering of obstacles in software creation, a massive surge in research speed, and the birth of a new computing model. Generative AI moves manual programming to a higher level of abstraction, allowing developers to describe desired features in natural language. This enables the creation of functional software versions in days rather than months, making custom software economically viable for small towns, midsize manufacturers, and specialized research projects that previously could not afford it.

The researchers suggest that computer science programs must shift their focus from merely writing code toward verifying and directing AI-generated systems. Because this requires a deep grasp of fundamentals, they propose courses where students must complete assignments without any AI assistance. They emphasize that humans remain essential for making value judgments and taking responsibility when goals like safety and speed conflict.

In what the authors call "Generative Computing," upcoming architectures and autonomous coding tools are anticipated to decode what humans intend and create their own logic and necessary code on the fly during execution. This shift requires a complete rethinking of how safety, privacy, and correctness are verified. The paper also calls on the discipline to address the enormous energy consumption of current AI through more efficient algorithms and to better weigh the social consequences of technological progress on the labor market and democracy. The signatories conclude that the future of AI cannot be left to economic forces alone, urging engagement with policymakers and civil society.
