Brookfield is pursuing a major artificial intelligence data center in northern England as the continent works to build out supporting infrastructure. Energy company Drax Group announced it has finalized an agreement with Brookfield alongside the asset manager platform Radiant to cooperatively establish a data center facility on the Drax Power Station grounds located in North Yorkshire.

The initiative aims initially for up to 100 megawatts of capacity targeted to become operational beginning in the first quarter of 2028. An additional 500 megawatts of capacity is planned to be integrated between 2028 and 2031, with intentions to secure over 1 gigawatt of capacity at the location at a later date.

Brookfield projects that aggregate investment, inclusive of semiconductors, might surpass £20 billion, translating to approximately $26.8 billion. The opening phase involves an investment commitment of £2.5 billion. Project leadership noted the venture provides a valuable framework for domestic researchers and startups to access high-performance computing locally.

The initial phase relies on grid power to serve commercial customers starting in early 2028. This initial segment remains contingent upon governmental and regulatory planning approvals, network permissions, and securing a tenant, with final investment decisions still pending.

While the United States has advanced rapidly in constructing massive data centers for artificial intelligence, European nations have encountered slower progress due to elevated energy expenses and grid connection delays. Conversely, Nordic territories continue to attract considerable infrastructure commitments owing to abundant power supplies and available land.
