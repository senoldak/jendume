Google has revised its privacy policy to introduce an exception regarding how personal data from Gmail, Google Drive, and Google Photos can be utilized. Taking effect in mid-October, the updated policy modifies a previous strict promise by allowing exceptions when users choose to make data from these services accessible to other Google features that support personalized advertising, such as Search and the Gemini app.

The mechanism relies on Personal Intelligence, a feature Google launched in January to let Gemini and Search tools draw on connected applications to tailor their responses. For example, a user asking for restaurant recommendations near a booked hotel can have their email booking automatically referenced. Google representatives emphasize that ads will follow the conversation context and user queries rather than directly targeting the inbox or photo library itself.

According to the company, advertisers do not receive the contents of Drive, Gmail, or Photos, nor do they gain access to sensitive details such as names and email addresses. The Personal Intelligence feature is currently operating as a beta for personal accounts of individuals over 18 and is rolling out globally, excluding regions like the European Economic Area, Switzerland, the UK, and Nigeria. Users retain the option to disconnect these services at any time through their account settings.

The integration of advertising into AI-driven search experiences mirrors broader industry trends, as platforms increasingly monetize conversational interfaces. With similar ad rollouts occurring across competing AI ecosystems, balancing personalization with user privacy remains a central challenge for tech companies and regulators alike.
