Google DeepMind has revealed the next generation of its AI-powered Gemini Robotics platform, version 2.5. This update brings robotic AI significantly closer to completing real-world tasks with the same proficiency as humans. According to the company, Gemini-powered robots are already being integrated into Mercedes-Benz factories to perform actual work, signaling a move toward a future where autonomous machines operate seamlessly alongside human workers.

The platform functions through several computational layers that bridge the gap between user instructions and physical movement. At its core is 'Gemini Embodied Reasoning,' a vision-language model that uses live video feeds to plan how to accomplish a specific task. This plan is then executed by the Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model, which generates the specific movements for the robot's actuators. This integrated approach allows the robot to understand its environment and react to it in real-time.

DeepMind reported that Gemini Robotics 2.5 excels in tasks requiring high degrees of manual dexterity. The model underwent evaluation across more than 200 activities, including using a pipette in a lab setting and serving food with tongs. A standout demonstration featured Apptronik’s Apollo 2 humanoid robot, which successfully tied a bow using its five-fingered hands. Manipulating deformable objects like ribbons has historically been one of the most difficult challenges in robotics due to their unpredictable shapes.

This advancement represents a major milestone in the integration of AI into the physical workforce. To ensure these systems remain safe as they become more capable, Google also introduced 'ASIMOV-jailbreak,' an open-source safety benchmark. This tool is designed to test and secure robotic AI against potential failures or malicious instructions, providing a framework for safe human-robot collaboration in industrial and domestic environments.
