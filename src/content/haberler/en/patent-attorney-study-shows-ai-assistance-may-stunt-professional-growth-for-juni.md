A new three-month field experiment conducted across eleven U.S. intellectual property law firms has shed light on how artificial intelligence impacts professional expertise. The study involved 133 patent attorneys and examined the effects of InFlow, an unreleased AI assistant from Google Labs. Researchers found that AI assistance significantly boosted immediate performance, particularly helping junior lawyers with fewer years of experience catch up to their senior peers during patent drafting tasks.

However, a different picture emerged when the AI was taken away during a subsequent review task. While senior attorneys retained their performance advantages, junior attorneys showed no lasting skill benefit, with their performance even splitting into more errors and mixed results. The findings suggest that relying on AI for drafting tasks can sometimes replace rather than accelerate genuine skill-building among entry-level professionals.

The research evaluated participants across tasks such as enforceability, technical accuracy, and clarity, using a blinded review process by an independent firm. Although AI successfully narrowed the performance gap between less and more experienced workers during assisted tasks, it exposed a vulnerability regarding internalized judgment when the tools were removed.

Similar patterns have surfaced in other industries, including software development and management consulting, raising broader questions about cognitive debt and the erosion of professional expertise. Experts argue that while individual firms benefit from immediate automation efficiencies, the long-term cost of stunted talent development could spread across entire professions.
