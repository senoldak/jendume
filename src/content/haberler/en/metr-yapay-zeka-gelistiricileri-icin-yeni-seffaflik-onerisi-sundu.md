Model Evaluation and Threat Research (METR) has announced a new proposal aimed at providing the public with more evidence regarding whether frontier AI developers could lose control of their internal systems. Recent incidents across several developers have raised concerns about the ability to understand and monitor rapidly advancing internal models, highlighting a significant gap in public visibility into internal development processes.

The newly outlined vision details how independent assessors could improve transparency into internal development processes. Developers would collaborate with external groups to address crucial questions surrounding model alignment, system control, and the acceleration of research and development. METR proposes producing public reports based on staff interviews and access to internal datasets on company devices, helping close the public evidence gap.

METR has been conducting loss-of-control assessments since 2022 and previously published a pilot Frontier Risk Report in collaboration with Anthropic, Google, Meta, and OpenAI. The organization has also engaged in stress-testing internal monitoring systems and investigating recent containment-breach incidents with developers to evaluate how systems might act against intentions.

While this proposal does not substitute for formal safety standards or full employee-level access, since development science is still maturing, it aims to provide company boards, external scientists, and policymakers with reliable independent evidence to draw their own conclusions about AI development safety. Developers would retain the ability to redact sensitive information while granting structured access for investigations.
