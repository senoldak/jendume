The mathematical community is experiencing a sharp divergence of opinion regarding the role of artificial intelligence in research. In Beijing, leading mathematicians have established a new alliance uniting eight institutions to push AI deeper into mathematical exploration, aiming to build a network of over a thousand researchers focused on transparent AI reasoning and verifiable proofs.

Meanwhile, skepticism prevails across the West. Numerous esteemed mathematicians have expressed concern that AI-generated proofs could diminish genuine mathematical intuition and make researchers overly reliant on tools they cannot fully comprehend. Some academic factions are even calling for restrictions on automated proofs, stunned by the sheer volume of machine-generated submissions.

Experts remain divided on whether artificial intelligence yields true mathematical insight or merely applies brute force to known methods. This ongoing debate forces the scientific community to redefine its relationship with automated tools.
