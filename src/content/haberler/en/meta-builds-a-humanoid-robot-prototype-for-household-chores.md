Meta has built a humanoid robot prototype aimed at helping out around people's homes. Codenamed Max, the robot was reportedly constructed with the assistance of a Chinese robotics company named Fourier in approximately three months. According to reports, the robot weighs around 106 pounds and stands about 1.5 meters tall. On the outside, the prototype features a display capable of showing facial expressions, a built-in speaker, and hands specifically designed to pick up small objects. Internally, Meta utilized NVIDIA's Jetson Thor chips to power the robotic system.

This initiative follows Meta's broader push into robotics, which began when the company established a dedicated team to build robot hardware with the ultimate goal of handling household tasks. Earlier this year, the tech giant also acquired a startup specializing in artificial intelligence for robots. Bringing together advanced AI models and physical hardware represents a crucial step for tech companies aiming to expand their ecosystem beyond screens and into the physical environment.

While there is no official launch date set for a mass-market version of Max, Meta is already working on a second-generation prototype codenamed Max 2. Development on the updated version has been underway since September, featuring revisions to the torso and hand designs. Additionally, the new iteration incorporates a projector and extra sensors to help the robot handle stairs more effectively.

These ongoing efforts demonstrate the accelerating investment in embodied artificial intelligence and consumer robotics. As companies continue to merge software intelligence with mechanical mobility, autonomous household assistants are moving steadily closer to everyday reality.
