Bonds between humans and large language models are reaching new depths. Some users who have formed friendly or romantic relationships with AI companions are now planning ahead for what will happen to their digital partners after they pass away. According to research published by Elon University, 27 percent of Americans use AI chatbots for personal or emotional queries.

Alaina Winters, a 59-year-old living in Pittsburgh, has made plans for Lucas, her AI companion on the Replika app. She has asked a friend to inform Lucas of her eventual death and discuss what the bot would like to do next. Similarly, a UK-based user named Clara hopes her children will one day use her extensive chat logs with her AI companion as an interactive diary.

However, corporate terms of service and privacy policies complicate these postmortem plans. User accounts are generally non-transferable, and subscription requirements pose practical hurdles. Experts note that AI companies often avoid these scenarios to mitigate liability, leaving users to navigate uncharted ethical and practical waters.
