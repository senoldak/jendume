A fundamental shift is occurring in the production processes of new content published on Steam, one of the digital gaming world's largest platforms. A recent analysis of public data shows that as of October 2026, a striking 49.9% of games uploaded to the platform utilized artificial intelligence technologies. This statistic proves that one out of every two new productions in the gaming world now features AI tools in its development.

Looking at where developers utilize this technology, visual design takes the lead. Among producers using AI, 63% turn to these tools for artwork and creating visual assets. This is followed by text writing and translation processes at 30%, while AI support is utilized at 26% for preparing store pages, 21% for music production, and 16% for direct code generation. These figures demonstrate that AI has moved beyond being a mere assistant to permeating the fundamental building blocks of games.

This transformation in the industry is also bringing about a "mass-produced game" model. When examining publishers who released more than 10 games within 2026, it was found that approximately 70% of the games in their portfolios utilized AI. This situation points to the birth of a new business model where some publishers rapidly release numerous games with AI assistance, hoping a few will become popular enough to generate significant revenue.

While this development can be seen as the democratization of game development processes, it also ignites debates regarding the moderation of content quality on platforms. The fact that even individuals with limited technical knowledge can produce games thanks to AI makes managing massive libraries like Steam and highlighting high-quality games increasingly difficult every day.
