BlackBerry executives have identified artificial intelligence-driven machinery as a significant expansion path for the former smartphone giant. Once a dominant mobile phone manufacturer, the Canada-based enterprise has successfully reinvented itself through its QNX operating system. The software, which powers features from braking systems to semi-autonomous driving, is currently embedded in more than 275 million vehicles worldwide. However, BlackBerry is increasingly looking beyond the automotive industry to capitalize on embodied intelligence and automated systems. Robotics has emerged as one of the fastest-growing business segments within QNX. Unlike generative AI, physical automation allows smart hardware to interact safely and reliably with the real world. This expanding focus is directly contributing to the enterprise's growing financial commitments. The organization recently confirmed that its QNX royalty commitments surpassed $1.2 billion, expanding by more than $250 million across two quarters. Growth is attributed to automakers transitioning to advanced computing systems, expanded software offerings, and rising demand for real-world automated applications. Investors have responded positively to the turnaround, with BlackBerry shares surging amid the strategic shift. Furthermore, BlackBerry highlighted its ongoing partnership with Nvidia, noting that Nvidia has standardized on QNX as the platform of choice for its safety stack, opening new doors in automated mobile robots and autonomous forklifts across global markets.
