Instagram head Adam Mosseri has addressed the growing trend of AI accounts stealing work from real comedians and visual artists to create manipulated videos. As highlighted by affected creators like comedian George Brett Olson, whose work was ripped off by an AI account with hundreds of thousands of followers, perpetrators are using synthetic videos of voluptuous young women to drive traffic to OnlyFans pages. In response to these incidents, Mosseri admitted that the platform faces a tricky problem.

Mosseri stated that trying to completely ban AI content from the platform is not practical because it will become harder to identify and not all of it is harmful. However, he emphasized that Instagram is very invested in driving traffic to original creators and is looking into what can be done about wholesale content theft. While users can already submit traditional copyright claims, Mosseri noted that this approach is often insufficient because many performers and dancers do not go through the formal process of copyrighting their material.

Copyright infringement and theft are not entirely new issues, but generative artificial intelligence makes it easier than ever for bad actors to rip people off with minimal effort. By setting up simple AI workflows, creators can pump out endless low-effort content without needing to perform or draw anything themselves. It remains to be seen how Meta and Instagram will evolve their moderation policies to protect creators from systemic AI theft beyond relying on piecemeal reports.
