Britain's largest power plant site is set to host the country's largest AI datacentre following a £20bn infrastructure agreement in North Yorkshire. The owner of the Drax power plant has partnered with infrastructure investor Brookfield and AI platform Radiant to develop a massive facility designed to reach an eventual capacity of more than 1 gigawatt, with operations slated to begin by early 2028.

During the initial phase, Drax will provide land access and grid connections on-site, with ambitions to supply direct power to the datacentre by the early 2030s. Drax Chief Executive Will Gardiner noted that the project will secure the long-term future of the site, protect hundreds of jobs, and strengthen sovereign AI capabilities in the region.

The Drax facility, which currently produces nearly 5 percent of the UK's electricity by burning biomass pellets, faces scrutiny from analysts over carbon emissions and sustainability claims. The UK government previously indicated a more limited role for subsidies moving forward, prompting the company to seek new revenue streams through technology partnerships.

Under the first phase, the datacentre aims to become operational by the first quarter of 2028 with up to 100 megawatts of compute capacity. The announcement follows a government policy draft describing datacentres as fundamental to the national economy, though congested grid infrastructure continues to spark debates over how surging power demands will be met sustainably.
