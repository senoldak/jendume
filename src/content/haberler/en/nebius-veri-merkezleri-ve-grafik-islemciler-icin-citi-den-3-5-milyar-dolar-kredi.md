Amsterdam-based AI cloud infrastructure company Nebius has secured a $3.5 billion credit facility with Citi to finance the expansion of its data centre network and acquire more graphics processing units. Announced on Thursday, the secured facility carries a 364-day maturity with an interest rate starting at the Term SOFR benchmark plus 2.75% annually, which can adjust up to 3.50%. The company stated that the capital will be directed towards infrastructure development, hardware purchases, and general corporate operations.

Nebius Chief Operating Officer Ophir Nave noted that this facility primarily covers the company's near-term expansion. For subsequent phases, Nave indicated that Nebius could borrow against its assets, backed by more than $40 billion in long-term contracts from investment-grade corporate customers. The firm's ongoing capital strategy also relies on customer prepayments and positive operating cash flow.

The financing arrives amid growing investor scrutiny over how emerging AI cloud specialists, commonly known as neoclouds, fund their rapid build-outs. Earlier this month, default insurance costs on debt issued by competitor CoreWeave surged to elevated levels, sparking wider industry caution. However, Nebius Chief Revenue Officer Marc Boroditsky clarified that the company does not hold comparable levels of distressed debt, pointing out that computing capacity prices are escalating faster than anticipated due to severe hardware shortages.

Nebius, which operates computing infrastructure across Finland, France, the United Kingdom, Spain, Estonia, and Iceland, also launched live pricing feeds for preemptible spot GPUs during Nvidia's GTC conference in Berlin. Alongside an expansion of its European AI Builder Program with $10 million in compute grants, the company collaborated with Nvidia and robotics startup Sereact to deploy machine learning training systems for logistics warehouse automation.
