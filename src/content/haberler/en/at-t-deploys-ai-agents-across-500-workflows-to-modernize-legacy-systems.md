Telecommunications company AT&T has transitioned nearly 500 agentic AI workflows into active production. Luke Corbin, Assistant Vice President of Digital Transformation and AI at AT&T Business, revealed that the company's platform processes approximately 45 billion AI tokens daily and executes over 1 million transactions per month. AT&T plans to triple the number of these autonomous agentic workflows within the next year.

Rather than automating isolated tasks, AT&T focuses on completely redesigning business processes using autonomous AI agents. Spearheaded by its Business Agent Foundry unit established in early 2025, the effort systematically eliminates manual handoffs, redundant steps, and software dependencies. The deployment bridges decades-old mainframe computers with modern cloud environments. For instance, automating manual bifold ticket processing for fiber installation order changes reduced the provisioning time for dedicated internet connections in fiber-ready buildings by half.

Instead of managing countless separate models, AT&T uses a small, centralized cluster of specialized planning and retrieval agents. While employees can build personal productivity agents, all customer-facing workflows undergo strict security, privacy, and compliance reviews. Every workflow initially operates under human supervision and shifts to fully autonomous execution only after reaching accuracy rates in the mid-to-upper 90% range, backed by continuous performance monitoring.

Carrying legacy code and infrastructure accumulated over its 151-year history, AT&T faces substantial costs in maintaining older systems and training staff. Management notes that offsetting these costs through AI workflows justified the operational shift, with production AI investments yielding a fivefold return within a single year. Additionally, sales agents now automatically verify customer locations and fiber availability before human representatives spend time on prospects.
