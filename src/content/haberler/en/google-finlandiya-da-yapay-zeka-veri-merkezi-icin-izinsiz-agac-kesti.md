Finnish authorities ordered Google to halt construction at two northern sites after the company cleared hundreds of thousands of trees without completing mandatory environmental impact assessments. The Finnish Licensing and Supervision Authority (LVV) intervened following the clearing of 330 hectares near the town of Muhos and a secondary site in Otanmäki. Conservationists reported that roughly 250,000 trees were removed in Muhos, alongside an estimated 150,000 trees felled in Otanmäki.

The cleared sites form part of Google's 13 billion euro AI infrastructure plan, representing the largest corporate project of its type in Finland. Tech giants have flocked to the Nordic country to run artificial intelligence systems, attracted by its cool climate, geological stability, and abundant land. Currently, Finland hosts 145 new datacentre projects across various development stages, but the unauthorized clearcutting has triggered an intense national debate over the cost of the AI boom.

Antti Järvinen, Google's country manager for Finland, publicly apologized for the clearings, claiming the company operated under its understanding of local laws at the time. However, environmental groups highlighted severe disruptions, including runoff contaminating streams feeding the Oulujoki River and displaced wildlife entering nearby residential villages. Because the felling occurred illegally, the harvested timber cannot be sold commercially, compounding the waste.

Academic experts, including researchers from the University of Helsinki, noted that foreign datacentres yield minimal lasting benefits for local economies despite consuming significant renewable power reserves. More than 93,000 citizens have signed a petition demanding stricter regulation and compensation fees for affected communities. The controversy highlights the growing friction between corporate AI development schedules and domestic environmental standards across Europe.
