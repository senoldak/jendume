Anthropic filed paperwork to go public, highlighting the evolving and uncertain regulatory and political environment as a primary business risk factor. With its IPO expected and new congressional representatives slated to take office, the company faces a narrow window before political scrutiny intensifies. While the current Congress has not heavily constrained AI companies, voters across party lines increasingly oppose data centers and express concern over AI displacing jobs. Campaign advertisements targeting data centers have surged, and candidates in several states have made opposition to these facilities a core part of their platforms. In Ohio, data center records and past tax breaks have become a focal point in the senate race, while candidates in states like New Jersey, Texas, and Michigan advocate for stricter regulations, closed-loop cooling systems, or pauses on AI model training. Public sentiment and potential legislative shifts indicate that AI companies and their energy-intensive data centers will face tougher oversight. Political campaigns targeting data centers have involved significant spending by candidates and outside figures, reflecting the deep public resistance to such infrastructure. By timing its IPO now, Anthropic may be securing a brief respite before the political environment becomes significantly more hostile. Campaign spending has exceeded tens of millions of dollars across multiple battleground states where data centers and tax breaks remain central debate topics among voters and candidates alike.
