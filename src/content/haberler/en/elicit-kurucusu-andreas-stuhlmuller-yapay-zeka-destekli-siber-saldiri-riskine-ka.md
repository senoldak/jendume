Andreas Stuhlmüller, cofounder and CEO of the artificial intelligence research company Elicit, has warned that it is plausible for AI-powered cyberattacks to target power grids, financial systems, internet infrastructure, or water supplies. This warning follows a recent incident in France where hundreds of supermarkets belonging to major chains were hit by a cyberattack that exploited vulnerabilities in internet-connected refrigeration controllers, causing temperatures to rise, destroying food, and sparking fires. Although authorities launched an investigation into the incident, there is currently no confirmed evidence that artificial intelligence was involved in the French supermarket attack. However, Stuhlmüller points out that AI capabilities are advancing to the point where they could make certain cyberattacks faster, cheaper, and easier to scale. Artificial intelligence tools can assist malicious actors with analyzing software, identifying vulnerabilities, and automating parts of cybersecurity research without requiring entirely new attack types. To prepare for potential major disruptions, Stuhlmüller offers specific recommendations, including keeping physical cash on hand, investing in backup power solutions like lithium iron phosphate batteries and solar panels, securing alternative internet access, and storing at least two weeks worth of water and nonperishable food. While experts note that there is no immediate proof of an impending catastrophic AI-driven attack on heavily protected infrastructure, the warning underscores the growing importance of emergency preparedness for various digital and physical disruptions.
