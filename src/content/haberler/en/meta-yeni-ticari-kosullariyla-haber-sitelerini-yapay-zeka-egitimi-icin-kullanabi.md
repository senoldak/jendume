Meta is set to implement new commercial terms on October 30, 2026, which grant the company broad permissions to scan, collect, and use content from business websites for product development and artificial intelligence training. Legal experts warn that this clause could serve as a legal basis for scraping news publisher content without explicit licensing agreements.

The updated terms apply to businesses and news organizations utilizing tools such as Meta Business Suite, Facebook, and Instagram. According to legal scholars like Cornell University professor James Grimmelmann, the wording opens the door for Meta to utilize publisher content to train AI models, potentially creating a direct competitor to news outlets. Media lawyer Matt Topic estimates that the value of such content could run into millions of dollars in licensing fees.

Meta representatives maintain that the update merely consolidates existing scraping provisions and will not alter how publisher sites are scanned, adding that site owners can still use robots.txt files to manage access. However, legal analysts debate whether these commercial terms might override standard blocking instructions. Businesses that refuse the terms are required to stop using Meta’s commercial tools entirely.

This ultimatum places online publishers in a difficult position, forcing them to weigh social media traffic against intellectual property protection. Industry groups, including the US-based News/Media Alliance, have formally objected to the policy, intensifying ongoing debates over AI training data and copyright law.
