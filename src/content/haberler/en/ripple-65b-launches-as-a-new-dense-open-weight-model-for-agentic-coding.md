Ricursive has introduced Ripple-65B, a new AI model specifically designed for agentic coding and software development tasks. Featuring 65 billion parameters, the model utilizes a "dense" architecture, distinguishing it from the increasingly common "Mixture-of-Experts" (MoE) designs. By opting for this streamlined structure, the developers aim to reduce infrastructure complexity while maintaining high-level performance in coding benchmarks.

In the current AI landscape, models are often scaled to massive sizes, which can lead to significant computational overhead. Ripple-65B challenges this trend by proving that a dense model can achieve frontier-level results. It was pretrained on 12 trillion tokens and supports a context window of up to 1 million tokens. This capability allows the model to process extensive codebases and complex software projects in a single pass, making it a powerful tool for developers.

The model's performance is highlighted by its score of 80.5 on the Terminal-Bench 2.1 benchmark, which the developers claim is the highest among US open-weight models of its class. A notable aspect of this release is its cost-efficiency; the total research and development cost was reported at $32 million. The team estimates this to be roughly one-tenth of the cost associated with previous leaders in the open-weight coding space.

Ripple-65B is released under the Apache 2.0 license, making it accessible for developers to integrate into their own workflows. Beyond cloud-based deployment, the model is optimized for local hardware, including NVIDIA DGX Spark, thanks to experimental quantization builds. This versatility underscores a shift toward making high-performance AI more accessible, moving beyond the reliance on massive, centralized computing clusters.
