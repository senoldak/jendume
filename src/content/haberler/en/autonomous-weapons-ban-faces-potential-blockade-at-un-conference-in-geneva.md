The United Nations Convention on Certain Conventional Weapons (CCW) Review Conference in Geneva has reached a juncture regarding the future of autonomous weapon systems. While a ban on these systems, which are becoming increasingly common in modern conflict and can select and attack targets without human intervention, is on the agenda, major military powers including the US, Russia, and China are expected to block the initiative.

Autonomous weapons raise serious ethical questions regarding accountability on the battlefield and the protection of civilians. Systems that make decisions without human oversight challenge the moral boundaries of what are often termed "killer robots." Reports from the Ukrainian defense industry indicate that fully autonomous drones have previously been used in combat, resulting in casualties.

There are 128 states party to the treaty, and a significant majority, approximately 130 nations, support restrictions or a total ban on autonomous weapons. About 70 countries are ready to begin negotiations on the matter. However, the CCW operates on a consensus basis, allowing a single state to stall action. Major powers argue that existing international law is sufficient, proposing an investigation into how current rules apply rather than enacting a new ban.

Experts note that major military powers are reluctant to agree to a wide-ranging ban due to fears of losing strategic advantages over rivals. Concerns that defensive systems might also be captured by such a ban further complicate negotiations. If this conference fails to produce results, advocates for a ban aim to move the issue to the UN General Assembly, where negotiations can be initiated with a simple majority, potentially accelerating the process.
