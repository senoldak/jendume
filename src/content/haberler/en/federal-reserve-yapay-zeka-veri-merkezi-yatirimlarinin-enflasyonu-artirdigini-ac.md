Federal Reserve officials have pointed out that the massive boom in artificial intelligence data center investments is contributing to inflationary pressures across the broader economy. According to Federal Reserve Bank of Chicago president Austan Goolsbee, AI-tied inflation is expanding beyond directly adjacent industries, causing prices to rise in unrelated spaces due to straight, old-fashioned overheating demand. Economists warn that when aggregate demand outpaces productive capacity, it can lead to higher prices, reduced consumption, and potential economic slowdowns.

The construction boom has already triggered knock-on effects, such as consumer electronics becoming practically unaffordable and severe shortages of skilled labor available for housing projects. While grocery prices have yet to be directly impacted, economists are wondering whether it is only a matter of time. As inflation proves persistent, the central bank faces difficult choices regarding whether raising interest rates will be able to meaningfully address the rising price pressures.

Meanwhile, tens of billions of dollars continue to be funneled into AI data centers by major tech companies, masking fundamental problems in the economy and raising concerns about potential consequences if the AI bubble were to burst. Sectors facing immediate construction boom effects are struggling with soaring costs, while the central bank signals that continuing to raise rates remains an option if inflation persists, which could ultimately make investment tougher for AI companies as well.
