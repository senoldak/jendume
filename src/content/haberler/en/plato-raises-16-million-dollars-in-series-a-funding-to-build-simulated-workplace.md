Plato, a San Francisco startup building simulated workplaces for artificial intelligence agents, has raised 16 million dollars in a Series A funding round led by Nava Ventures. The round included participation from Gradient Ventures, Transpose Platform, and Microsoft AI chief Mustafa Suleyman. This new financing brings the company's total funding to 19 million dollars following a seed round previously led by Gradient Ventures.

The startup replicates the software and tasks of real-world jobs, including customer support, finance, accounting, human resources, and healthcare administration. AI laboratories use these simulated environments to train agents through trial and error, testing their capabilities without risking real financial assets or sensitive records. According to Plato co-founder and chief executive Robert Farlow, mastering complex work comes from experience, and the firm evaluates both final outcomes and operational processes to ensure rules are followed. Backer Mustafa Suleyman has also argued that artificial intelligence must be provably under control before public release.

Founded in 2025, Plato will use the fresh capital to compensate professionals for recreating their jobs as simulations and to deploy AI agents into real tasks for enhanced learning. Demand for structured training grounds for autonomous agents is rapidly expanding across the technology sector. As the industry pushes for safer deployment, other firms like micro1 and Patronus AI are also investing heavily in testing environments, company data integration, and simulation tools.
