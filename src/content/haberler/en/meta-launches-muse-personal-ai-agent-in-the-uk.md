Meta has launched its personal AI agent, Muse, in the United Kingdom. Available to users aged 18 and over as of November 23, 2026, the system is designed to autonomously manage daily tasks. Muse can handle activities such as booking travel, purchasing gifts, sending emails, and organizing calendars on behalf of the user. The platform is accessible via iOS, Android, the web, and WhatsApp. The system moves from simple information retrieval to "agentic" capabilities where the system executes actions. Security is a central pillar of this rollout. Meta emphasizes that Muse cannot make purchases or send emails without explicit user permission. The system does not have access to user passwords or payment methods, and every action is monitored by a separate "security agent" to ensure it aligns with the permissions granted by the user. To enhance functionality, Meta has partnered with major British businesses, including Marks & Spencer, Currys, Sainsbury’s, and easyJet, allowing Muse to navigate these platforms more effectively. Already successful in the US market, Muse is powered by "Muse Spark," a model developed by Meta’s super intelligence lab. Despite the convenience, the technology faces scrutiny. UK regulators have noted that while AI agents can drive innovation, they also introduce risks related to fraud and data misuse. Reports have surfaced regarding technical glitches during internal testing, though Meta maintains that these issues have been largely addressed. AI agents are becoming more integrated into daily life, and the balance between autonomy and human oversight remains a focus for developers.
