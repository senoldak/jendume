Foundation Future Industries has officially unveiled its second-generation humanoid robot, the Phantom MKII, alongside the Cortex II artificial intelligence system that controls it. The startup aims to deploy the new machine in environments ranging from construction sites to industrial factories. Company leadership has also indicated potential applications in defense and logistics, sparking discussions on the future of autonomous systems.

The Phantom MKII features significant hardware upgrades compared to its predecessor, including weather-resistant joints and modular parts that allow for field repairs. Operating on a battery that can last up to six hours under certain conditions, the robot is capable of walking while carrying heavy payloads. Redesigned actuators have successfully reduced component size while increasing overall performance and lowering manufacturing costs.

Powering the hardware is the Cortex II AI system, which is engineered to learn physical tasks by observing human workers. According to the company, the system can acquire certain skills through a minimal number of demonstrations. Foundation plans to begin manufacturing the new robots in early 2027, scaling up production from several hundred units to thousands by the end of the year in the San Francisco Bay Area.

While the primary commercial focus remains on construction and heavy industry, the company's openness to military applications has drawn considerable attention. Leadership argues that ground-based robotic logistics could eventually reduce reliance on traditional air strikes, though the intersection of humanoid robotics and warfare continues to raise important ethical questions across the technology sector.
