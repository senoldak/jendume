Hewlett Packard Enterprise (HPE) has unveiled HPE Spaceborne Computer-4, an advanced computing module designed to power artificial intelligence workloads and conduct the first high-performance computing (HPC) benchmark tests on the lunar surface. The initiative aims to build the computational backbone required for long-term space exploration and the emerging lunar economy.

The system will be integrated into Astrolab’s FLIP rover as part of a mission scheduled for launch in late 2026 under NASA’s Commercial Lunar Payload Services (CLPS) initiative. The rover, carrying the computer module, will be transported to the lunar south pole aboard Voyager Technologies’ Griffin-1 lander to evaluate advanced compute capabilities under extreme extraterrestrial environments.

During the mission, the computer will deploy large language models (LLMs) to interpret scientific telemetry, process visual feeds, and support autonomous monitoring without constant Earth connectivity. It will also process LiDAR data for high-resolution mapping, navigation, and terrain modeling. Through NASA’s FLUID experiment, the hardware will demonstrate decentralized federated learning coordinating neural network models trained on data from Earth, the Moon, and the International Space Station.

Engineered specifically for the harsh conditions near the lunar south pole, the fanless and lightweight unit features low-power central and graphics processing units. Storage resilience is provided by KIOXIA solid-state flash drives, while Deloitte will run its Silent Shield system to detect anomalies and test predictive cyber analysis on the Moon.
