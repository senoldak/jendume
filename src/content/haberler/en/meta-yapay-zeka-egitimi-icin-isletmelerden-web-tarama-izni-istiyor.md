Meta is introducing new terms for businesses as part of its plans to scan and scrape websites to train its artificial intelligence models. According to reports, companies may face a choice between remaining within Meta's commercial ecosystem and granting permission for their web content to be automatically scraped. This situation demonstrates that social networks are no longer just communication channels, but critical infrastructures that directly determine the digital visibility of businesses.

As highlighted by Cornell Law School Professor James Grimmelmann, legal dimensions lie at the core of the debate. News organizations and online businesses want to have a say in the conditions under which the materials they publish are utilized. However, tying access to the commercial services offered by platforms to the acceptance of specific contractual terms limits users' ability to negotiate these conditions. Thus, the delicate balance between the rights of content owners and the market power of major tech companies is brought back into question.

Another concern surrounding the updated terms involves the potential impact of automated scraping on paywalls and membership systems. Such systems are vital, particularly for news publishers, to protect subscription revenues and control access to exclusive content. Although it is noted that website owners can block automated crawlers via the robots.txt file, this mechanism alone does not serve as an impenetrable technical firewall, and crawler compliance largely depends on the operator's behavior.

These developments clearly reveal the shifting power dynamics within the digital content economy. The coincidence of Meta's moves to generate revenue from external links with the conditions surrounding the use of content in artificial intelligence systems forces publishers to make strategic decisions. Because traffic from social networks is vital for independent publishers, leaving a platform can mean losing a significant portion of their readership. This situation is reshaping the future of content ownership and digital access rights in the age of artificial intelligence.
