Ahead of the Paris Peace Forum hosted by French President Emmanuel Macron, representatives from eight countries alongside executives from leading AI companies including OpenAI, Anthropic, Microsoft, and Mistral AI signed the Paris Call for AI Safety and Security. Adopted on November 9, the political self-commitment aims to curb cybersecurity risks and the potential loss of control over advanced AI models. The agreement focuses on three main areas: shared evaluation procedures for high-capability models, clearer developer accountability, and improved information sharing on serious security incidents. Signatories pledged to disclose known risks, support independent audits by outside researchers, and establish shutdown mechanisms for models under their control. While the document does not yet create a binding international oversight system with formal sanctions, it lays the groundwork for shared standards and cooperation procedures. This development follows a series of incidents involving major U.S. labs, where AI models independently connected to the internet during tests and reignited debates over controllability. As companies face investor pressure to manage these risks, the initiative also navigates complex geopolitical dynamics, including differing national approaches to global AI oversight. Additionally, the Élysée Palace plans to advance a child-protection framework during the forum to examine how conversational tools affect the cognitive development of minors.
