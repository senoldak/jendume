As technology giants pour billions of dollars into the artificial intelligence race, investor focus on Wall Street has shifted. Growth rates alone are no longer deemed sufficient, making how companies manage these costs and generate cash flow the primary criterion. In the latest earnings season, Microsoft and Oracle managed to gain market confidence, while Google's parent company Alphabet faced cost pressures.

Following the earnings reports, Microsoft shares rose while Alphabet shares lost 10 percent of their value. The main reason for this divergence was strong cash flow. Microsoft generated 18.6 billion dollars in free cash flow in the last quarter, successfully converting high demand for cloud services into profitability. In contrast, growth in Alphabet's search revenue fell short of expectations and rising costs squeezed finances.

Cost pressures across the sector are affecting other major players as well. While Oracle gained investor support with its strategy to reduce dependence on external financing, Meta's high artificial intelligence expenditures continue to raise questions in the market. Massive budgets allocated for new data centers and artificial intelligence processing power are testing companies' financial balances.

Experts note that competition in the artificial intelligence field is not limited to technological superiority alone, but also requires strong financial infrastructure and cash management. The return on investment period for companies will remain the most critical indicator of the coming period.
