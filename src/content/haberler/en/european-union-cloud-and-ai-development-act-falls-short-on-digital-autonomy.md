The European Union's proposed Cloud and AI Development Act, designed to strengthen the bloc's cloud and artificial intelligence sectors, is facing significant criticism. Researchers at the Institute for Information Law at the University of Amsterdam have concluded that the legislative proposal falls short of its stated goal to reduce dependence on non-European providers. Currently, a small number of U.S. technology giants control the vast majority of the European cloud market, and the region spends hundreds of billions of euros annually on foreign IT products.

While the European Commission aims to spur investment in data center expansion and streamline administrative rules, the researchers argue that these measures undermine digital sovereignty. They warn that the draft legislation could inadvertently prevent public organizations from choosing European alternatives. Furthermore, the rapid expansion of data centers lacks adequate focus on sustainability and community interests. Experts argue that true digital autonomy requires robust public procurement requirements, democratic participation, and genuine support for open European alternatives rather than just expanding infrastructure.

The legislative proposal seeks to make investments in large-scale data center expansion more attractive by cutting bureaucratic hurdles and easing licensing demands. It also establishes standards for public-sector procurement of cloud services. However, researchers point out that providers and developers from outside the EU would still retain a strong foothold under the current framework, potentially undermining national ambitions to achieve greater digital sovereignty.

The researchers assessed the proposal across five distinct areas and concluded that the focus on expanding data center capacity is insufficient. They emphasize that achieving digital self-governance demands transparent ecological guidelines, civic engagement, stringent public purchasing mandates, and robust backing for local and open-source alternatives to effectively counter the dominance of non-European providers.
