The integration of large language models into creative processes has introduced a new and controversial business model to the literary world. South African writer Coral Hart has created a notable case in the publishing industry by publishing over 370 novels since February 2025, all assisted by artificial intelligence. Hart, who has 14 years of experience in the publishing industry, has transformed her traditional writing career into a fully digital and rapid production process by utilizing a variety of AI tools.

The author's workflow relies on the specific strengths of different AI models. Leveraging ChatGPT for its proficiency in dialogue, Hart utilizes other models like Grok for more "spicy" or adult-oriented scenes. By combining these tools, she claims she can draft an entire book in a single sequence. These works, published under more than ten different pen names, are reportedly achieving commercial success, which Hart cites as validation for her approach.

This development brings significant debate regarding the future of literature and the definition of "authorship." The shift from human-driven creativity to an algorithmic production process has drawn intense criticism from both readers and fellow authors. Despite receiving thousands of negative messages, Hart maintains that this method is an irreversible reality of the modern era.

For the publishing industry, this situation represents the fine line between the democratization of content creation and the commodification of quality. A process that once took months to write a novel has now reached a speed where one book is produced every 1.75 days. This pace serves as a concrete example of how quickly AI can replace human labor in commercial fiction, particularly in genres like romance that rely on established formulas. Whether such "mass production" models will become standard practice in the publishing industry remains a subject of ongoing debate.
