US stock markets experienced a severe downturn driven by a rapid decline in tech stocks tied to artificial intelligence. The tech-heavy Nasdaq 100 index—home to major tech companies like Apple, Nvidia, Microsoft Corp, and Amazon—dropped 2.6 percent, tracking toward a $1 trillion drop. The losses were further accelerated as Amazon shares shed about two percent at the opening of trading. Although Amazon's third-quarter earnings report showed continued momentum in its cloud computing unit, the company's capital expenditures for 2026 ballooned to $220 billion, largely due to AI investments, failing to ease underlying market fears.

This rout followed a massive plunge in shares of Google's parent company Alphabet, which plummeted over 12.6 percent. It marked the company's worst single trading day ever, wiping out over $500 billion in market value. These events have intensified worries about a potential AI bubble, as trillions of dollars remain tied up in major tech companies that still struggle to prove the commercial viability of the technology. Many market participants are closely evaluating these massive financial commitments.

Market observers are increasingly questioning whether the hyperscale approach—where tech giants build massive, energy-intensive data centers—is sustainable compared to more cost-effective and lightweight alternatives. Experts note that if the growth hypotheses and revenue projections fail to materialize, it could trigger a significant ripple effect across the broader economy. Stakeholders continue to watch whether these massive capital investments will ultimately yield sustainable returns.
