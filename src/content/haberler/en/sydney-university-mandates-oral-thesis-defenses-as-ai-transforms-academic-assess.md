The rapid advancement of artificial intelligence is fundamentally transforming how universities assess student performance, pushing institutions away from traditional written assignments. The University of Sydney has become the first major Australian institution to mandate oral examinations for all students enrolled in its intensive honours programs, requiring them to verbally defend their research before a panel of professors. University officials state that written research has become an unreliable form of assessment due to the widespread availability of advanced text-generation tools.

While some students have expressed frustration and stress over the abrupt change, noting that oral components can account for a significant portion of their final grades, faculty members are working to adapt to the new guidelines. For instance, engineering students faced interviews making up half of their overall grade while faculty rushed through assessing two hundred and fifty students in two days, whereas history students encountered pass-or-fail hurdle requirements for their twenty-thousand-word theses.

Other prominent institutions, including the University of Melbourne, Monash University, and the Australian Catholic University, are also introducing similar oral assessment formats. Educators emphasize that these measures are necessary to demonstrate academic integrity, maintain social licences, and secure external accreditation in an educational landscape reshaped by artificial intelligence. University management notes that guidance is provided to students to manage the transition and address concerns about language fluency.
