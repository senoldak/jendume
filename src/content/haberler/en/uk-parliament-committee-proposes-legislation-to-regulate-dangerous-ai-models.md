The UK Parliament's Business, Innovation, Science and Trade Committee has proposed a significant shift in AI governance with the introduction of a new "AI Safety and Security Bill." This legislative proposal calls for the creation of a powerful regulatory body with the authority to halt the training, testing, and deployment of dangerous AI models. Currently, the UK’s AI Safety Institute (AISI) conducts critical testing, but its operations rely on voluntary cooperation, and it lacks the legal power to block the release of potentially harmful systems.

This development marks one of the most concrete efforts to address the risks associated with the rapid, unchecked growth of AI technologies. Committee Chair Liam Byrne described ’s AI leaders as "modern Dr. Frankensteins" who create systems without fully understanding their consequences. He argues that the AI sector requires strict oversight mechanisms similar to those in the aviation, pharmaceutical, or nuclear industries. The proposed legislation would mandate that companies substantiate their safety claims with evidence and allow independent researchers to audit their systems.

From an economic perspective, the committee estimates that AI could boost the UK economy by up to £400 billion over the next decade. However, they emphasize that this growth must be built on a foundation of safety. The proposal also includes stronger legal protections for whistleblowers who face corporate pressure when raising safety concerns, alongside mandatory incident reporting requirements.

While the UK government has not yet committed to the bill, lawmakers are pushing for the regulations to be enacted in the next parliamentary session. The UK’s upcoming G20 presidency in 2027 is viewed as a strategic opportunity to advocate for these rigorous safety standards on a global scale. This move signals a potential transition from an era of self-regulation by AI companies to a more transparent, state-supervised framework, aiming to balance innovation with public safety.
