Google Research has spent decades combining foundational computer science with applied research across a wide range of scientific fields. In recent evaluations, senior vice president James Manyika emphasized that artificial intelligence has already triggered more scientific progress than the general public realizes. While consumers often focus on everyday chatbots, the research lab has maintained a genomics program for 12 years, a connectomics brain-wiring program for 10 years, and a quantum computing moonshot for 13 years. Beyond fundamental science, the division works on translational challenges such as disaster forecasting and medical applications. The flood forecasting initiative is part of a broader vision to ensure no human is caught by surprise by natural events like earthquakes, wildfires, and floods. At the same time, the team has progressed toward its goal of supporting one thousand languages by hitting a milestone of three hundred languages. Manyika noted that a significant portion of research resources is dedicated to addressing risks, hallucinations, and misuse through safety innovations like privacy tools and content detection systems. Collaborations with external academic institutions, such as long-term projects with Harvard and the Howard Hughes Medical Institute, underpin these scientific achievements. While these complex scientific breakthroughs operate at arm's length from everyday users, researchers acknowledge that the most tangible and immediate public experiences with artificial intelligence will continue to occur in labor markets and education.
