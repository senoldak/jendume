Abdulrahman Alfozan, a former lead engineer who worked at OpenAI for about a year, was arrested following allegations that he bugged an executive conference room in San Francisco. According to court filings, Alfozan consulted AI chatbots on how to install the listening device and access recordings remotely. The high-level meeting room frequently hosted discussions containing confidential technical and strategic information, which Alfozan was not authorized to access. Staff discovered the suspicious recording device wrapped in toilet paper behind a television on September 30, and a newly installed security camera captured him checking the location on October 5. Investigators noted that Alfozan had booked a one-way flight to Saudi Arabia for his family prior to his arrest. Officials pointed out his ties to a venture capital firm with connections to the Saudi royal family, raising questions about potential state-sponsored espionage, though he has not been formally charged with corporate espionage or stealing trade secrets. Alfozan is currently in detention facing up to five years in prison and a 250,000 dollar fine if convicted. OpenAI stated that the company takes security seriously and utilized its own artificial intelligence systems to help uncover and address the sophisticated security threat. The company emphasized that its security program is designed to protect against various threat actors ranging from insider risks to nation states.
