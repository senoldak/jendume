The race to build winning AI assistants continues to attract significant capital as startups redefine how users interact with software. Wabi, which now describes itself as a personal AI agent, announced that it has raised $55 million in a new funding round, bringing its valuation to $1.25 billion.

Founded by Eugenia Kuyda, who previously created the AI companion startup Replika, Wabi originally allowed anyone to build applications using text prompts. Last month, the startup announced a strategic pivot to transform into an AI messenger, branded as Wabi 2.0, designed to act as a personal agent that generates necessary interfaces on the fly.

The platform can be used to build mini apps for various tasks such as meal planning, tracking local restaurants, organizing date nights, creating fitness routines, and designing specialized trivia games. This agentic approach addresses the growing demand for AI tools that go beyond simple text boxes and command lines.

The latest funding round was led by IVP, with participation from Andreessen Horowitz, Forerunner, Amazon Alexa Fund, and notable angel investors including Shopify CEO Tobi Lütke and Salesforce CEO Marc Benioff. The investment highlights strong market enthusiasm for agentic AI applications.

With fresh capital and a clear vision for user interaction, Wabi aims to solidify its position in the rapidly expanding AI agent ecosystem, opening new possibilities for how individuals manage daily digital tasks.
