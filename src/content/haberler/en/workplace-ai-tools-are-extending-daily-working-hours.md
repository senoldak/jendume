The business world has long anticipated that artificial intelligence would revolutionize productivity and streamline human labor. However, recent data suggests that the reality is diverging from these expectations, as employees who heavily rely on AI tools are finding themselves working longer hours rather than finishing tasks faster. A new analysis by ActivTrak reveals that the push to automate labor is, in some cases, backfiring, leading to increased workloads for white-collar workers.

The study examined anonymized work activity from over 9,100 employees across more than 450 companies between April and September. While non-AI users maintained an average workday of 7.6 hours, heavy AI users recorded workdays ranging from 8.4 to 9.1 hours. This discrepancy translates to up to 90 minutes of additional labor per day for those integrating AI into their daily routines.

Experts suggest that the extra time is often consumed by the need to review, edit, and refine AI-generated outputs, which can sometimes be more time-consuming than performing tasks manually. Furthermore, the constant switching between various AI applications and the fragmentation of focus sessions are contributing to longer workdays. Rather than replacing jobs entirely, some employers appear to be using AI as a justification to demand higher output, effectively turning the promise of efficiency into a cycle of increased labor. Employees are finding that instead of saving time, they are spending more hours managing the complexities introduced by these new tools.
