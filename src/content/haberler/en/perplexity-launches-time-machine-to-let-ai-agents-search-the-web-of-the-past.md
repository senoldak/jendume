Perplexity has integrated a new tool called Time Machine into its Search and Context APIs, enabling AI agents to view the web as it appeared on a specific past date. This development aims to solve a critical issue in AI training and testing: preventing agents from 'cheating' by looking up future outcomes for past events, such as earnings calls or election results. By applying a date cutoff to search and page-reading tools, Time Machine ensures that results are limited to copies saved before that specific point in time.

The company utilized this tool to train an AI model for forecasting 2026 political events listed on Polymarket. In its internal testing, the trained model achieved a score of 0.80, slightly outperforming the market's 0.79. Perplexity claims that its tool is more effective at preventing the leakage of future information compared to competitors. According to the company's automated checker, the information leakage rate for its tool was 0.25%, compared to 1.10% for the rival service Exa.

This launch highlights the intensifying competition in building web-based tools specifically for AI agents. The release follows a similar product launch by Epochal, a startup founded by three xAI co-founders, just one day prior. While Epochal claims its archive spans over 30 years, Perplexity's Time Machine currently offers a 365-day cutoff. As web archives become a contested battleground for AI development, the landscape is shifting, with over 241 news outlets now blocking the Wayback Machine to prevent AI companies from scraping their historical articles.
