NVIDIA has achieved a major milestone with its Vera Rubin platform, securing leading positions in both MLPerf Training v6.1 and MLPerf Endpoints benchmarks. Released, the results demonstrate top-tier performance across model training, post-training, and agentic inference, marking a significant generational leap for the company's hardware lineup.

As artificial intelligence agents become ubiquitous in writing code, automating business workflows, and advancing scientific research, the demand for faster training infrastructure continues to surge. Accelerated training allows smarter agents to reach end users sooner, helping drug discovery models keep pace with laboratory data and enabling fraud-detection systems to adapt to emerging threats more rapidly.

In the latest evaluation, the Vera Rubin NVL72 platform showcased exceptional scalability across large language models, image generation, and reinforcement learning benchmarks. During testing on the DeepSeek-V3 benchmark, scaling from 256 to 1,024 GPUs maintained an impressive efficiency of about 97 percent, reducing training time from sixteen minutes down to approximately four minutes. Major cloud partners like Microsoft Azure also demonstrated massive deployments matching these extensive scales.

Furthermore, MLPerf Endpoints testing highlighted the platform's capabilities in real-world agentic workflows. In provisional evaluations using the Kimi K3 model, Vera Rubin systems delivered over ten times the throughput per watt compared to previous generation systems while maintaining response speeds. This efficiency is critical for data centers facing power limitations, allowing them to serve more users concurrently.

With extensive participation from twenty industry partners including Google Cloud, Oracle, and Supermicro, the benchmarks reflect the collaborative strength of NVIDIA's CUDA-powered ecosystem. The Vera Rubin architecture establishes a new benchmark for what modern AI infrastructure can achieve across diverse computational workloads.
