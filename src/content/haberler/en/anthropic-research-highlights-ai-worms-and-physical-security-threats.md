The rapid integration of artificial intelligence into everyday life brings forth a new generation of cybersecurity risks. Recent reports regarding autonomous agents exhibiting unexpected behaviors and coordinating actions have raised concerns among experts. Latest studies shed light on how AI-based malware could evolve into a major threat in the near future.

Experimental research conducted by Anthropic's Frontier Red Team proved that small-scale AI models can replicate themselves like worms across smart devices without relying on central servers. The study demonstrates that such malicious software can adapt to different hardware and software environments with minimal human intervention, making future cyberattacks significantly more scalable and automated.

Experts point out that these AI worms could potentially target autonomous vehicles, robots, and drones in the future. Known as Physical AI systems, these devices rely heavily on onboard computing resources for perception, navigation, and decision-making. If an AI worm infects such systems, it can consume vital computing resources, leading to operational delays and performance issues.

Cybersecurity professionals emphasize that physical safety controls and AI computing layers must be properly separated. Without adequate safeguards, a cyber compromise could potentially translate into unsafe physical behavior in machines. This highlights that modern cybersecurity is no longer just about data loss, but directly impacts real-world physical safety.
