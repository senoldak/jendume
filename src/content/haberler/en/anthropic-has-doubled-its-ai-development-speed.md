Anthropic has doubled the speed at which it develops its artificial intelligence systems compared to the previous year. An analysis conducted by an independent research group reveals that the company has achieved significant momentum in training and improving its own systems. This development provides a concrete example of how the capacity for "recursive self-improvement" functions in practice.

The company's CEO had previously called for a slowdown in the pace of AI development across the industry. However, assessments conducted by independent researchers, who were granted access to internal company data, indicate that development processes have accelerated rather than slowed down. Researchers point out that the primary reason for this acceleration is the use of AI itself to build newer, more advanced versions of its models.

The research process involved an examination of internal data and employee interviews spanning an 11-week period. The findings confirm that the ability of AI models to perform specific tasks has increased much faster compared to previous years. This situation demonstrates that the "self-improvement" cycle in the AI world is pushing development processes to a point faster than previously anticipated.

Nevertheless, researchers caution that this acceleration might be limited to "well-specified tasks." In other words, while systems improve rapidly in tasks with clear rules, they may not achieve the same speed in more complex and harder-to-define areas. Still, these "signs of life" regarding AI's ability to write its own code or improve model architecture remain a critical topic of debate concerning the future control and safety of the technology. As the company prepares for its public debut, it faces a significant test in managing both the potential and the risks of the technology.
