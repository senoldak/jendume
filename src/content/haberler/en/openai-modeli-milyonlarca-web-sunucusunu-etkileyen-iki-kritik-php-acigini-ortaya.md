Security researchers using an advanced model developed by OpenAI have uncovered two critical security flaws within the core programming language that powers the vast majority of web infrastructure globally. The vulnerabilities are so severe that administrators managing affected systems were advised to immediately take their servers offline and apply the latest security patches. Following the disclosure, various high-profile platforms, including major online encyclopedias, gaming networks, and government portals, temporarily went dark as a precaution.

The widespread impact of the discovery highlights the severe challenges organizations face in swiftly implementing necessary patches. According to official security advisories, the disclosed vulnerability information could potentially be used to reverse-engineer attacks, take control of victim servers, and compromise sensitive data. Leading security agencies recommended that organizations unable to patch their systems immediately should keep their servers offline to prevent potential breaches and unauthorized access.

What makes this discovery particularly extraordinary is the method of identification used by the researchers. Experts at the cybersecurity firm Calif utilized the GPT-5.6 Sol model as part of a collaborative initiative to uncover the underlying flaws in a matter of days. They subsequently reported the issue to the PHP Foundation. Independently, another major security firm also found the flaws and filed a separate report, categorizing the issue as one of the most severe vulnerabilities ever discovered in software history.

These architectural flaws had reportedly been present in various software versions dating back to two decades ago. Enabled by modern artificial intelligence initiatives, the discovery underscores the unprecedented capability of current models in deep code analysis and automated vulnerability detection. While the technology firm highlights this as clear proof of advanced capabilities, the incident has sparked wider discussions about the chaotic disclosure of complex exploits and the broader implications of automated hacking prowess.
