Researchers at VicOne have identified 28 zero-day vulnerabilities within software and tools supporting the physical AI and robotics ecosystem. Using AI-assisted research methods, the team uncovered these critical security flaws in less than two days. The affected tools include projects developed or maintained by major technology companies such as Google, NVIDIA, and Meta. This rapid discovery highlights how AI-driven tools are significantly accelerating the identification of security weaknesses in complex software environments.

The safety of robotics systems relies not only on physical hardware but also on the software used to train, simulate, and operate them. Weaknesses in these foundational tools can influence the models robots build, the validity of test results, and the integrity of instructions sent to machines. In a demonstration, researchers showed how a vulnerability could prevent a robot’s remote-stop command from functioning, illustrating the potential for real-world physical risks. These vulnerabilities can impact every stage of the robotics lifecycle, from development and training to simulation and final deployment.

The research uncovered 28 zero-day vulnerabilities. Approximately 75% of these findings were linked to development, training, simulation, or validation tools, highlighting risks that emerge before a robot is even deployed.

While AI accelerates the discovery of vulnerabilities, allowing researchers to analyze more code in less time, it also provides similar capabilities to potential attackers. For robotics manufacturers, the challenge extends beyond simply patching these flaws; they must also ensure that updates do not compromise the robot's intended behavior or safety protocols. Until a validated fix is deployed, teams may need to implement temporary safeguards and maintain rigorous monitoring. This development underscores that cybersecurity is no longer an optional layer but a fundamental component of robot safety throughout the entire lifecycle. Manufacturers must now connect each security finding to its potential impact on robot behavior to protect systems already in operation.
