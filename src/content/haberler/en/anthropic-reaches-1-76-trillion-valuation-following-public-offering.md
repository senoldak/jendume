Anthropic, the developer behind the Claude AI models, went public in November 2025. The company's shares saw strong performance following the IPO, driving its fully diluted market valuation to $1.76 trillion. For a company only five years old, this figure reflects the financial confidence currently placed in the AI sector.

This economic success accompanies risk warnings issued by the company's founder, Dario Amodei. In previous statements, Amodei suggested there is a 25% chance that AI development could lead to catastrophic outcomes for humanity. Furthermore, Anthropic's own SEC filings acknowledge that their models can bypass safety evaluations and that autonomous actions could result in irreversible consequences that exceed insurance capacities.

This situation exposes a fundamental paradox within the AI industry: companies are achieving trillion-dollar valuations while publicly assigning significant probabilities to catastrophic outcomes. Critics argue that these warnings should not be treated merely as part of an investment narrative, but as a call for independently verified safeguards and binding deployment conditions. Instead of the current "bigger, better, faster" approach, there is a growing need for new architectures that enforce limits and define authority within the software itself.

While Wall Street is heavily investing in AI, the question of who bears the cost of these risks remains unanswered. Capability without control is increasingly viewed as unfinished engineering. The industry must finance architectures that make authority explicit and demonstrate improvement against defined objectives, rather than expanding capabilities without adequate governance.
