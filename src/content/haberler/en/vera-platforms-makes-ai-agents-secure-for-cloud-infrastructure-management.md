Founded by University of Michigan researchers, the Vera Platforms startup is developing a new solution to make AI agents designed to manage critical cloud operations more reliable. While AI models used in software and operations processes are successful at generating code, they pose serious risks when operating on actual production infrastructure. The company's management states that live infrastructure does not tolerate errors and an incorrect command can lead to data loss.

Researchers found that current language models are insufficient in operational monitoring and infrastructure management. For example, popular models like Claude only limit themselves to the default region when cleaning cloud resources and ignore resources in other regions. This proves the lack of infrastructure awareness in artificial intelligence. Vera Platforms developed three main products to solve this problem: vera-gym, vera-bench, and vera-agents.

The developed vera-gym environment offers a high-fidelity simulation mimicking real production infrastructure, allowing AI to practice in a safe space. This aims to prevent disasters that could occur in a real cloud environment. It is observed that the success rate of leading models like Claude and Codex in DevOps tasks remains around 50 percent.

The CloudOpsBench test includes complex operational tasks such as deleting storage units and database scaling processes. Prepared by senior infrastructure operators, these scenarios aim to measure the real-world capabilities of AI models. Models that pass through this process, called vera-agents, are planned to be released in the first quarter of 2027.

Cloud infrastructure management, despite being critical, is defined as a tiring job with a high margin of error. Vera Platforms' vision is built on delegating these processes to AI to reduce operational workload and make systems more predictable. The safe management of these complex processes by AI agents is considered a significant step in the software world.
