A new report released by the American Civil Liberties Union (ACLU) reveals that law enforcement agencies are increasingly using automated license plate readers and AI-powered analysis to track drivers based on travel patterns. According to the findings, police are identifying and stopping motorists even when there is no evidence that a crime has been committed. The technology pieces together where vehicles travel, how often they visit specific locations, and whether their movements match patterns that investigators consider suspicious.

The report highlights real-world incidents, including a September 2025 stop where a driver traveling from Virginia to Florida was pulled over and questioned by an FBI agent because her route triggered an AI red flag. In another case from September 2024, a federal jury found that a Texas county violated a driver's constitutional rights after surveillance technology flagged his return trip from a business trip. Privacy advocates warn that ordinary road trips are increasingly subjected to unwarranted police scrutiny as these surveillance systems expand.

Companies such as Flock Safety provide these advanced systems to law enforcement, enabling algorithms to spot recurring travel patterns and connect plate records with other investigative data. However, experts point out that gaps in camera coverage can lead investigators to draw incorrect conclusions. Because roadside cameras capture license plates automatically, drivers have no easy way to opt out of this mass tracking, raising serious questions about civil liberties and data privacy.
