Open-source AI coding tool OpenCode announced that attackers broke into an older system, affecting 1.4% of users on its online platform where API keys are stored. As the team scrambled to respond to continuous attacks over forty-eight hours, users began reporting unauthorized charges and spending alerts on their accounts across multiple AI providers, with some individual charges reaching hundreds or over $1,000.

During the incident, the OpenCode team attempted to use AI models for defensive analysis, but safety filters blocked the models from examining the malicious payloads. This limitation reignited discussions surrounding the asymmetry problem in AI security, where strict guardrails fail to distinguish between attackers and incident responders. Later, the team gained access to alternative programs that improved their investigative abilities.

Following the breach, tech companies took swift action. Anthropic and Google blocked the exposed API keys, while OpenAI worked with OpenCode to restore access and issue refunds for affected customers. OpenCode stated that refunds for unauthorized charges are currently underway and instructed affected users to revoke and replace their keys.

This episode adds to a rising number of AI-enabled cyber incidents, emphasizing the urgent need for better security measures and nuanced guardrails that do not impede legitimate defensive work. OpenCode noted that efforts to investigate and secure the affected systems remain ongoing.
