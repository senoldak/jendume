NVIDIA announced the addition of four new artificial intelligence models to the Cosmos 3 family to accelerate autonomous vehicle and robot development processes. Released on November 5, 2026, these models focus on predicting the outcomes of physical environments and movements using "world model" technology. The company positions these new tools as a virtual simulation crew for autonomous driving development teams.

Built on the Cosmos3-Nano architecture, the models help developers overcome the difficulty and cost of collecting real-world data. Instead of testing dangerous traffic scenarios on actual roads, these systems enable the generation of synthetic data in virtual environments. One of the models, Sim-Auto, can generate simultaneous video from 11 different camera angles while producing LiDAR sensor data consistent with the visual output.

Focusing on robotics, the Sim-Bimanual model simulates the movements of dual-arm robots. This model predicts how the environment will visually change after an action performed by a robot, allowing tasks like object grasping to be examined in a virtual setting. NVIDIA emphasizes that these models do not just provide realistic images but also ensure consistency between sensor geometry and visual data.

Each model, featuring 16 billion parameters, has been released as open-weight. This allows developers to fine-tune the models with their own data and use them in commercial projects under the OpenMDW 1.1 license. However, efficient operation of the systems requires specific hardware infrastructure, such as NVIDIA GPUs and specialized software toolkits.
