Scottish biotech startup PhaSER Biomedical has secured a $2.7M (£2m) financial award from the Gates Foundation to support its efforts in finding new treatments for conditions including tuberculosis and malaria. The two-year funding reaffirms the organization's involvement in the foundation's collaborative global health initiative, supplying researchers with access to shared laboratory instruments and specialized biological models.

The new financial support comes after a previous $2.3M allocation from the same foundation. Throughout that timeframe, investigators utilized PhaSER's 8HUM platform for studies concerning tuberculosis, malaria, schistosomiasis, and Chagas disease. Standard laboratory rodents eliminate medications much faster than human patients do, rendering conventional tests potentially inaccurate. Independent studies demonstrated that drug compounds behave in 8HUM subjects much more similarly to how they interact within human patients.

PhaSER was established to commercialize access to this specific model, which was developed by researchers at the University of Dundee alongside industry collaborators. Beyond its biological offerings, the firm is engineering HERALD, an artificial intelligence application designed to forecast adverse reactions in individuals consuming multiple medications concurrently. The initiative has additionally obtained public sector financial backing through the United Kingdom medicines regulator.

Corporate executives stress that human-relevant evidence early in development assists in rescuing promising therapeutic compounds and minimizes development timeframes. As part of its organizational expansion, PhaSER is actively recruiting specialists in artificial intelligence and computer modeling while appointing new industry veterans to its board.
