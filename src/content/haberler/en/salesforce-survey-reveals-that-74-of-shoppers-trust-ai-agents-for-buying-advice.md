A new research study surveying over 2,500 shoppers across the United States, the UK, Canada, and Australia highlights the growing role of automated assistants in retail. According to the findings, 20 percent of holiday shoppers utilize smart recommendation software as their initial destination for research and advice, with 74 percent of buyers trusting guidance from these shopping assistants.

Online retail traffic directed from intelligent software tools to digital storefronts has expanded significantly. Consumers who follow algorithmic shopping referrals spend 8 percent more than the average buyer and acquire 23 percent more goods per transaction. Furthermore, commercial brands implementing these digital helpers are observing rapid sales acceleration compared to competitors without them.

The study projects that AI-powered purchasing suggestions will influence a massive volume of consumer transactions during major sales events, reaching $75.8 billion in total value. Additionally, 30 percent of buyers noted discovering an unfamiliar brand through digital guidance, while 11 percent stated they would actively avoid a merchant if instructed by an automated tool.

Despite high confidence in product suggestions, consumers maintain strict boundaries regarding financial autonomy. Only 9 percent of users permit personal digital assistants unconstrained access to private banking details, and 73 percent feel uncomfortable sharing credit card information. Nearly half of all surveyed individuals express zero interest in automated systems completing financial transactions on their behalf.
