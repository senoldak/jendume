Shifts in corporate strategies regarding artificial intelligence have brought new realities to light. Because technology cannot fully perform certain complex tasks, firms are turning back to experienced employees, leading to predictions that layoffs will further decrease. In the automotive sector, Ford has hired numerous experienced engineers over a span of three years to compensate for shortcomings in production and quality control systems driven by artificial intelligence. While automated controls catch some issues, they cannot fully replicate years of human experience and decision-making, testing the heavy reliance on technology in the field. A similar trend is observed across other industries as well. According to industry surveys, a significant portion of human resources managers report rehiring staff for positions previously eliminated due to artificial intelligence tools. Reasons include failing to achieve expected efficiency and the ongoing need for experienced worker supervision. Experts note that companies moving too quickly into artificial intelligence are now witnessing where the technology falls short in practice. However, this renewed demand for workers does not necessarily mean positive news for wages. Comprehensive research covering hundreds of professions indicates that real wage growth in areas heavily impacted by artificial intelligence has lagged behind other fields. Companies still need employees, but the gains brought by new technologies are not immediately reflecting on payrolls.
