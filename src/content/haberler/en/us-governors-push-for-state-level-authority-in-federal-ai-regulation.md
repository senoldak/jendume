The National Governors Association (NGA) released its principles for AI governance on November 22, 2026. Led by governors from Maryland, Indiana, North Carolina, and Utah, this framework serves as a response to the federal AI legislation currently under development in Congress. The governors advocate for a system where the federal government manages national and international risks, while states retain the authority to protect their own residents and govern AI usage within their jurisdictions.

This proposal highlights the ongoing tension between fostering innovation and ensuring public safety. Governors are wary of federal rules overriding state-level protections, which could lead to a fragmented regulatory landscape. For the tech industry, this means navigating a complex web of potentially conflicting state and federal requirements. The plan calls for independent safety reviews of advanced AI models and mandates the reporting of serious security incidents. Furthermore, it suggests that data centers should largely bear the cost of their own energy consumption.

Currently, states are taking independent action. California has sued OpenAI over rogue agents, Texas has taken legal action regarding children's safety, and New York has banned AI chatbots from impersonating humans. Meanwhile, President Donald Trump has expressed concerns that heavy regulation could stifle technological progress. This situation underscores the urgent need for a cohesive national framework that balances federal oversight with state-level autonomy. The governors emphasize that no federal policy will be effective without their input.
