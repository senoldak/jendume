Recent legal decisions in Germany have challenged the liability shield traditionally enjoyed by search engines regarding AI outputs. Munich and Aachen regional courts have established that Google's AI search summaries are not merely neutral search references but independent statements generated by the company itself. In a case from Munich, Google settled after its AI falsely linked local publishers to fraud schemes. Following this precedent, the Aachen Regional Court issued an injunction on September 24, 2026, barring Google from spreading false statements generated by its AI search mode about a local physician. The court emphasized that Google provides AI-generated search and must therefore bear responsibility for the resulting answers, setting a potential fine of up to 250,000 euros per violation. The legal reasoning highlights that Google's AI produces independent and substantive statements by evaluating and combining content from various third-party websites. The court noted that Google could check such statements against source websites to prevent errors. If higher courts uphold this reasoning, it could fundamentally disrupt the economics of AI search and invite widespread claims from publishers whose content feeds these AI models. In an extreme scenario, Google might eventually face compensation claims from creators whose sources are used for AI-generated answers, completely upending the current economic model of AI-driven search engines.
