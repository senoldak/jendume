Anthropic has officially become the first company exclusively focused on artificial intelligence to go public. Following an initial public offering price of $83, shares surged past $111, a gain of over 30 percent as investors rushed to capitalize on the debut. However, this momentum proved difficult to sustain throughout the trading session, and the stock closed its first day on the NASDAQ around the $96 mark.

The volatility continued into the second day, with pre-market trading showing a decline of over nine percent. This market performance reflects growing investor skepticism regarding the long-term sustainability of the AI industry. Despite the company achieving a valuation exceeding $1 trillion, concerns persist over its mounting debt and significant financial losses. Investors are scrutinizing whether the massive capital requirements of AI development can be balanced with long-term profitability.

The market is conducting a stress test on the sector's ability to transition from high-growth potential to actual financial viability. The company reported a net loss exceeding $100 billion so far this year, reflecting the immense costs associated with training and scaling advanced models. This IPO serves as a confidence test for the broader AI sector, as market participants weigh the potential for technology against the reality of substantial financial burn rates.

The debate about whether the AI industry is currently in a bubble is being fueled by these financial disclosures. As debt piles grow and losses mount, investors are shifting their focus from pure innovation metrics to fiscal discipline. This transition represents a phase in the AI industry where financial performance is as important as technological breakthroughs.
