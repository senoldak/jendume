Telecommunications giant Verizon raised its forecasts while announcing a major infrastructure move to bolster its artificial intelligence capabilities. The company unveiled an agreement worth more than two billion dollars with Amazon Web Services (AWS) to support generative and agentic AI workloads, which can carry out tasks independently. Under the agreement, Verizon will provide high-capacity network connections and help distribute computing power across multiple sites. This deal follows a previous agreement in July worth more than one billion dollars to connect Google data centers over unused fiber networks. As artificial intelligence models demand unprecedented processing power, telecommunication companies are increasingly aligning with cloud providers to handle heavy workloads. During the quarter ending in September, Verizon reported strong subscriber additions and lifted its financial forecasts for the year. The company maintained its plan to spend between sixteen billion and sixteen point five billion dollars on its network and other assets. Chief executive Dan Schulman noted that the company is only one year into a multi-year growth story following a broader corporate restructuring. Across the industry, the surging demand for computing capacity continues to drive massive data-center and cloud partnerships, cementing the critical role of robust digital infrastructure in the future of artificial intelligence.
