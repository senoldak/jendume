As artificial intelligence technologies permeate various aspects of everyday life, they are increasingly becoming assistants in the realm of romantic relationships. A recent survey published by Match Group, the owner of platforms like Tinder and Hinge, highlights how individuals seeking companionship are turning to AI chatbots for guidance and support.

The research reveals that a notable percentage of singles in the UK have used artificial intelligence to get dating advice, assess potential partners, or craft messages and profiles. Adoption rates are particularly high among younger generations, soaring to over half for millennials and reaching two-thirds for members of Gen Z. Users note that the intense pressure to make a good first impression on dating apps drives them to seek technological assistance.

Users deploy chatbots not only to write messages but also to act as mentors, helping them manage dating anxiety, evaluate matches, and rethink overlooked profiles. While some individuals credit AI with speeding up the dating process and helping them find compatible partners, others express concern that offloading communication to machines might diminish genuine human interaction. Some participants even worry that relying entirely on algorithms might rot their conversational skills.

Meanwhile, trust and safety officials take a firm stance against fully autonomous bot-to-bot dating, arguing that genuine human connection cannot be achieved by having artificial assistants converse on behalf of people in a world already struggling with alienation. Despite the rise of chatbot advisors, many singles still prefer relying on traditional human networks like friends and family for emotional sounding boards.
