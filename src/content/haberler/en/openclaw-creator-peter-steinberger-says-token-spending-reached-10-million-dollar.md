Peter Steinberger, creator of the open-source AI agent OpenClaw and current OpenAI employee, revealed significant spending figures at Cloudflare’s Connect conference in San Francisco. Steinberger estimated that he spent about 10 million dollars on AI tokens during a session with Cloudflare chief technology officer Dane Knecht. He noted that not all of that token usage proved useful.

Tokens serve as the fundamental units AI companies use to charge for text processed by models. Knecht pointed out during the discussion that Steinberger maintains an almost unlimited token budget, a luxury few developers possess. Steinberger argued that current infrastructure has not caught up yet, questioning why many processes still rely on local computers rather than the cloud.

Steinberger joined OpenAI in February after establishing OpenClaw, which transitioned to a foundation to remain open source. Earlier in the year, he noted that joining OpenAI helped him overcome previous token constraints. During an appearance at Y Combinator’s Startup School, he explained that the main limitation preventing AI agents from running continuously is cost rather than technology, noting that few people are willing to spend that many tokens.

The conversation also touched upon agent safety and permissions. Steinberger suggested that having agents negotiate permissions with secondary agents increases trust and safety. While many expect personal AI agents to become commonplace for assisting daily tasks, high token costs remain a key hurdle for broader adoption.
