Humyn Labs has successfully closed a $47 million Series A funding round to provide the training data necessary for robotic systems and AI models to operate in the physical world. The India-based startup aims to bridge the gap in real-world data required by U.S.-based robotics and AI companies to train their models. The global demand for high-quality data sets, essential for robots to perform human-like tasks, serves as the primary driver for the startup's growth.

The company utilizes a proprietary two-camera hardware system to record people performing everyday tasks and activities. Its software then processes and labels this footage, defining what happens in each video clip so that AI can learn from it. Beyond simple video collection, the startup tracks hand movements and interactions with objects, transforming raw recordings into structured data sets. This process is critical for enabling robots to become more capable in complex physical environments.

The funding structure consists of $26 million in equity and $21 million in venture debt. The company reported generating $10 million in revenue between April and October 2026, with an additional $20 million in signed contracts currently in place. Furthermore, the startup noted that another $20 million in deals is pending finalization.

Founded in February 2026, the startup currently serves 12 paying customers. Operating data collection activities in over 20 countries across Latin America and Asia, the company focuses on meeting the data needs of the robotics world with its 51 full-time employees. Its data collection networks, concentrated in hubs such as Brazil, India, and South Korea, allow the company to offer global diversity. This development highlights that data collection processes in the field of physical AI are becoming professionalized and reaching significant industrial scale.
