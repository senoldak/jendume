European infrastructure companies are utilizing Nvidia DSX to construct their computational facilities, as announced at the GTC conference. The reference architecture and software suite combine processors, networking, power distribution, and thermal management into a unified system designed to operate efficiently. This hardware approach allows facilities to minimize cooling water consumption significantly through closed-loop liquid systems. Thermal fluid circulates past the processors and transfers heat outward via dry coolers instead of relying on traditional evaporation methods. A facility scaled to two gigawatts using this blueprint could conserve 54 million litres of water daily according to the company. Additionally, a specific software tool named MaxLPS included in the platform allows systems to achieve identical computational performance while lowering electrical consumption by up to 30%. Testing conducted by Nscale at its Iceland site on four racks of GB300 servers demonstrated inference throughput rose by 49% within the same power budget, while another location in Portugal is currently being planned using digital twin simulations before physical construction begins. Multiple engineering and industrial firms across the continent, including Siemens, Schneider Electric, Dassault Systèmes, ABB, Siemens Energy, Hitachi Energy, and Trane Technologies, are contributing power, thermal, and simulation designs to support the platform. Separate grid trials in the United Kingdom with National Grid and Nebius also demonstrated how processor clusters can dynamically adjust electrical draw by up to 40% during simulated power grid fluctuations while maintaining critical computational workloads.
