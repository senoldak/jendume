Awear AI, established by Ash Saulsbury, Lila Tretikov, and Jan Eißfeldt, emerged from stealth at the Qualcomm Snapdragon Summit to introduce its Agentic System One platform. The company enables users to switch between smart eyewear brands without losing the personal data and preferences accumulated by their AI assistants. The platform features an encrypted communications network and a memory vault designed to preserve information across personal devices and cloud storage.

Currently, Meta ties its smart glasses to its proprietary AI services, while Google collaborates with eyewear makers using Gemini. Awear AI offers an alternative to big tech's data grip by providing an encrypted architecture where not even the company can access consumer information. The platform anonymizes transactions and operates across external AI models to give eyewear brands flexibility.

The system includes strict safeguards requiring explicit user authorization for consequential actions like purchases, ensuring decisions remain reversible. The founders emphasize that users retain control of their encryption keys, protecting personal histories against future changes in ownership. Awear AI plans to generate revenue by providing software and connected services to hardware manufacturers.

Co-founder Lila Tretikov states that the company intends to make its memory-transfer format openly available, drawing a parallel to her experience with the Wikimedia Foundation to build structural trust. Awear AI's technology is initially rolling out for glasses powered by Qualcomm's Snapdragon AR1+ processor.
