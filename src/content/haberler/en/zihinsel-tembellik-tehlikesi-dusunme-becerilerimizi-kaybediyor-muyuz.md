As artificial intelligence becomes deeply embedded in daily routines, scholars are increasingly examining its psychological and cognitive impacts on society. In a new book exploring the friction between human thought and automated tools, Massachusetts Institute of Technology Professor Eric So highlights the hidden costs of relying too broadly on artificial intelligence for everyday problem-solving.

Observations from academia indicate that a significant portion of queries made by students and professionals are low-effort requests aimed at bypassing initial difficulties. So notes that humans possess a natural biological instinct to conserve mental energy, which, combined with social pressures to keep up with competitive environments, creates a constant pull toward automated solutions that he terms 'AI gravity.'

To counteract this trend, the research emphasizes the value of 'productive struggle'—the idea that wrestling with initial failures and complex material is essential for acquiring deep knowledge and retaining critical thinking skills. Just as industrialization required society to intentionally adopt physical exercise to stay healthy, the digital age demands a conscious effort to preserve and train our cognitive capabilities rather than surrendering them entirely to machines.
