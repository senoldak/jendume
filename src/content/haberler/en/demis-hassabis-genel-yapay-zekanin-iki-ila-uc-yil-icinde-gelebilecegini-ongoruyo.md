The race to develop artificial general intelligence (AGI) is moving faster than expected, according to Google DeepMind co-founder Demis Hassabis. In a recent interview, the Nobel Prize-winning pioneer shortened his previous timeline, stating that human-level AI could arrive within two to three years, potentially placing it within reach by 2028.

This growing confidence is largely driven by rapid progress in autonomous agents. Unlike traditional chatbots that require constant prompting, modern autonomous systems can execute multi-step tasks, utilize various tools, and make decisions independently. Hassabis noted that 's advanced AI agents serve as a foundational practice run for true AGI, while also urging governments and the public to take approaching developments seriously.

Despite impressive milestones achieved by contemporary models, significant technical challenges remain before systems can genuinely match human intelligence. Researchers are still working to solve continuous learning, long-term memory limitations, and foundational reasoning errors. While current AI can write sophisticated code or solve complex mathematical problems, it can still produce basic logical mistakes or confidently state incorrect information.

Achieving AGI would introduce unprecedented flexibility into everyday tools, allowing systems to adapt to unfamiliar problems with minimal supervision. In professional and scientific sectors, such capabilities could accelerate complex research, administrative workflows, and drug discovery—an area where Hassabis previously transformed protein structure prediction through AlphaFold.

While the exact timeline and universally accepted benchmarks for AGI remain subjects of discussion, experts emphasize that the coming years will likely bring significantly more autonomous and capable systems into daily life.
