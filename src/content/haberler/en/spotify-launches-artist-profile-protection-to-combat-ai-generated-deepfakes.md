Spotify launched its "Artist Profile Protection" tool for all artists on November 17, 2026, marking a significant step in the fight against unauthorized content. This new feature allows artists to monitor music uploaded under their names and remove AI-generated deepfakes or incorrectly attributed tracks from their profiles. As AI-generated content floods streaming platforms, the ability for artists to safeguard their digital identities has become increasingly vital.

Through this tool, artists can receive alerts whenever new music is submitted under their name. If a release does not belong to them, they can immediately remove the association to prevent it from appearing on their profile. For those seeking greater control, Spotify offers an advanced setting that requires manual approval for all new releases before they go live on an artist's page. This provides a necessary layer of defense against impersonation and metadata errors that have long plagued streaming services.

This development comes at a critical time for the music industry. Reports from last month indicated that major labels, such as Sony Music, had requested the removal of over 260,000 deepfake songs from digital platforms. This figure is nearly double the number disclosed by the label in March, highlighting the rapid escalation of the issue.

Spotify spent eight months beta-testing this feature with select artists to refine the tool and ensure it does not disrupt release schedules. The company aims for this solution to set a new industry standard for content management. In the era of generative AI, protecting one's digital identity is no longer just a technical preference; it is a fundamental necessity for maintaining the integrity of an artist's work and ensuring that fans are listening to authentic music. By putting these controls directly into the hands of creators, the platform is shifting the balance of power back to the artists.
