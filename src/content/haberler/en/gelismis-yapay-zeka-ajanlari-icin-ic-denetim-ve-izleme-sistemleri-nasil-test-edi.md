New evaluation methods are being examined to determine whether AI developers' agents can bypass security and oversight measures without being stopped. Currently, developers rely heavily on monitoring systems to catch potentially harmful behaviors. In this context, researchers are exploring how an assessor can gather evidence regarding the reliability of these monitoring mechanisms.

The proposed approach focuses on five core areas: monitor coverage, monitor system vulnerabilities, monitor evasion, human response, and overall impact. Monitor coverage examines what fraction of internal agentic inference is and should be monitored. Since AI agents often require broad affordances like internet access, noticing harmful actions necessitates understanding them within a broader context. Assessors review inference logs to estimate this coverage accurately.

Regarding system vulnerabilities, the framework investigates whether AI agents can exploit bugs to disable monitoring or compromise its infrastructure. Because monitoring systems involve complex interactions across multiple APIs and engines, vulnerabilities can easily emerge. Red-teaming these systems is essential to uncover such weaknesses before they can be exploited by misaligned agents.

The effectiveness of monitors is further tested by searching for attack opportunities in real transcripts and assessing how easily monitors can be fooled. Analyzing hundreds of thousands to millions of transcripts helps ensure that monitors are not missing severe incidents. This rigorous testing allows developers to build confidence in the safety and robustness of advanced AI systems.

Developers expect to update these core sub-questions as new capabilities and misaligned behaviors emerge. Ensuring robust monitoring is essential for maintaining control over autonomous AI operations and keeping pace with technological advancements.
