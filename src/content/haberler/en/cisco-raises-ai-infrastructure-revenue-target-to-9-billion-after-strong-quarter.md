Networking hardware giant Cisco reported fiscal first-quarter financial results that exceeded market expectations, driven by a surge in demand for artificial intelligence infrastructure. The company announced that it now expects revenue from hyperscalers—large-scale cloud and data center providers—to reach $9 billion during this fiscal year. This updated projection represents more than double the revenue figure recorded in fiscal 2026, signaling a significant shift in the company's growth trajectory within the hardware sector.

Financial data reveals that Cisco's total revenue grew by 24% compared to the $14.88 billion reported in the same period a year ago. The company's bottom line also saw a substantial boost, with net earnings climbing to $4.6 billion, or $1.14 per share. This is a marked increase from the $2.9 billion, or 72 cents per share, earned in the previous year. CEO Chuck Robbins noted that modern organizations are increasingly relying on the company's technology to expand and protect their digital ecosystems as they enter the era of autonomous AI agents.

Cisco's stock performance has reflected this renewed optimism, with shares rising approximately 35% since the start of the calendar year. This growth significantly outperforms the Nasdaq index, which gained 9% over the same period. After initially trailing behind during the early stages of the generative AI boom, the company is now successfully positioning itself as a critical provider for the physical backbone of AI operations. The focus on scaling and optimizing environments for complex AI workloads has become a primary driver for investor confidence.

Looking ahead to the second fiscal quarter, the company issued a positive forecast, projecting revenue between $18 billion and $18.2 billion. This outlook surpasses the average analyst estimate of $17.88 billion. Additionally, Cisco expects adjusted earnings per share to fall between $1.34 and $1.36, beating the consensus forecast of $1.23. The company's ability to exceed these benchmarks underscores the accelerating pace of infrastructure spending by major tech firms as they race to build out their AI capabilities.
