Artificial intelligence laboratories have heavily relied on model benchmarking as a system to measure whether one product outperforms another. However, the methods driving these evaluations remain complex and are still going through a maturation phase. Irene Solaiman, chief policy officer at Hugging Face, emphasized that testing needs to evolve into a far more consistent science if the industry plans to rely on them moving forward.

One of the most persistent hurdles in benchmarking has long been the reproducibility of results. Without proper levels of access to the underlying model, verifying outcomes becomes challenging. Solaiman noted that open weights play a significant role in this dynamic, adding that the ecosystem still lacks a meaningful way to compare different evaluations against each other as testing frameworks rapidly lose their effectiveness over time.

Recently, the concept of embedded evaluators—placing third-party observers directly inside organizations—has gained traction as labs grapple with safety incidents and potential harms. Yet doubts persist regarding how neutral or effective these observers will truly be in practice. For instance, decisions by firms to hire outside consulting entities as evaluators have previously drawn considerable criticism regarding their qualifications for such complex tasks.

Evaluations are now transitioning from merely testing the core model to assessing the entire surrounding system, including harnesses and agents. The harness specifically has a massive impact on performance, meaning a weaker model paired with a better harness can easily outperform a frontier model. Solaiman pointed out that agentic evaluations are both difficult and expensive, indicating that a more uniform, industry-wide approach may be required for benchmarking to remain a dependable tool.
