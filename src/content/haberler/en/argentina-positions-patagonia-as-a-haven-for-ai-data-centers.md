Argentine President Javier Milei is seeking to position his country as a welcoming destination for artificial intelligence data centers by offering a hands-off approach and minimal regulations. As communities in the United States and Europe push back against the massive facilities powering AI, Milei is highlighting Patagonia's vast open spaces, chilly climate, and strong winds to attract foreign tech companies and venture capitalists.

To lure investors wary of Argentina's economic instability, Milei has proposed an incentive scheme that would reduce the corporate income tax rate to 15 percent for projects worth at least 1 billion dollars in emerging industries. Energy companies like Pampa Energía are preparing to supply natural gas to power these massive server farms, while renewable energy firms such as Green Capital are planning multi-billion dollar projects in the region.

Local landowners and sheep farmers in provinces like Chubut are also leasing land for these upcoming data center campuses as traditional wool farming declines. Green Capital has secured leases covering a significant amount of land, promising to generate its own electricity through wind, solar, and gas turbines to avoid straining the local power grid.

However, critics and environmental researchers warn that the lack of strict regulations could turn Argentina into an untested laboratory without proper safeguards for water and energy consumption. Furthermore, some investors remain cautious, waiting to see if future political shifts will disrupt current free-market reforms. Construction on major projects is not expected to begin until around 2028, with proponents hoping it will bring substantial investments and high-skilled jobs.
