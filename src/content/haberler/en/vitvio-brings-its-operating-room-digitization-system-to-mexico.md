London-based startup VitVio is expanding its reach into Latin America by deploying its Vetro system at Hospital Español de Veracruz in Mexico. The technology uses a network of cameras and sensors to generate a live 3D model of the operating room, monitoring equipment, staff activity, and surgical workflows in real-time.

The primary goal of Vetro is to streamline surgical operations by identifying bottlenecks, tracking equipment usage, and automating the generation of operative notes. By handling these administrative tasks, the system aims to reduce waste and free up doctors and nurses to focus more on patient care. Hospital leadership views this implementation as a key component of their modernization strategy, designed to improve overall efficiency and increase the number of procedures they can accommodate.

This deployment highlights the growing trend of integrating advanced monitoring systems into healthcare environments to optimize resource management. VitVio emphasizes that the system is designed to strip personal details from the data, focusing exclusively on surgical stages and equipment rather than individual staff members. Despite these assurances, the presence of cameras in sensitive medical environments raises ongoing questions regarding data privacy, storage policies, and staff surveillance. The company has yet to disclose specific details regarding data retention periods or storage locations.

This move marks VitVio’s first entry into the Latin American market, following previous deployments in Poland, New York, and various NHS hospitals in England. The agreement with the hospital group includes the potential to expand the system to 60 operating theatres across seven different hospitals. Founded in 2024, the startup has secured a total of $10 million in funding to date. As hospitals worldwide seek to improve efficiency, VitVio’s approach underscores the critical role of data-driven insights in managing complex surgical environments.
