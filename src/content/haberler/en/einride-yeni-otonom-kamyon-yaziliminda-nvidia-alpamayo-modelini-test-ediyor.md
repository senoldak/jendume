Einride has announced an expansion of its collaboration with Nvidia to evaluate the Alpamayo artificial intelligence model within its autonomous truck software. According to the announcement, the first real-world road evaluation targets the second half of 2027. During the initial testing phases, the model will not directly control the truck; instead, it will generate its own decisions while the existing driving system operates, allowing technical teams to compare the outputs.

This initiative will be integrated as part of the company's proprietary driving system. The engineering team will adapt the Alpamayo model to its own vehicles, routes, and operational data gathered from operations. Through additional training and model distillation processes, the company plans to transition a general-purpose driving model into a structure specifically suited for freight transport, aiming to reduce development time and costs when shifting to new vehicle types and routes.

The initial evaluations will utilize a shadow mode method. In this setup, the AI model operates on live data without issuing any direct commands to the vehicle. The results it produces are compared against the behaviors of the driving planner and human drivers, keeping vehicle control strictly within existing safety systems so that the model's suggestions and the truck's movements remain separated.

Nvidia’s Alpamayo platform offers simulation tools and physical artificial intelligence datasets alongside its core model family. Developers can utilize these resources to process driving data, adapt models to their specific conditions, and evaluate them in simulations. This new effort expands upon the previously announced Hyperion partnership, incorporating computing and sensor architecture designed for heavy vehicles.
