Technology leaders in the corporate world are reevaluating how to safeguard data security amidst constantly evolving artificial intelligence models. Companies often find themselves repeatedly rebuilding their infrastructure for models that function merely as temporary tenants. The primary focus for chief information officers is not selecting a specific model, but managing this technological transition without compromising data integrity.

Transitioning from testing phases to production environments brings practical challenges such as privacy, authentication, and cost constraints. Redesigning security mechanisms from scratch for every new model slows down operational processes and hampers agility. Consequently, model updates turn into lengthy and costly engineering projects.

While models can be updated or replaced over time, corporate assets and data remain permanent. Therefore, governance mechanisms must be established directly at the data layer. When rules are anchored to the data rather than the specific model, security and compliance standards are preserved even if the underlying technology changes.
