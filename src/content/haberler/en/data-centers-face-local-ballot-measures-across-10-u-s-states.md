On November 3, 2026, voters across 10 U.S. states participated in elections featuring 41 local ballot measures focused on the development of AI data centers. These measures range from outright bans on new facilities to the implementation of stricter regulatory requirements for ongoing projects. The votes highlight a growing conflict between the infrastructure demands of major technology companies and the economic and environmental concerns of local residents.

Data centers are the backbone of modern AI, providing the necessary computing power for cloud services and sophisticated algorithms. However, their operation requires massive amounts of electricity and water for cooling. Research from the Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory indicates that these facilities could consume 11.8% of total U.S. electricity by 2030. This surge in demand places significant pressure on power grids, raising questions about whether existing infrastructure can handle the load without increasing costs for residential customers.

Because electricity markets operate across large regions, power demand in one location can influence prices far beyond its borders. Regional market monitors have identified data center demand as a primary driver of rising capacity market prices. This has sparked a debate over whether households are effectively subsidizing infrastructure built primarily for large tech corporations. Since 2024, residential electricity prices have increased by over 10% nationally, with some areas like Ohio seeing hikes exceeding 20%.

The debate involves competing interests. Tech companies argue that data centers are engines of economic growth, creating construction jobs and generating tax revenue. They warn that restrictive policies could drive investment to more favorable locations. Conversely, local residents are raising alarms about noise pollution, land usage, and the environmental impact of high water consumption. With Ohio alone hosting 21 of the ballot measures, the outcomes of these votes will dictate the future landscape of AI infrastructure and the conditions under which developers must operate.
