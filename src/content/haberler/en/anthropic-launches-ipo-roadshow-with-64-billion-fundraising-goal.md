Anthropic has officially initiated the roadshow for its initial public offering, marking a significant step in its transition to a public company. The firm plans to offer 825 million shares of common stock, with a target price range between $70 and $78 per share. At the upper end of this range, the offering aims to raise approximately $64.35 billion. The company is scheduled to price its shares on November 18, with plans to list on the Nasdaq under the ticker symbol "ANT."

This public offering underscores the massive scale of capital flowing into the artificial intelligence sector. A notable aspect of the IPO is the strong support from the company's ecosystem; seven major suppliers, including Nvidia, Google, Microsoft, Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Co., Broadcom, Advanced Micro Devices, and Micron Technology, have committed to purchasing $26 billion worth of shares through a private placement. This move highlights the deep financial and strategic integration between Anthropic and the hardware and cloud infrastructure giants that power its operations.

Financially, Anthropic is demonstrating rapid expansion. In its most recent quarter, the company reported $17 billion in revenue alongside an operating loss of $536 million. According to company filings, revenue generated during the first nine months of the year was roughly seven times higher than the total revenue recorded for the entirety of 2025. Market analysts project that the company could reach approximately $52 billion in revenue by the end of the year, with expectations for that figure to grow more than tenfold by 2030.

To sustain this growth, Anthropic is expanding its portfolio beyond core model releases, focusing on tools that enhance user productivity. The company introduced Claude Meetings, an assistant for video conferences that retrieves business information and generates project plans, alongside Claude Whiteboard, which allows users to brainstorm and revise AI-generated content. Additionally, the launch includes Claude Sheets, a spreadsheet editor with a natural language interface, and the general availability of Claude Data, which converts files into interactive dashboards. These tools are designed to integrate AI more deeply into daily business workflows, further cementing the company's position in the enterprise software market.
