Apple has introduced new security controls to its macOS operating system to limit the access AI agents have to user disk data. Announced on the company's developer blog, this update aims to make the 'full disk access' granted to AI applications like ChatGPT, Claude, and Gemini more transparent and controlled. The new system ensures that users must take explicit actions before granting such high-level access to any AI application. These protections are particularly vital for hardware like the Mac Studio, which is marketed specifically for running local AI models.

The initiative stems from concerns that some developers have been utilizing full disk access in ways that could expose sensitive information, including files, mail, messages, and browsing history, without the user's full awareness. Apple's new controls require users to review and approve specific actions, such as reading, changing, deleting, or exporting data, through a clear and cautionary pop-up screen. This screen explicitly states that the Mac will be unable to protect data from the chosen AI app, forcing users to acknowledge the potential risks before proceeding.

As AI agents gain more autonomous capabilities, the risks associated with granting them broad system access grow substantially. If an AI agent behaves unpredictably, it could potentially leak or misuse private data. The company emphasized its dedication to making sure that users fully grasp the implications of their choices before they authorize these permissions, enabling them to better safeguard their personal information.

The feature is currently available in the macOS 27.3 beta 2 release. While there is no official word on when this will be rolled out to all users, the update represents a significant step in addressing the security challenges posed by increasingly autonomous AI software. By forcing users to pause and review permissions, Apple aims to mitigate the risks of AI agents going rogue and misusing private data.
