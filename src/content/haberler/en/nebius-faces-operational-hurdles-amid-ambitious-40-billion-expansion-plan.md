Nebius, a key player in the "neocloud" sector, provides the massive computing power essential for training and running advanced artificial intelligence models. The company recently unveiled an ambitious $40 billion spending plan for 2027. However, this aggressive growth strategy is facing a significant test due to delays in data center construction and the inherent risks of its financing model.

The company’s business model relies heavily on customer pre-payments and loans backed by long-term contracts. Essentially, Nebius is leveraging the future value of data centers to finance their current construction. While this approach can be highly efficient when projects proceed on schedule, any disruption in construction timelines can threaten the entire financial structure.

The primary challenge currently lies in delays at a data center site in New Jersey. The capacity delivery under a contract with Microsoft, worth up to $19.4 billion, has fallen behind schedule. These delays stem from operational issues faced by the third-party operator of the site, as well as interventions by local regulators regarding site operations.

For the broader artificial intelligence landscape, this situation highlights the fragility of the underlying infrastructure. Major technology companies depend on third-party providers to secure the hardware needed for AI development. Any delay in these construction projects directly impacts the AI roadmaps of these tech giants. As companies like Microsoft tighten their oversight of third-party partners, it underscores the critical importance of reliability in the AI supply chain. While Nebius maintains that its self-managed projects are on track, the market remains cautious about the potential for further operational hurdles.
