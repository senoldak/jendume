Instagram head Adam Mosseri stated in a recent podcast interview that he expects public concern and frustration regarding artificial intelligence to escalate in the coming years. According to Mosseri, this sentiment is particularly strong within creative industries, where professionals are grappling with the rapid integration of AI tools. He noted that while some fears are well-founded, others stem from the inherent uncertainty of such a transformative technological shift.

A primary source of friction for creators is the use of their original work to train AI models without explicit consent or compensation. Concerns also extend to job security, intellectual property rights, and the fear of being marginalized by automated systems. Mosseri acknowledged these frustrations, emphasizing that different professional groups are being impacted by the same technology in vastly different ways.

Regarding platform policy, Mosseri argued that a total ban on AI-generated content is not a viable or practical solution for Instagram. Instead, the platform is moving toward a model of transparency and identification. Instagram has already begun requiring AI-driven accounts to label themselves, a move intended to help users distinguish between human-led profiles and those operated by algorithms. This labeling initiative aims to maintain trust as synthetic media becomes more prevalent.

Mosseri also addressed how AI content affects Instagram's ranking systems, clarifying that the platform does not automatically penalize AI-generated posts. However, he expressed skepticism about whether synthetic content consistently matches the quality and engagement levels of organic human creations. While the platform has been tightening its oversight of AI profiles since earlier this year, challenges such as deepfakes and low-quality automated content remain key areas of focus for the company's moderation teams.
