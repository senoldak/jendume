SoftBank made a massive bet on natural gas power plants to meet the surging energy demands of AI data centers, but the strategy has hit a significant roadblock. The investment firm announced a $7.1 billion quarterly loss, with more than half of that figure stemming from losses related to power-generating equipment. The company had paid nonrefundable deposits to turbine manufacturers to secure orders, aiming to corner the market by ordering 13 gigawatts of turbines—nearly 50% more than it actually needed. This aggressive procurement strategy was intended to bypass long wait times for grid connections, but it ultimately resulted in a costly miscalculation.

The energy sector was caught off guard by the speed and scale of AI’s power requirements, leading many hyperscalers to view natural gas as a reliable energy security blanket. However, this reliance comes with risks, such as winter supply curtailments when demand spikes. Industry analysts suggest that data center operators are now realizing that direct grid connections, supplemented by large-scale battery storage, offer a more cost-effective and flexible solution than building dedicated gas plants. While SoftBank’s losses highlight the volatility of the current electricity mania surrounding AI, they are unlikely to trigger a mass cancellation of gas power projects across the industry. The sheer scale of AI’s power demand remains too significant for such localized setbacks to derail the broader infrastructure build-out, as the industry continues to navigate the complex intersection of energy and computing.
