SAP has decided to extend access to its AI agent development platform, Joule Studio 2.0, to customers using on-premise systems. However, this access is strictly linked to the company's cloud transformation program, known as RISE. To utilize these new AI capabilities, customers are expected to commit to migrating their infrastructure to the cloud and maintain specific service ratios. This requirement has sparked concerns that smaller enterprises, particularly those operating with a single ERP system, might face significant barriers to entry.

User groups argue that the current commercial model is primarily designed for large-scale enterprises with multiple ERP systems. According to this perspective, businesses with a single system are effectively forced to convert their entire on-premise maintenance agreements into cloud subscriptions to gain access to AI features. SAP, however, rejects these claims, stating that eligibility is not determined by the number of ERP instances and that single-instance customers can also qualify if they meet the necessary technical and commercial criteria.

This development highlights the gap between SAP's "autonomous enterprise" strategy and the current technological reality of its customer base. While the company aims to migrate its users to cloud-based systems, many businesses continue to rely on legacy ECC systems. Data indicates that only 39% of the 35,000 global ECC customers have migrated to the next-generation S/4HANA platform. Furthermore, SAP faces a gap of approximately 2 billion euros in its target to transition customers away from on-premise support. This strategic move serves as a critical indicator of both the adoption rate of AI technologies and the pace of cloud transformation within the enterprise software sector.
