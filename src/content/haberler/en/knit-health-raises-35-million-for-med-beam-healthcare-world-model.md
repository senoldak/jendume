Knit Health, a startup founded in 2025 by scientists from the University of California, Berkeley, has raised $35 million in a Series A funding round led by NEA. The round included participation from Town Hall Ventures, SignalFire, and existing backers. The capital is earmarked for the rollout of Med-BEAM, which the company describes as a 'world model' for the healthcare industry, across various health systems and clinical groups.

Unlike traditional large language models that primarily process clinical notes, Med-BEAM is trained on billions of real-world healthcare decisions and hundreds of millions of longitudinal patient records. This allows the model to understand the trajectory of a patient's health, predicting likely future care needs and simulating how different treatment paths might alter clinical outcomes. The company emphasizes that the model is trained exclusively on de-identified data with documented usage rights.

Performance metrics released by Knit Health suggest a significant leap in efficiency. In comparative tests predicting clinical actions for hospitalized patients over a 24-hour window, Med-BEAM was reportedly up to four times more accurate than frontier AI models. Furthermore, the model operated 100 times faster and was 250 times more cost-effective than the alternatives tested. A key feature of the system is its ability to cross-check predictions against real outcomes, flagging instances where clinical evidence is insufficient for a confident prediction.

CEO Jonathan Kolstad noted that healthcare currently operates too often on chance factors, such as which hospital a patient enters. By integrating Med-BEAM into workflows like care management, specialist scheduling, and clinical trial recruitment, the company seeks to bring more consistency to the sector. The investment brings Knit Health's total funding to $46.6 million, positioning it to expand its engineering teams and broaden the deployment of its predictive technology.
