Centrist parties backing Ursula von der Leyen in the European Parliament split over whether artificial intelligence companies should answer for harm caused by their models. Socialist and Green lawmakers called for a new liability act, while the centre-right argued that Europe needs less regulation rather than more.

The European Commission originally proposed AI liability rules as a directive in 2022, but withdrew them last year as part of an effort to simplify EU law and boost the bloc's competitiveness. Alex Agius Saliba, a Maltese vice-president for the Socialists and Democrats, urged the Commission to bring the legislation back, calling the withdrawal a big mistake.

Socialists and Greens argued that AI firms should be held liable for damages like any other product manufacturer. Dutch Green MEP Kim van Sparrentak compared faulty AI to dangerous electronics, while centre-right Swedish MEP Arba Kokalari countered that the EU already has a comprehensive rulebook through the AI Act and should focus on competitiveness, private investments, and computing infrastructure.

Jozef Síkela, the commissioner for international partnerships, explained that the Commission withdrew the rules because EU countries and MEPs could not reach an agreement. Similar debates are ongoing in Washington regarding who bears responsibility when autonomous AI systems cause unexpected harm.
