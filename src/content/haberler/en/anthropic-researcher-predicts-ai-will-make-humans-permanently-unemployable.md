Tao Lin, a safety researcher at the prominent AI firm Anthropic, has shared alarming views regarding the future of human labor. In an hour-long interview with Palisade Research, Lin stated that almost all humans on Earth are likely to become permanently unemployable within a short span of time. According to Lin, while people might briefly transition to new occupations, they will eventually find themselves unable to compete with AI and will have to rely on state financial support with no hope of finding employment again.

The timing of these comments is particularly noteworthy as Anthropic prepares for what is expected to be one of the largest initial public offerings (IPOs) in history. Lin emphasized that AI will soon be more capable than individuals and will be able to 'defeat' them personally. While these warnings sound like science fiction, they are part of a broader discourse within the AI industry where the perceived power of a model directly correlates with investor interest and company valuation.

However, critics point out that current evidence for a coming 'jobpocalypse' remains thin. While AI models are being integrated into workplaces, they often produce errors or 'slop' that can create more work for humans rather than less. Many hiring slowdowns observed recently are attributed more to post-pandemic economic shifts than to AI displacement. Despite this, the narrative of an all-powerful AI remains a central theme in the safety and ethics discussions led by major labs like Anthropic.

Ultimately, the researcher's vested interest in the upcoming IPO cannot be ignored. As Anthropic employees hold equity that will convert to common stock, portraying the company's technology as world-altering—even in a frightening way—can drive up demand. This situation illustrates a paradoxical marketing strategy where fear serves as the ultimate sales pitch, blurring the lines between genuine safety concerns and corporate promotion.
