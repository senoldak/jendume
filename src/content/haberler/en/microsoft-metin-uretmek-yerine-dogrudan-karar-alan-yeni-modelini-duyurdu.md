Microsoft has expanded its artificial intelligence portfolio with the introduction of a new decision-making model called Microsoft-Decision-1. Rather than writing paragraphs of prose or engaging in open-ended chat, this model reads inputs and scores a closed set of options, returning calibrated probabilities in a single pass.

Built upon Alibaba's Qwen3.5-9B architecture, the model is tailored for routing, classification, and verification tasks where software applications need immediate, actionable outputs. According to company benchmarks, it achieves high accuracy with a fast response time, making it significantly quicker and more cost-effective for specific workflows.

Tested across various internal applications such as feedback sorting and quality control, the model offers developers a streamlined tool for automated decisions. However, experts note limitations including its closed weights and text-only constraints, emphasizing that applications still require careful threshold definitions and human oversight.
