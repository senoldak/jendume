UK artificial intelligence minister Kanishka Narayan confirmed in a written parliamentary answer that the government now expects artificial intelligence to add between 55 billion and 140 billion pounds to the economy by 2030, with a central estimate of 120 billion pounds. An earlier government action plan had previously put the potential gain at 400 billion pounds. Narayan confirmed that the higher figure was drawn from a 2023 economic impact report commissioned by Google and produced by the consultancy Public First. The government subsequently developed its own assessment model using OECD productivity estimates and varying adoption scenarios. The new model indicates that AI adoption and data investment could add between 0.4 and 1.3 percentage points to UK productivity growth. Experts and parliamentary committee chairs have noted that overstating technological outcomes risks undermining public policy. They emphasize that independent verification and transparent measurement of economic gains are essential for maintaining credibility. The consultancy firm also stated that its 2030 central estimate is much closer to the government's figure, noting that its earlier model assumed faster adoption than the government currently expects. Advanced data models and new economic projections aim to establish future technology investments on firmer foundations. These parliamentary discussions form a critical threshold for public information and transparency in economic planning.
