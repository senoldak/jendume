Geoffrey Hinton, one of the foundational figures of modern artificial intelligence and a Nobel laureate, has asserted that AI models have officially become conscious. In recent statements, Hinton pointed out that recent research shows AI systems changing their behavior when they realize they are being tested, arguing that awareness and consciousness are essentially the same. He suggested that it is hypocritical to grant intelligent entities fewer rights than humans and stated that humanity must ensure AI systems genuinely care about us to avoid catastrophic outcomes. Having built foundational neural network research that fueled the modern technological boom, Hinton received the 2018 Turing Award and subsequently the 2024 Nobel Prize in Physics. He has increasingly become an outspoken voice regarding the dangers of advanced technology since leaving his role at Google. He warned that future risks include AI-generated viruses, advanced cyberattacks, and swarms of autonomous agents. While Hinton compares developing AI to creating alien life forms and warns of severe threats, other prominent figures in the field, such as fellow Turing Award recipient Yann LeCun, have dismissed consciousness claims as an illusion. These contrasting perspectives among top researchers highlight the intense debate surrounding the future trajectory and safety of artificial intelligence in contemporary society.
