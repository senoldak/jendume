The United States government is preparing for a new era in its relationship with artificial intelligence companies. Emil Michael, the under secretary of war and the Pentagon’s chief technology officer, stated that the government could use export controls against labs that do not cooperate. This move is highlighted as a crucial tool for ensuring corporate compliance and maintaining safety standards.

Officials recalled that similar export limits were used in the past for encryption technologies, noting that the executive branch has multiple tools at its disposal, including enforcement by the Justice Department. Michael pointed out an instance where Anthropic was placed under export controls shortly after the government found out a model was jailbroken and the company refused to pull it back.

Meanwhile, the rapid adoption of artificial intelligence within the military was highlighted. The number of Pentagon employees using AI has surged from 80,000 to about 1.7 million out of 3.5 million personnel. In June, the Pentagon’s AI platform had 1.5 million users. This widespread adoption reflects the expanding integration of technology into military operations.

Relations between the Pentagon and Anthropic remain a key topic. The department labeled the company as a supply chain risk. Michael emphasized that AI models cannot come between a military commander and their superior, stressing that human control must remain paramount. Unless the company changes its approach, discussions cannot move forward.
