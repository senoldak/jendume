Recent data shared at industry gatherings revealed the clear gap between expectations and realities regarding advanced systems in the corporate world. While a large portion of board members believe that such systems will increase efficiency and reduce costs, the proportion of organizations that have actually achieved their goals remained very low. Evaluations show that a notable segment of businesses exceeded their budgeted limits in spending yet still plan to increase their investments in the upcoming period. This picture clearly demonstrates the strategic misalignments and uncertainties regarding return on investment in transformation processes. Industry analysts state that instead of expecting direct cost savings from these technologies, organizations should approach workflows from a broader perspective. The majority of companies struggle with how to integrate the capacity increases provided by these systems into operational models. Observations indicate that only a limited portion of employee time is freed up thanks to these tools. This situation is evaluated as a result of structural difficulties encountered during the integration into business workflows. Making the resulting capacity usable requires directing teams toward more strategic and high-value areas. This transition process forces institutions to review their training budgets and reconsider their operational models. Sector reports predict that technology spending will constitute a certain percentage of total budgets in the coming period, though a significant portion of leaders may struggle to meet targeted financial outcomes.
