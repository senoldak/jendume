AI pioneer Andrew Ng has spoken out against the narrative that machines will eventually take over the world, arguing that such fears are being utilized to limit industry competition. Speaking during a program appearance, the prominent researcher stated that artificial intelligence safety frameworks should not be built upon exaggerated and frightening scenarios. Ng emphasized that certain major corporations use these safety arguments to protect their market positions and hinder fair competition within the sector.

Ng criticized leading closed-model developers such as OpenAI and Anthropic, suggesting that heavily funded corporations benefit from regulations that stifle free and open-source alternatives. He noted that billions of dollars in investments enable these dominant players to push for rules that block free competitor options under the guise of safety. While arguing against unnecessary regulatory overreach, Ng acknowledged that government bodies should still focus on blocking harmful content such as deepfakes and similar malicious outputs.

Addressing artificial general intelligence, Ng noted that reaching human-level or superhuman machine intelligence within the current decade remains an unlikely scenario. He dismissed rapid runaway self-improvement theories as overhyped, pointing out substantial physical and infrastructural bottlenecks in the sector. The researcher explained that building advanced systems requires overcoming major hurdles rather than following an effortless trajectory.

Building massive data centers and securing adequate energy supplies present significant physical hurdles that prevent instantaneous advancement. Ng's assessment underlines the practical limitations facing the rapid scaling of advanced artificial intelligence systems. By highlighting these physical constraints, the researcher demonstrated that many popular claims regarding imminent technological singularity and exponential runaway growth lack realistic foundations.
