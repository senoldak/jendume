OpenAI has intensified its security measures to protect its proprietary artificial intelligence technologies from foreign intelligence threats. During internal security briefings, the company warned personnel about honey trap tactics, where intelligence agents use romantic relationships or friendly approaches to gather sensitive information. Examples shared with staff included agents striking up casual conversations in public spaces, such as parks, before steering discussions toward confidential work matters.

The security risks extend beyond personal interactions to physical espionage methods, including eavesdropping in public spaces, offering financial incentives, and shoulder-surfing device screens. Consequently, employees were advised to use privacy screen protectors, erase whiteboard notes after meetings, and avoid wearing company-branded apparel in public. Similar warnings were reportedly extended to workers at Anthropic and Google.

Espionage concerns were further heightened after a covert recording device was discovered in an OpenAI meeting room; the U.S. Department of Justice has charged a former employee over it.

As artificial intelligence becomes a pivotal element of global economic and technological power, the physical and digital protection of leading research labs has transitioned into a matter of paramount strategic importance. These comprehensive security measures reflect the intense espionage pressures facing major artificial intelligence research centers worldwide.
