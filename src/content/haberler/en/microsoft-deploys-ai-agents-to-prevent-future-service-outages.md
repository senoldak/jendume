Microsoft has declared a "Code Red" alert following a massive service outage in mid-November that crippled email and corporate services globally. The software giant is planning to deploy AI agents to help engineers diagnose problems and analyze data faster. The ultimate goal of this initiative is to reduce the resolution time for future global incidents to under one hour.

The company is increasingly relying on autonomous systems to manage complex cloud infrastructure. During the recent disruption, which affected Exchange Online and related office suites, engineers struggled to navigate fragmented internal tracking systems, resulting in over 11,000 messages exchanged across multiple communication channels. Management described the chaotic environment as akin to a "fog of war," noting that the incident caused roughly a decade's worth of acceptable failures.

The shift toward AI-driven diagnostics follows the company's announcement of significant downsizing. Microsoft has offered buyouts to 60,000 employees, representing over a quarter of its remaining workforce. Observers are questioning whether AI agents can bridge the gap left by the reduction in human personnel.

The decision to integrate AI agents into core engineering operations is controversial. Other major tech companies have previously suffered critical incidents due to malfunctioning or recklessly used AI agents. As Microsoft attempts to restructure its engineering practices, the industry is watching closely to see if these automated tools can provide the necessary stability or if they introduce new layers of complexity to the systems that power the modern digital world.
