Toronto-based credential management company 1Password has introduced "1Password for Agents," a platform designed to let personal AI agents log into online services on a user's behalf without ever accessing the underlying password. The system enables agents to handle tasks such as booking trips, paying recurring bills, and preparing expense reports safely.

To complete multi-step tasks, autonomous software assistants often require access to private accounts. Many users currently copy and paste raw passwords directly into conversational interfaces, creating uncontrolled copies across remote systems. 1Password emphasizes that giving software agents persistent and direct access to sensitive credentials introduces substantial security risks.

The new workflow operates through explicit authorization and isolation. When an AI agent encounters a login interface, it verifies its identity with 1Password using its own digital key. The account owner receives a notification detailing which account the agent wants to access and why. Upon user approval, 1Password automates the authentication process on the secure login interface while withholding sensitive details from the automated assistant, keeping the master credential securely inside the personal vault.

Launch partners integrating with the platform include Meta Superintelligence Labs' Muse assistant, OpenAI's ChatGPT Work systems alongside Anthropic's Claude Duet integrations and associated developer ecosystems. Other companies including Browserbase, Browser Use, Perplexity, and Ramp have also joined the ecosystem to support safer automation.

The feature expands upon the Secure Agentic Autofill capability piloted with Browserbase in October 2025. As security researchers warn about the risks of AI agents being exploited to target web services, 1Password notes that its enterprise vault protects more than 1.6 billion credentials across over 200,000 businesses.
