KPMG has completed an experimental parallel audit of a FTSE 250 company, deploying more than 100 AI agents to work alongside a traditional audit team. Known internally as "Project Exponential," the initiative aimed to test the capabilities of autonomous agents across every phase of an annual audit. The results showed that, in several instances, the AI agents were able to dig deeper into individual questions and formulate better inquiries than the human auditors themselves. The agents independently collected, analyzed, and documented data, drafting conclusions that were then reviewed by human staff. While the technology demonstrated significant potential, it was not entirely autonomous. Human intervention remained critical, particularly when the agents struggled to correctly match complex regulations against specific company documents. This underscores that while AI can handle labor-intensive tasks, the final accountability and oversight must rest with human professionals.

This development carries significant implications for the professional services industry. As firms explore these technologies, they are grappling with questions regarding fee structures and the future of entry-level roles. If AI can handle the bulk of data-heavy audit work, the traditional model of training junior auditors through repetitive tasks may need to be completely reimagined. Regulatory bodies are also closely monitoring these shifts. There is a growing concern that AI agents, by digging deep into a company's data, might uncover findings that could be perceived as sales pitches for additional consulting services, potentially threatening auditor independence. Regulators emphasize that regardless of the technology used, the auditor remains fully accountable for the results. As rivals also experiment with similar parallel audits, the industry is clearly moving toward a future where AI is deeply embedded in financial oversight, though the path to full-scale adoption remains complex. Given the industry's history, including past fines of 21 million pounds, maintaining strict independence remains a top priority.
