OpenAI continues its commitment to leveraging machine learning models to solve some of the oldest problems in mathematics and science. During a recent podcast appearance, OpenAI's Sam Altman asserted that humanity deserves fundamental knowledge and announced that the company will persist in publishing breakthrough after breakthrough. Altman confirmed that the firm holds additional unpublished discoveries in its vaults.

However, this rapid pace has sparked both excitement and anxiety within the academic community. OpenAI's recent release of 722 manuscripts tackling 400 problems on GitHub left scientists overwhelmed by a massive wave of information. Thore Husfeldt of the IT University of Copenhagen noted that having years of research suddenly undercut by machine output can be psychologically taxing for many researchers.

Meanwhile, AI models' growing problem-solving capabilities have upended foundational concepts. For instance, the apparent disproving of the exponential time hypothesis (ETH) forced numerous experts to scramble and catch up. While Linköping University professor Peter Jonsson acknowledged that undermining long-standing research can be frustrating, he emphasized that learning from such findings is what science is all about.

UC Santa Barbara professor Daniel Lokshtanov described the mood as a giant bomb dropped into the field, whereas Husfeldt likened it to illuminating a dark room with floodlights of knowledge. Although OpenAI's recent proofs lack peer review or independent verification, the generational shift driven by artificial intelligence continues to transform scientific study rapidly.
