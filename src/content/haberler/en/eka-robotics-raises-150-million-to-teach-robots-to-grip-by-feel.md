Eka Robotics has completed a $150 million Series A funding round for its technology that enables robots to grip objects using touch and force feedback alongside sight. Announced in October 2026, the round was led by Andreessen Horowitz (a16z) and F-Prime, valuing the company at $1.45 billion post-money.

The firm combines vision with feedback from force sensors embedded in the robot's hand, allowing the machine to feel a grip slipping and correct it in real time. Co-founder Pulkit Agrawal noted that while vision helps locate an object, the actual grabbing and searching happen through physical forces.

The company designed a custom hand featuring more force sensors than standard grippers. Costing less than $1,000, the hardware is rated for 5 million duty cycles with a goal of reaching 20 million. The Vision-Force-Action model is trained using reinforcement learning inside a physics simulator before being deployed to real hardware.

Eka states that its robots can pick untrained objects at a rate of 1,500 per hour, roughly two and a half times the human rate of 600. The same robot can screw in light bulbs and insert wires while handling objects up to 1 kg with a success rate exceeding 95%.

Currently running pilots with industrial customers, the startup will use the new funds to train its model on additional tasks and accelerate customer deployments. Industry experts note that the ultimate test will be operating continuously on real assembly lines without breaking.
