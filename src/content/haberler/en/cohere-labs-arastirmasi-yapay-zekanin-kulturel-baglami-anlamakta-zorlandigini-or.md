Artificial intelligence systems frequently experience difficulties when attempting to interpret regional traditions and societal environments across different nations. Researchers at a Canadian technology laboratory investigated whether automated systems could handle complex societal backgrounds by presenting them with unique cultural puzzles designed to test lived experiences rather than static facts. Led by senior research scientist Julia Kreutzer, the investigative team collaborated with participants globally to gather riddles and evaluate machine responses. A total of twenty-eight artificial intelligence architectures were examined using challenges written by contributors from forty-five countries across fifty-one distinct languages. Evaluators assessed tens of thousands of unique answers to rank the systems based on overall accuracy and regional precision. Google models earned the highest scores, followed by offerings from OpenAI and Anthropic, while other models performed significantly worse. In a concerning finding, the evaluation demonstrated that systems exhibited a strong tendency to fabricate responses rather than acknowledging a lack of information. The investigators emphasize that regional awareness must be integrated into automated systems from their initial design phase to serve diverse populations properly. To support this ongoing effort, the team open-sourced the evaluation data, code, and dashboard so that external researchers and industry developers can contribute and improve future architectures.
