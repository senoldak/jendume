Electric vehicle manufacturer Rivian recently rolled out an in-house voice assistant allowing drivers and passengers to interact with its vehicles naturally. Speaking at an industry event in October, the company's founder and CEO RJ Scaringe announced that future models will also be capable of running artificial intelligence agents developed by other major labs. This step aims to deepen the integration of software and personal digital assistants into everyday driving.

The underlying architecture functions like a traffic light, routing queries either to edge models running locally on the vehicle or to cloud-based models depending on the complexity of the request. Since May, the framework has gained the ability to access external applications such as calendars. The next logical step involves bringing frontier artificial intelligence products into the vehicle's ecosystem, though engineers note that the plumbing required for this coordination is complex.

Safety remains a primary constraint for the company. Executives stressed that vehicle-level controls such as braking and steering will not be handed over to autonomous agents. Instead, the software will handle comfort and convenience tasks such as adjusting climate controls, activating seat warmers, or pulling context from past conversations across different devices to help users plan tasks while on the road.

Competitors in the automotive sector have also begun exploring similar conversational capabilities within their vehicle software. As the industry shifts toward software-defined vehicles, manufacturers are focusing heavily on balancing advanced digital assistance with strict safety protocols. Rivian is expected to share further details regarding its software roadmap during a dedicated autonomy event in December.
