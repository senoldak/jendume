Researchers at KIMM in South Korea have created an innovative prototype designed to match the position of sound with visual elements on a display. The system utilizes screen vibrations to generate audio while dynamically shifting the location of the sound source in real time. Placed behind the panel, eighty vibration elements activate specific target areas rather than moving the entire surface. This approach resolves the common issue of audio and visuals originating from different points by synchronizing the sound output zone with the movement of the visual source.

The system analyzes how the panel responds to vibrations to calculate the exact voltage required for each individual element. This ensures that sound is emitted exclusively from the targeted area. During tests performed by the research team, target region selection accuracy reached 99.9 percent. The vibration amplitude difference between active and inactive zones measured up to 35 decibels. The prototype spans approximately 100 inches, combining a transparent LED film with an aluminum panel structure.

Throughout this process, artificial intelligence assumes the responsibility of identifying the position and size of sound sources within the image. Sound separation techniques isolate the desired audio from mixtures and reproduce it through the corresponding screen area. The modular design enables components to be combined or separated based on different screen sizes, while supporting low-frequency sound production when multiple modules operate together.

The findings have been shared academically, and the team reported securing a South Korean patent for the modular acoustic emission system. However, no commercial release date or consumer pricing has been announced yet, as the measurements reflect laboratory conditions rather than final consumer products.
