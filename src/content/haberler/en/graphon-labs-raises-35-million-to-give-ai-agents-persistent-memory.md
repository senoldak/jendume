AI agents are becoming increasingly capable of writing code and executing complex tasks. However, one of their most significant weaknesses remains the inability to track past conversations or evolving company data effectively. San Francisco-based Graphon Labs has secured $35 million in a new funding round to address this critical gap by providing AI agents with persistent memory.

The system developed by the company allows AI models to retain information within a connected network structure, rather than treating data as isolated fragments. Built on a mathematical framework known as "graphons," the technology maps the relationships between a company’s documents, videos, and images. This enables the AI to "remember" not just the content, but where the information originated and how it has changed over time.

This technology aims to prevent the loss of context, which is a major hurdle in corporate environments. Standard methods often struggle to maintain relationships and timing as data volume grows. Graphon Labs’ system achieved a 93.9% score on LoCoMo, a benchmark for memory over long conversations. Furthermore, it scored 81.7% on ATM-Bench, a benchmark covering video, audio, images, and text, outperforming the next system on the public leaderboard by 5 points.

The company plans to use the new capital primarily to expand its engineering and research teams. Currently utilized by large organizations such as the San Francisco 49ers, Hitachi, and HP, this system is helping transform AI from a tool that provides momentary answers into an assistant with institutional memory. Looking ahead, the company aims to integrate this technology into personal devices like phones and wearables.
