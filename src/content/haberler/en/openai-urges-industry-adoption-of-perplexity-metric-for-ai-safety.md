OpenAI Chief Scientist Jakub Pachocki published internal perplexity measurements tracking model capabilities from GPT-3.5 to current frontier systems. Although sharing these measurements gives competitors technical visibility, the company decided that establishing shared industry safety limits and scientific standards outweighs competitive risks.

Pachocki explained that frontier AI progress fundamentally relies on training better data predictors or compressors on large datasets. As this core compression metric improves, unpredictable latent capabilities emerge naturally. Internal evaluation benchmarks show that log-perplexity (cross-entropy loss) stood at approximately 0.99 for GPT-3.5, before dropping to 0.91 in GPT-4, which enabled structured chain-of-thought reasoning. As losses dropped below 0.8, transformative capabilities emerged in fields such as cybersecurity and advanced mathematics.

When model performance reached a log-perplexity of approximately 0.76, internal systems began resolving long-standing open mathematical problems. Pachocki noted that as AI increasingly contributes to its own research, fundamental pretraining advancements could accelerate rapidly as early as 2027. Further progression into the 0.68 to 0.70 log-perplexity window is expected to unlock additional transformative capabilities that current benchmarks might not adequately capture.

To mitigate emerging risks, Pachocki proposed establishing common limits based on perplexity that developers commit not to cross until AI alignment and auditing tools reach verifiable milestones. He urged frontier labs to agree on shared norms for red-teaming and safety monitoring as new intelligence thresholds are reached.
