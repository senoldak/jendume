San Francisco-based startup The Biological Computing Co. has released a new artificial intelligence video model accelerated by insights drawn from living brain cells, the company announced on Thursday. Named TBC Mischief 1, the model transforms still images into short video clips. Built upon Wan 2.2, an open video model created by Alibaba, the new system is currently available on developer platforms fal and OpenRouter.

According to the company, TBC Mischief 1 runs approximately five times faster than Wan 2.2 while maintaining the same level of quality. The model is priced at $0.005 per second of video generation, making a five-second clip cost 2.5 cents. However, the company has not yet published independent benchmark tests to substantiate its speed claims.

The living neurons remain secured inside TBC's laboratory. The company studies how lab-grown networks of neurons react to various signals and subsequently translates these findings into a compact software add-on capable of running on standard AI chips. Jon Pomeraniec, co-founder and chief operating officer at TBC, stated that the company is building a platform capable of reshaping the economics of AI at scale.

The launch follows a strategic agreement signed with Amazon Web Services in September to distribute TBC's models to business customers. Elsewhere in the field, other organizations are exploring direct biological computing; notably, a rack of living neurons is already operational in Singapore. These developments highlight the growing convergence of biological insights and artificial intelligence infrastructure.
