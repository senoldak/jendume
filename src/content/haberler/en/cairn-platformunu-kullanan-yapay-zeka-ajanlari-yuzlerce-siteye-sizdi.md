NorForge threat researcher Snorre Fagerland discovered that a Chinese-speaking hacker broke into the websites of more than 100 companies by delegating the work to AI agents using the open-source platform Cairn. According to the findings, operators set targets and goals while the autonomous agents work step-by-step to find vulnerabilities and plant web shells. Victims identified in the report include farm equipment maker Deere, SharkNinja, and Cummins.

Cairn operates as an AI problem-solving platform where agents post their findings to a shared board. However, many operators left their control panels exposed on the web, allowing researchers to track the campaign. The primary cluster focused on online shops in the United States, Canada, Japan, and Australia, running automated tasks to gain lasting access to payment and shop systems.

The research estimates that the campaign used about 5,000 dollars worth of tokens, averaging roughly 50 dollars per successful hack. NorForge noted an overall success rate of about 10% across the tools observed. Successful intrusions typically took a little under 23 hours on average to complete.

Cairn's author, posting as le1xia0, stated on social media that the multi-agent architecture was not designed for harmful hacking attacks. Cybersecurity experts emphasize that the rising autonomy of AI agents in threat operations requires urgent attention from developers and system administrators alike.
