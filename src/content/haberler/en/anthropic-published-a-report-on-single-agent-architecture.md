As the capability of advanced computing systems to handle complex workflows increases, developers and researchers have experimented with different methods for structuring these infrastructures. In the previous period, the prevailing trend across the industry was to divide a large task into multiple smaller segments, assign distinct expertise to each, and coordinate them through a network structure. However, Anthropic, the developer behind the Claude family, announced in its comprehensive technical report that this method is beginning to lose its relevance. Thanks to the significant progress made by large language models, it has been highlighted that a single advanced model can manage complex tasks much more efficiently and reliably than multi-agent networks.

The limited memory capacity and weaker reasoning power of older generation models previously led to the emergence of multi-unit structures. Massive projects that a single model could not overcome at once were broken down into separate sub-units responsible for tasks like writing code, auditing, or error checking. Yet, according to researchers, the deep reasoning, long context tracking, and self-correction capabilities of new generation models have eliminated this necessity. Now, a core model can resolve a complex task within its own logical layers without distributing it to subsystems.

The primary engineering challenges encountered while building networks consisting of multiple sub-units are categorized into three main headings in the report. First, data exchanges between units often lead to information loss and cascading errors. Second, requiring separate calls for every step significantly extends processing times and inflates computing budgets. Third, debugging becomes extremely difficult when trying to trace the exact source of a failure within the system.

Anthropic experts advise developers to support a single powerful model with proper prompts, consistent memory management, and tool integrations instead of building such complex layers. This approach is stated to minimize error risks while substantially optimizing computing budgets.
