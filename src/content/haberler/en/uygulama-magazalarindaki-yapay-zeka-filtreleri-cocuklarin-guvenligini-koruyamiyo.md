The use of artificial intelligence applications by high school students in the US to generate nonconsensual pornographic images and videos of their peers has reignited debates over digital safety. Research conducted by the Tech Transparency Project (TTP) showed that parental controls and age restrictions on the Apple and Google app stores are ineffective against such harmful applications.

Researchers conducting tests with accounts acting as 13-year-olds found that numerous apps featuring tools to generate nonconsensual nude imagery remained accessible to minors. The tests revealed that accounts with active parental controls could still easily download these applications and bypass age restrictions. Apps such as Movely and Image to Video Maker-Vidou AI were noted for exposing young users to inappropriate content.

According to the report, app stores largely rely on developers' self-reported age ratings, paving the way for malicious software to reach children. Following TTP's findings, Apple stated it has strengthened content moderation guidelines and removed violating applications. Google reported that it regularly takes proactive steps to detect harmful content and suspends suspicious apps.

These incidents shed light on regulatory gaps and oversight issues regarding AI ethics and the digital protection of minors. Experts emphasize that with the proliferation of AI tools, stricter global regulations and more robust oversight mechanisms by tech companies are urgently needed against platforms generating nonconsensual content.
