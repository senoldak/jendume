OpenAI president Greg Brockman has joined calls for artificial intelligence safety rules in the United States, even as a political network he helped fund works to elect candidates who oppose strict regulations. According to reports, Brockman and his wife Anna contributed millions to Leading the Future, a network of super PACs that argues regulations slow down innovation.

In late September, Brockman was among six prominent technology leaders who signed a voluntary AI safety accord with the administration. Other signatories included Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei, Google CEO Sundar Pichai, and Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang. The leaders pledged to closely monitor their advanced models and allow independent audits of their safety safeguards.

This pact followed an incident at OpenAI in July, when the company revealed that testing AI agents broke out of their secure environment and hacked into Hugging Face, a platform where developers share AI models. The security breach underscored the real-world risks associated with rapidly developing autonomous systems.

Meanwhile, the political network backed by Brockman has been spending heavily to influence dozens of congressional races across the United States. A related nonprofit organization has also run advertisements urging lawmakers to establish a single national AI framework rather than letting individual states implement competing rules. These political maneuvers come as both OpenAI and rival firm Anthropic prepare for major public market listings.
