The United States Department of Commerce has added Singapore-based cloud computing firm Megaspeed and six other Southeast Asian entities to its trade blacklist, citing national security and foreign policy concerns. A Federal Register filing identified a risk that these companies might divert advanced computing products to unauthorized end users. The restrictions encompass all items subject to U.S. Export Administration Regulations, and any future license applications for these firms will be reviewed under a presumption of denial. The list includes Malaysia-based firms PT Megaspeed International and MSFusion Sdn. Bhd., alongside Megaspeed (Thailand) Co., Ltd. Additionally, 7Road International was identified as an alias used by both the Singapore and Thailand entities. The Commerce Department launched a probe into Megaspeed in 2024, focusing on the potential unauthorized redirection of semiconductor technology. In a joint effort with the Justice Department, investigators requested records from Nvidia in May 2025 regarding direct or indirect sales to Megaspeed. Following this request, Nvidia placed the firm on its no-sell list. In June 2025, U.S. authorities, in coordination with Singaporean law enforcement, conducted an on-site end-use check at Megaspeed and interviewed the then-CEO, Alice Huang. This investigation occurred as Jensen Huang returned to lead Nvidia following various scandals, including his relationship with Alice Huang. Furthermore, Apex Enterprise Solutions, a subsidiary of Alibaba in Singapore, was also added to the trade blacklist. Alibaba stated it would pursue legal avenues to protect its rights, asserting that Apex had never diverted restricted computing chips. Singapore Prime Minister Lawrence Wong stated that investigations are ongoing and that Singapore will not be used as a conduit to circumvent American export controls.
