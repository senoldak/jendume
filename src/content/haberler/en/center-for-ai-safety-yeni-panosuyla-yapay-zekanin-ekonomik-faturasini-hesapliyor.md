Efforts to measure the workforce impacts of rapid technological advancements have entered a new phase with the launch of a specialized tracking tool. The Center for AI Safety (CAIS) has introduced the Wage Loss Dashboard, designed to translate AI-related workforce reductions into precise financial costs for households and local economies.

According to the dashboard data, the country has recorded just over 117,000 job cuts explicitly attributed to AI this year, translating to an estimated $8.5 billion in annual wage losses. By breaking down these figures by state and dividing them by population, the tool calculates the average annual wage loss per person. In California, for instance, a high concentration of tech sector layoffs brings the estimated average loss to $56 per person, while Alaska records an estimated $14 per person.

The dashboard aggregates data from monthly layoff reports and federal labor statistics to map out these economic ripples. Researchers note that a single layoff reduces income for local businesses, creating a cascading effect across groceries, housing, and services. However, the creators acknowledge certain limitations, as the tool does not factor in jobs created by AI, productivity gains, or secondary economic stimuli, focusing strictly on publicly stated workforce reductions.

In addition to tracking layoffs, the dashboard incorporates metrics from the Remote Labor Index to monitor how quickly autonomous agents are evolving in freelance tasks. The percentage of professional-quality remote tasks completed by top AI agents has risen from 2.5 percent to 15.8 percent over several months. Researchers emphasize that combining layoff data with the rising capability of AI agents helps policymakers and workers respond with grounded information rather than speculation.
