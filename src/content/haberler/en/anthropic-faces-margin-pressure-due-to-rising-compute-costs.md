Anthropic has updated its financial filings as it prepares for an initial public offering. While the company reported a strong revenue of $17 billion in the third quarter, the costs associated with compute power are increasingly weighing on its profit margins. The share of revenue consumed by compute costs rose to 58%, up from 48% in the previous quarter, signaling a tightening of operational margins.

The immense computational power required to train and run advanced AI models remains the largest operating expense in the industry. To keep pace with surging demand, the company has been forced to rely on short-term, higher-cost compute agreements. The compute bill for the third quarter alone reached $10 billion, with $5 billion of that amount tied to short-term commitments that carry significantly higher rates than multi-year arrangements.

Although the company is working on "Anthropic Direct" to build its own infrastructure and reduce reliance on third parties, it remains dependent on "neocloud" providers like SpaceX, Nscale, Akamai, and Volta to meet immediate needs. This highlights the vulnerability of major AI labs to the broader infrastructure buildout. While investors currently view the revenue growth as a positive sign, managing these compute costs remains a critical challenge for the company's long-term financial health. The future trajectory of the company will depend heavily on its ability to balance infrastructure investments with operational efficiency as it scales its AI capabilities.
