Residents in the former coal-mining community of Hazle Township, Pennsylvania are mobilizing en masse against a massive Amazon Web Services facility. The data center project, developed by NorthPoint Development and dubbed Project Hazelnut, attempted to smooth over local opposition by offering a 10,000 dollar direct cash dividend to every single resident in the township. Despite reports noting that nearly 17 percent of the local population lives below the federal poverty level, the community has firmly organized to reject both the campus and the accompanying power lines.

The unprecedented financial incentive has failed to sway locals, with many characterizing the payout as a bribe. Homeowners and local business owners warn that property values will drop dramatically if the massive facility moves forward, rendering the cash offer insignificant compared to the long-term community costs. Mary Fox, executive director of the Pennsylvania Ethics Commission, noted that the developer's strategy is highly unusual and creative for a region widely resistant to such facilities.

Tensions are further heightened by the shifting stances of state and local officials. Pennsylvania Governor Josh Shapiro previously utilized an executive order to fast-track data center facilities across the state while referring to the initiative as a technology campus. Meanwhile, Hazle Township supervisor Anthony Griguoli has faced direct questioning from residents over the controversial payout scheme while his office works on zoning ordinances to accommodate the project.
