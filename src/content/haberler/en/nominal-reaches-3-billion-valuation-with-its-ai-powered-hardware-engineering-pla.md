Nominal has secured $240 million in a Series C funding round, pushing its valuation to $3 billion. The company, which specializes in software that helps engineers test and analyze complex hardware such as aircraft, satellites, and robots, has tripled its valuation since March, when it raised $80 million at a $1 billion valuation.

At the core of its offering is "Nominal Analyst," an AI agent that aggregates test data from hardware and provides engineering context. This technology allows engineers to perform complex analyses in near real-time, tasks that previously took hours or even days. This shift is particularly impactful in defense technology and advanced manufacturing, where precision and speed are critical.

Currently, the platform is used by over 80 commercial and government organizations, including five of the seven largest defense contractors. The U.S. Air Force recently selected the company for a $71.2 million strategic funding increase to support the development of collaborative combat aircraft. This partnership underscores the growing trust in AI-driven engineering solutions for national security projects.

Hardware engineering is entering an "AI moment" where the technology provides tangible operational value rather than just accelerating existing workflows. The company plans to use the new capital to develop further AI agents and applications that span the entire lifecycle of a machine, from requirements checks to fleet health monitoring. Expansion into new industries, such as automotive, and strategic acquisitions are also part of the roadmap.

As the company emphasizes, physics still dictates the rules in engineering, and AI must prove its worth in real-world testing environments. By bridging the gap between digital design and physical reality, these AI-powered tools are set to provide a significant competitive advantage to companies building the world’s most advanced machines.
