Google is expanding its artificial intelligence portfolio with the introduction of the Gemini 4 Neon model. According to the company, the new model is designed as a general-purpose tool capable of handling complex coding tasks, multistep agentic workflows, and analytical reasoning. Following the release of its flagship Carbon model and the cost-effective Hydrogen model, Google is now striking a balance between performance and economy with Neon. Surprisingly, benchmarks published by the company show that Neon outperformed the stronger Carbon model on several specific tasks.

According to Google's published benchmarks, Gemini 4 Neon outranked midtier rivals such as Claude Opus 5.5 and GPT-6.2 Sol in areas including knowledge work, reasoning, automation, multimodal understanding, cybersecurity, science, and math. Independent research further revealed that Neon achieved a score of 61 on the Intelligence Index, placing it third overall right behind the leading models from rival developers.

Regarding pricing, Gemini 4 Neon is available at a rate of 3 dollars for every million incoming text elements, while utilizing stored preliminary tokens costs 15 cents per million units, and generating responses requires 15 dollars per million tokens. This places its cost directly between competing midtier models in the industry. As performance levels among various providers grow increasingly comparable, cost efficiency is becoming a critical factor for enterprise customers deciding which tools to integrate into their workflows.

The new model is rolling out to Google AI Pro and Ultra subscribers within the Gemini application. Developers can also access Neon through the enterprise software interface, the Google Antigravity platform, and other business offerings. Google's rapid release cadence underscores its ongoing commitment to providing versatile and competitive options for developers and businesses alike.
