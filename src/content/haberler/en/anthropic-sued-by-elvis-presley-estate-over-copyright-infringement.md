The stakeholders managing Elvis Presley's music catalog have filed a copyright infringement lawsuit against AI company Anthropic. Filed on October 23rd, the legal action claims that the company trained its artificial intelligence models on 274 Elvis songs without authorization. The plaintiffs are demanding $41 million in compensation for the alleged unauthorized use of these musical works.

The stakeholders in the back catalog include Authentic Brands Group, Raleigh Music Publishing, and multiple trusts. According to the lawsuit, Anthropic bypassed digital rights management protections and removed copyright information from tracks such as Don't Be Cruel and Can't Help Falling in Love. Lead attorneys argue that the company has built its business model on copyright infringement at an unprecedented scale.

Anthropic is already facing multiple lawsuits from major music publishers and record labels, including Sony, Warner, and Universal Music, over using thousands of copyrighted works for model training. Each of these lawsuits seeks damages reaching into the billions of dollars. The music industry's pushback underscores the mounting legal and financial risks facing generative AI developers.

Anthropic has previously maintained that training its models on copyrighted content falls under fair use. In response to the new filing, company representatives stated that they disagree with the claims and intend to defend themselves in court. The outcome of this case could set a significant precedent for how AI developers source and utilize copyrighted material.
