As artificial intelligence systems become increasingly proficient at mimicking human behavior, conversation, and reactions, questions regarding their inner mental states continue to grow. However, a comprehensive study conducted by researchers at Ludwig Maximilian University of Munich reveals that human perception remains deeply discerning on this matter.

Published in the scientific journal Cognition, the study involved nearly eleven hundred participants who evaluated characters described as either artificial intelligence systems or humans exhibiting identical behaviors. The findings showed that while responsive artificial agents were recognized as being aware of their surroundings, a striking boundary emerged when participants were asked specifically about consciousness. Even the most responsive artificial intelligence was rated as significantly less conscious than the least responsive human.

The researchers emphasize that people draw a fundamental distinction between mere awareness and true consciousness. While awareness is readily attributed to agents that detect and respond to environmental stimuli, consciousness carries deeper implications of subjective experience that participants consistently reserve for human minds. This distinction highlights the importance of careful language use when discussing artificial intelligence capabilities and avoiding hyped descriptions in public discourse.
