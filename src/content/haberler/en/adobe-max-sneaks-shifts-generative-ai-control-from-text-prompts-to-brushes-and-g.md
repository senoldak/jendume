At the MAX 2026 event in Miami Beach, Adobe showcased 10 research initiatives designed to redefine how humans interact with generative AI. During the 'MAX Sneaks' session, the company demonstrated tools that prioritize brushes, drags, and gestures over traditional text-based prompting. Adobe's core argument is that while chat interfaces are popular, they lack the precision required for high-end visual work, where intent is often discovered through the act of creation rather than pre-defined descriptions.

The showcased projects span video, audio, and imaging. 'Project Scene Sweep' enables a single edit to persist across multiple shots and camera angles in a video sequence, while 'Project Hands On' allows users to reshape objects, such as a mug's handle, by simply dragging control points on the canvas. These advancements leverage faster AI models that can now respond to human input in near real-time, a significant leap from earlier iterations of the Firefly engine.

This strategic direction is backed by Adobe Labs' research titled 'Beyond the Prompt,' which involved studies with 82 creative professionals and a review of 27 research papers between 2024 and 2026. The findings suggest that creatives find their direction by making things, not by describing them upfront. By moving toward graphical interfaces, Adobe aims to make AI feel like a natural extension of the artist's hand rather than a separate automated process.

While these 'Sneaks' are currently research previews and not yet final products, Adobe has launched the Adobe Labs Playground to gather user feedback. Early previews of 'Deep Brush' and 'Hands On' are already available for testing. The company plans to use these insights to shape the next generation of its creative suite, potentially integrating these intuitive AI controls into flagship software like Photoshop and Premiere Pro.
