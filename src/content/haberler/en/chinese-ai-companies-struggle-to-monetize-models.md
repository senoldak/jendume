The revenue gap between US and Chinese AI companies is becoming increasingly apparent. As of September 2026, the combined annual revenue of US-based giants like OpenAI and Anthropic has reached $105 billion, while the total revenue of China's six leading AI firms remains at just $11 billion. This disparity highlights the fundamental strategic differences between the two regions.

Chinese companies predominantly choose to release their models as "open-weight," allowing developers to download and run them on their own infrastructure. While this strategy has led to rapid adoption and high usage rates, it severely limits the companies' ability to generate direct revenue. Third-party providers often offer these models at prices significantly lower than the official rates, creating intense competition. In some instances, models are being sold by third parties at 87% below their official pricing.

In contrast, US companies maintain strict control over their revenue streams by offering their models through closed systems. This approach ensures that users remain dependent on the company's own platforms. To overcome these revenue pressures, Chinese firms are exploring alternative strategies. Instead of relying solely on model usage fees, they are forming partnerships with cloud service providers and securing enterprise-level licensing agreements.

This situation illustrates the delicate balance between "openness" and "profitability" in the AI industry. While Chinese companies have secured a strong position in the technological ecosystem through the widespread adoption of their models, they must now develop new business models to convert this popularity into financial success. Industry analysts note that the gross profit margins for these Chinese firms currently range between 20% and 40%. Moving forward, it is expected that these companies will evolve from simple model developers into providers of integrated enterprise solutions.
