Engineers at Rutgers University have created a rapid noninvasive brain communication system that utilizes artificial intelligence to process neural signals into text at unprecedented rates. Named ChatBCI-Assist, the technology allows users to convey their thoughts efficiently without spelling out every individual character. The approach aims to reduce fatigue for people experiencing severe motor and speech limitations caused by conditions such as advanced amyotrophic lateral sclerosis.

The research was led by Laleh Najafizadeh at the Rutgers School of Engineering and detailed in IEEE Transactions on Biomedical Engineering. While individuals facing advanced motor degeneration retain their cognitive functions, their capability to express ideas physically becomes severely restricted. Traditional communication relies on specialized cameras tracking eye movements or slow letter selection methods.

The newly engineered system instead utilizes a sensor-equipped headwear device that measures electrical signals from the scalp as users observe flashing elements on a computer display. By integrating a specialized language model, the system predicts contextually appropriate expressions based on ongoing interactions. Crucially, the language model executes locally on the user's computer rather than utilizing external cloud services, safeguarding personal health data and functioning independently of stable internet access.

During semantic evaluations, the AI-supported configuration achieved nearly triple the speed of baseline communication alternatives. Researchers are currently preparing for clinical evaluations involving patients to transition the technology into practical medical applications.
