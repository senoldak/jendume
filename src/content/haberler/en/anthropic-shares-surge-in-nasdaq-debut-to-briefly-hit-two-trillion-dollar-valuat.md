Artificial intelligence company Anthropic made a landmark debut on the Nasdaq stock exchange, marking a major milestone for the booming AI industry. Shares of the AI giant opened 34 percent higher on Thursday, briefly pushing the company's valuation to around 2 trillion dollars as investors looked past broader tech sector jitters. The shares initially opened at 111.50 dollars before settling around 98 dollars, remaining well above the 83 dollar pricing set on Wednesday.

Founded five years ago by former OpenAI researchers focused on safety, Anthropic became the first major pure-play AI model developer to list on Wall Street. The company raised a record 81.3 billion dollars in the offering, surpassing the previous record set by SpaceX earlier in the year. The initial public offering valued the maker of the Claude chatbot at roughly 1.5 trillion dollars, which scaled up as trading commenced.

Anthropic's financial growth has surged at an unprecedented pace, with revenue reaching 17 billion dollars in the third quarter compared to 1.4 billion dollars a year prior. Much of this revenue is driven by enterprise customers utilizing its models for tasks such as computer coding. However, heavy computing costs remain a central focus for investors, with the company disclosing approximately 900 billion dollars in commitments for computing capacity.

Chief Executive Dario Amodei stated at the Nasdaq ceremony that the financial milestone strengthens the company's resolve to prioritize safe AI development. While the debut was celebrated by investors, it also drew scrutiny from critics and protesters outside the exchange who raised concerns about AI safety and commercial pressures. Despite these debates, the listing provides ordinary investors with their first direct opportunity to invest in a standalone artificial intelligence giant.
