San Francisco-based startup Town, which provides workplace AI assistants to individual employees, has raised 108 million dollars in a Series B funding round that values the company at 1 billion dollars. Forerunner Ventures and Menlo Ventures co-led the financing round, with participation from existing investors such as a16z and First Round Capital.

Town provides each employee with an assistant known as a 'Townie,' which connects to corporate emails, documents, and calendars to manage daily tasks. The newly expanded platform allows these assistants to communicate with one another, hand off work across teams, and collaborate with external partners while strictly adhering to user permissions and data privacy standards.

The company states that customers own their contextual data by default and Town does not use connected data to train underlying models. Instead, Town leverages models from major AI labs, picking the most suitable model for each specific task to avoid single-lab lock-in. Town officially launched publicly in June 2026 with a 55 million dollar Series A and has experienced rapid adoption since.

Thousands of businesses now rely on Townies for daily operations, reflecting surging demand for collaborative enterprise AI tools. Co-founder Jean-Denis Greze noted that while AI has previously made individuals faster on their own, it is now enabling entire teams to synchronize and accelerate together.
