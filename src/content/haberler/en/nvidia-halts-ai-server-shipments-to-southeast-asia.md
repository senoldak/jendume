NVIDIA has reportedly put a halt on shipments of AI server hardware to four Southeast Asian countries, including Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand, and Indonesia. Manufacturing partners such as Dell and Supermicro informed clients in the region that the chipmaker is currently blocking server orders from being dispatched. It is unclear whether this stoppage is entirely temporary. The move follows closely behind the US Commerce Department's efforts to close regulatory loopholes that previously allowed Chinese labs to access powerful chips through regional cloud providers.

In recent years, Chinese AI entities leveraged gaps in US export controls by renting compute power through cloud providers in Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand, and Indonesia that housed NVIDIA GPUs. To counter this, the Commerce Department added Megaspeed, a company at the center of the controversy, to its Entity List. NVIDIA and other firms now require a special US license to ship regulated goods to Megaspeed. Meanwhile, the Justice Department is investigating whether Megaspeed violated export rules. Under the new framework, data centers serving restricted clients were given 90 days to acquire a proper license.

Industry executives point out that the sudden rollout has created significant confusion in the region due to a lack of clear execution guidelines. The surging demand from Chinese tech firms had previously triggered a massive data center construction boom across Southeast Asia. NVIDIA booked substantial financial figures through its regional footprint, reporting 104 billion dollars in revenue via its Singapore subsidiary in 2025, which marked a 72 percent increase from the previous year.

The suspension of shipments poses logistical challenges for regional cloud operators and tech infrastructure projects. As stakeholders scramble to navigate the new licensing requirements, the tighter enforcement shows how regulatory measures are reshaping the global semiconductor and artificial intelligence sector.
