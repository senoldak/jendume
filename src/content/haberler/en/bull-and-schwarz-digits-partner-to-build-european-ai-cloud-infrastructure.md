French technology company Bull and Schwarz Digits, the IT division of the Schwarz Group which owns Lidl and Kaufland, have announced a partnership to build a dedicated European AI and cloud computing infrastructure. Unveiled at the German government’s 2026 Digital Summit in Berlin, the collaboration aims to develop an end-to-end technology stack ranging from physical servers to software layers. The memorandum of understanding (MoU) signed by the two companies represents a strategic move to strengthen Europe's digital sovereignty.

Under the agreement, Bull will contribute its expertise in high-performance computing (HPC) and AI systems, while Schwarz Digits will provide its STACKIT cloud platform. The servers, which form the core of the infrastructure, will be manufactured at Bull’s facility in Angers, France. These systems will utilize Bull’s proprietary direct liquid cooling technology to maximize energy efficiency. Furthermore, Bull’s BullSequana AI platform will be made available to corporate and public sector clients through the STACKIT Marketplace.

The primary motivation behind this partnership is to ensure that data, particularly in strategic and regulated sectors, remains under European jurisdiction and control. The companies intend to demonstrate that Europe can deliver high-performance innovation on its own terms. Bull’s previous success in building the JUPITER supercomputer in Germany, described as Europe’s most powerful, serves as a significant technical benchmark for this new venture.

Future plans include the co-design of servers based on Open Compute Project (OCP) standards, integration with enterprise software like SAP RISE, and exploring the inclusion of quantum computing. Bull, which reports approximately €720 million in revenue and employs 3,000 staff across 32 countries, seeks to bolster Europe's technological resilience through this initiative. The announcement follows calls from German Chancellor Friedrich Merz for Europe to accelerate the development of its own AI capabilities.
