German Chancellor Friedrich Merz has emphasized that Europe must accelerate the development of its own artificial intelligence technology to avoid becoming dependent on external providers. Speaking at a conference in Berlin, Merz described digital sovereignty as an existential question for the continent. The goal is to develop and operate technology across the entire supply chain, from hardware to software, ensuring that Europe maintains control over the systems underpinning its economy. This shift is critical because countries that do not control their own AI infrastructure risk relying on those that do, potentially compromising their economic and political autonomy.

Germany faces significant hurdles in this transition. Economic research indicates that the country is not yet fully prepared to capitalize on the AI boom, citing a relatively small high-tech sector, weak digital public services, and a persistent reliance on paper-based administrative processes. To address these gaps, the government has established a dedicated ministry for digital affairs and is prioritizing digital transformation as a core government agenda.

Financial commitments are beginning to materialize, with major companies, including Amazon and Nvidia, pledging over €225 billion in investments. Furthermore, Germany has set an ambitious target to quadruple its AI computing power by 2030. Beyond domestic efforts, the government is seeking partnerships with like-minded nations, including France, Canada, and several Asian countries, to strengthen Europe's position in the global tech landscape. As Brussels revises its regulatory framework to compete with the United States, these strategic moves represent a pivotal effort to secure Europe's digital future.
