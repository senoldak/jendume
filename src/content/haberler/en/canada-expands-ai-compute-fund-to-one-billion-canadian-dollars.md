The Canadian government has announced an additional 700 million Canadian dollars for its AI Compute Access Fund, raising the total fund to 1 billion Canadian dollars from the previous 300 million. The expansion aims to help smaller companies cover the high costs of cloud computing required for artificial intelligence development and commercialization. The new phase offers larger subsidies specifically for companies utilizing Canadian-owned computing resources to stimulate local provider demand and build domestic capacity.

The initial funding round under this expansion focuses specifically on health and life sciences projects. Additionally, government officials announced that 45 companies received more than 52 million Canadian dollars from the fund's first phase, bringing the total support to over 118 million Canadian dollars for 89 firms across the country. These measures align with the broader national artificial intelligence strategy unveiled earlier.

Ottawa has also pledged to bring 500 megawatts of its own artificial intelligence computing online. Previous initiatives included backing local funding rounds and supporting regional businesses in adopting advanced technologies. Foreign entities have also shown interest due to the country's available energy and land resources for data centers.

Government officials emphasize that Canadian ideas require domestic computing power to secure the nation's technological future. By offsetting cloud-compute costs and favoring home-grown infrastructure, the fund ensures that local organizations can build, test, and run artificial intelligence products effectively within the country.
