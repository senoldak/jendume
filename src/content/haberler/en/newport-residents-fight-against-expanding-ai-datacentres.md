Dale Johnston and his neighbors in Newport, South Wales, find themselves living in the shadow of a massive grey datacentre that towers over their suburban homes. Built to provide the immense computing power required by advanced AI models, this facility is part of a nationwide datacentre boom in the UK. While local politicians herald these developments as job creators, residents like Johnston are deeply concerned about lost sunlight, constant noise from cooling systems, and the potential fumes from backup diesel generators.

Across the UK, hundreds of hyperscale datacentres are waiting to connect to the national grid. The government views this expansion as an inevitable revolution that must be embraced, establishing dedicated AI growth zones to accelerate planning and grid connections. However, anti-datacentre groups are springing up across the country, arguing that these resource-hungry and polluting facilities primarily benefit major technology firms while offering little tangible value to local communities.

Globally, spending on new AI facilities is projected to reach unprecedented levels, mirroring a broader international backlash seen in countries like the United States. As grassroots opposition grows, campaigners emphasize that alternative pathways, such as stricter regulations and publicly-owned data infrastructure, must be considered to balance technological progress with community welfare.
