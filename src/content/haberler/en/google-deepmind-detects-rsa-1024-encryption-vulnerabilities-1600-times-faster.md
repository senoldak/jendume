Google DeepMind researchers have developed a new AI-driven approach to identify vulnerabilities in the RSA-1024 encryption system, a cornerstone of digital security. Conducted in collaboration with the University of California San Diego and the French research center Inria, the study demonstrates that AI agents can uncover cryptographic weaknesses 1600 times faster than the best-known previous methods.

The research primarily focused on identifying security flaws in digital signature mechanisms. The AI agents proved that in scenarios where an attacker controls a significant portion of the signed data block, existing protection layers fail to provide the expected security. The developed algorithm not only offers a speed advantage but also improves resource efficiency, reducing processor time by 3.5 times.

Another significant aspect of the study focuses on batch factorization. Neural network agents improved the asymptotic efficiency of calculating multiple keys of the same length simultaneously. As a result, the analysis cost for each new 1024-bit key has been reduced by 30 times compared to traditional techniques. This implies that attackers could more easily distribute computational costs across different targets.

Experts emphasize that these findings do not affect 2048-bit and larger keys, which are widely used in online banking and messaging applications. However, the study serves as a reminder that cryptographic assumptions must be regularly re-evaluated. In the future, automated cryptanalysis methods are expected to become an essential stress-testing tool for evaluating the resilience of encryption standards.
