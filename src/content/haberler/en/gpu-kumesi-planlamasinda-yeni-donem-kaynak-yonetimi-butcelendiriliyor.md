Thousands of advanced graphics processing units used to train modern artificial intelligence models represent one of the most valuable and scarce resources in research laboratories. Traditionally, priority-based scheduling systems have often led to inefficiencies over time due to issues like resource hoarding and priority inflation.

To address these challenges, the infrastructure team at the Allen Institute for AI (Ai2) has transitioned from operational task-based scheduling to an administrative budgeting process. The newly implemented hierarchical system allows managers to proportionally allocate GPU time based on strategic research goals before workloads are even submitted.

This approach prevents hardware from sitting idle while ensuring that high-impact research receives the necessary computational power without disruption. By treating hardware allocation like an investment strategy, this model sets a new precedent for managing large-scale AI infrastructure efficiently.
