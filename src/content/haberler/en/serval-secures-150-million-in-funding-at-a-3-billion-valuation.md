Serval has completed a $150 million Series C funding round to scale its enterprise automation platform. Led by Sequoia, the round included participation from investors such as Elad Gil, Evantic, First Round, General Catalyst, Redpoint, Conviction, and Headline. Founded in 2024, the company has now reached a valuation of $3 billion.

The company aims to transform information technology (IT) operations using AI agents. Positioned as an alternative to traditional enterprise software platforms, Serval automates manual processes ranging from resolving technical issues to managing system access permissions and tracking hardware assets. This approach is designed to accelerate business workflows while reducing human error.

The company's product, Catalyst, allows businesses to design and manage their own automated workflows. This system keeps the AI agents' access to data and actions under the control of IT teams. For sensitive operations, a human-in-the-loop mechanism is implemented to ensure security and oversight.

Founders Jake Stauch and Alex McLeod designed the platform to reduce the daily workload of companies and increase efficiency. Serval aims to redefine the services offered by established giants like ServiceNow with a more modern, AI-centric approach. This investment is seen as a clear indicator of how rapidly AI agents are assuming a central role in the enterprise software landscape and reshaping the digital infrastructure of the business world.
