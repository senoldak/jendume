Zendesk has overhauled its pricing strategy for customer service software, transitioning to an outcome-based model. Instead of charging based on the number of users or seats, the company now bills businesses for each specific problem resolved or task completed by its artificial intelligence. Under this new framework, clients establish an outcome budget and pay exclusively for verified results. Zendesk monitors the quality and completeness of these tasks to ensure that the budget is allocated toward tangible business contributions. This allows companies to scale their usage gradually, increasing spending only as the AI takes on more operational responsibilities.

The platform also introduces the Open Service Directory, a public registry where brands can list their official support channels. This feature enables independent AI assistants to locate verified support resources, even for organizations that do not utilize Zendesk software. Furthermore, the new Bridge tool allows businesses to integrate external AI agents, such as ChatGPT, into their existing workflows. Administrators retain full control over agent permissions, defining specific actions like reading or updating tickets, while maintaining human oversight through an observability dashboard.

Recent research highlights the evolving relationship between consumers and automation. Data indicates that over half of individuals feel comfortable utilizing personal AI assistants for daily tasks. However, the vast majority of respondents maintain that human assistance must remain a readily available option. This initiative follows the acquisition of Forethought, marking a significant expansion in the company's AI agent capabilities. By linking costs directly to performance and providing tools for agent management, the company aims to foster a more transparent environment for human-AI collaboration.
