Xiaomi has reached a significant milestone in humanoid robotics technology. The company released a new video demonstrating its robots successfully completing a full eight-hour shift within an automotive factory. This development proves that these machines are capable of playing an active role not just in controlled laboratory settings, but in dynamic and unpredictable industrial environments.

The robots are tasked with part classification and assembly processes. The most notable feature is the system's ability to independently detect and correct anomalies. While traditional automation systems tend to halt when encountering deviations from pre-programmed paths, these next-generation robots can adapt to environmental changes in real-time. This capability prevents minor fluctuations on the production line from disrupting the entire workflow.

The power behind this system is a specialized model called Xiaomi-Robotics-0. This model is based on a "Vision-Language-Action" (VLA) architecture. With 4.7 billion parameters, this structure allows the robot to instantly convert visual input and instructions into physical actions. Consequently, the robot can successfully manage the process even when part positioning is not perfectly identical.

Compared to previous tests, this shift duration highlights the durability of the technology. The company had previously conducted a three-hour trial in a casting workshop. In this new test, the system achieved a success rate of 90.2%. This figure concretely demonstrates the robot's competence in precision assembly tasks.

Although no timeline has been announced for mass production or widespread deployment in factories, these trials indicate that humanoid robots are rapidly progressing toward becoming the factory workers of the future. The next objective is to scale this technology to longer shifts and integrate it into various other stations.
