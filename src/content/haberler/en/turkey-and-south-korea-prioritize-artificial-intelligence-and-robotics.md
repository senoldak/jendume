Technology cooperation between Turkey and South Korea has emerged as a central agenda item ahead of a presidential visit to Seoul, focusing heavily on advanced technological systems. In statements provided before the diplomatic meetings, Turkish leadership emphasized that artificial intelligence, semiconductors, and robotics will spearhead bilateral technological collaboration. The two countries are aiming for a balanced trade volume of 15 billion dollars, addressing a current economic deficit where imports significantly outweigh exports. South Korean investors are being encouraged to invest in electronics, semiconductor technologies, machinery, industrial automation, and biotechnology within Turkey. Concrete examples of ongoing industrial cooperation include electric vehicle production at Hyundai's facilities in Turkey and mutual interest in aerospace engine development such as the PD170 motor. Furthermore, smart transportation systems, railway logistics, and nuclear energy projects continue to be evaluated under bilateral memorandums of understanding signed during previous diplomatic visits. In 2025, Turkey's exports to Korea stood at 1.14 billion dollars while imports reached 10.26 billion dollars, highlighting the urgent need to bridge the trade gap through joint production and strategic investments. Previous agreements signed during bilateral visits continue to pave the way for feasibility studies in energy, wind power, and plasma technology. These collaborative efforts seek to accelerate Turkey's transition into advanced technology manufacturing, strengthen ties in the global AI hardware ecosystem, and foster deeper supply chain integration between industrial partners.
