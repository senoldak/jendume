The Epoch Capabilities Index (ECI), which tracks and compares the performance of AI models, has fundamentally updated its measurement methodology. ECI now employs a "Bayesian" statistical modeling approach to evaluate model performance. This shift aims to make the process of determining how capable AI models are relative to one another more transparent and reliable.

As AI models evolve rapidly, comparing them through standard benchmarks becomes increasingly complex. Different models exhibit varying strengths across different tests. ECI adapts the "Item Response Theory" (IRT) framework—originally developed for educational testing to compare students across different exams—to the field of artificial intelligence. This system normalizes a model's success based on the difficulty of the benchmark, creating a standardized capability score.

The previous methodology focused on finding fixed values that provided the best fit for the observed data. However, this approach often struggled to fully capture uncertainties when calculating capability gaps between models. The new Bayesian modeling offers probabilistic estimates, allowing for a more mathematically precise determination of the likelihood that one model is more capable than another.

This update provides a more scientific foundation for debates surrounding which AI models are the "best" or most capable. It is particularly effective at distinguishing performance differences among frontier models—the most advanced systems currently available. The ECI team notes that this change will help in better understanding model capabilities and provide a more robust framework for tracking the progress of artificial intelligence.
