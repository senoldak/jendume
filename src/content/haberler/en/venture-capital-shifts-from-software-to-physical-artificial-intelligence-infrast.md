The first week of November 2026 kicked off with notable investment rounds and acquisitions focusing on the physical foundation of artificial intelligence. Three of the four transactions announced on the first business day disclosed a combined value reaching 466 million dollars, while the financial terms of the Salesforce and Cresta agreement remained undisclosed. These figures clearly indicate that investor focus is evolving from pure software model development toward the physical infrastructure required to keep artificial intelligence running.

The day's transactions spanned four distinct areas. Northwood Space secured 375 million dollars in a Series C round, while Tutor Intelligence raised 75 million dollars in a Series B round for warehouse and factory robots. Additionally, Omen AI acquired Aquatic Labs for 16 million dollars to bolster data center cooling technologies. Northwood's round alone accounted for over eighty percent of the total disclosed funding, with the company stating the new capital will scale production and expand its field network.

Over the past two years, the vast majority of artificial intelligence investments went to model developers. However, this recent wave proves that the layers powering, transporting data for, and cooling the hardware are gaining critical importance. For instance, Tutor Intelligence offers warehouse robots through an hourly rental model rather than direct sales, transforming robotics from a heavy capital expenditure into a manageable operating expense for businesses.

This industry-wide evolution is further supported by major platforms acquiring vertical artificial intelligence startups. Cresta's acquisition by Salesforce demonstrates that consolidation in customer service and agent technologies is accelerating. Prominent venture funds making multiple investments in physical infrastructure and robotics on the same day clearly confirm that global capital views hardware and field technologies as the primary growth frontier.
