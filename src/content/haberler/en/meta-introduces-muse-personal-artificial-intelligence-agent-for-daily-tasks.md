Meta has introduced Muse, a new personal artificial intelligence agent designed to help users fill out forms, organize calendars, pay bills, and make reservations. Described as an agent built for everyone, the tool arrives as artificial intelligence adoption accelerates globally. However, this push for mass integration raises important questions about human psychology and cognitive health. Psychologists point out that excessive reliance on large language models and artificial intelligence tools can lead to cognitive biases, depleted brain chemistry, and the atrophy of neurological skills. A phenomenon known as cognitive offloading occurs when frequent artificial intelligence use bypasses the mental effort required to build deep neural connections. The brain depends on struggle and overcoming failure to rewire and strengthen its pathways, and eliminating this friction reduces overall cognitive reserve over time. Social media platforms are meticulously designed to maximize user attention, leading individuals to trade their privacy for immediate psychological rewards. To combat over-reliance, experts suggest introducing intentional friction into daily routines, such as removing artificial intelligence applications from phones, limiting screen time, and dedicating specific hours to screen-free activities to keep the brain sharp. While these digital tools provide immense everyday convenience, delegating critical thinking tasks to automated systems ultimately diminishes our long-term cognitive resilience and problem-solving capacity.
