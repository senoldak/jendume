Google DeepMind has achieved two significant breakthroughs in the field of artificial intelligence. The company has launched Gemini Robotics 2.5, a model designed to enable robots to operate with greater precision and safety in the physical world, and AlphaGenome 2, a system capable of analyzing genetic data to open new avenues in disease treatment and drug development.

Gemini Robotics 2.5 is engineered to help robots transition from controlled laboratory settings into dynamic, real-world environments where they can collaborate alongside humans. The new model enhances dexterity, reliability, and safety. In real-world proof-of-concept tests at Mercedes-Benz, the model achieved a 96% success rate in packing automotive parts. Furthermore, it completes tasks 20% to 30% faster compared to previous versions. The model is designed to work natively with various robot platforms, including those from partners like Apptronik and Boston Dynamics.

Meanwhile, the AlphaGenome 2 model marks a revolutionary step in biological research. By predicting how DNA sequences and variations affect human cell types, the system aims to accelerate drug discovery. The model now includes DNA methylation, a key aspect of epigenetics, providing deeper insights into the origins of diseases. Experts from the University of Cambridge highlight that the model's ability to adapt to new single-cell data without retraining makes it a powerful tool for genetic research.

These advancements prove that artificial intelligence is not limited to generating text or images; it is now providing tangible solutions in critical fields such as physical robotics and life sciences. With these models, DeepMind aims to elevate both industrial automation and the pace of discovery in the medical world.
