Logistics company GXO has announced plans to accelerate the deployment of adaptive technologies across its facilities in the US in 2027. The rollout will begin in priority sites and expand over the coming 12 months. The plan includes various automation solutions such as pallet mobile robots and robotic arms, which will handle repetitive material transport and tasks like unloading and packaging.

GXO Americas and Asia Pacific CEO Michael Jacobs stated that modern supply chains require efficient and adaptable solutions. GXO and Apptronik are working on the deployment of up to 1,000 Apollo humanoid robots over a multi-year period. The company also previously expanded its partnership with Agility Robotics to use Digit robots in North Carolina and Tennessee facilities. Digit robots had previously moved over 100,000 totes in a Georgia facility.

According to company executives, these investments ensure that supply chains become more efficient and adaptable. The spread of warehouse automation contributes to better workforce planning and higher service levels. Apptronik CEO Jeff Cardenas stated that they achieved 96 percent efficiency in logistics tasks when compared against human performance benchmarks.

On the other hand, GXO CEO Patrick Kelleher acknowledged that humanoid robots have not yet provided a return on investment. Alongside humanoid robots, GXO has also deployed the Exotec Skypod system for fashion brand Guess, an Autoload system in Poland, and Dexory's automated inventory robot in the Netherlands, which can save significant labor time for customers.
