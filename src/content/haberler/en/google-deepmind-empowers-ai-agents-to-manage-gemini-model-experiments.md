Google DeepMind has implemented a strategic shift in its development process, granting AI agents the authority to run experiments for its upcoming Gemini models. According to VP of Research Koray Kavukcuoğlu, the lab has moved away from maintaining separate teams for specialized projects like AlphaFold or nuclear fusion. Instead, the organization is now relying on a single, general-purpose algorithm to handle both scientific research and product development. In this new framework, each generation of Gemini is treated as a hand-built, unique architecture, while parallel teams simultaneously work on its successors.

Under this new operational model, AI agents are permitted to control a portion of the compute resources to execute experiments independently. Despite this autonomy, Kavukcuoğlu notes that the lab remains far from achieving full recursive self-improvement. To ensure safety, DeepMind does not reward the agents' internal thought streams, often referred to as "Chain of Thought", during the training phase. This deliberate choice ensures that the agents' intentions remain unfiltered and accessible for review in natural language before any actions are taken.

Kavukcuoğlu also addressed the current limitations of the technology. While modern AI can visually depict complex physical processes like fluid dynamics and collisions with high accuracy, it lacks a precise, fundamental understanding of these phenomena. He argues that no existing benchmark can accurately predict when models will bridge these gaps to reach Artificial General Intelligence (AGI). In his view, the era of AGI will only truly begin when everyday AI products earn enough public trust for people to delegate significant decisions to them, mirroring the trust placed in human collaboration.
