Massive data centers powering artificial intelligence models require significant amounts of water for cooling systems. New research reveals that one in four data centers worldwide is located in areas where water demand already exceeds available supply. These facilities use evaporative cooling systems to remove heat from equipment, a process where a portion of the water evaporates and does not return to the local water system. This highlights that artificial intelligence, beyond being a digital cloud technology, leaves a substantial physical footprint on the world's resources.

During hot and dry periods, the water demand of data centers can conflict with the needs of cities and agricultural regions. Local communities face the risk of their water supplies being strained by large technology facilities. Of the 9,347 data centers studied, 41% are located in cities, while 28% are in agricultural areas. An analysis of the Potomac River basin shows that data centers consume an average of 4 million gallons of water per day, but this demand can spike to 15 million gallons on peak days.

Under a scenario of 3°C of global warming, the share of data centers exposed to water shortages could rise to one-third. Experts point out that a lack of standardized reporting on water usage makes it difficult for local governments and residents to assess the impact of these projects. It is emphasized that communities should demand clarity on how a proposed facility will affect local water sources and how it plans to manage consumption during droughts before construction begins. Transparency is emerging as a fundamental requirement for both environmental sustainability and social acceptance.
