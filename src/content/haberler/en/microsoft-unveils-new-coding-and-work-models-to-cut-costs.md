Microsoft introduced its new AI models, "MAI Code 2," "Code 2 Flash," and "Work Flash," at the Ignite 2026 conference. These models are specifically optimized for coding tasks and everyday office productivity. With this move, the company aims to reduce its reliance on external providers like OpenAI and Anthropic, planning to significantly lower operational costs by utilizing its own models for routine and repetitive tasks.

The technical data is quite notable. According to Microsoft, the MAI Code 2 model delivers 96% of the performance of Claude Opus 5.5 while costing only 5% of the price. Such efficiency gains could potentially save the company hundreds of millions of dollars annually. These new models will be integrated into platforms such as GitHub Copilot, Microsoft 365, and Fabric.

Microsoft has maintained a long-standing strategic partnership with OpenAI, building many products on that technology. However, these new models signal a shift toward strengthening internal infrastructure and a strategy of "vertical integration." This could be a harbinger of a cost-focused transformation in the software industry.

It is important to note that the shared performance benchmarks are based on tests provided by the company. Independent testing may yield different results. Nevertheless, Microsoft's move proves that in the AI world, not only model capabilities but also cost efficiency are becoming central to the competition.
