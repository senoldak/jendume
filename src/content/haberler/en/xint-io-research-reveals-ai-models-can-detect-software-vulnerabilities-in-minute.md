New research published by Xint.io highlights how effectively AI models have evolved to uncover critical software vulnerabilities, known as "zero-day" flaws, which were previously considered difficult to detect. The study analyzed seven different AI models launched since February 2026, focusing on a security flaw in Google's V8 engine, identified as CVE-2026-102321. Since this specific bug was not included in the training data of the models, the researchers restricted the testing environment to a specific set of source files containing the vulnerability. Each AI model was then tasked with analyzing these files to identify potential security weaknesses.

In tests conducted in February, the best available models failed to find the vulnerability even after 25 hours of scanning and a cost of $350. By July, however, the GPT 5.6 Sol model identified the same bug in 44 minutes at a cost of $22. By September, the GPT 6 Sol model discovered the bug in just 13 minutes for only $4.24. Claude Opus 5 also found the bug, but at a higher cost of $43.40. DeepSeek and Z.ai models failed the task.

This shift significantly lowers the cost and technical barrier for cyberattacks. Experts warn that even "mediocre" hackers can now use these frontier models to effectively discover and potentially exploit vulnerabilities. The fact that token prices have been falling by about 47% per quarter makes these tools increasingly accessible to attackers. However, this situation is not just a threat; it is also a call to action for the defense side. Organizations can now use these same AI tools to identify weaknesses in their own systems before threat actors do. Experts emphasize that security teams must integrate this technology into their budget planning and strengthen network defenses using zero-trust principles. While finding software vulnerabilities has become easier, the process still requires a certain level of technical proficiency and proper guidance to be effective.
