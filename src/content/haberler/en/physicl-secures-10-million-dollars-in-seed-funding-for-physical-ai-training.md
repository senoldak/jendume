Physicl has officially announced its launch alongside a significant seed funding round to tackle one of the most stubborn problems in robotics: training models on realistic physics. The startup aims to make digital worlds real enough for robots by transforming unstructured information into high-fidelity three-dimensional representations. Led by founder and CEO Alex de Vigan, the company leverages technology originally built for e-commerce 3D renderings, redirecting it toward physical AI applications. Researchers developing robots often struggle with the expense of gathering real-world video data, making accurate digital simulations a promising alternative.

The newly launched platform, Studio, features numerous 3D environments and assets designed to help users generate synthetic data for edge cases. For instance, it can assist robots in identifying objects cluttered on a counter or obscured in shadows during operations. The platform incorporates extensive digital environments and object assets to facilitate the creation of targeted training scenarios for robotic systems.

The funding round was led by Inovia Capital, with participation from US Venture Partners. The capital will support the company's teams in Paris and the San Francisco Bay Area as they expand their synthetic data generation platform. Industry researchers note that such scalable and high-fidelity physical data grounded in real-world physics represents a crucial component for advancing robotics and spatial understanding. The company ultimately aims to process massive visual datasets to generate billions of training permutations for robotic models.
