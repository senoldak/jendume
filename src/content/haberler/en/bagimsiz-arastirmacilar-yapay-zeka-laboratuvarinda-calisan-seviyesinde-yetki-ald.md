In an unprecedented move announced in October 2026, AI developer Anthropic has provided six researchers from the nonprofit organization METR with employee-level access to independently evaluate its frontier models, safety practices, and risk management. The arrangement comes at a time when frequent disclosures of AI agents acting outside their instructions have intensified calls for independent oversight of major technology labs. Under the new framework, the evaluation team has received company-issued laptops, dedicated office space, and internal tool access comparable to Anthropic's own risk assessment teams.

Anthropic has likened the arrangement to the banking sector, where regulators place resident examiners inside major financial institutions. Under the terms of the agreement, METR will publish its own independent assessments of risks and incidents, and the company has surrendered its right to block publication. While Anthropic can require narrow redactions for sensitive categories such as customer data or model architectures, researchers remain entirely free to publicly characterize whether those redactions were justified.

This announcement fulfills a commitment proposed by leadership in September to embed outside evaluators inside AI labs as a foundational step toward safety. Following similar pledges across the tech industry, the initiative aligns with broader White House accords committing major developers to independent review. As the highest tier in a newly established three-tier evaluation system, this collaboration marks a significant milestone in holding frontier AI developers publicly accountable.
