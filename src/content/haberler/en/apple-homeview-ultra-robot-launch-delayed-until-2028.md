The highly anticipated HomeView Ultra smart home robot from Apple is facing significant delays, with reports suggesting the launch could be pushed back as far as 2028. The primary obstacle is not the hardware, which has reportedly been ready for nearly two years, but rather the software, which has yet to reach the level of polish required for a consumer release. Apple is reportedly unwilling to ship a product that offers an incomplete experience.

Internal software teams have pushed for the delay, aiming to narrow the device's feature set to ensure reliability. The focus is shifting toward core capabilities such as FaceTime integration and basic home automation, such as controlling lights. This strategy reflects a commitment to delivering a device that performs a few key tasks flawlessly rather than a complex, buggy product.

Codenamed J595, the device is expected to feature a 9-inch screen mounted on a robotic arm. Equipped with cameras and sensors, the robot is designed to track users during FaceTime calls. While earlier reports suggested a design reminiscent of Pixar's iconic desk lamp, the current focus remains on perfecting the interaction between the software and these physical components.

From a financial perspective, the device is positioned as a premium product. The price is expected to exceed 1,000 dollars, with potential increases depending on the cost of components like RAM. This robotic venture represents Apple's latest attempt to define the next generation of smart home technology, where hardware and AI-driven software converge to create a more intuitive living environment.
