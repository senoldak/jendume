Baseten has announced the release of joey-1, the latest addition to a specialized category known as 'decision models.' Unlike traditional AI models designed to generate long-form text, joey-1 is optimized to answer yes-or-no or multiple-choice questions, providing a specific answer along with a probability score. This focused approach allows the model to operate significantly faster and at a fraction of the cost of general-purpose models.

Developed by Base Labs, joey-1 is a 2-billion parameter open-weights model. It joins the larger mammoth-1 and elephant-1 models released by the company. The model is specifically designed for high-volume tasks such as routing support tickets, detecting spam, or filtering harmful prompts. Built on Alibaba’s Qwen3.5-2B architecture, joey-1 was trained on a dataset of 6.21 million decision examples to ensure accuracy in its specific domain.

In terms of cost and performance, joey-1 sets a new benchmark. Priced at just $0.01 per million tokens, the model can process a typical 2,000-token request in approximately 20 milliseconds. This makes it about 2.5 times faster than the elephant-1 model. According to Baseten, sorting 10 million support tickets a month would cost roughly $50 with joey-1, compared to $500 using the mammoth-1 model, representing a significant saving for enterprise users.

Baseten claims joey-1 is the strongest decision model with fewer than 3 billion parameters. It currently ranks 37th out of 230 models on the Jev Decision Index, holding the top spot for models under 4 billion parameters. With Microsoft recently launching its own Qwen-based decision model at $0.042 per million tokens, Baseten’s aggressive pricing highlights the growing competition in the AI infrastructure market. The company, which raised $1.5 billion at a $13 billion valuation in June, continues to expand its footprint in the AI sector.
