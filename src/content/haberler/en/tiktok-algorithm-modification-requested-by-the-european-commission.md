The European Commission has formally requested that TikTok modify the algorithm used in its recommendation feed. According to the Commission, the current system of suggesting similar content based on watch history and likes can cause negative themes related to sadness, loneliness, and body image to appear repeatedly. Officials state that this cycle can negatively impact the mental health of sensitive users and children.

The Commission noted that existing measures are insufficient to mitigate these risks and called for changes to the recommendation system. Key proposals include prioritizing user-specified preferences and removing video watch time as a signal used in recommendations. Furthermore, the institution emphasizes that user controls for the content feed must be made more accessible and the algorithm should undergo regular testing.

This inquiry is part of a broader investigation conducted by the European Union into TikTok. Authorities warn that if violations are confirmed, TikTok's parent company, ByteDance, could face fines of up to 6 percent of its total annual global turnover. This regulatory move marks a significant step in the ongoing efforts to oversee and limit the societal impacts of automated recommendation engines.

AI-driven recommendation systems on social media platforms have increasingly become the focus of regulatory bodies worldwide. By continuously analyzing user habits and feeding similar content, these systems can expose young individuals to potentially harmful loops repeatedly. The step taken by the European Commission intensifies the pressure on platforms to design systems that prioritize transparency and user safety.
