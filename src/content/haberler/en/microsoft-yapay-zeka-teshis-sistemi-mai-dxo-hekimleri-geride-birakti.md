Microsoft has unveiled MAI-DxO, a new artificial intelligence system that demonstrated higher diagnostic accuracy than physicians in complex medical cases. According to a peer-reviewed study published in Science, the system achieved an 83.2% accuracy rate on New England Journal of Medicine cases, compared to 36.1% for generalist physicians using reference resources. The research evaluated both accuracy and cost metrics across multiple foundational models and real-world clinical scenarios.

The system coordinates multiple AI agents that act like a panel of doctors, questioning patients, ordering tests, and debating diagnoses. Microsoft AI CEO Mustafa Suleyman described the result as an important step toward "medical superintelligence," emphasizing that healthcare delivery currently faces a growing mismatch between the demand and supply of medical expertise. Company executives noted that orchestration helps optimize both accuracy and operational costs.

The study examined performance across various underlying AI models. When powered by newer models such as GPT-5.5, MAI-DxO increased accuracy on complex cases while maintaining efficient costs. However, performance improvements were less pronounced in emergency department cases where baseline models already scored high. Weaker models also benefited significantly from the orchestration layer, showcasing broader optimization capabilities across different tiers of artificial intelligence.

Microsoft has established a new partnership with Beth Israel Deaconess Medical Center in Boston to evaluate the technology's effectiveness in real-world urgent care and emergency settings. The company is also working with the Mayo Clinic to train a foundational healthcare model, aiming for major breakthroughs in clinical decision support and medical records management. Suleyman expressed strong confidence in the rapid evolution of healthcare models over the coming years.
