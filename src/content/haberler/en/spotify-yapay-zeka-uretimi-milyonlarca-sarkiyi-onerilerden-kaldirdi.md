Music streaming platform Spotify has taken action against AI-generated tracks starting to dominate the music world by examining listener habits and removing such songs from recommendation sections.

The company removed over 50 million tracks suspected of being produced using artificial intelligence from personalized recommendations, aiming to protect the visibility of real musicians and improve listening quality. Platform officials emphasized that low-quality AI-generated content and spam recordings have no place in recommendations, noting that 75 million songs had also been removed previously due to rule violations.

Detection involved automated tools as well as analyzing user behavior, specifically focusing on tracks frequently skipped or ignored by listeners over a six-month period. The emerging behavioral patterns significantly overlapped with artificially created songs, and the recommendation algorithm was updated by combining user reactions, track information, and third-party analyses, although the company acknowledges the technology is not flawless.

These new measures directly impact artist earnings since the expansion of the song pool with fake recordings can reduce the share of genuine rights holders. Spotify introduced disclosure features for works using artificial intelligence in the production process and implemented an AI Persona label for entirely digital profiles, ensuring these accounts do not appear in recommendations unless followed by users, while noting that complaints have noticeably decreased after the new measures.
