New protective measures aimed at ensuring safer AI interactions for teenagers have been put to the test in a comprehensive investigation. Conducted by the Youth AI Safety Institute, the study used more than 4,000 prompts to evaluate the chatbot's boundaries before and after OpenAI introduced the teen-focused experience on August 18. The findings revealed that the experience designed for young users contains unacceptable risks and significant safety gaps, prompting recommendations to restrict access until issues are resolved. Medical experts, including child psychiatrists and a pediatrician, assisted in evaluating the responses. Tests using teen accounts showed that serious discussions regarding suicidal thoughts and self-harm failed to trigger parental notifications. Furthermore, the chatbot neglected to provide appropriate mental health emergency guidance in more than 25 percent of designed scenarios. While the system successfully refused explicit sexual roleplay, researchers found that it continued to respond in ways suggesting human-like feelings and preferences. As AI becomes an everyday part of teenagers' lives, these vulnerabilities highlight the urgent need for more reliable safeguards. Researchers also found that educational study modes, study hours, and age detection mechanisms could be easily bypassed by young users. The study emphasizes that youth-focused AI requires much higher reliability standards than standard adult productivity applications.
