Artificial intelligence systems are expanding from software development into the complex engineering tasks of the physical world. Three separate studies published by OpenAI, Google Cloud, and Google DeepMind reveal that AI agents are now being deployed in areas such as circuit board design, structural calculations, and molecule development. Rather than simply answering isolated questions, these systems aim to manage multi-day workflows from start to finish.

OpenAI announced that its GPT-6 Astra model designed a device enclosure and printed circuit board without direct human intervention. While noting that prototyping times have significantly decreased, the company acknowledged that gaps remain in areas such as manufacturing tolerances. Meanwhile, Google Cloud introduced the Physical-Engineering's Last Exam test, developed in partnership with Mercor, consisting of 85 tasks. None of the 14 frontier models tested managed to exceed 50 percent success, with the top result reaching 48.3 percent.

Google DeepMind unveiled the Cheminformatics Skills package aimed at drug discovery workflows. Tests involving lung cancer treatments showed that computational workflows which traditionally take days were completed in roughly one hour. However, DeepMind emphasized that all proposed designs still require rigorous experimental verification.

The findings collectively demonstrate that while AI serves as a powerful accelerator in engineering projects, human supervision remains vital for safety and validation.
