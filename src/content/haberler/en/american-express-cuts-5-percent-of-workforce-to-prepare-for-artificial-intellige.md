American Express is cutting about 5 percent of its global workforce and taking a 336 million dollar restructuring charge as part of its preparations for artificial intelligence. Company executives stated that the restructuring aims to make the organization leaner and more agile by reducing management layers and increasing spans of control before automating processes. Management emphasized that operational structures must be optimized before introducing automated systems across the enterprise.

The company emphasized that it will not let AI agents run rogue through its systems. To protect customers and merchants, American Express will only allow outside software and agents to access its systems through approved API or Model Context Protocol channels. This follows the publication of its Intent Intelligence framework, which sets out guidelines to confirm customer purchasing intentions before an AI agent completes a transaction. These safety protocols highlight the strict governance required as financial institutions adopt autonomous agent technologies.

Financial results released alongside the announcement showed that revenue rose 9 percent to a record 20.1 billion dollars, while net income increased 28 percent to 3.7 billion dollars. A portion of the restructuring costs was funded by pre-tax gains from selling the company's stake in Global Business Travel Group. The restructuring highlights the broader financial sector trend of leveraging artificial intelligence for personalized customer experiences while strictly managing operational risks and efficiencies.
