Europe needs to foster its own local technology champions to remain competitive and capture value in the rapidly evolving AI economy. Alexander Rinke, co-founder and co-CEO of Celonis, emphasized this necessity during his appearance at the HumanX event in Amsterdam in September 2026. According to Rinke, the concept of "sovereign AI" is critical for Europe, meaning that organizations and public agencies must maintain full control over their data and AI infrastructure, specifically by owning their model weights and keeping sensitive information within their own environments.

Rinke argues that creating local AI leaders is essential to prevent the economic gap between Europe and the United States from widening further. This strategy is not driven by a lack of trust in American technology, but rather by the desire to ensure that the economic upside of the AI revolution is captured locally. Celonis implements this vision by helping customers tune open-source models for specific business tasks without sending process data outside of their secure environments. The company claims that this approach can yield results equal to or better than those achieved by frontier models.

The core of Celonis's strategy lies in what it calls "process intelligence," a technology that acts like an x-ray machine for business data, mapping how work is actually performed across systems. The company recently acquired Ikigai Labs, an MIT spin-off, to integrate specialized models for tabular and time-series data. These models serve as tools for large language models, assisting with complex tasks such as supply chain forecasting or insurance claim settlements.

Reflecting on the company's journey, Rinke stresses that startups should prioritize building for customers rather than chasing venture capital. Founded 15 years ago and now employing approximately 3,500 people, Celonis advocates for a growth model rooted in solving real-world problems. Rinke believes that even in a booming market like AI, proving the value and repeatability of a product before scaling is the most fundamental requirement for long-term success.
