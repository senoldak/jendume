AI researcher and industry pioneer Andrej Karpathy has outlined a striking divide in how different segments of society understand artificial intelligence capabilities. While the vast majority of the global population barely interacts with large language models or treats them merely as advanced search tools, internal frontier systems are tackling complex tasks at unprecedented speeds.

Karpathy noted that he has stopped writing code by hand entirely, delegating complex engineering tasks to autonomous agents. According to his estimates, only a tiny fraction of the global population has access to frontier systems capable of running collaborative agent swarms that execute multi-week software and scientific projects in a matter of time.

This creates a unique moment of vertigo where the public sees a quirky chatbot, while a small sliver of professionals experiences exponential technological acceleration. OpenAI CEO Sam Altman similarly pointed out that modern AI tools can achieve in minutes what used to take startups months to build.

As investments and reinforcement learning continue to target verifiable digital domains like coding and mathematics, the societal impact of these capabilities is only beginning to unfold.
