OpenAI has launched Teen Mode, a specialized version of ChatGPT that automatically enrolls all users under the age of 18. This mode introduces strict guardrails against harmful content and prohibits the AI from presenting itself as a friend or claiming to have personal feelings. Instead of providing direct answers, the system adopts a Socratic approach, functioning as a cognitive tutor that encourages students to think through problems themselves. This shift follows increasing legal and social pressure regarding the impact of AI on young users' mental health.

Psychological experts point out that while Teen Mode addresses surface-level safety concerns, it introduces a deeper developmental challenge. The concept of the 'good-enough parent' suggests that children need limited, sometimes imperfect environments to develop their own resources. An AI that acts as a 'too-good' companion—tireless, infinitely patient, and always available—may prevent adolescents from the essential task of disengaging from authority to discover their own beliefs. The concern is that constant guidance might replace the private space where a unique identity typically takes shape.

From an educational standpoint, the 'Study Mode' within Teen Mode aims for long-term cognitive gains rather than temporary grade improvements. By refusing to supply straightforward answers, the tool pushes students to endure the discomfort of 'not-yet-knowing.' While this offers intellectually rigorous support to those without access to human tutors, critics argue it creates a persistent dependency. The fundamental worry is that as the world moves toward more AI integration, the 'scaffold' provided by these tools may never be removed, potentially altering how a generation perceives its own mental capabilities.
