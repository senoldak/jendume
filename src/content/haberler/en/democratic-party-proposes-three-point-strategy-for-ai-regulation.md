Nathan E. Sanders and Bruce Schneier have proposed a three-point policy strategy for the Democratic Party to address the regulation and societal impact of artificial intelligence. The proposal argues that AI is a technical issue and a central force driving economic disruption and political corruption. Voters are increasingly demanding government action, with surveys showing that 84% of voters believe AI poses a threat to American workers. Furthermore, 61% of Republicans support stricter regulation, showing that this concern crosses party lines.

The first plank of the proposed strategy focuses on restraining the power of big tech companies. It specifically addresses the environmental and energy impacts of large datacenters. The authors cite a UK experience in which a datacenter developer secured over 200 million pounds in public guarantees before failing to meet renewable energy promises, demonstrating why binding agreements are necessary. The proposal suggests levying an excise tax on the energy consumed by these facilities, with the recovered funds directed toward payroll tax reductions and unemployment insurance.

The second plank advocates for the development of "public AI" models under democratic oversight, as an alternative to corporate-driven systems. These models would be incentivized to create public good rather than private profit, ensuring ethical compliance and fair compensation for content creators. The third plank focuses on empowering voters and workers regarding where and how AI is deployed. This includes reforms such as giving workers a significant share of board seats in large corporations to influence decision-making on AI usage.

This policy framework provides a roadmap for the Democratic Party ahead of the 2028 election cycle to challenge the influence of tech billionaires and align the party's platform with public demands. Sanders and Schneier emphasize that taking immediate legislative action is essential to demonstrate a commitment to the change voters are demanding and to prevent tech companies from prioritizing shareholder value over the public interest.
