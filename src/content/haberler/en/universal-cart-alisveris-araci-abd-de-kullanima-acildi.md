Google has announced that Universal Cart, a tool that lets users collect and monitor products found across search and within its AI application, is now available to users in the United States. Initially previewed at an event in May, the tool aims to make online shopping easier to manage by allowing users to save items they like and return to them later without having to hunt down the same products again.

The tool functions as a central hub for browsing and tracking potential purchases by gathering items from multiple retailers. Additionally, it helps shoppers stay on top of deals and availability by sending notifications when a saved product drops in price or when an out-of-stock item returns to shelves.

During the payment process, the system can factor in credit card perks and surface relevant promo codes to assist users in choosing how to pay. Checkout is tied to the Universal Commerce Protocol (UCP), an open standard introduced earlier in the year designed to help AI agents and retailers collaborate across various stages of the shopping journey, including checkout.

Google continues to deepen its investment in AI-powered shopping, with Universal Cart serving as its latest effort to enhance convenience. It joins features like conversational product discovery and automated store inventory checks. As various technology companies introduce agentic commerce features, the industry is increasingly focused on making chatbots practical tools beyond basic question-answering.
