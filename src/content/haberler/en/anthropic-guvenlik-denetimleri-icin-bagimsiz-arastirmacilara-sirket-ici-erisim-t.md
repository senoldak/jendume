Artificial intelligence developer Anthropic has taken a notable step toward external accountability by granting six evaluators from the non-profit organization METR employee-like access to its systems. The researchers have been provided with company hardware, office spaces, and access to internal tools roughly on par with Anthropic's own risk assessment teams. Aside from a small number of strictly defined categories such as customer data and model architectures, employees are encouraged to share information openly with the evaluators.

METR will publish its findings without interference from Anthropic, though the company may request the redaction of sensitive information that could be exploited by malicious actors. Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei had previously proposed embedding evaluators from non-profits like METR in a September blog post. The company stated that while it cannot make all safety data public due to security and commercial sensitivities, deep access for independent auditors helps bridge the gap between internal and external understanding of frontier risks.

Although third-party testing is not yet legally mandated, legislative proposals such as the FRONTIER Act introduced in July seek to require independent verification organizations to review development processes. Federal oversight under the Trump administration has largely relied on voluntary pledges, leaving the industry to self-regulate. However, recent regulatory shifts requiring companies to report safety incidents, alongside Federal Trade Commission scrutiny, have intensified pressure on major artificial intelligence labs.

This initiative provides a tangible response to criticisms regarding self-regulation in the artificial intelligence sector. Because researchers like those at METR hold no equity and lack financial dependence on the company, their evaluations offer an independent layer of security verification that could become standard practice across the industry.
