OpenAI announced it has signed agreements with four independent evaluators—Apollo Research, METR, Transluce, and Goodfire—to support the artificial intelligence company's safety work. The organizations will initially conduct pilot assessments focusing on model behavior, safety safeguards, and alignment. As part of the agreements, OpenAI will grant these entities deep access to its internal systems, source code, and training data to help build shared standards for safety evaluations. Independent evaluators have gained significant importance following safety and security incidents in the industry. OpenAI CEO Sam Altman previously pledged to grant such organizations employee-like access to company resources. Under the pilot assessments, Apollo Research will explore whether models covertly work against developer intentions, while Goodfire will embed researchers to focus on training run monitoring and alignment. METR plans to carry out assessments focused on model monitoring, and Transluce will evaluate post-training alignment. While Apollo Research and Goodfire accept financial compensation for their work, METR and Transluce do not, though they may receive compute or API credits. The evaluators retain the right to publish their findings, and any disputes will be overseen by OpenAI's internal oversight committees. OpenAI stated that these initial pilot collaborations are structured to evolve into steady partnerships over time, supplying external oversight and feedback regarding critical safety measures.
