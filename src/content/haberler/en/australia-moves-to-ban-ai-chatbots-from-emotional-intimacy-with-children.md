Australia's parliament is advancing a new digital duty of care bill designed to prevent AI chatbots from fostering emotional intimacy and dependence among children. The legislation successfully passed the House of Representatives, moving next to the Senate for further review and potential amendments. The chatbot rules were introduced via specific legislative amendments proposed by independent MP Kate Chaney, which the government accepted to address growing concerns regarding children substituting human relationships with AI companions. The wording specifically addresses minors and does not extend restrictions to adult users engaging with AI companions.

Prime Minister Anthony Albanese urged parliament to act decisively, highlighting stories of individuals identifying non-person chatbots as their best friends as a major wake-up call for lawmakers. The broader framework establishes comprehensive online safety standards for social media platforms, games, applications, and chatbots, replacing several existing codes with a unified duty of care obligation. Communications Minister Anika Wells emphasized that the regulations aim to keep Australians safe online by capturing harmful content, although the bill faced opposition from parties criticizing the legislative approach.

The Greens support the bill and have initiated a formal Senate inquiry scheduled to report in late November, with Labor expressing hope for final Senate approval by the end of 2026. Alongside existing national measures such as the under-16 social media ban, this new regulatory step reflects broader international concerns regarding the psychological impact of AI companions on younger users and aims to establish strict digital boundaries.
