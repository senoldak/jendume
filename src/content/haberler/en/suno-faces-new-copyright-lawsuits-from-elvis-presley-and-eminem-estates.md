Suno, the AI-powered music generation platform, is facing a significant legal challenge regarding copyright infringement. The estates of Elvis Presley and the publisher representing Eminem have filed two separate lawsuits in federal court, alleging that the platform used their copyrighted music without authorization. These lawsuits have reignited intense debates regarding the legitimacy of the data used to train AI models and the protection of artists' vocal identities.

The complaints, filed by Elvis Presley Enterprises and Eminem’s publisher Eight Mile Style, allege that Suno scraped millions of songs and lyrics from the internet without permission. The Elvis estate specifically invokes the ELVIS Act, a Tennessee law designed to protect an individual's voice, arguing that the platform produces songs that mimic Presley’s unique vocal identity. Meanwhile, the Eminem publisher claims that the platform’s output replicates lyrics almost word-for-word.

In its defense, Suno maintains that its platform is designed to help users create original music and that it actively blocks the use of specific artist names or songs in prompts. The company argues that training on music constitutes "fair use" and points to licensing deals it has secured with major industry players like Warner Music Group. However, the plaintiffs argue that these safeguards are easily bypassed and that the platform encourages users to replicate specific artist styles.

This legal battle represents a critical turning point for the AI industry. The music sector is witnessing a major clash between companies claiming to democratize creativity and rights holders fighting to protect the labor and identity of artists. The court's ruling could set a definitive precedent for how future AI models select training data and how the digital assets of artists are protected in the age of generative intelligence.
