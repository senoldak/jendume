Google cofounder Sergey Brin announced during a discussion that the company's Gemini models have started contributing to the creation of subsequent versions. Brin pointed to recent releases as evidence that researchers and models are working together to accelerate progress. Recursive self-improvement means artificial intelligence assists in building better systems, which speeds up the release pace of new models. A safety and security report published by Google DeepMind indicates that AI now automates certain parts of research, though overall research acceleration remains low for now, with risk rated as currently low.

According to the report, artificial intelligence authors a majority of production code and bug fixes at Google DeepMind, while still requiring human oversight and review. Fully delegated pull requests increased from 7.5% in April to 11.9% in September, showing a steady rise in automated coding tasks. About 88% of tasks still require human steering or human-written code. Humans remain key to choosing research directions, designing new experiments, and diagnosing training problems. Argon solves only around half of the challenges in internal tests, showing that full automation requires further advances.

While industry observers debate the timeline for fully recursive self-improvement, researchers interviewed by Gladstone AI note that human guidance remains essential for setting research directions and solving complex training problems. As computational power and investments grow, the role of AI in its own evolution continues to expand. Meanwhile, political discussions around regulating self-improving technologies are emerging in the United States, with lawmakers proposing bills and companies calling for international standards to ensure safety and alignment.
