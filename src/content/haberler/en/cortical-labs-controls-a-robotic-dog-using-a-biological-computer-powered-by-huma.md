Living cells have been utilized to create a biological computer capable of controlling the movements of a robotic dog. Its creators note that using cells to replicate the digital neural networks of artificial intelligence models could yield more efficient and adaptable computers. The start-up Cortical Labs has developed a biological computer named CL1, which can be trained to navigate toward a target in a simulated two-dimensional environment. In subsequent tests, this training data was deployed to direct the movements of an actual robotic dog. Independent researchers who were not involved in the project state that a paper published by the company, which has yet to undergo peer review, demonstrates improvements in the neural network over time despite being fed identical digital data, suggesting successful training. Cortical Labs previously demonstrated its technology in 2021 by playing the computer game Pong. However, these recent experiments indicate potential applications within physical robotics as well. The company highlights that biological computers could operate with lower energy consumption compared to digital counterparts, offering a distinct advantage in robotics where battery life remains a primary constraint. The cells utilized within CL1 computers originate from stem cells generated via donated human blood. The development team states there is no evidence of pain or suffering within these systems, though they acknowledge the need for ongoing careful monitoring alongside independent groups.
