One of the primary challenges in developing quantum artificial intelligence is the limitation of hardware resources and the accumulation of errors as systems scale. Traditional approaches require adding more physical units (qubits) to increase network capacity, which leads to complex systems that are difficult to control and prone to errors. Researchers from the University of Science and Technology of China have developed an alternative solution that increases processing capacity without increasing the number of physical units.

The research team constructed high-dimensional quantum neurons by utilizing the internal energy levels of physical units. Unlike conventional encoding methods that typically use only two energy levels, this new approach incorporates multiple energy levels of a unit directly into computational processes. By leveraging quantum features such as superposition and entanglement, this method offers a more efficient machine learning architecture that bypasses hardware constraints.

During the experiment, the team constructed a multilevel quantum processor using five energy levels of a calcium ion. This system was trained to classify clothing images from the Fashion-MNIST dataset. In tests conducted on 395 samples, the system achieved a classification accuracy of 95.7%. The results showed that while the two classes overlapped in the output space before training, they were clearly separated on opposite sides of the decision boundary after training.

This study demonstrates that the internal energy levels of quantum systems can serve as controllable, trainable computational resources. This approach to overcoming hardware bottlenecks paves the way for more powerful and scalable solutions in quantum AI applications by enhancing the capacity of small-scale quantum systems. The researchers suggest that this method could become a fundamental building block for developing more complex quantum artificial intelligence models in the future.
