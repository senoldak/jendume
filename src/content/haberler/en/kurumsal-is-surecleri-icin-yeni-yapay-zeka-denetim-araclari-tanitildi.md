Enterprise software provider ServiceNow has announced new governance mechanisms designed to help businesses manage autonomous artificial intelligence agents securely. Revealed in October 2026, the updates introduce a cryptographically signed credential system called AI Passport, which defines an agent's identity, permitted actions, and accessible systems to ensure tools remain within approved boundaries.

Developed in partnership with Nvidia, the infrastructure links enterprise policies with execution-time enforcement. For instance, a customer service agent can be configured to automatically process refunds below a specific threshold while routing higher requests for human review. If an agent exceeds its authority, organizations can pause, restrict, or deactivate it by revoking credentials.

In addition to governance, the company updated its EmployeeWorks portal, allowing workers to build specialized assistants and automate routine tasks without writing code. A new ToolSmith environment translates natural-language instructions into integrations across more than 75,000 application programming interfaces. Furthermore, new cost management capabilities track spending on frontier models and infrastructure by user and department.

These developments address the growing challenge of managing shadow AI and fragmented tool usage within large organizations. Companies like NTT Data are beginning to adopt these governance platforms to simplify access and oversight. While financial resilience features are available now, AI Passport and cost management tools are slated for release later this quarter.
