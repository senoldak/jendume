Major banks and retailers are starting to push back against a new wave of customers: artificial intelligence personal agents sent by consumers to find better savings, shop for deals, and manage subscriptions. Institutions such as JPMorgan Chase, Amazon, Charles Schwab, Neiman Marcus, and Kohl's have taken steps to block agents like Meta Muse from operating within their digital storefronts and customer accounts.

Industry experts suggest that blocking AI agents may not be a winning long-term strategy for businesses. Research by Accenture indicates that 65 percent of consumers are open to using financial assistants powered by generative AI, and 71 percent would welcome an AI assistant embedded in their mobile banking app. Meanwhile, an audit by Billions Network of 51 large U.S. retailers found that only 11 have established a deliberate policy regarding AI agents, with many blocking them inadvertently through older bot rules.

Security concerns and fraud risks are frequently cited by companies implementing these blocks. Payment network executives point out that fraudulent agents could steal credentials, while legitimate agents might execute unintended transactions. However, analysts note that these restrictions also reflect corporate desires to protect profit margins and control data access within their digital ecosystems.

In contrast, some retailers like Walmart are welcoming AI agents, ensuring they see the same prices and offers as human customers. This evolving landscape highlights a tension between security compliance and consumer convenience as agentic artificial intelligence reshapes commerce and banking.
