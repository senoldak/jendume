Security company XBOW successfully utilized AI agents running OpenAI's GPT-5.6 Sol model to discover a critical exploit chain in Google's Chrome browser within six days. The achievement earned the company a 250,000 dollar reward from Google's Chrome bug bounty program. The developed attack chain combined three previously unknown bugs, bypassing browser sandbox layers to execute code on Windows computers.

This milestone demonstrates the transformative power of artificial intelligence in the cybersecurity sector. Complex vulnerability chains that previously required months of painstaking work by teams of human researchers can now be identified much faster with the assistance of AI agents. XBOW reported that the computational cost for this six-day effort was approximately 40,000 dollars.

Throughout the process, the AI agents scanned Chrome's source code, tested ideas, and built proofs of concept, while human researchers provided context, set targets, and guided the results. Google has since addressed all three bugs and shipped security updates to users. According to company estimates, the vulnerabilities had exposed roughly one billion Chrome users on Windows before the patches were applied.

Industry experts point out that since the AI model used in this process is publicly accessible, restricting powerful models is no longer an effective solution. Instead, the focus must shift toward helping defenders find and fix critical vulnerabilities faster using the same advanced technological capabilities.
