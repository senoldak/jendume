Syncere, a Palo Alto-based robotics startup, has raised $11 million in pre-seed funding to accelerate the production and delivery of Lume, a robotic floor lamp designed to perform household chores. The funding round was led by a16z Speedrun, Andreessen Horowitz’s early-stage program, with participation from Draper Associates, Physical World, Sequel, and SeaX Ventures. The company intends to use the capital to fulfill its first orders, expand manufacturing, and adapt its core robotics technology for commercial use cases.

Lume functions as a sculptural floor lamp by day and transforms into a mobile domestic assistant when needed. It is designed to navigate home environments to handle repetitive tasks such as folding laundry, tidying rooms, and making beds. Starting at $2,699, the robot features an onboarding process that allows owners to teach it specific preferences and help it adapt to the unique layout of their homes. Syncere emphasizes that the robot's capabilities will improve over time through software updates, eliminating the need for frequent hardware replacements.

Aaron Tan, co-founder and CEO of Syncere, points out that home robotics faces a deployment challenge often mistaken for a data problem. He argues that while robots require real-world data to become reliable, they cannot access that data until they are trusted enough to enter people's homes. Lume aims to break this cycle by entering homes as a functional piece of furniture first. This strategy allows the company to gather essential real-world usage data to refine its AI models while providing immediate value to the consumer.

Founded less than a year ago by researchers from Stanford, Harvard, and UC Berkeley, Syncere has quickly moved from concept to production. The founding team includes veterans from companies like Apple, AMD, and GM. Lume has already garnered significant attention, being named one of TIME’s Best Inventions of 2026 and securing millions of dollars in pre-orders. While competitors like Norway’s 1X offer humanoid robots for $20,000, Syncere is betting that a more affordable, furniture-integrated approach will lead the way in household autonomy.
