Accidents where wildlife intersects with transportation routes pose severe risks to both animals and critical infrastructure like trains and airplanes. Traditional deterrents often fail as animals grow accustomed to them, inspiring a new approach from Stockholm-based innovators.

Originating from an AI lab at KTH Royal Institute of Technology, the startup builds edge devices equipped with motion sensors and cameras. These devices process data locally to identify approaching animals and emit species-appropriate sounds to scare them away safely.

The system learns from experience and varies its responses to prevent animals from habituating to repeated signals. Currently, hundreds of these devices are deployed across airfields, railway corridors, and critical infrastructure in North America, Europe, and Australia.

The company plans to scale its operations and integrate the software onto mobile machinery such as trains and drones. This expansion marks a step forward in bringing physical artificial intelligence out of controlled environments and into the natural world.
