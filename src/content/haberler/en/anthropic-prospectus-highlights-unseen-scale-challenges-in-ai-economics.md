As Anthropic PBC prepares for what could be one of the largest initial public offerings in history, its prospectus lists dozens of detailed risk factors. From existential threats to complex security challenges, the document outlines numerous warnings for potential investors. However, one critical economic concept is noticeably absent from the list: diseconomies of scale, which occur when a company's average cost per unit increases as it grows.

Historically, enterprise software and cloud computing benefited immensely from low marginal costs and traditional economies of scale. As cloud providers expanded their operations using commodity servers, the unit cost of delivering storage and compute decreased over time, leading to high profitability. Frontier artificial intelligence model vendors, by contrast, face a fundamentally different economic reality that disrupts this established playbook.

Unlike traditional cloud businesses, artificial intelligence companies require specialized, expensive hardware from day one. As models rapidly advance, their processor requirements continue to accelerate, meaning vendors cannot rely purely on commodity hardware to achieve cost efficiencies. Furthermore, the human side of the industry faces similar pressures. While cloud providers could leverage existing administrative personnel more efficiently as they grew, frontier artificial intelligence firms must continuously scale up their expert workforce with deep specializations for each new model generation.

These combined hardware and human capital pressures create unprecedented economic challenges for frontier model developers. As token costs become an increasingly significant expense for consumers and businesses, companies are forced to seek out greater efficiency or alternative lower-cost models. Whether the industry can eventually reach a plateau where traditional economies of scale take over remains one of the defining financial questions for future investors.
