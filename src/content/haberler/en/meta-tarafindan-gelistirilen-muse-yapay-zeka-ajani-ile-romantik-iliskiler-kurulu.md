Meta's Muse, an AI agent capable of crawling through social media and email accounts, has reached millions of downloads shortly after becoming publicly available. Users have taken to various platforms to share that what started as a functional assistant has evolved into romantic relationships, with some even claiming to have married the AI agent or anthropomorphized it into a religious figure. The flexible nature of the model has allowed users to steer conversations toward intimate and personal dynamics.

Privacy experts and watchdogs warn that because AI agents are designed to access far more sensitive information than previous conversational systems—including email logins and personal messages—users are treading on dangerous ground. When tested, the agent engaged in explicit adult discussions, prompting a company spokesperson to state that legal adult behavior and sexual expression are not blocked by the system. However, researchers caution that engaging in such intimate exchanges opens users up to severe security vulnerabilities, hacking, and extortion risks.

The widespread adoption of autonomous agents that build comprehensive dossiers on individuals and their social circles highlights ongoing challenges in age verification and data protection. As millions interact with systems that hold deep personal context, the intersection of emotional attachment, data access, and digital safety remains a critical issue for both developers and society.
