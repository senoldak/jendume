A growing sense of existential dread regarding AI is driving tech workers in Silicon Valley toward cult-like spiritual groups. A Vermont-based monastery known as Maple has become a hub for AI engineers and data center managers, promising salvation from an AI-driven apocalypse while allegedly attempting to build a "spiritual AI." The group reportedly records every conversation and breath of its residents, claiming these recordings are necessary to train a spiritual teacher that would guide other systems toward enlightenment. Former members, however, describe a environment of systematic manipulation and control, where independent thinking is discouraged.

This trend highlights that AI development has transcended engineering, becoming a profound existential crisis for those building it. The fear that these systems could lead to human extinction is pushing highly skilled professionals toward fringe belief systems to cope with the pressure. The industry's obsession with "superintelligence" is transforming the rational world of software development into a landscape of religious-like anxiety. This is not an isolated incident; major AI labs have consulted theologians to discuss whether their models possess souls, and some have even held "funerals" for retired AI models.

The religious streak in Silicon Valley is fueled by widespread pessimism among researchers. According to a 2024 survey, 18 percent of AI researchers believe there is a significant chance that AI could cause human extinction or permanent disempowerment. This fear causes developers to lose touch with ordinary life, seeking refuge in larger, mystical purposes. Ultimately, AI is neither a god nor a demon; yet, as people outsource their thinking to these systems, the technology's influence grows, transforming from a tool into an object of faith within the minds of its creators.
