As artificial intelligence tools continue to automate more parts of our daily lives, our reliance on these systems steadily grows. Students use ChatGPT for homework, programmers turn to Claude for code generation, and regular users are now experiencing AI agents firsthand with the launch of Meta's personal assistant Muse. As this trend continues, questions arise about where it ultimately leads.

Experts warn that society is on a path of slowly ceding control to AI models, a phenomenon dubbed 'gradual disempowerment.' The threat extends beyond mere job displacement, potentially affecting human cognition and agency itself. Jay Chooi, founder and CEO of Robocurve, noted that this issue sits at the very top of his list of concerns, leaving many people feeling powerless.

Jan Kulveit, a researcher at Charles University and coauthor of the paper coining the term, argued that this dynamic does not stem from a deliberate power grab by AI, but is instead incentivized by the perceived value these systems bring to economic and cultural functions. Research has increasingly linked reliance on AI tools to diminished critical thinking skills and lower brain activity during cognitive tasks.

Hugging Face AI research scientist Margaret Mitchell pointed out that when people stop practicing the skills delegated to AI agents, they lose those abilities entirely and become fully dependent. Experts emphasize that the true risk of automation is not necessarily a rogue AI taking over, but rather humans willingly surrendering their cognitive autonomy through everyday convenience.
