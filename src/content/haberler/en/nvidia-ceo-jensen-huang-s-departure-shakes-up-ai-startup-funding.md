Jensen Huang, who co-founded Nvidia and led the company for 33 years, has officially stepped down, sending shockwaves through the artificial intelligence ecosystem. Historically, the company has been a prolific investor, allocating substantial funds to startups and participating in numerous funding rounds as a leading financial backer. Investors warn that Huang's absence leaves the startup ecosystem without one of its primary champions and with significantly reduced capital available for new ventures.

Nvidia's interim CEO, Colette Kress, is expected to adopt a more conservative approach, particularly regarding investments in AI startups positioned at the core of massive data center build-outs. Under Huang, Nvidia backed major players like CoreWeave, Mistral AI, Hugging Face, and Reflection AI, frequently acting as the largest investor. Experts note that a potential slowdown in these investments, especially in regions outside of America, could create a major funding and ecosystem gap.

While investors hope that alternative cloud providers and chipmakers might step in to fill the void, many doubt that major tech rivals will allocate hundreds of millions to pre-revenue infrastructure startups in the manner Nvidia previously did. With Nvidia anticipated to write smaller checks and exercise greater selectivity, traditional venture capitalists may also hesitate before backing high-valuation startups.

Despite these profound structural shifts, industry observers agree that Nvidia's investment slowdown will not single-handedly halt the broader AI boom. Founders and leaders emphasize that the talent pool and the momentum of the ecosystem remain resilient beyond any single individual.
