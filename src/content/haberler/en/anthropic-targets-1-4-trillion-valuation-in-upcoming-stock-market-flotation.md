Anthropic, the developer behind the Claude chatbot, is positioning itself for a major milestone as it prepares for its stock market flotation. The company is targeting a valuation of up to $1.4 trillion in its upcoming initial public offering (IPO) on Wall Street. While market expectations had previously hovered around the $2 trillion mark, investor sentiment has cooled recently, leading the company to set more conservative targets as questions mount regarding the durability of the current industry boom.

To achieve this, Anthropic aims to raise up to $64.4 billion by offering 825 million shares, priced at up to $78 per share. Beyond the public offering, the company has secured a separate private placement deal with key cornerstone investors, including Google’s parent company, Alphabet, and chipmaker Nvidia, which is expected to bring in an additional $26 billion. Trading of the company's shares is anticipated to commence as early as next week.

This financial maneuver comes at a critical juncture for the industry. Investor jitters have intensified following recent US midterm election results, which saw victories for candidates opposed to large-scale data center developments. These political outcomes have exacerbated existing concerns about whether companies can generate a timely return on their massive spending on infrastructure. Analysts suggest that the market is currently using these events as an excuse to correct valuations that had become overheated due to the rapid pace of development.

Despite these market pressures, Anthropic’s internal performance remains robust. As of October 31, the company reported an annual revenue of $81 billion, marking a $10 billion increase from the previous month. While these figures demonstrate significant operational growth, the broader debate over the financial sustainability of the sector continues to influence investor appetite. As the company prepares to join the ranks of major publicly traded firms, such as SpaceX, which holds a valuation of $1.78 trillion, this IPO serves as a significant litmus test for the future of the industry.
