Gabriel Ocana Santero, founder of biotech startup Exin Therapeutics, recently connected an AI agent to a custom-built surgical setup to successfully perform a brain injection on a living mouse. During the experiment, the AI agent controlled a motorized immobilization device, a camera, an injection needle, and the delivery of a gene therapy compound aimed at reducing seizures in an epileptic mouse.

The research team asked the AI model to choose a treatment from nineteen options in a spreadsheet. After a veterinary surgeon performed an initial craniotomy, the AI agent selected the therapy, brain region, and injection coordinates, subsequently managing the robotic arms and injection parameters. The team initially tested Anthropic’s Claude Opus 5.5 model, which refused to guide the needle due to safety concerns, prompting researchers to switch to Claude Sonnet 5, which agreed to proceed.

The procedure was not without technical hiccups. A glitch disrupted the equipment coordinate system, and although the agent recognized the erroneous position, its safety instructions prohibited it from modifying the underlying code. The team resolved this by using a separate AI agent on Opus 5 to update the code without disclosing the live surgery context, extending the surgery duration from a typical forty-five minutes to slightly over an hour.

Despite these challenges, the researchers were impressed by the agent's performance and plan to test other models like Grok. This experiment highlights the growing integration of AI agents into physical laboratory environments, allowing them to interact directly with real-world equipment while maintaining human oversight for safety.
