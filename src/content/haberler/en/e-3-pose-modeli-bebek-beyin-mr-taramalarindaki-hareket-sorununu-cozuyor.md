Monitoring fetal brain development during pregnancy presents difficulties due to continuous movement within the womb. While routine prenatal ultrasounds flag potential abnormalities for magnetic resonance imaging, movement artifacts frequently lead to missed or incorrect clinical diagnoses. To resolve this specific challenge, scientific teams have created a deep learning architecture designed to handle motion during fetal scans.

Named E(3)-Pose, the system embeds fundamental physics principles directly into its neural network rather than simply learning physics parameters from raw data. By integrating the left-right symmetry of the fetal head into the core architecture, the model accurately determines brain orientation even when the fetus shifts position. This research project was officially presented at the European Conference on Computer Vision.

Project participants noted that shifting heads during imaging procedures create coverage gaps in two-dimensional image slices, hindering physicians from properly reviewing critical brain regions. Researchers collaborated closely with clinical experts to acquire specialized MRI sequences and test the scanning system directly on hardware.

Currently trained primarily on third-trimester fetuses, the scientific team aims to expand the model to image second-trimester fetuses, which move more frequently and represent a vital window for early medical intervention.

The research received financial and operational support from multiple academic funds, research centers, and national health institutes.
