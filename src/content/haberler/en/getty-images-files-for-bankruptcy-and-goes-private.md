The ability of artificial intelligence to easily generate desired images and videos through both free and paid subscriptions has pushed traditional media platforms into financial distress. Getty Images, which has long stored media content for a fee and paid royalties to creators, reached a point where it could no longer cover its expenses and filed for bankruptcy. The company, which also owns iStock, had to take this step due to mounting costs and debt burdens.

Getty Images, carrying approximately $2.1 billion in debt, had previously planned to merge with Shutterstock, but that deal was canceled due to costs. Under the bankruptcy process, the Getty family and Koch Equity Development will form a new partnership with a $533 million budget to take the company private, removing it from the stock exchange.

The restructuring process, including clearing debts and going private, is expected to be completed by the end of the year. During this transition, stock media services will continue without interruption, with the ultimate goal of making the platform competitive again.

This process clearly demonstrates how artificial intelligence technologies threaten traditional business models and force even long-standing companies into major structural transformations. The ongoing financial contraction in the stock photography sector holds the potential to fundamentally reshape copyright relations between media creators and platforms in the future.
