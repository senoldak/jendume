German Chancellor Friedrich Merz and NVIDIA CEO Jensen Huang met at the Kraftwerk venue in Berlin to outline Germany's national AI roadmap and discuss industrial applications. As part of the Invest in Germany Summit, federal ministries signed a Joint Declaration of Intent with NVIDIA to advance AI models for physical systems, bridging Germany's engineering heritage with modern artificial intelligence.

The initiative focuses on empowering small and medium-sized enterprises, known as the Mittelstand, to leverage their operational data through AI integration. Chancellor Merz emphasized that opening company doors to AI data usage presents a major national advantage. Jensen Huang noted that AI serves as a powerful augmenting instrument rather than a replacement for human capability, praising Germany's scientific and mathematical excellence.

During the announcement, NVIDIA revealed new investments in four German startups as part of a broader nearly $10 billion commitment across Europe. The newly backed companies include Agile Robots, Avelios Medical, Mirelo, and Reflex Aerospace. These startups operate across robotics, healthcare workflows, audio generation, and high-performance satellite design.

Additionally, Siemens announced a partnership with NVIDIA to advance autonomous manufacturing using AI solutions at its Erlangen facility, achieving significant cost savings and reduced engineering efforts. Meanwhile, researchers utilized Europe's largest supercomputer, JUPITER at the Jülich Supercomputing Centre, to run a record-breaking 104-qubit fault-tolerant quantum simulation.
