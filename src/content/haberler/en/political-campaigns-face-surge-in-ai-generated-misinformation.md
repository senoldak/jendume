The use of artificial intelligence in election cycles has introduced a new form of misinformation often referred to as "slopaganda." The widespread use of AI-generated clips and images in political campaigns is creating significant monitoring challenges for major internet platforms. Current data indicates that these deceptive contents are spreading rapidly, making it increasingly difficult for voters to distinguish between reality and fabrication.

A recent analysis reveals that more than $187 million has been spent on nearly 300 ads that were generated or enhanced by AI. The report found that over 90% of this spending came from Republican candidates or associated groups. However, experts warn that this figure is likely an undercount, as many of these advertisements lack the necessary disclosure labels required to identify them as synthetic content.

Platforms are adopting varying approaches to this issue. While some companies require prominent labels for AI-generated political content, others place the responsibility entirely on the advertiser. The lack of comprehensive federal legislation exacerbates the problem, leading to inconsistent enforcement across different services. Although some state-level laws exist, candidates do not always comply with these requirements.

Furthermore, the susceptibility of AI models to generating misleading content remains a critical concern. Studies testing various popular models have shown that they can be manipulated to produce deceptive political material. While some progress has been made in safety protocols for certain models, the potential for these tools to undermine election integrity persists. Although several legislative bills have been introduced in Congress to address this issue, none have advanced out of committee. This ongoing stalemate highlights the widening gap between rapid technological advancement and the regulatory frameworks intended to govern it.
