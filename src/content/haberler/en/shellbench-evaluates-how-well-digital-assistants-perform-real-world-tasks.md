Digital assistants are no longer just answering questions; they are increasingly taking on complex tasks like managing emails, organizing calendars, and analyzing documents. However, the reliability of these agents in real-world conditions has remained a significant question. ShellBench was developed to address this gap. Built upon more than 10 million anonymized user requests, this testing system evaluates agents across 100 distinct real-life scenarios. Researchers assess these models not only on the accuracy of their answers but also on their ability to complete tasks thoroughly and operate securely.

The test results reveal a reality of the current industry. Even the most advanced models struggle to meet all requirements in approximately 40 percent of the assigned tasks. Finding the right information is insufficient; the agent must use that information to complete the task flawlessly while adhering to security protocols. Notably, on the security front, 15.7 percent of the trials failed to pass at least one security check. This indicates that AI agents have not yet reached a level of fully autonomous reliability.

The balance between cost and performance is another critical finding. For instance, there can be more than a fivefold difference in processing costs between two models with similar success rates. This suggests that businesses and individual users need to select models based on their specific workload and economic efficiency rather than just popularity. ShellBench indicates the transition from the era of 'correct answers' to the era of 'correct actions' in the AI world. Success is measured by how intelligent a model is and by how accurately it can perform complex, security-sensitive tasks in the real world. These tests are expected to become a standard benchmark for proving model reliability.
